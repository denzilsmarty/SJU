## Overview

Saint Joseph's University (SJU) has articulated a formal alignment between the Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm (IPP) and its institutional mission, undergraduate learning outcomes, and *ALWAYS. FORWARD. Strategic Plan 2026*. This article explains those connections in detail, addresses whether adopting the IPP requires changing SJU's existing mission statement, and maps IPP language onto SJU's core learning outcomes. It also examines how the Universal Apostolic Preferences (UAPs) of the Society of Jesus shape SJU's institutional priorities.

---

## 1. How the IPP Connects to the *ALWAYS. FORWARD.* Strategic Plan 2026

SJU's *ALWAYS. FORWARD. Strategic Plan 2026* explicitly names Ignatian pedagogy and the Universal Apostolic Preferences as institutional priorities.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] The strategic plan calls for:

- **academic excellence**
- **expanded experiential learning**
- **integrated academic and co-curricular opportunity**
- **belonging and human connection**
- **formation in Ignatian spirituality and pedagogy**
- **the Universal Apostolic Preferences**
- **future-focused adaptation**[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

The SJU working document *Forming the Future* argues that the IPP can function as an **integrating architecture** across all of these aims.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] The IPP's five dynamics—context, experience, reflection, action, and evaluation—map directly onto strategic priorities:

| Strategic Priority | IPP Dynamic(s) Most Directly Engaged |
|---|---|
| Experiential learning | Experience, Action |
| Belonging and human connection | Context, *cura personalis* |
| Ignatian pedagogy formation | All five dynamics |
| Academic excellence | Experience, Reflection, Evaluation |
| Future-focused adaptation | Context (AI, digital ecosystem) |
| Universal Apostolic Preferences | Context, Action |

Importantly, the IPP does not replace existing strategic instruments. It provides a **common process language** that connects the General Education Program, Cornerstone Core Curriculum, professional accreditation standards, and unit-level strategies without superseding any of them.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

## 2. Institutional Learning Outcomes the IPP Supports

SJU's undergraduate learning outcomes include:

1. **Communication**
2. **Critical thinking and inquiry**
3. **Ethical reasoning and social justice**
4. **Ignatian values and diverse perspectives**[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

The *Forming the Future* document states explicitly that "the SJU undergraduate learning outcomes in communication, critical thinking and inquiry, ethical reasoning and social justice, and Ignatian values and diverse perspectives can all be designed and assessed through repeated IPP cycles."[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

The IPP's recursive logic makes it a design and assessment framework *for* these outcomes, not a parallel or competing framework. Faculty design experiences, structure reflection, and assign action tasks in ways that generate assessable evidence for each outcome category.

## 3. How IPP Language Maps onto Communication and Critical Thinking Outcomes

### Communication

The IPP's **Action** dynamic explicitly encompasses communicating findings to a public audience, creating something useful or truth-bearing, and advocating through organized civic response.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Action is defined broadly to include "applying a concept to a novel problem; revising a design, argument, diagnosis, or performance; ... communicating findings to a public audience."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Within the Arts, Design, and Communication domain, the document notes that making and performance create rich cycles of experience, critique, revision, and public action, with evaluation integrating craft, concept, process, response to critique, and responsibility to subjects and audiences.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

### Critical Thinking and Inquiry

The **Reflection** dynamic is the primary home of critical thinking within the IPP. Reflection is defined as the disciplined process by which learners grasp the significance of experience, drawing on memory, understanding, imagination, and feeling; examining causes, assumptions, motives, implications, alternatives, and consequences; and seeking greater freedom for choice.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

A disciplined sequence embedded in the IPP's reflection stage maps directly onto critical thinking outcomes:

1. **Attention**: What does the evidence actually show?
2. **Interpretation**: What patterns, causes, assumptions, or concepts explain it?
3. **Interior response**: What may my response reveal or conceal?
4. **Ethical and social meaning**: Who benefits, who bears risk, whose voice is absent?
5. **Discernment**: Which response is more truthful, responsible, and conducive to human flourishing?[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

The document explicitly warns that "reflection without knowledge becomes opinion; action without analysis becomes impulse," underscoring that the IPP treats critical inquiry as foundational, not optional.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Sample strong learning outcomes illustrating the IPP–critical thinking connection include:

> "Students will analyze how a decision distributes benefits and risks among stakeholders, consult affected perspectives, and justify a proportionate response."[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

> "Students will use relevant evidence and theoretical frameworks to explain a structural inequity and evaluate at least two interventions, including likely unintended consequences."[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

These formulations replace vague character claims (e.g., "students will understand social justice") with assessable reasoning tasks, making IPP-designed outcomes directly compatible with standard critical thinking rubrics.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

## 4. Does Adopting the IPP Require Changing SJU's Existing Mission Statement?

**No.** The *Forming the Future* document is explicit: the IPP does not replace or alter the SJU mission statement, the General Education Program, the Cornerstone Core Curriculum, professional accreditation frameworks, learning outcomes, or unit strategies.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

SJU's mission already commits the University to:

- rigorous, student-centered education rooted in the liberal arts
- personal excellence, professional success, and engaged citizenship
- whole-person care, critical thought, ethical decision-making, social justice, and finding God in all things[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

The document's proposed institutional statement of the IPP for SJU is written *to serve* this mission, not to revise it:

> "At Saint Joseph's University, the Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm is a shared, adaptable way of designing and reviewing educational work. Rooted in the Jesuit tradition and open to people of all faiths and convictions, it begins with the concrete learner and context; creates rigorous encounters with knowledge, practice, persons, and the world; cultivates disciplined reflection; supports free and responsible action; and evaluates learning, impact, and design in order to begin again. It serves SJU's mission to prepare students for personal excellence, professional success, and engaged citizenship through education of the whole person."[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

The document explicitly labels this an "SJU interpretation proposed for discussion" and notes it is not a quotation from a Society of Jesus document.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] The IPP functions as a **common process grammar** layered across existing institutional architecture, not a substitute for it.

## 5. How the Universal Apostolic Preferences Shape SJU's Institutional Priorities

The Society of Jesus's Universal Apostolic Preferences (UAPs) for 2019–2029 identify four global calls:

1. **Show the way to God through discernment and the Spiritual Exercises**
2. **Walk with the excluded**
3. **Accompany young people in creating a hope-filled future**
4. **Collaborate in care for our common home**[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

The *Forming the Future* document clarifies that the UAPs do not alter the five IPP dynamics. Instead, they **sharpen the contexts, experiences, and actions** to which Jesuit education must attend.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

At the institutional level, the *ALWAYS. FORWARD.* strategic plan explicitly names the UAPs as a priority alongside Ignatian pedagogy.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] This means that the UAPs function as a horizon-setting lens for which **contexts** SJU investigates (e.g., excluded communities, ecological limits), which **experiences** it designs (e.g., accompaniment of youth, care for creation), and which **actions** it authorizes and evaluates (e.g., civic engagement, community partnerships).

The connection can be visualized as follows:

| UAP | IPP Implication for SJU |
|---|---|
| Discernment and the Exercises | Deepens the Reflection and Action dynamics; informs *cura personalis* |
| Walking with the excluded | Shapes Context inquiry; demands attention to who is absent from institutional processes |
| Accompanying young people | Reinforces advising, mentoring, and co-curricular formation through all five dynamics |
| Care for our common home | Adds ecological consequence to Reflection and Evaluation; informs Action toward sustainability |

The UAPs thus operate one level above the IPP: they define the *moral and apostolic horizon* within which SJU uses the IPP to design educational, co-curricular, and institutional practice.

## 6. The Four Cs and Institutional Formation Outcomes

The ICAJE document *Human Excellence* (2015) articulates the desired qualities of Jesuit-educated persons as four terms—**conscience, competence, compassion, and commitment**—known as the Four Cs.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] These are formation qualities or habits, not sequential stages and not additional IPP dynamics.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Repeated IPP cycles are the *means* by which SJU education can contribute to these qualities over time. The relationship is:

- **Conscience** ← developed through disciplined Reflection, ethical analysis, and Discernment
- **Competence** ← developed through rigorous Experience and Evaluation in disciplinary practice
- **Compassion** ← cultivated through Context attention, *cura personalis*, and encounter with affected communities
- **Commitment** ← expressed through Action that is proportionate, free, and accountable

The document cautions that SJU should not substitute local or unofficial variants of the Four Cs for the globally promulgated ICAJE formulation.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## 7. *Cura Personalis* as an Enabling Condition

*Cura personalis*—care for the whole person in their concrete particularity—is not a sixth IPP dynamic. It is the **relational condition** that makes each dynamic possible and honest.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] It requires knowledge of learners, trust, appropriate challenge, and adaptation to persons within communities and structures. The document warns against reducing it to "being nice" or treating it as synonymous with individualized instruction.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

At the strategic level, *cura personalis* connects to SJU's commitment to belonging and human connection named in *ALWAYS. FORWARD.*[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

## 8. Institutional Commitments If SJU Adopts the IPP

If SJU formally adopts the IPP as a shared educational framework, the working document proposes eight institutional commitments:

1. **Disciplinary rigor**: IPP work meets the epistemic and professional standards of the field.
2. **Whole-person attention**: Learning engages intellect, affect, imagination, embodiment, relationship, and responsibility as appropriate.
3. **Freedom and pluralism**: Inquiry is non-coercive and protects conscience and academic freedom.
4. **Care with challenge**: *Cura personalis* joins personal knowledge and support to high expectations.
5. **Justice and the common good**: Context and consequence include excluded people, structural conditions, and creation.
6. **Integration**: Core, professional, co-curricular, and relational learning reinforce rather than fragment one another.
7. **Evidence and revision**: Claims are tested, consequences reviewed, and designs improved.
8. **Institutional congruence**: Policies, resources, incentives, and technologies support the educational commitments they proclaim.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]
