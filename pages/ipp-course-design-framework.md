# The IPP Course Design Framework and Design Canvas

The Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm (IPP) offers Saint Joseph's University a shared, adaptable architecture for designing courses, programs, advising processes, and institutional decisions. This article explains how to structure educational work using the five IPP dynamics, what the SJU Design Canvas asks of each designer, whether every lesson must include all five elements, and how the IPP differs from other course-planning frameworks.

---

## 1. The Five Dynamics as a Recursive Spiral

The IPP names five dynamics: **context, experience, reflection, action, and evaluation**. They should be visualized as a spiral rather than a line: context surrounds the learning encounter; experience, reflection, and action form its central movement; and evaluation judges learning and consequences before returning the learner and educator to a newly understood context. [^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

The paradigm is explicitly *not* a five-box checklist or a compulsory linear sequence. Real learning moves among the dynamics: new context can change the meaning of an experience; reflection can reveal that more experience is needed; evaluation can become experience for a new cycle; action can expose assumptions that require reconsideration. [^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

| Dynamic | Governing question | Primary educational work | Typical failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Context | Who is learning what, where, under which conditions and histories? | Diagnose readiness, identity, prior knowledge, culture, power, purpose, and environment | Treating learners as interchangeable |
| Experience | What reality will the learner encounter and appropriate? | Create direct or vicarious engagement of intellect and affect | Confusing activity or exposure with learning |
| Reflection | What does this mean, why does it matter, and what is being asked of us? | Examine evidence, assumptions, feeling, perspectives, consequences, and value | Opinion-sharing without disciplined inquiry, or steering to a preferred answer |
| Action | What interior choice or outward response follows? | Decide, practice, create, serve, revise, advocate, research, or commit | Activity without discernment, or insight without response |
| Evaluation | What was learned, what changed, what resulted, and what should happen next? | Assess mastery, judgment, growth, impact, and design; give feedback | Reducing evaluation to grades or grading moral worth |

[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## 2. Structuring a Course Using the Five Dynamics

### 2.1 Context

IPP context includes the learner's prior knowledge, experiences, feelings, values, readiness, social relationships, culture, and material conditions, as well as the institutional setting and wider economic, political, religious, technological, and cultural environment. The educator's own assumptions and location are also part of context. [^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Context is not a demographic preface completed once at the start of a semester. It is an ongoing inquiry into what makes this learning possible, difficult, meaningful, risky, or distorted. Useful practices include prior-knowledge probes, low-stakes diagnostic work, transparent learning purposes, accessibility checks, community agreements, and analysis of disciplinary history. [^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

**Guardrail:** Context becomes stereotyping if educators infer beliefs or capacity from group identity, or voyeurism if students are pressed to disclose trauma or private experience. The proper standard is informed adaptation with high expectations and appropriate support. [^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

### 2.2 Experience

Experience is any direct or vicarious encounter with the subject matter that the learner appropriates through the senses, intellect, imagination, memory, and affect. The category is wider than "experiential education": a student's sustained encounter with a difficult theorem or poem can be an Ignatian experience if it is intellectually real, personally appropriated, and open to reflection. [^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Strong experience is aligned to a significant learning purpose, authentic enough to reveal the complexity of the subject, accessible and safe enough for meaningful participation, prepared by necessary knowledge and skill, structured so that learners must notice rather than merely attend, capable of engaging appropriate affect without exploiting it, and followed by reflection, response, and feedback. [^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Examples across disciplines include close reading, archival investigation, data collection, laboratory experimentation, clinical practice, design studio, community-engaged learning, internship, debate, and oral history. [^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

**Guardrail:** Exposure alone is not experience in the IPP sense. A trip, guest speaker, service day, or simulation may confirm prejudice if learners are unprepared. Faculty should not create avoidable harm in the name of "transformative" learning. [^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

### 2.3 Reflection

Reflection is the disciplined process by which learners grasp the significance of experience. It is the pivotal dynamic connecting experience to responsible action. Ignatian reflection is not synonymous with journaling, introspection, or stating an opinion—it is epistemic, affective, ethical, social, and when appropriate, spiritual. [^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

The 1993 source document suggests reflection can move through five kinds of question: (1) Attention—what happened?; (2) Interpretation—what patterns, causes, or concepts explain it?; (3) Interior response—what draws, troubles, or energizes me?; (4) Ethical and social meaning—who benefits, who bears risk, whose voice is absent?; (5) Discernment—which possible response is more truthful and responsible? [^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Reflection may be analytic, dialogic, oral, visual, quantitative, embodied, or design-based—it is not always written and personal. [^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

**Guardrail:** Reflection becomes coercive when the instructor predetermines the morally acceptable insight. Faculty should grade the quality of attention and reasoning—not the student's belief or emotional disclosure. [^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

### 2.4 Action

In the IPP, action begins in interior appropriation—a judgment, choice, priority, or commitment that follows reflection—and may then become outward conduct. Reflection is incomplete if it produces understanding without considering response; action is irresponsible if it bypasses reflection (ICAJE, 1993, nn. 59–62). [^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Action may include applying a concept to a novel problem, revising a design or argument, making a professional choice, creating something useful or truth-bearing, serving with a community partner, communicating findings to a public audience, advocating civically, or committing to learn before taking further action. A clarified professional commitment or revised research design can be genuine action even when a student lacks authority to solve a structural problem during a course. [^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Action does not require service or advocacy; it must fit the discipline, role, and context. Action must not reward students who have more time, money, mobility, or institutional access. [^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

### 2.5 Evaluation

Evaluation has at least four objects: (1) academic learning—what knowledge and skills have been demonstrated; (2) quality of judgment—how well evidence, values, perspectives, and consequences have been integrated; (3) action and impact—what was done and with what results; and (4) educational design—what the teacher, program, or institution should change. [^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Faculty may assess the quality of ethical analysis, reflective practice, professional responsibility, collaboration, and evidence-based action when these are legitimate learning outcomes. They should not claim to measure the state of a student's conscience, compassion, faith, political virtue, or human worth. Rubrics should describe observable qualities in work, not identity claims about the person. [^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## 3. A Course-Level Design Standard

An IPP-informed course need not display five labeled units. It should, however, be possible to answer the following questions for any course: [^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

- **Context:** What do we know about the learners, discipline, histories, communities, professional setting, and institutional conditions?
- **Experience:** What significant realities, questions, texts, data, practices, or persons will students encounter?
- **Reflection:** Where and how will students examine meaning, evidence, affect, assumption, perspective, value, and consequence?
- **Action:** What will students decide, create, practice, communicate, change, or carry forward?
- **Evaluation:** What evidence will show academic learning and responsible judgment, how will feedback be used, and how will the course itself improve?

IPP design begins with worthy purposes, not with adding reflective assignments after a course is built. Outcomes can integrate disciplinary mastery and responsible use without becoming vague character claims. For example: a weak outcome like "students will become compassionate leaders" should be rewritten as "students will analyze how a decision distributes benefits and risks among stakeholders, consult affected perspectives, and justify a proportionate response." [^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

### Sample Learning Cycle

The 1993-based source document illustrates this with an allocation-of-scarce-health-resource topic: [^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

- **Context:** Students examine the health system, legal rules, histories of exclusion, institutional constraints, and their own prior assumptions.
- **Experience:** Teams analyze a realistic case, data, patient narratives, and the roles of clinicians, administrators, and families.
- **Reflection:** Students compare ethical frameworks, identify affective responses and biases, test assumptions, and listen to perspectives with unequal power.
- **Action:** Each team produces and defends an allocation protocol and communication plan; individuals articulate what they would do in role.
- **Evaluation:** Faculty assess technical accuracy, ethical reasoning, feasibility, stakeholder engagement, and response to critique. Students conduct a post-decision review; feedback becomes the context for a revised case.

The same architecture can govern a literary interpretation, engineering design, business strategy, teacher residency, research practicum, or artistic performance. [^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

## 4. The SJU IPP Design Canvas

The SJU Design Canvas can be used for a class, course, program, advising process, co-curricular experience, policy, or institutional initiative. It addresses the following sections: [^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] [^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

**A. Purpose and Horizon**
- What academic, professional, human, and public goods are sought?
- Which SJU mission commitments and formal outcomes apply?
- What would success look like without making claims about a person's moral worth?

**B. Context**
- Who are the learners and other affected persons?
- What prior knowledge, cultures, identities, resources, and constraints matter?
- What histories, power relations, disciplinary norms, technologies, and community conditions shape the work?
- What accessibility, consent, privacy, or safeguarding requirements apply?

**C. Experience**
- What will learners encounter directly or vicariously?
- Why is this experience significant and authentic?
- What preparation and support are necessary?
- How will learners be required to notice, practice, or inquire?

**D. Reflection**
- What evidence and concepts will learners use?
- What assumptions, emotions, perspectives, interests, and consequences will be examined?
- How will reflection avoid compulsory disclosure and predetermined agreement?
- Where will silence, dialogue, critique, or revision occur?

**E. Action**
- What decision, creation, practice, communication, service, research, or commitment follows?
- Is it proportionate to the learner's knowledge, role, and power?
- Who participates in defining the action and who bears its risks?
- What accountability, continuity, or repair is required?

**F. Evaluation and Next Cycle**
- What evidence demonstrates academic mastery and responsible judgment?
- Who provides feedback, including affected partners where relevant?
- What consequences, inequities, and unintended effects will be reviewed?
- What should the learner, educator, program, or institution change next?

## 5. Does Every Lesson Need All Five Elements?

No. The paradigm should be visible across an appropriate learning arc, but not every individual lesson need explicitly activate all five dynamics. Some moments primarily provide experience; others deepen reflection or evaluate action. Over the arc of a course or program, all five should be meaningfully present and connected. [^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

Similarly, programs should map where learners repeatedly encounter significant experiences, practice reflection, act with increasing responsibility, and receive feedback over time. The aim is developmental coherence, not five-fold labeling of every course. [^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

## 6. Design Review Rubric

The following rubric evaluates a *design*, not the virtue of an educator or student. It should support peer consultation and improvement, not become a compliance score detached from context. [^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

| Dimension | Emerging | Developing | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Mission language is appended but not connected to outcomes | Academic and formative purposes are named | Purposes are integrated, specific, and assessable without moral scoring |
| Context | Learners and conditions are assumed | Some prior knowledge and access needs are considered | Learner, disciplinary, social, institutional, and power contexts shape the design |
| Experience | Activity or content exposure dominates | Experience is aligned but weakly appropriated | Significant direct or vicarious encounter engages disciplined attention and appropriate affect |
| Reflection | Opinion or personal disclosure is requested | Structured prompts connect experience and concepts | Evidence, assumptions, affect, perspectives, consequences, and discernment are integrated |
| Action | No response, or generic activism, is expected | Application or choice follows learning | Interior and/or outward action is proportionate, free, role-aware, and accountable |
| Evaluation | Grade only, or character claim | Learning and reflection receive feedback | Mastery, judgment, consequence, equity, and design are reviewed; feedback begins a new cycle |
| Relationship | Contact is transactional | Support and challenge are present | Trust, clarity, availability, learner agency, and appropriate boundaries embody cura personalis |
| Evidence | IPP label substitutes for evaluation | Some local evidence is collected | Claims are calibrated; multiple forms of evidence drive revision |

## 7. How the IPP Differs from Backward Design and Other Frameworks

The IPP converges with several modern pedagogical frameworks but should not be collapsed into any of them. [^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

**Backward design and authentic assessment:** Both backward design and IPP begin with purpose—what students should know and be able to do—and align evidence and learning activities accordingly. The IPP adds a *recursive* rather than only linear logic and asks not just what learners know but who they become through the design. [^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

**Kolb's experiential learning cycle:** Experience, reflection, conceptualization, and experimentation recur in Kolb's model. The IPP is not derived from Kolb; it gives context, affect, relationship, freedom, justice, and evaluation a wider role. [^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

**Deweyan experiential education:** Like Dewey, the IPP holds that experience becomes educational through continuity, interaction, and reflection. The IPP explicitly situates experience within discernment, formation, faith, justice, and responsible choice. [^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

**Freirean praxis:** Freirean approaches share a justice orientation with the IPP, and both attend to reflection and action. The IPP retains its Ignatian theological anthropology and protects learner freedom against prescribed ideological conclusions. [^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

**Universal Design for Learning (UDL):** UDL offers more specific design guidance for access and representation; the IPP supplies a wider formative horizon. The two are complementary. [^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

The practical conclusion is *eclecticism with criteria*: the 1993 document explicitly invites educators to adopt sound methods that support its purposes. SJU need not choose between the IPP and evidence-informed teaching. The IPP should guide *why* and *toward what ends* methods are selected; learning science and disciplinary research should guide how reliably they work. [^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

## 8. Common Questions

**Must every lesson use all five elements?** No. The paradigm should be visible across an appropriate learning arc. Some moments primarily provide experience; others deepen reflection or evaluate action. Over a course or program, all five should be meaningfully present and connected. [^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

**Is reflection always written and personal?** No. It may be analytic, dialogic, oral, visual, quantitative, embodied, or design-based. Personal appropriation matters, but disclosure is not required. [^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

**Does action require service or advocacy?** No. Action includes interior choice and responsible application. It must fit the discipline, role, and context. Service and advocacy are important forms when educationally and ethically appropriate. [^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

**Can the IPP be assessed?** Design quality, learning processes, demonstrated capabilities, and consequences can be assessed. A person's interior moral or spiritual state should not be scored. Institutional claims require evidence proportional to their strength. [^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

**Can a non-Catholic educator use the IPP authentically?** Yes. The source text explicitly presents the paradigm as accessible to those who do not share Christian faith. Authentic use requires honesty about the tradition's roots, respect for pluralism, and commitment to its human and educational demands. [^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]
