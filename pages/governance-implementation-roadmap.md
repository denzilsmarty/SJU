# Governance, Implementation Roadmap, and Institutional Commitments

This section addresses the institutional structures, decision-making frameworks, leadership models, and multi-phase implementation strategy that Saint Joseph's University (SJU) has developed to adopt, govern, and sustain the Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm (IPP) as a shared educational framework. It draws from SJU's working source-of-truth document *Forming the Future* (Version 0.1, 23 August 2026).[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

---

## What This Section Covers

This section answers core institutional questions about how SJU will implement the IPP:

- **Governance and stewardship**: Who owns the IPP framework after it is adopted, how is it revised, and what authority structure guides it?
- **Implementation roadmap**: What are the concrete phases, activities, and timelines for rolling out the IPP across the university?
- **Institutional commitments**: What explicit promises is SJU making if it adopts the IPP, and how do those commitments translate into policy and practice?
- **Quality guardrails**: Where are firm institutional boundaries to prevent misuse of the IPP rubric or framework?
- **Faculty and staff development**: How will educators be formed, supported, and recognized for adopting the paradigm?
- **Public communication**: How will SJU present the IPP to students, families, and the broader public?
- **Strategic alignment**: How does the IPP connect to SJU's existing mission, strategic plan, and learning outcomes?

Taken together, these articles constitute the institutional commitment and governance apparatus that makes the IPP not merely a pedagogical concept, but a durable, accountable, and transparent practice across the university.

## How the Three-Phase Implementation Roadmap Fits In

The [Three-Phase Implementation Roadmap](three-phase-implementation-roadmap) describes SJU's structured approach to rolling out the IPP in sequence. The roadmap moves from establishing shared meaning (Phase 1), to testing in authentic settings (Phase 2), to integrating and sustaining the framework (Phase 3).[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] Each phase has distinct activities, leadership responsibilities, and success conditions. The roadmap is paired with governance guardrails that apply throughout all phases.

## How the Eight Institutional Commitments to the IPP Fit In

The [Eight Institutional Commitments to the IPP](eight-institutional-commitments) translate the IPP's abstract five dynamics—context, experience, reflection, action, and evaluation—into eight durable institutional obligations. These commitments cover disciplinary rigor, whole-person attention, freedom and pluralism, cura personalis, justice and the common good, integration, evidence and revision, and institutional congruence.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] They function as quality standards and enabling conditions for the paradigm to operate faithfully across the institution. If SJU formally adopts the IPP, it is pledging to honor these eight commitments in policy, culture, and practice.

## How the Cross-Functional Steward Model and Editorial Group Fits In

The [Cross-Functional Steward Model and Editorial Group](cross-functional-steward-model) describes the governance structure created in Phase 1 to own and maintain the IPP source-of-truth document. The steward and editorial group is drawn from faculty governance, Mission, academic leadership, student-facing divisions, assessment, accessibility, and student representatives.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] This group ensures that revisions to the framework are vetted, authoritative, and representative of the university's diverse constituencies.

## How the IPP Rubric Governance Guardrails and Misuse Prevention Fits In

The [IPP Rubric Governance Guardrails and Misuse Prevention](ipp-rubric-governance-guardrails) article establishes firm institutional boundaries around how the IPP rubric may and may not be used. The rubric is designed to evaluate educational designs—courses, programs, advising processes, policies—not the persons who create or deliver them.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] This article clarifies what constitutes legitimate use, what uses are prohibited, and where the institution must hold its ground to prevent the paradigm from being weaponized or misappropriated.

The [Review Cycle, Version Control, and Source-of-Truth Maintenance](review-cycle-and-version-control) article governs how the IPP source document evolves after it is adopted. ## How Faculty and Staff Formation, Recognition, and Resource Alignment Fits In

The [Faculty and Staff Formation, Recognition, and Resource Alignment](faculty-staff-formation-and-recognition) article describes Phase 3 of the implementation roadmap and details how SJU will support educator development. It outlines a seven-stage formation sequence—orientation, experience, design studio, peer reflection, action, evaluation, and dissemination—along with guidance on recognition, protected time, and resource alignment to signal that the institution genuinely values the work it asks educators to undertake.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

## Interconnection: How These Elements Work Together

These sections are not independent. Together, they form a coherent governance and implementation system:

1. **The Eight Commitments** define what SJU is pledging to do.
2. **The Three-Phase Roadmap** sequences the work over time.
3. **The Cross-Functional Steward Group** ensures representation and accountability throughout.
4. **The Rubric Guardrails** protect the integrity of the framework against misuse.
5. **The Review Cycle** creates conditions for evolution and refinement.
6. **The Strategic Alignment** shows that the IPP serves, rather than competes with, SJU's existing mission and direction.
7. **The Faculty Formation sequence** ensures that educators are not left to figure out the paradigm alone, and that the institution recognizes and resources the work.
8. **The Institutional Statement and Primer** make the framework transparent and accessible to all stakeholders.

Taken together, these elements answer the essential institutional question: *How will Saint Joseph's University adopt, govern, implement, sustain, and communicate the Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm in a way that is transparent, accountable, inclusive, and aligned with its existing mission and commitments?*
