# Mission Priorities: Implementation and Institutional Action

## Overview

Jesuit universities translate Mission Priority Examen (MPE) findings into concrete institutional actions through comprehensive frameworks that integrate mission across all dimensions of university life. The MPE process itself serves as a structured approach to institutional reflection grounded in the Ignatian Examen tradition, enabling universities to discern how they embody their Jesuit and Catholic identity and translate strategic commitments into lived practice.[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be]

## Major Mission Priorities Emerging Across Jesuit Universities

Fordham University's Mission Priority Examen identified four primary institutional priorities that reflect broader commitments across Jesuit higher education:[^chunk:4558a42e-859a-573c-bdc1-5cc18cc8ecba]

**Priority 1: Strengthen Jesuit and Catholic Mission-Based Culture**

Universities recognize that while Jesuit and Catholic identity is valued, there exists significant lack of clarity and underappreciation for this mission among faculty, staff, and students.[^chunk:4558a42e-859a-573c-bdc1-5cc18cc8ecba] Implementation requires adopting a developmental approach to mission education that clearly articulates and communicates the institution's Jesuit and Catholic identity to every employee from recruitment through retirement.[^chunk:4558a42e-859a-573c-bdc1-5cc18cc8ecba] This includes:[^chunk:4558a42e-859a-573c-bdc1-5cc18cc8ecba]

- Developing mission-centric approaches to recruitment, hiring, and orientation of new faculty, with ongoing mission opportunities throughout their careers
- Creating similar formation programs for staff at all levels through collaboration between Human Resources and Mission Integration offices
- Establishing formation programs for Cabinet members, VPs, deans, and chairs through mission seminars and forums
- Fostering ongoing mission awareness with trustees through pilgrimages, seminars, and special events
- Advancing Jesuit and Catholic identity with alumni, parents, and families through Development and University Relations

**Priority 2: Forge Community by Supporting Faith and Spirituality Across All Religious Traditions**

Mission priorities increasingly emphasize creating inclusive spiritual communities that honor multiple faith traditions while maintaining Catholic and Ignatian foundations.[^chunk:4558a42e-859a-573c-bdc1-5cc18cc8ecba] Universities implement this through:[^chunk:4558a42e-859a-573c-bdc1-5cc18cc8ecba]

- Liturgies, retreats, prayer communities, and guided reflection exercises that deepen student understanding of their faith
- Ministry to Catholic, Orthodox, Jewish, and Muslim communities
- Models for appreciating differences and engaging constructively across faith perspectives

Fordham's recent hiring of a rabbi and imam positions the university to foster well-informed appreciation for diverse faith traditions.[^chunk:017e4fb4-b98a-5fa1-8242-8b3e4943a9c6]

**Priority 3: Advance Transformative Teaching and Education**

Implementation of transformative education operates at multiple stages of student development:[^chunk:4558a42e-859a-573c-bdc1-5cc18cc8ecba]

*For Undergraduate Students:*
- First-Year Experience focusing intensive teaching resources on crucial early college transition
- Core Curriculum revision emphasizing the Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm to embody Jesuit pedagogy and mission
- Interdisciplinary courses taught with emphasis on ethics and social justice
- High-impact practices including community-engaged learning, mentoring, guaranteed internship placements (Fordham Internship Promise), study abroad, and career advising

*For Graduate Students:*
- Training with ethical lens and commitment to social justice through curricular and co-curricular means
- Internships and clinic placements in service-oriented fields
- Interdisciplinary approaches ensuring graduates are prepared "to the front lines of building opportunity, navigating ethics and power, teaching, counseling, and caring for those in need"[^chunk:4558a42e-859a-573c-bdc1-5cc18cc8ecba]

**Priority 4: Advance Public Impact Research**

Jesuit universities embrace a moral imperative to focus research on mattering to the world.[^chunk:4558a42e-859a-573c-bdc1-5cc18cc8ecba] Implementation includes:[^chunk:4558a42e-859a-573c-bdc1-5cc18cc8ecba]

- Creating transdisciplinary research hubs that unlock interdisciplinary puzzle-solving
- Focusing on priority research areas: migration, climate change, artificial intelligence, and democracy
- Implementing Laudato Si' as mandated by Pope Francis through seven-year Green Plans for Environmental Justice
- Integrating student participation in faculty research

## Translating Mission into Concrete Institutional Actions

### Leadership and Governance Integration

Universities operationalize mission commitments through intentional leadership selection and formation. President Tania Tetlow's appointment at Fordham exemplifies this approach.[^chunk:7c1e5dbd-be5a-5a5b-85fb-5af80f42479b] The presidential search committee, composed of faculty, students, and trustees including Jesuits, prioritized mission qualities and conducted Ignatian discernment exercises. Tetlow's background—daughter of a former Jesuit and Fordham alumna, with uncle renowned for Jesuit spirituality scholarship—gave her deep understanding of Catholic social teaching and Ignatian spirituality.[^chunk:7c1e5dbd-be5a-5a5b-85fb-5af80f42479b]

As president, she demonstrates mission-centered leadership through:[^chunk:7c1e5dbd-be5a-5a5b-85fb-5af80f42479b]

- Making explicit reference to Jesuit identity and mission in every speech to students, alumni, faculty, and staff
- Beginning each Cabinet meeting with prayer
- Serving as spokesperson for Jesuit and Catholic mission in American media
- Establishing close working relationships with the Jesuit Superior General and Vatican Curia
- Leading Cabinet in understanding what being Jesuit and Catholic means in each administrative portfolio

At the board level, mission integration requires structured engagement and ongoing formation.[^chunk:7c1e5dbd-be5a-5a5b-85fb-5af80f42479b] Trustees participate in retreats featuring mission panels, deep-dive sessions on diversity and inclusion in Catholic context, and Ignatian pilgrimages to Rome that frame questions about Jesuit identity in relation to financial sustainability. The Board's Mission and Identity Committee has been redesignated as the Mission and Social Justice Committee to highlight commitment to diversity, equity, inclusion, and belonging.[^chunk:7c1e5dbd-be5a-5a5b-85fb-5af80f42479b]

### Institutional Integrity and Equity

Commitment to diversity, social justice, and environmental stewardship operates through multiple institutional structures. Fordham elevated its Chief Diversity Officer to Cabinet position as Vice President for Equity and Inclusion (VPEI), making this leader a vital member of senior leadership charged with:[^chunk:8d6422a7-1960-50ec-94ec-0c4ff976299d]

- Advancing academic excellence through diverse perspectives
- Fostering culture where all feel welcome and cared for
- Promoting equitable access to opportunities for marginalized students and employees

The Office of Human Resources plays an exceptional role in mission integration, focusing the department on fostering campus partnerships, providing exceptional service, and implementing innovative technologies for whole-person care—body, mind, and spirit.[^chunk:8d6422a7-1960-50ec-94ec-0c4ff976299d] This operationalizes core mission values of inclusivity, engagement, fairness, and commitment to health and well-being.

Institutional commitment to equity includes:[^chunk:8d6422a7-1960-50ec-94ec-0c4ff976299d]

- Policies and training deterring bias against potentially marginalized groups
- Accessible and transparent recruitment practices
- Shared Governance Working Group ensuring governance principles apply across the university
- Flourish Program (launched 2021) developing employee leadership skills and enabling participation in shaping the university's future
- Comprehensive salary review ensuring transparent and equitable compensation
- Curated benefits packages reflecting Jesuit values, including long-term retirement investments and commitments to physical and behavioral well-being
- 2024 Investment Office engagement with board, students, faculty, and community on socially responsible investing aligned with Laudato Si'[^chunk:8d6422a7-1960-50ec-94ec-0c4ff976299d]

### Role of Campus Ministry and Student Affairs

Campus ministry and student affairs operationalize mission through spiritual formation and integral student development. Implementation of Priority 2 (faith and spirituality across traditions) is "largely assumed by Campus Ministry within Mission Integration."[^chunk:017e4fb4-b98a-5fa1-8242-8b3e4943a9c6]

Student affairs collaborates with Academic Affairs and Mission Integration on the First-Year Experience, which provides mentorship, connection, and support through *cura personalis* (care of the whole person), guided reflection experiences, and access to communities of learning, faith, and service.[^chunk:017e4fb4-b98a-5fa1-8242-8b3e4943a9c6]

The broader campus community reflects mission commitment through multifaith approaches. President Tetlow wrote community emails celebrating major religious holidays and explained their relationship to Catholic beliefs, subsequently inviting community members to describe their own faiths.[^chunk:7c1e5dbd-be5a-5a5b-85fb-5af80f42479b] This creates "a place where faith is deeply rooted and broadly welcoming" that prepares students "to engage thoughtfully and compassionately in an increasingly pluralistic world."[^chunk:35f3f8d1-0109-5418-87ec-fb90d21615a7]

## Implementation and Integration Framework

Mission Priorities are operationalized through systematic collaboration among university divisions. Progress depends on:[^chunk:017e4fb4-b98a-5fa1-8242-8b3e4943a9c6]

1. **University Marketing and Communications with Mission Integration**: Recent brand audience research (August 2024) revealed that 29% of 8,000 surveyed prospective and current students cited Jesuit mission as their reason for attending, with an additional 27% of current students appreciating the mission despite initially feeling negative about it. However, 40% of current undergraduates reported the mission did not affect their experience, underscoring need for clearer mission communication from recruitment phase.[^chunk:017e4fb4-b98a-5fa1-8242-8b3e4943a9c6]

2. **Academic Affairs, Student Affairs, and Mission Integration**: Implementation of First-Year Experience with mentorship and guided reflection addressing academic, relational, and mental health challenges.[^chunk:017e4fb4-b98a-5fa1-8242-8b3e4943a9c6]

3. **Provost's Office, Deans, Chairs with Mission Integration**: Mission-centric approach to faculty recruitment, hiring, orientation, and ongoing formation[^chunk:017e4fb4-b98a-5fa1-8242-8b3e4943a9c6]

4. **Office of Human Resources with Mission Integration**: Mission programming for employees throughout their Fordham careers[^chunk:017e4fb4-b98a-5fa1-8242-8b3e4943a9c6]

5. **Development and University Relations with Mission Integration**: Mission programming for alumni and donors; securing funding for mission infrastructure[^chunk:017e4fb4-b98a-5fa1-8242-8b3e4943a9c6]

Mission Priorities 3 and 4 (transformative teaching and public impact research) already enjoy broad faculty appeal and are key components of the emerging strategic plan, making their implementation highly likely to be advanced and funded.[^chunk:017e4fb4-b98a-5fa1-8242-8b3e4943a9c6]

## Challenges to Implementation

Universities acknowledge obstacles to mission implementation. Despite high-quality mission-based programming, participation levels remain relatively low. For example, Fordham secured a New York State Accessibility Awareness grant but staff and faculty participation was disappointing.[^chunk:8d6422a7-1960-50ec-94ec-0c4ff976299d] In response, HR redoubles efforts in mission-related onboarding and collaborates with Mission Integration in developing programming for ongoing employees.[^chunk:8d6422a7-1960-50ec-94ec-0c4ff976299d]

Universities also navigate tensions between Jesuit and Catholic dimensions of mission. Some community members willingly endorse Jesuit aspects while being "far less sanguine about" Catholic foundations.[^chunk:7c1e5dbd-be5a-5a5b-85fb-5af80f42479b] Institutions in secularizing contexts like New York City work to be "invitational and welcoming" while resisting mission dilution, "working hard to defend faith, but also defend religion as such."[^chunk:35f3f8d1-0109-5418-87ec-fb90d21615a7]

Jesuit presence considerations also affect implementation. While declining Jesuit membership in the United States may cloud perception of mission opportunity in higher education, universities emphasize that Jesuit identity does not depend exclusively on Jesuit presence.[^chunk:8d6422a7-1960-50ec-94ec-0c4ff976299d] "Every member of the community must embody our mission," requiring that universities form lay people for Jesuit mission at precisely the moment when such formation is most urgent.[^chunk:35f3f8d1-0109-5418-87ec-fb90d21615a7]

## The MPE Process as Implementation Tool

The Mission Priority Examen itself functions as a structured mechanism for translating mission commitments into action. The MPE process follows eight steps over approximately two semesters, beginning with agreement between Provincial and institution on focus and scope, moving through self-study and peer review, and concluding with Superior General's affirmation or guidance.[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be]

Peer visitors—experts in mission implementation from other Jesuit universities—provide crucial feedback and share best practices. This network effect amplifies implementation capacity across AJCU schools, with Provincial Assistants for Higher Education tasked with follow-up on mission priority implementation through annual visits and ongoing support.[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be]

The MPE process explicitly links to the Society of Jesus' Universal Apostolic Preferences (UAPs), ensuring institutional action aligns with Jesuit-wide priorities:[^chunk:6855f939-6c7c-5ec8-9e40-b24804c193a8]

- Showing the way to God through Spiritual Exercises and discernment
- Walking with the poor, outcasts, and those whose dignity has been violated in reconciliation and justice
- Accompanying young people in creating hope-filled futures
- Collaborating in care of the common home through integral ecology approaches

## Strategic Integration and Alignment

Mission priorities align with and reinforce institutional strategic plans. Fordham's four mission priorities are well-positioned for integration because they reinforce key goals articulated in the emerging strategic plan emphasizing transformative education, public impact research, and collaborative community grounded in trust and hope.[^chunk:017e4fb4-b98a-5fa1-8242-8b3e4943a9c6]

They are likewise well-aligned with the United States East Asia Province's Apostolic Plan and grounded in the Society's Universal Apostolic Preferences, positioning them for engagement by the Jesuit community as it aligns efforts with the university's apostolic plan.[^chunk:017e4fb4-b98a-5fa1-8242-8b3e4943a9c6]

Environmental stewardship exemplifies this alignment. The inclusion of climate change and environmental justice as research foci aligns with UAP No. 4 (Care for our Common Home) and is explicitly articulated in Province Priority 3 as "engaging with greater freedom in the mission of ecological and social justice."[^chunk:017e4fb4-b98a-5fa1-8242-8b3e4943a9c6]

## Conclusion

Jesuit universities translate Mission Priority Examen findings into concrete action through comprehensive frameworks integrating mission across leadership, curriculum, research, student formation, and institutional operations. Implementation requires intentional collaboration among academic affairs, student affairs, mission integration, human resources, and development; structured formation of leaders at all levels; alignment of hiring and promotion practices with mission commitments; and honest acknowledgment of obstacles to mission embodiment. Campus ministry and student affairs play essential roles in spiritual formation and integral development, while faculty engagement in transformative teaching and public impact research provides academic expression of institutional mission. Success depends on viewing mission not as a specialized departmental function but as the defining framework for all institutional decision-making and action.
