# Jesuit Universities as Agents of Social Transformation

## The Call to Serve Marginalized Communities

Jesuit universities are called upon to be agents of social and spiritual transformation in contemporary society.[^chunk:e4a4773d-2c71-5aa7-938b-dfbb03bba574] This calling reflects a fundamental commitment to entering into the lives of those whose dignity has been stripped away by poverty, racism, displacement, xenophobia, and other dehumanizing forces.[^chunk:e4a4773d-2c71-5aa7-938b-dfbb03bba574]

Fr. Arturo Sosa, S.J., Superior General of the Society of Jesus, articulated this vision in his address to the AJCU Trustee Forum, emphasizing that a university positioned as a venture aimed at transforming society must gravitate toward the margins of society.[^chunk:e4a4773d-2c71-5aa7-938b-dfbb03bba574] Such institutions throw open their doors and windows to people at the margins, who in turn bring with them "a new breath of life that will be a source of life in abundance for all."[^chunk:e4a4773d-2c71-5aa7-938b-dfbb03bba574]

## What Prioritizing Work with Marginalized Communities Means

For Jesuit institutions to prioritize work with marginalized communities represents a fundamental reorientation of institutional purpose. It requires moving beyond traditional academic functions to actively engage with those excluded by existing structures and interests that dominate the world.[^chunk:e4a4773d-2c71-5aa7-938b-dfbb03bba574] This commitment extends across institutional operations, as demonstrated by Jesuit universities establishing dedicated offices and divisions focused on mission integration and community engagement.

At John Carroll University, for example, the Office of University Mission & Identity works to form a community for leadership and service, with Campus Ministry functioning as a hub for faith that does justice.[^chunk:49dc1795-ab73-5478-bfa7-60d746f61e31] Similarly, Loyola University Maryland has identified Equity and Inclusion as a core mission priority, reflecting the institutional commitment to serve populations historically marginalized from higher education.[^chunk:538a060a-4e2d-55d8-becd-cdd8e5bf65c1]

## Mission Priority Examens and Institutional Transformation

The Mission Priority Examen (MPE) represents a structured institutional process through which Jesuit colleges and universities advance their transformation efforts. All Jesuit colleges and universities are required to participate in the MPE, engaging in institutional reflection on their Jesuit, Catholic mission and how they advance established Mission Priorities.[^chunk:538a060a-4e2d-55d8-becd-cdd8e5bf65c1]

### The MPE Process

The MPE operates through a multi-stage process designed to ensure comprehensive institutional discernment. Schools commit to participate during a designated year, after which the National Coordinating Committee sends descriptive documents and offers guidance.[^chunk:538a060a-4e2d-55d8-becd-cdd8e5bf65c1] Schools and their respective Province Higher Education Assistants select peer visitors, submit local implementation approaches, and gather institutional opinions on mission priorities.[^chunk:538a060a-4e2d-55d8-becd-cdd8e5bf65c1]

Following self-study and peer committee visitation, draft reports are submitted for institutional feedback and factual correction.[^chunk:538a060a-4e2d-55d8-becd-cdd8e5bf65c1] The Provincial Superior and Jesuit Conference President subsequently provide their advice to the Father General, who communicates findings and guidance back through the Provincial to the institution and other local constituencies.[^chunk:538a060a-4e2d-55d8-becd-cdd8e5bf65c1] Institutions then work to implement their mission priorities in light of the Father General's response.[^chunk:538a060a-4e2d-55d8-becd-cdd8e5bf65c1]

### Strategic Integration Through Ignatian Pedagogy

The MPE process incorporates Ignatian Examen sessions—similar to listening sessions—to facilitate institutional reflection on mission priorities and the characteristics of Jesuit higher education.[^chunk:e4a4773d-2c71-5aa7-938b-dfbb03bba574] At Spring Hill College, the MPE recommendations integrate with strategic planning processes, addressing institutional challenges and opportunities through examination of how three strategic pillars—Jesuit Catholic Mission, Academic Excellence, and Enhanced Campus Resources—support student success and leadership development with significant impact on society.[^chunk:e4a4773d-2c71-5aa7-938b-dfbb03bba574]

This integration includes evaluating institutional dynamics, assumptions, and potential biases that might represent barriers to transformational work.[^chunk:e4a4773d-2c71-5aa7-938b-dfbb03bba574] The process culminates in actionable recommendations, resource allocation decisions, and implementation timelines that connect departmental and divisional planning to the college's overall strategic plan.[^chunk:e4a4773d-2c71-5aa7-938b-dfbb03bba574]

### Outcomes and Priorities

Loyola University Maryland's first MPE process, initiated in 2019, identified three mission priorities: Ignatian Formation, Equity and Inclusion, and Environmental Sustainability.[^chunk:538a060a-4e2d-55d8-becd-cdd8e5bf65c1] The university began its second round of MPE examination in 2026, with institutional reflection guided by the USA East Province Apostolic Plan priorities and the university's strategic plan, "Together We Rise."[^chunk:538a060a-4e2d-55d8-becd-cdd8e5bf65c1]

## Conclusion

The Mission Priority Examen process represents a concrete mechanism through which Jesuit universities institutionalize their commitment to social transformation. By requiring systematic reflection on mission alignment, community engagement with marginalized populations, and strategic resource allocation toward equitable outcomes, the MPE ensures that the call to transformation remains central to institutional identity and operations. Through this rigorous discernment process, Jesuit universities position themselves as authentic agents of social and spiritual change.
