# Institutional Application and Course Design at SJU

The Institutional Application and Course Design section at Saint Joseph's University (SJU) provides a comprehensive framework for translating the Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm (IPP) into concrete educational practice across all domains of university life. This section addresses how the IPP—with its five dynamics of **context, experience, reflection, action, and evaluation**—operates within courses, programs, co-curricular settings, and emerging contexts such as generative AI.

## Structure and Scope

This section is organized around eight interconnected topics that move from foundational course design through discipline-specific application, program-level coherence, co-curricular extension, non-traditional education formats, and institutional readiness for an AI-shaped future. Each article assumes that the IPP is not a five-box checklist or fixed template, but rather a recursive, scalable architecture that educators and administrators can adapt to their particular contexts, learners, and disciplines.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## The IPP Course Design Framework and Design Canvas

The foundation of institutional application is the **IPP Course Design Framework and Design Canvas**, which translates the five dynamics—context, experience, reflection, action, and evaluation—into a shared language for course planning. The Design Canvas guides faculty through structured inquiry: Who is learning what, where, and under which conditions and histories? What reality will the learner encounter and appropriate? What does this mean, why does it matter, and what is being asked of us? What freedom and responsibility will the learner exercise? What have we and the learner discovered about learning and its consequences?

Crucially, the framework does not require that every lesson or course contain all five elements in sequence. Instead, it invites educators to understand the IPP as a recursive spiral in which context surrounds the encounter, experience and reflection and action form the central movement, and evaluation returns the learner to a newly understood context.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## Applying the IPP Across Disciplines

One of the IPP's defining strengths is its **adaptability across all disciplines**. The paradigm is explicitly designed to be flexible and recursive, translating into the concrete demands of humanities, natural sciences, mathematics, computing, engineering, business and organizational leadership, health professions and nursing, and the arts, design, and communication.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

A common misconception is that experience must mean "experiential learning" (field work, simulation, hands-on activity). In fact, experience in the IPP is any direct or vicarious encounter with subject matter that the learner appropriates through the senses, intellect, imagination, memory, and affect.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] A sustained mathematical proof, a close reading of a philosophical text, archival research, laboratory experiment, clinical simulation, and design critique all qualify as Ignatian experience, provided they are intellectually real, personally appropriated, and open to reflection.

Similarly, reflection is not confined to journaling. It is epistemic, affective, ethical, and social—asking both "What is true?" and "What is happening in me and among us as I encounter it?"[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] It can be analytic, dialogic, oral, visual, quantitative, embodied, or design-based.

## Sample Learning Cycle: Health Resource Allocation

To illustrate how the five dynamics work in concert, the institutional framework includes a **complete, concrete example drawn from health professions and nursing education**: the allocation of a scarce health resource.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] This case is especially instructive because clinical practice already cycles through context, encounter, reflection, action, and review, and because resource allocation is technically demanding, ethically contested, emotionally weighted, and structurally revealing.

In context, students examine the health system and its legal rules, histories of exclusion from care, institutional constraints on resource supply, and their own prior assumptions about who deserves care. They then encounter a realistic scenario requiring judgment under uncertainty. Through structured reflection, they examine evidence, assumptions, feeling, perspectives, and consequences. They propose and defend an allocation decision, then evaluate both the decision's outcomes and what they have learned about themselves, the system, and the moral weight of such choices. This same architecture—with discipline-specific content and practices—can be adapted across SJU's schools and colleges.

## Program-Level Coherence

While the IPP can structure individual courses, its power increases when it is made visible and developmental across an entire **program**. The 1993 source text states that "a cycle may operate within a class meeting, a course, a clinical rotation, a program, an advising relationship, or an institutional decision."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] 

Program-level coherence mapping identifies where, across the arc of a degree, learners repeatedly encounter significant experiences, practice increasingly sophisticated reflection, act with growing responsibility, and receive formative feedback. A useful map distinguishes between introduction, reinforcement, and integration of key practices and dispositions. The goal is **developmental coherence, not five-fold labeling of every course**.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dff-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

This commitment reflects General Congregation 34's Decree 17, which calls Jesuit universities to examine themselves and be accountable for whether their actual priorities serve the mission of rigorous teaching, research, interdisciplinary work, service, and the integration of faith and justice.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## Co-Curricular Applications

The IPP is not confined to the classroom. The 1993 source text *Ignatian Pedagogy: A Practical Approach* expressly extends the paradigm to academic and non-academic experiences alike.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] SJU's working framework explicitly names **advising, community engagement, campus ministry, athletics, residence life, student leadership, and career development** as settings where the IPP can coordinate formation without subordinating any one domain to another.

This is grounded in the paradigm's architectural logic: any setting in which a person encounters reality, makes meaning, exercises freedom, acts responsibly, and learns from consequences is a site where the cycle can operate.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Co-curricular contexts typically run shorter, less formally structured cycles—a coaching conversation, a retreat day, a career-exploration exercise—but the underlying recursive logic of context → experience → reflection → action → evaluation remains the same.

**Cura personalis** operates as an enabling condition across all these domains, ensuring that attention to the concrete, whole learner is not the exclusive province of any single office or pedagogy.

## Graduate, Online, Adult, and Continuing Education

The IPP applies across all educational settings at SJU, including **graduate, online, adult, and continuing education programs**.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Adapting the IPP to non-traditional formats requires disciplined translation rather than wholesale importation of assumptions built for residential, undergraduate, synchronous classrooms.

For adult learners, the IPP's **context dynamic becomes especially critical**. Adult learners bring substantial professional and life experience, and context should recognize—rather than romanticize—it.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] This means attending to work and caregiving conditions that limit time and availability, licensure standards and organizational constraints, and financial and family obligations that shape how and when learning occurs.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Context in the IPP is not a demographic preface completed once at the start of a term. It is an ongoing inquiry into what makes learning possible, difficult, meaningful, risky, or distorted.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## Inclusion, Accessibility, and Safeguarding

The IPP as articulated for SJU treats **inclusion, accessibility, and safeguarding not as afterthoughts**, but as intrinsic requirements flowing from the paradigm's deepest commitments: *cura personalis*, attention to the concrete learner, and respect for freedom of conscience.

Inclusion flows from the context dynamic, which requires educators to know and account for the identities, prior knowledge, cultural frameworks, and access needs of actual learners. Universal design—the principle of creating learning experiences that are usable by all people, to the greatest extent possible, without need for adaptation—aligns with the IPP's commitment to meet learners where they are and create conditions for genuine encounter with subject matter.

Safeguarding policies protect students during reflective exercises by establishing when and how personal disclosure can be invited versus required, how sensitive material is handled, and what support is available when reflection surfaces trauma or moral distress.

## IPP and Generative AI in an AI-Shaped University

Generative AI has entered the context of nearly every educational encounter. At SJU, the guiding framework is the **IPP itself**, which asks a more fundamental question than simple permission or prohibition: *what forms of human judgment, authorship, relationship, and responsibility is this educational design meant to cultivate?*[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

The institutional working document states plainly: "In an AI-shaped university, the IPP becomes more—not less—important."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Generative systems can assist research, feedback, accessibility, and rehearsal. They cannot assume responsibility, enter a mutual relationship, discern a vocation, or substitute for the human encounter at the heart of *cura personalis*.

This conviction reflects broader wisdom from Jesuit leadership. Superior General Adolfo Nicolás's 2010 address warned against the "globalization of superficiality" as a threat to depth of thought and imagination.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Arturo Sosa's 2018 address identified the digital ecosystem as a new cultural paradigm and located the Jesuit university within a mission of reconciliation with God, one another, and creation.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0] AI-generated fluency is precisely the form of superficiality these addresses anticipate: information that is instantly synthesized and elegantly expressed, but untethered to lived experience, critical judgment, or moral agency.
