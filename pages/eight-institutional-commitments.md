# Eight Institutional Commitments to the IPP

Saint Joseph's University's working source-of-truth document for the Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm (IPP) proposes eight explicit institutional commitments that SJU should make if it adopts the IPP as a shared educational framework.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] These commitments translate the paradigm's five dynamics—context, experience, reflection, action, and evaluation—into durable institutional obligations that span policy, culture, and practice.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

---

## The Eight Commitments

The document states the commitments as follows[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]:

1. **Disciplinary rigor** — IPP work meets the epistemic and professional standards of the field.
2. **Whole-person attention** — Learning engages intellect, affect, imagination, embodiment, relationship, and responsibility as appropriate.
3. **Freedom and pluralism** — Inquiry is non-coercive and protects conscience and academic freedom.
4. **Care with challenge** — *Cura personalis* joins personal knowledge and support to high expectations.
5. **Justice and the common good** — Context and consequence include excluded people, structural conditions, and creation.
6. **Integration** — Core, professional, co-curricular, and relational learning reinforce rather than fragment one another.
7. **Evidence and revision** — Claims are tested, consequences reviewed, and designs improved.
8. **Institutional congruence** — Policies, resources, incentives, and technologies support the educational commitments they proclaim.

---

## How the Commitments Relate to the Five IPP Dynamics

The five dynamics—context, experience, reflection, action, and evaluation—are described as a spiral rather than a linear sequence.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] The eight commitments function as *enabling conditions* and *quality standards* for that spiral to operate faithfully across an institution. Each commitment maps onto the dynamics in overlapping ways:

- **Disciplinary rigor** anchors the experience and evaluation dynamics: the document warns that "reflection without knowledge becomes opinion; action without analysis becomes impulse."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **Whole-person attention** corresponds most directly to the context dynamic, which requires attending to who the learner actually is—their intellect, affect, culture, embodiment, and relationships—and to the experience dynamic, which insists on cognitive *and* affective engagement.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **Freedom and pluralism** protects the integrity of reflection and action: the educator may not manufacture assent or predetermine the morally acceptable insight.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **Care with challenge** (*cura personalis*) is described not as a sixth IPP step but as "a condition for truthful context, trust, feedback, and freedom."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] It undergirds all five dynamics.
- **Justice and the common good** sharpens the questions that reflection must examine and the criteria by which action is judged—extending reflection to structural conditions, power, and creation.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **Integration** corresponds to the program-level and institutional scale of the IPP, ensuring that the recursive cycle is coherent across courses, co-curricula, and advising rather than fragmented.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]
- **Evidence and revision** maps directly onto evaluation, which the document defines as assessing mastery, judgment, impact, and educational design in order to "begin a new cycle."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **Institutional congruence** addresses the document's claim that "the institution teaches": policies, incentives, technologies, spaces, workloads, and decision processes form people as surely as syllabi do.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## Are These Commitments Binding on All Departments?

The document is explicit that these commitments are proposed for discussion, not promulgated policy. The working document describes itself as "an interpretive working document, not a promulgated policy of Saint Joseph's University or the Society of Jesus," designed to establish common vocabulary and a practical standard for discussion and design.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

The implementation roadmap calls for faculty governance review, voluntary pilots, and an invitational approach to formation.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] The document specifically warns that SJU "should not certify individuals as 'Ignatian' based on a short course, require performative mission language in every syllabus, or use the IPP rubric for personnel judgment without validated purpose, due process, and contextual interpretation."[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

At the same time, the document does state that "institutional expectations remain clear where mission responsibilities are formally assigned,"[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] suggesting that the commitments carry real weight even if universal, identical compliance is not mandated. Mission effectiveness is said to grow through invitation, intellectual seriousness, protected time, and evidence that the institution aligns workloads and rewards with the educational work it asks people to do.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

## Freedom and Pluralism in Relation to Catholic Identity

The third commitment—freedom and pluralism—could appear to conflict with SJU's Catholic and Jesuit identity, but the document argues these are complementary rather than contradictory.

The 1993 source text itself describes the IPP as "profoundly human and usable with those who do not share [Christian] faith."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] The document translates this into what it calls "faithful pluralism" for SJU, which means[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]:

- The University names its Catholic and Jesuit commitments *without disguising them*.
- Students encounter theological and philosophical questions with intellectual seriousness.
- People of every faith and of no religious affiliation are treated as participants, not mission objects.
- No student is required to simulate belief or disclose private spiritual experience.
- Multiple traditions and lived perspectives can illuminate the search for truth and justice.
- Common educational goods—integrity, wisdom, solidarity, responsibility, care, and the common good—are pursued through reasoned dialogue.

Concretely, a non-Catholic educator can use the IPP authentically, provided they are honest about the tradition's roots, respect pluralism, and commit to its human and educational demands.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] Students should be able to question institutional and religious claims without penalty, and viewpoint diversity does not require treating every claim as equally credible or permitting dehumanizing conduct.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

In short, the document positions Catholic identity as the horizon that gives the IPP its animating purpose, while freedom and pluralism protect the integrity of the inquiry that horizon demands.

## What 'Institutional Congruence' Means

The eighth commitment—institutional congruence—is grounded in the document's assertion that universities are themselves formative actors, not merely containers for formative classrooms.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

The document states: "A university's operating system creates context and experience; signals what merits reflection; authorizes or blocks action; and rewards particular outcomes."[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] If institutional arrangements contradict what educators are asked to enact—for example, by rewarding only publications while asking faculty to invest deeply in student formation—then the institutional context undermines the very commitments the IPP requires.

An IPP-informed institutional decision process therefore applies the five dynamics to governance itself, asking[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]:

- **Context**: Who is affected? What histories, power differences, data, constraints, and mission commitments matter? Who is absent from the room?
- **Experience**: What evidence, testimony, and stakeholder encounter do decision-makers need?
- **Reflection**: What assumptions, interests, fears, tradeoffs, and likely consequences require discernment?
- **Action**: Who decides, who implements, what is reversible, and what safeguards and accountabilities apply?
- **Evaluation**: What indicators, voices, review dates, and repair mechanisms will govern revision?

Practically, the document calls for recognition, workload, and resources to be aligned with the desired educational work,[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] and for the IPP framework to be incorporated into program review and selected design processes without imposing a single method.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] Institutional congruence, in sum, means that SJU's structures should themselves embody what it claims to teach.

## Relationship to the Proposed Institutional Statement

The eight commitments accompany a proposed institutional statement for discussion that reads, in part: the IPP "begins with the concrete learner and context; creates rigorous encounters with knowledge, practice, persons, and the world; cultivates disciplined reflection; supports free and responsible action; and evaluates learning, impact, and design in order to begin again."[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] The document notes explicitly that this statement is an SJU interpretation proposed for discussion and is not a quotation from a Society of Jesus document.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

The commitments are also framed as an integrating architecture for SJU's existing mission, undergraduate learning outcomes (communication, critical thinking and inquiry, ethical reasoning and social justice, and Ignatian values and diverse perspectives), strategic plan, and programs—without replacing the General Education Program, Cornerstone Core, professional accreditation standards, or unit strategies.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]
