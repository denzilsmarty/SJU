# Navigating Globalization and Contemporary Challenges

## The Challenge of Knowledge Societies and Inequality

Globalization has fundamentally transformed how knowledge functions in society. Globalization has created "knowledge societies," in which development of persons, cultures and societies is tremendously dependent on access to knowledge in order to grow.[^chunk:eb2b19d4-419c-5d7f-abb3-4dd2680d8804] However, this transformation has created new inequalities: globalization has created new inequalities between those who enjoy the power given to them by knowledge, and those who are excluded from its benefits because they have no access to that knowledge.[^chunk:eb2b19d4-419c-5d7f-abb3-4dd2680d8804]

For Jesuit universities, this raises critical questions about their social responsibility. They must ask: who benefits from the knowledge produced in our institutions and who does not? Who needs the knowledge we can share, and how can we share it more effectively with those for whom that knowledge can truly make a difference, especially the poor and excluded?[^chunk:eb2b19d4-419c-5d7f-abb3-4dd2680d8804]

## Secularism and Fundamentalism: Twin Challenges to Faith and Reason

Our globalized world has seen the spread of two rival "ism's" that pose distinct challenges to Jesuit higher education.[^chunk:eb2b19d4-419c-5d7f-abb3-4dd2680d8804] On one hand, a dominant "world culture" is marked by an aggressive secularism that claims that faith has nothing to say to the world and its great problems (and which often claims that religion, in fact, is one of the world's great problems). On the other hand, the resurgence of various fundamentalisms appears as often fearful or angry reactions to postmodern world culture, which escape complexity by taking refuge in a certain "faith" divorced from or unregulated by human reason.[^chunk:eb2b19d4-419c-5d7f-abb3-4dd2680d8804]

Both secularism and fundamentalism exclude the possibility of fruitful dialogue and effective cooperation between reason and religious faith.[^chunk:eb2b19d4-419c-5d7f-abb3-4dd2680d8804] In Europe, this secularism is particularly aggressive and anti-Church; by contrast, Japan has maintained a secularism that is peaceful and respectful of religions for 300 or 400 years.[^chunk:379d97ca-c840-50ad-9c8f-720bdb6991ef]

### The Jesuit Response: Integration of Faith and Reason

The Jesuit tradition of learned ministry has always combined a healthy appreciation for human reason, thought, and culture, on the one hand, and a profound commitment to faith, the Gospel, the Church, on the other.[^chunk:eb2b19d4-419c-5d7f-abb3-4dd2680d8804] This commitment includes the integration of faith and justice in dialogue among religions and cultures. The training of the early Jesuits, for example, included the study of pagan authors of antiquity, the creative arts, science and mathematics, as well as a rigorous theological course of study.[^chunk:eb2b19d4-419c-5d7f-abb3-4dd2680d8804]

The life and achievements of Matteo Ricci exemplify how this training that harmoniously integrated faith and reason, Gospel and culture, bore creative fruit.[^chunk:eb2b19d4-419c-5d7f-abb3-4dd2680d8804] As secularism and fundamentalism spread globally, Jesuit universities are called to find new ways of creatively renewing this commitment to a dialogue between faith and culture that has always been a distinguishing mark of Jesuit learned ministry.[^chunk:eb2b19d4-419c-5d7f-abb3-4dd2680d8804]

## The Globalization of Superficiality

Beyond these ideological challenges, globalization presents a more subtle threat: the "globalization of superficiality." When one can access so much information so quickly and so painlessly; when one can express and publish to the world one's reactions so immediately and so unthinkingly in one's blogs or micro-blogs; when the latest opinion column or newest viral video can be spread so quickly to people half a world away, shaping their perceptions and feelings, then the laborious, painstaking work of serious, critical thinking often gets short-circuited.[^chunk:351ca433-6b01-5fc5-a795-fb9f7f4aebb3]

This superficiality manifests across multiple dimensions of human experience. When one can become "friends" so quickly and so painlessly with mere acquaintances or total strangers on social networks – and if one can so easily "unfriend" another without the hard work of encounter or, if need be, confrontation and then reconciliation – then relationships can also become superficial.[^chunk:351ca433-6b01-5fc5-a795-fb9f7f4aebb3] When one is overwhelmed with such a dizzying pluralism of choices and values and beliefs and visions of life, then one can so easily slip into the lazy superficiality of relativism or mere tolerance of others and their views, rather than engaging in the hard work of forming communities of dialogue in the search of truth and understanding.[^chunk:351ca433-6b01-5fc5-a795-fb9f7f4aebb3]

People lose the ability to engage with reality; that is a process of dehumanization that may be gradual and silent, but very real.[^chunk:77780b39-930f-537f-b638-88a23d0b9b83] People are losing their mental home, their culture, their points of reference.[^chunk:77780b39-930f-537f-b638-88a23d0b9b83]

## Market Economy Pressures on University Mission

Globalization has also intensified market forces on higher education institutions. Whether universities like it or not, the academy cannot evade from the forces of the market.[^chunk:71347df1-4b49-537d-aabd-99715fae0d7a] The financial limitations faced by universities not subsidized with public funds force them to depend ever more on the financial support of their students, and to make recourse to various systems for raising funds to secure the necessary resources to operate.[^chunk:71347df1-4b49-537d-aabd-99715fae0d7a]

This creates a fundamental threat to institutional autonomy and mission. The growing costs of education and the trend to privatization imply a progressive dependence on financial subsidies, which can turn into a veritable social mortgage.[^chunk:71347df1-4b49-537d-aabd-99715fae0d7a] It may happen that not all the sponsors or trustees are always disinterested, nor identify with the mission statements and the orientation of the university. The autonomy itself of the university and the freedom of research and instruction are at stake.[^chunk:71347df1-4b49-537d-aabd-99715fae0d7a]

Moreover, making the market and the economic interest the only driving force in society cannot come from God. The frightful results of economic globalization that have been introduced, against all ethics, are obvious: dehumanization, individualism, lack of solidarity, social fragmentation, a widening of the already existing gap between rich and poor, exclusion, lack of respect for human rights, economic and cultural neo-colonialism, exploitation, deterioration of the environment, violence, frustration.[^chunk:71347df1-4b49-537d-aabd-99715fae0d7a]

## Reinventing Jesuit University Mission in a Globalized Context

### Responding to Globalization with Critical Analysis

Ignatius would be fascinated by the phenomenon of globalization, with its incredible opportunities and threats, and would not run from the challenges that it involves.[^chunk:71347df1-4b49-537d-aabd-99715fae0d7a] To the universities corresponds an indispensable role in the critical analysis of globalization, with its positive and negative connotations, to orient the thought and the action of society.[^chunk:71347df1-4b49-537d-aabd-99715fae0d7a] In Ignatian language, it is a matter of an authentic process of discernment, in order to discover what is coming from the good spirit and what is coming from the bad.[^chunk:71347df1-4b49-537d-aabd-99715fae0d7a]

The university as a university has its word to say on these topics, which touch on fundamental aspects of the person and society. Various Jesuit universities are already making efforts, depending on their contexts, to address questions such as ethnic minorities, cultural pluralism, diversity, interreligious dialogue, migrants, refugees, injustice, poverty, exclusion, unemployment, the crisis of democracy.[^chunk:71347df1-4b49-537d-aabd-99715fae0d7a] However, it is not enough to denounce: it is necessary to also pronounce and propose.[^chunk:71347df1-4b49-537d-aabd-99715fae0d7a]

### Promoting Depth of Thought and Imagination

In response to the globalization of superficiality, Jesuit higher education must promote in creative new ways the depth of thought and imagination that are distinguishing marks of the Ignatian tradition.[^chunk:77780b39-930f-537f-b638-88a23d0b9b83]

Depth of thought and imagination in the Ignatian tradition involves a profound engagement with the real, a refusal to let go until one goes beneath the surface.[^chunk:77780b39-930f-537f-b638-88a23d0b9b83] It is a careful analysis (dismembering) for the sake of an integration (remembering) around what is deepest: God, Christ, the Gospel.[^chunk:77780b39-930f-537f-b638-88a23d0b9b83] The starting point will always be what is real: what is materially, concretely thought to be there; the world as we encounter it; the world of the senses so vividly described in the Gospels themselves; a world of suffering and need, a broken world with many broken people in need of healing.[^chunk:77780b39-930f-537f-b638-88a23d0b9b83]

Ignation contemplation is a very powerful tool, and it is a shifting from the left side of the brain to the right. But it is essential to understand that imagination is not the same as fantasy. Fantasy is a flight from reality, to a world where we create images for the sake of a diversity of images. Imagination grasps reality.[^chunk:77780b39-930f-537f-b638-88a23d0b9b83]

Critical to this approach is understanding that the depth of learning and imagination encompasses and integrates intellectual rigor with reflection on the experience of reality together with the creative imagination to work toward constructing a more humane, just, sustainable, and faith-filled world.[^chunk:b1536921-5466-57c2-9df8-88b2a76ab92b] The experience of reality includes the broken world, especially the world of the poor, waiting for healing.[^chunk:b1536921-5466-57c2-9df8-88b2a76ab92b]

### Building Universal Networks of Cooperation

One of the most positive aspects of globalization is that it has made communication and cooperation possible with an ease and at a scale that was unimaginable even just a decade ago.[^chunk:b1536921-5466-57c2-9df8-88b2a76ab92b] However, Jesuit universities have not yet fully mobilized this potential. Currently, there is only a commonality of Ignatian inspiration rather than a coherent "Jesuit university network": Each institution operates relatively autonomously of each other, and as a result, the impact of each as a proyecto social is limited.[^chunk:379d97ca-c840-50ad-9c8f-720bdb6991ef]

The challenge is to move beyond loose family relationships and re-imagine and re-organize universities so that, in this globalized world, they can more effectively realize the universality which has always been part of Ignatius' vision of the Society.[^chunk:379d97ca-c840-50ad-9c8f-720bdb6991ef] This requires moving from conferences and discussions to the establishment of operational consortia among universities focused on responding together to "frontier challenges" of the world which have a supra-national or supra-continental character.[^chunk:379d97ca-c840-50ad-9c8f-720bdb6991ef]

Three priority consortia have been proposed:[^chunk:379d97ca-c840-50ad-9c8f-720bdb6991ef]

1. **A consortium to confront creatively the challenge of aggressive "new atheisms"** and secularism, which differ significantly by region. While European secularism is particularly aggressive and anti-Church, other regions maintain peaceful, pluralistic approaches.[^chunk:379d97ca-c840-50ad-9c8f-720bdb6991ef]

2. **A consortium focused on analyses and solutions to world poverty, inequality, and injustice**, addressing challenges of development, meaning, suffering, violence, and war.[^chunk:379d97ca-c840-50ad-9c8f-720bdb6991ef]

3. **A consortium focused on global environmental degradation**, which affects more directly and painfully the lives of the poor, with a view to enabling a more sustainable future for the world.[^chunk:379d97ca-c840-50ad-9c8f-720bdb6991ef]

Ignatius' vision of the world was clearly global. Although he wanted Jesuits to adapt to the place where they were working and learning the local language and culture – what we would call today "inculturation" – he wanted them to be available to "travel through the world and live in any part of it," always open to the MAGIS.[^chunk:71347df1-4b49-537d-aabd-99715fae0d7a] This is the way he experienced the tension between the local and the global, that is, thinking on a global level, but acting on the local.[^chunk:71347df1-4b49-537d-aabd-99715fae0d7a]

### Re-founding Universities Through Lay Partnership

A fundamental challenge to reinvention emerges from demographic realities. In 1995, when General Congregation 34 spoke about the diminishing number of Jesuits in universities, there were about 22,850 Jesuits in the world. By 2010, there were about 18,250 – about 4,600 fewer Jesuits.[^chunk:eb2b19d4-419c-5d7f-abb3-4dd2680d8804]

The proportion for education at large in the Society is estimated at 95% lay and 5% Jesuits.[^chunk:71347df1-4b49-537d-aabd-99715fae0d7a] For simple realism and by the Ignatian principle of accommodation to persons and times, the Society considers today the "partnership with others" to be one of the characteristics of their way of proceeding.[^chunk:71347df1-4b49-537d-aabd-99715fae0d7a]

The identity of Jesuit higher education is at stake, especially in the West and in the industrialized countries. The problem of the "next generation" is not an imaginary one. At the pace that the physical presence of the Jesuits is disappearing, the ethos of the institution, its Ignatian, Catholic, Christian culture, may also disappear, if no attention is paid to the preparation of the generation that is to take over.[^chunk:9cd06de5-b134-5497-82b6-f7dcc671cd03]

This responsibility falls above all on Jesuits themselves. Preparation in the vision and the shared mission between Jesuits and collaborators is a priority of the first order in higher education.[^chunk:9cd06de5-b134-5497-82b6-f7dcc671cd03] However, lay people are not called to be mini-Jesuits, but rather to live their own lay vocation. Respecting the way in which the Lord leads each person is fundamental to Ignatian spirituality.[^chunk:9cd06de5-b134-5497-82b6-f7dcc671cd03]

Concrete practices are helping to share the mission and deepen identity:[^chunk:9cd06de5-b134-5497-82b6-f7dcc671cd03]

- Courses for orientation or induction for new professors and board members, to share the ethos of education
- Programs of on-going formation, for both lay people and Jesuits, to form an apostolic team
- Priority given to identity and mission in the hiring of personnel
- The offering of the Spiritual Exercises to personnel in various modalities
- The decisive role of Jesuits, who should see ways of still being present, now no longer exercising power but still exercising influence in the institution

With the professionalism, commitment, and depth that lay collaborators bring, universities can continue dreaming, beginning new enterprises, and moving forward together.[^chunk:03c4079a-92d2-5150-b1ea-fca464c98472]

## Learned Ministry and the Future of Jesuit Universities

If Ignatius and his first companions were to start the Society of Jesus again today, would they still take on universities as a ministry of the Society?[^chunk:eb2b19d4-419c-5d7f-abb3-4dd2680d8804] This fundamental question requires universities to re-create themselves. Every generation has to re-create the faith, they have to re-create the journey, they have to re-create the institutions. If we lose the ability to re-create, we have lost the spirit.[^chunk:03c4079a-92d2-5150-b1ea-fca464c98472]

Research in Jesuit universities must always ultimately be conceived in terms of what General Congregation 34 calls "learned ministry" or the "intellectual apostolate."[^chunk:379d97ca-c840-50ad-9c8f-720bdb6991ef] It is always "ministry" or "apostolate": in the service of the faith, of the Church, of the human family and the created world that God wants to draw more and more into the realm of God's Kingdom of life and love.[^chunk:379d97ca-c840-50ad-9c8f-720bdb6991ef] It is always research that is aimed at making a difference in people's lives, rather than simply a recondite conversation among members of a closed elite group.[^chunk:379d97ca-c840-50ad-9c8f-720bdb6991ef]

The challenge could not be greater. It is necessary at all costs to not lose sight of the raison d'être of the university, as a center of integration of knowledge which proposes the search, not for the "narrow truth," but for the "whole truth," with an "accurate vision and comprehension of all things."[^chunk:71347df1-4b49-537d-aabd-99715fae0d7a] It is necessary to discern and to make a choice for the kind of greater service, which the university intends to give to Church and society. More than knowledge and science, it is wisdom, which academies should offer.[^chunk:71347df1-4b49-537d-aabd-99715fae0d7a]
