## How Often Is the Document Reviewed?

The document specifies a **two-phase review cadence**:[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

1. **Annual review during development** — while the document is still being refined, tested, and consulted upon before formal institutional adoption.
2. **At least triennial review after adoption** — once the framework has been formally adopted by SJU, a minimum review frequency of once every three years is prescribed.

The distinction reflects the different needs of the two phases. During development, the document is expected to change frequently as pilots are run, stakeholders consulted, and terminology resolved; annual review keeps it current with emerging practice and feedback. After adoption, the framework is presumed more stable; triennial review ensures it remains aligned with new Society documents and evidence without imposing unnecessary administrative burden.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

## What Triggers an Out-of-Cycle Review?

The document does not enumerate an explicit list of out-of-cycle triggers. However, the governance language does establish that review should occur **"against new Society documents and evidence,"** and that the guide should be revised whenever such developments arise.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] This implies that the following would warrant revision outside the scheduled cycle:

- Promulgation of new or revised documents by ICAJE or the Society of Jesus (for example, a successor to the 1993 *Ignatian Pedagogy: A Practical Approach* or a new General Congregation decree).
- Significant new empirical or practice-based evidence that changes the claims SJU makes about the paradigm's effects.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]
- Institutional changes at SJU—such as a new strategic plan, revised learning outcomes, or changes to the General Education Program—that affect the document's alignment with SJU mission and strategy.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

## Who Has Authority to Approve Revisions?

The document specifies that revisions should proceed through **a named steward, version history, consultation with faculty and student-facing units, and periodic review**.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] The document control block records the owner/steward field as "to be designated," meaning the authority has not yet been formally assigned at the Version 0.1 stage.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

Phase 1 of the implementation roadmap elaborates this further: it calls for the designation of a **cross-functional steward and editorial group**, followed by review of the draft with faculty governance, Mission, academic leadership, student-facing divisions, assessment, accessibility, and students.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] An authority policy governing sources is also to be resolved in this phase.

The governance guardrails make clear that the IPP rubric should not be used for personnel judgment without validated purpose, due process, and contextual interpretation—reinforcing the principle that the steward role carries interpretive, not disciplinary, authority.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

## The Difference Between Annual and Triennial Review

The two review cadences serve different institutional moments:

**Annual review (during development)** corresponds to Phase 1 and Phase 2 of the implementation roadmap: establishing shared meaning, resolving terminology, approving source authority policy, running voluntary pilots, and gathering baseline and implementation evidence.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] During this period the document is expected to be revised substantively—potentially including changes to the institutional statement, the design canvas, the rubric, or the source hierarchy—based on consultation and pilot feedback.

**Triennial review (after adoption)** corresponds to Phase 3: integrating and sustaining the framework, connecting IPP evidence to assessment and accreditation processes, and reviewing the source-of-truth document "on a published cycle."[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] At this stage, the expectation is that the framework is institutionally settled; the triennial rhythm ensures it is checked against any new Society documents, updated evidence, or changed SJU conditions without requiring perpetual revision.

In short, annual review reflects the instability of a living draft; triennial review reflects the relative stability of an adopted institutional standard.

## How Does Version 0.1 Become an Officially Adopted Document?

Version 0.1 is explicitly a *draft for discussion*, not an adopted policy.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] The implementation roadmap describes the steps through which it would become official:

**Phase 1 — Establish shared meaning**
- Designate a cross-functional steward and editorial group.
- Review the draft with faculty governance, Mission, academic leadership, student-facing divisions, assessment, accessibility, and students.
- Resolve terminology and approve an authority policy for sources.
- Produce a concise public primer derived from the full guide.
- Identify where existing SJU practice already embodies the paradigm.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

**Phase 2 — Test in authentic settings**
- Support voluntary pilots across different schools, modalities, and co-curricular units.
- Fund course and process redesign, not merely workshops.
- Gather baseline and implementation evidence.
- Include community partners and students in appropriate evaluation.
- Document tensions, adaptations, and counterexamples.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

**Phase 3 — Integrate and sustain**
- Incorporate the framework into faculty and staff formation, program review, and selected design processes.
- Align recognition, workload, and resources with the desired work.
- Create exemplars from SJU disciplines and units.
- Connect IPP evidence to existing assessment and accreditation processes.
- Review the source-of-truth document on a published cycle.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

Adoption is thus understood as an institutional process—not a single approval event—requiring consultation, piloting, evidence collection, and governance endorsement before the document transitions from Version 0.1 to a formally adopted institutional framework.

## Version History and Source-of-Truth Discipline

The document establishes several principles to maintain the integrity of the source-of-truth over time:

- **A four-tier source hierarchy** governs what counts as authoritative evidence for IPP claims, with Society documents in Tiers 1 and 2 taking precedence over institutional or secondary sources in Tier 4.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **Citation conventions** require numbered-paragraph references to the 1993 ICAJE document to ensure claims are traceable across editions.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **Calibrated claims** must distinguish what is well-supported by primary sources, what is supported by accumulated practice, what is promising but requires local evidence, and what is not established.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]
- **Version history** must be maintained alongside a named steward so that the document's evolution is transparent and traceable.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

These controls are designed to prevent local slogans, consultancy models, or secondary sources from being presented as if they were the Society's own definitions.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
