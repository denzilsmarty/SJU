## Overview

This article addresses how Saint Joseph's University (SJU) governs the use of the Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm (IPP) rubric, what counts as legitimate versus prohibited use, and where firm institutional guardrails apply. It draws from SJU's working source-of-truth document on the IPP (Draft 0.1, 23 August 2026).

---

## What the IPP Rubric Is Actually Designed to Evaluate

The IPP design-review rubric is explicitly framed as a tool for evaluating **educational designs**—courses, programs, advising processes, co-curricular experiences, policies, or institutional initiatives—not the persons who create or deliver them.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

The rubric assesses five dimensions of design quality:

| Dimension | Emerging | Developing | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Mission language appended but not connected to outcomes | Academic and formative purposes named | Purposes integrated, specific, and assessable *without moral scoring* |
| **Context** | Learners and conditions assumed | Some prior knowledge and access needs considered | Learner, disciplinary, social, institutional, and power contexts shape the design |
| **Experience** | Activity or content exposure dominates | Experience aligned but weakly appropriated | Significant direct or vicarious encounter engages disciplined attention and appropriate affect |
| **Reflection** | Opinion or personal disclosure requested | Structured prompts connect experience and concepts | Evidence, assumptions, affect, perspectives, consequences, and discernment integrated |
| **Action** | No response, or generic activism, expected | Application or choice follows learning | Interior and/or outward action proportionate, free, role-aware, and accountable |
| **Evaluation** | Grade only, or character claim | Learning and reflection receive feedback | Mastery, judgment, consequence, equity, and design reviewed; feedback begins a new cycle |
| **Relationship** | Contact is transactional | Support and challenge present | Trust, clarity, availability, learner agency, and appropriate boundaries embody *cura personalis* |
| **Evidence** | IPP label substitutes for evaluation | Some local evidence collected | Claims calibrated; multiple forms of evidence drive revision |

The rubric is intended to **support peer consultation and improvement**, not to function as a compliance score detached from context.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

## Why the Rubric Cannot Be Used for Personnel Evaluation

SJU's governance guardrails explicitly state that the IPP rubric should **not be used for personnel judgment without validated purpose, due process, and contextual interpretation**.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

This prohibition is rooted in several foundational principles of the paradigm itself:

1. **The rubric evaluates designs, not virtue.** The document is explicit: "This rubric evaluates a design, not the virtue of an educator or student."[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] Applying it to personnel decisions would require a categorical transformation the rubric was never built to support.

2. **Assessment must not become moral surveillance.** A core principle of the IPP is that evaluation "should not claim to measure the state of a student's conscience, compassion, faith, political virtue, or human worth."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] The same logic applies to faculty and staff: scoring a person's interior formation or mission commitment through a design rubric violates the paradigm's foundational respect for freedom.

3. **Faculty formation must not become a loyalty test.** The source document explicitly states: "Faculty formation should not become a loyalty test. Mission effectiveness grows through invitation, intellectual seriousness, protected time, resources, peer leadership, and evidence."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

4. **Implementation is meant to be invitational and developmental.** The implementation roadmap specifies that IPP adoption "should be invitational, developmental, and evidence-generating while institutional expectations remain clear where mission responsibilities are formally assigned."[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

## What Counts as Misuse of the IPP Rubric

The SJU document identifies several specific categories of misuse, both of the rubric and of the broader paradigm:

### Rubric-Specific Misuse
- Using the rubric **as a compliance score detached from context**.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]
- Using it for **personnel judgment** without validated purpose, due process, and contextual interpretation.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]
- Substituting the IPP **label** for actual evaluation (rated as "Emerging" in the Evidence dimension).[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

### Paradigm-Level Misuse
The source document provides an extensive list of things the IPP is **not**:[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

- A **five-box checklist** — naming all five terms does not make a course Ignatian.
- A **compulsory linear sequence** — the elements interact and recur.
- A **substitute for disciplinary expertise** — it depends on strong content and valid methods.
- A **religious compliance test** — it invites engagement with ultimate meaning while respecting conscience.
- A **synonym for service-learning** — many forms of engagement can embody the IPP.
- A **guarantee of justice or transformation** — poorly designed experience can reinforce stereotype.
- A **technique for eliciting personal disclosure** — students retain the right to privacy.
- A **therapeutic model** — educators must know when to refer students to qualified support.
- A **brand claim** — credibility depends on enacted practice, not a label.

### Assessment-Specific Misuse
Evaluation becomes misuse when it crosses into **moral scoring**: grading a student's faith, conscience, political conformity, or human worth.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Rubrics must "describe observable qualities in work, not identity claims about the person."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

### Reflection-Specific Misuse
Reflection becomes misuse when the instructor **predetermines the morally acceptable insight**, or when students learn to "imitate mission language for a grade" — i.e., performative compliance.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## Can an Institution Certify Someone as 'Ignatian' After a Short Training?

**No.** The governance guardrails explicitly state that SJU should **not certify individuals as 'Ignatian' based on a short course**.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

This guardrail reflects several deeper principles:

- **Formation is lifelong, not a credential event.** The document defines formation as "lifelong, accompanied integration of knowledge, experience, reflection, relationships, faith or ultimate meaning, and responsible action." It "is not completed at graduation," and by extension not completed after a brief training program.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

- **The IPP cannot be implemented by memo or workshop alone.** The 1993 source text (ICAJE) makes educator development central, noting that the paradigm "requires practice, shared reflection, and in many cases a change of heart, not merely distribution of a document."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

- **The proposed SJU faculty formation sequence is multi-phase**, moving through orientation, experience, design studio, peer reflection, implementation, evaluation, and advanced practice.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] A short course addresses at most Phase 1 of this trajectory.

- **Credibility depends on enacted practice, not a label.** The IPP "is not a brand claim" — its credibility rests on what is actually done and how the institution aligns.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## Is Performative Mission Language Required in Every Syllabus?

**No.** The governance guardrails are explicit: SJU should **not require performative mission language in every syllabus**.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

This prohibition reflects several interconnected concerns:

1. **The IPP is not a five-box checklist.** A course need not display five labeled units to embody the paradigm. What matters is whether the design actually answers the governing questions for each dynamic.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

2. **Performative language is specifically identified as a failure mode of reflection.** When students (or educators) learn to imitate mission language for external approval, reflection has become hollow. The document describes this as a distortion: "performative when students learn to imitate mission language for a grade."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

3. **Not every lesson needs all five elements.** The IPP "should be visible across an appropriate learning arc," not mechanically reproduced in every document.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

4. **Implementation must be invitational, developmental, and evidence-generating.** Mandating language in syllabi without pedagogical purpose is the kind of procedural compliance that the document contrasts with genuine mission alignment.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

5. **The institution teaches through alignment, not rhetoric.** The document's eighth institutional commitment is "institutional congruence: policies, resources, incentives, and technologies support the educational commitments they proclaim."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Appending mission language to syllabi without underlying design change is precisely what the rubric labels "Emerging" — mission language "appended but not connected to outcomes."[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

## The Implementation Roadmap and Guardrails in Context

SJU's governance guardrails are embedded within a three-phase implementation roadmap:[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

**Phase 1 — Establish Shared Meaning**
- Designate a cross-functional steward and editorial group.
- Review the draft with faculty governance, Mission, academic leadership, student-facing divisions, assessment, accessibility, and students.
- Resolve terminology and approve an authority policy for sources.
- Produce a concise public primer.
- Identify where existing SJU practice already embodies the paradigm.

**Phase 2 — Test in Authentic Settings**
- Support *voluntary* pilots across different schools, modalities, and co-curricular units.
- Fund course and process redesign, not merely workshops.
- Gather baseline and implementation evidence.
- Include community partners and students in evaluation.
- Document tensions, adaptations, and counterexamples.

**Phase 3 — Integrate and Sustain**
- Incorporate the framework into faculty and staff formation, program review, and selected design processes *without imposing a single method*.
- Align recognition, workload, and resources with the desired work.
- Create disciplinary exemplars.
- Connect IPP evidence to existing assessment and accreditation processes.
- Review the source-of-truth document on a published cycle.

All three phases are governed by the explicit statement that implementation "should be invitational, developmental, and evidence-generating while institutional expectations remain clear where mission responsibilities are formally assigned."[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

## Calibrated Claims: What SJU May and May Not Assert

The document establishes a four-level framework for calibrating institutional claims about the IPP:[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

| Claim Level | Status |
|---|---|
| What the IPP means, its purposes, and its place in Jesuit education | **Well supported by primary sources** |
| That structured experience, reflection, authentic action, feedback, and relational teaching improve learning | **Supported by accumulated practice and related research** |
| That a particular IPP implementation improves belonging, ethical reasoning, professional judgment, or community impact | **Promising but requiring local evidence** |
| That labeling a course 'IPP' causes formation; that every learner will reach the same values; that the paradigm outperforms all alternatives | **Not established** |

The prohibition on certifying individuals as Ignatian and on using the rubric for personnel evaluation both follow directly from this epistemological discipline: the evidence base does not support such claims, and the paradigm's own values of freedom and non-coercion would be violated by making them.
