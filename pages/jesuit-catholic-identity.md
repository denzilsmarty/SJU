## Understanding the Mission Priority Examen

The Mission Priority Examen is a comprehensive institutional self-assessment process required of all Jesuit colleges and universities.[^chunk:28ee9d8b-f65d-5276-83ba-a2e0ad82024b] This process is designed to explore areas of strength and weakness in the life of an institution's mission. The Examen is important for Jesuit colleges because it provides a structured opportunity for the entire campus community—faculty, staff, and students across all areas—to contribute their perspectives on institutional mission and identity.[^chunk:28ee9d8b-f65d-5276-83ba-a2e0ad82024b] Additionally, peer reviewers from other institutions evaluate the findings, bringing external perspective to the self-assessment process.[^chunk:28ee9d8b-f65d-5276-83ba-a2e0ad82024b]

## Gonzaga University's 2018-19 Mission Priority Examen Experience

During the 2018-19 academic year, Gonzaga University completed its Mission Priority Examen, representing a significant achievement in the university's approach to deepening its Jesuit, Catholic, and humanistic identity and practice.[^chunk:28ee9d8b-f65d-5276-83ba-a2e0ad82024b]

### Key Outcomes and Priorities Identified

The Examen process led to the identification of four institutional priorities:[^chunk:28ee9d8b-f65d-5276-83ba-a2e0ad82024b]

1. A Work Values project
2. A comprehensive Jesuit Identity Initiative
3. A reaffirmed mission-based commitment to Diversity, Equity and Cultural Fluency
4. Administrative Restructuring to support the vitality and viability of the institution in service to its mission

### Critical Gaps Identified

The final Mission Priority Examen report identified several areas requiring intentional focus:[^chunk:28ee9d8b-f65d-5276-83ba-a2e0ad82024b]

- Clarity and accountability in decision-making, resource allocation and priorities
- Hiring for Mission Formation and education about the Society of Jesus, the Catholic tradition, Ignatian spirituality, Jesuit pedagogy and contemporary mission leadership
- Integration of mission identity in curricula across disciplines and all areas of university life
- Jesuit engagement

### Organizational and Programmatic Changes

Following the Examen findings, Gonzaga implemented several concrete changes. The university merged University Ministry and the Office of Mission into a single Office of Mission and Ministry, reflecting the conviction that in a Jesuit context, "mission" and "ministry" are ultimately connected.[^chunk:28ee9d8b-f65d-5276-83ba-a2e0ad82024b] This unified office offered a Catholic Identity Series and other opportunities for intellectual engagement and reflection throughout the academic year.[^chunk:28ee9d8b-f65d-5276-83ba-a2e0ad82024b]

### Response to the Catholic Sexual Abuse Crisis

Amid these efforts to deepen mission identity, President McCulloh appointed a Commission on University Response to the Catholic Sexual Abuse Crisis to engage with the greater Gonzaga community.[^chunk:28ee9d8b-f65d-5276-83ba-a2e0ad82024b] The commission's goals include discovering how to stand in solidarity with victim-survivors of abuse, healing and repairing harm inflicted by abuse, and addressing systemic institutional and social failures.[^chunk:28ee9d8b-f65d-5276-83ba-a2e0ad82024b]
