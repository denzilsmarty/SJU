# Adolfo Nicolás on Depth and the Globalization of Superficiality (2010)

Adolfo Nicolás, S.J., Superior General of the Society of Jesus, delivered a landmark address in 2010 titled **"Depth, Universality, and Learned Ministry: Challenges to Jesuit Higher Education Today."** The address diagnosed a cultural condition he called the "globalization of superficiality" and proposed depth of thought, global collaboration, and learned service as the proper response of Jesuit universities.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

---

## What Nicolás Meant by the 'Globalization of Superficiality'

Nicolás's central diagnosis was that the worldwide spread of digital media and instant information access had produced not greater wisdom but a *globalization of superficiality*—a cultural drift away from sustained, disciplined thinking toward rapid, shallow engagement with ideas.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] The concern was not technology as such, but the human habits that an always-on information environment tends to cultivate: the preference for quick retrieval over prolonged inquiry, for confident opinion over hard-won understanding, and for the sensation of knowing over the labor of actually understanding.

The address is placed within the broader trajectory of Jesuit higher-education thought. Kolvenbach had already argued in 2000 that the measure of a Jesuit university is *who students become*, not merely what they know.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Nicolás sharpened that challenge by identifying superficiality as the specific contemporary threat to that formative ambition. If knowledge can be generated and retrieved instantly, the distinctive work of a university must be something more: the cultivation of depth—of thought and of imagination—that no search engine can replicate.

The address is listed as a Tier 3 authoritative interpretive source for the Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm (IPP), alongside addresses by Arrupe, Kolvenbach, and Sosa, meaning it carries substantial weight for interpreting the Jesuit charism in higher education but is subordinate to promulgated Society documents such as the 1993 *Ignatian Pedagogy: A Practical Approach*.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## Remedies Nicolás Proposed for Jesuit Higher Education

Nicolás's title—*Depth, Universality, and Learned Ministry*—names his three-part prescription:

1. **Depth of thought and imagination.** Against the globalization of superficiality, Jesuit universities must cultivate slow, disciplined, affectively engaged thinking—precisely the kind of appropriated understanding that Ignatius himself privileged in *Annotation 2* of the *Spiritual Exercises*, where he warns that much knowledge does not satisfy as deeply as inwardly grasping and "tasting" what is true.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Depth names both an intellectual standard (rigorous inquiry, mastery of evidence, sustained reflection) and a personal one (knowledge integrated with affect, experience, and commitment).

2. **Universality—global collaboration.** Nicolás called Jesuit universities toward a consciousness of global interdependence. The globalization of superficiality is itself a global phenomenon; responding to it requires networks of institutions capable of sustained, cross-cultural intellectual work. This connects to the IPP's concern that context always includes political, economic, religious, and technological environments, and that learners must be formed for a world they share with others.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

3. **Learned ministry—service shaped by scholarship.** Nicolás insisted that Jesuit higher education must join deep learning to service. This echoes Arrupe's 1973 critique of education for self-advancement and his call for graduates whose knowledge becomes solidarity.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] For Nicolás, "learned" ministry is the corrective both to activism without rigour and to scholarship without social purpose.

Together these three remedies extend the Ledesma–Kolvenbach fourfold account of university purpose (*utilitas*, *iustitia*, *humanitas*, *fides*) and sharpen it for an age in which sheer information abundance makes depth a scarce and precious good.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## Nicolás's Critique and Contemporary Digital and AI-Shaped Learning Environments

Nicolás delivered his address in 2010, before large-language models and generative AI became mainstream educational realities. His framework nonetheless anticipates the current situation with striking precision.

The 2026 Saint Joseph's University IPP guide—drawing on Nicolás alongside Sosa's 2018 observation that the *digital ecosystem* constitutes a new cultural paradigm—draws an explicit conclusion: **"In an AI-shaped university, the IPP becomes more—not less—important."**[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] The reasoning is directly Nicolasian:

- Generative AI systems can assist research, provide feedback, widen accessibility, and support rehearsal—but they cannot assume responsibility, enter a mutual relationship, discern a vocation, or substitute for the human encounter at the heart of *cura personalis*.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- When "information can be generated instantly and fluency can be simulated, the distinctive work of a Jesuit university is not merely transmission. It is the accompanied formation of persons who can encounter reality, discern what is true and worth doing, act with and for others, and learn humbly from what follows."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- Delegating essential judgment to a generative system, or counterfeiting human care through automated interaction, is explicitly identified as inconsistent with the paradigm.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Applying Nicolás's own categories to the current moment:

| Nicolás's remedy | AI-era manifestation of the threat | Jesuit response |
|---|---|---|
| **Depth** | AI-generated fluency mimics understanding without the labor of appropriation | Prioritize reflection, discernment, and affectively engaged encounter with evidence |
| **Universality** | Algorithmic personalization narrows information exposure | Design for cross-cultural, interdisciplinary, and multi-perspectival inquiry |
| **Learned ministry** | AI can produce outputs; it cannot form a conscience or a vocation | Center human relationship, accompaniment, and accountable action |

The IPP's five dynamics—context, experience, reflection, action, and evaluation—function as structural safeguards against AI-amplified superficiality precisely because they insist that learning must be personally *appropriated*, not just received; reflected upon, not merely encountered; and enacted through accountable choice, not just displayed as output.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## Nicolás in the Jesuit Higher Education Tradition

Nicolás's 2010 address occupies a defined place in the chain of authoritative Jesuit higher-education thought:

- **Arrupe (1973, 1980):** critique of dehumanising technical excellence; call for service and solidarity.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **GC 34, Decree 17 (1995):** affirmation of the Jesuit university as a site of teaching, research, interdisciplinary work, and faith–justice integration.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **Kolvenbach, Santa Clara (2000):** the measure of a Jesuit university is *who students become*; educated solidarity requires contact, inquiry, and reflection.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **Kolvenbach (2001):** recovery of the Ledesma fourfold purposes (*utilitas*, *iustitia*, *humanitas*, *fides*).[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **Nicolás (2010):** globalization of superficiality; depth, universality, learned ministry.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **Sosa (2018):** reconciliation, global citizenship, and the digital ecosystem as a new cultural paradigm.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Nicolás thus functions as the hinge figure between Kolvenbach's emphasis on *who students become* and Sosa's engagement with the *digital ecosystem*—naming the specific cultural mechanism (the globalization of superficiality) that makes depth both more difficult and more urgently necessary.
