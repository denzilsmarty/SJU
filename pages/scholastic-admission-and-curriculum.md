## Admission Criteria for Scholastics

The Jesuit educational system implemented careful selection procedures for admitting scholastics. A fundamental principle was that "care should be taken to be strict rather than easy in accepting such students."[^chunk:edab30e0-a1da-5398-8732-cb477ff6cee1] Moreover, "a careful choice should be made of those who are to be admitted, by subjecting them to an individual examination before admission."[^chunk:edab30e0-a1da-5398-8732-cb477ff6cee1]

The Society pursued an innovative admission strategy by accepting "young men whose good habits of life and talent give hope that they will become both virtuous and learned."[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb] This approach allowed the Society to expand its membership beyond the scarcity of already-learned and virtuous candidates. The admission process recognized that "those who are both good and learned are relatively few," making it necessary to invest in the formation of promising youth.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb]

Once admitted, scholastics entered a structured probationary system. After the proper foundation of "abnegation of themselves and the needed progress in virtues" was established, scholastics would "be trained in letters and in the manner of employing them, as a help to know and serve better God, our Creator and Lord."[^chunk:6ad612e4-d2d0-5f75-b6e1-c7b7d9f783d3]

## Health and Spiritual Guidelines

### Physical Health Regulations

The Constitutions emphasized careful attention to the bodily welfare of scholastics in colleges. "Special attention should be given to their abstaining from studies at times inopportune for bodily health, to their taking sufficient sleep, and to their observance of moderation in mental labors so as to be able to keep at them longer both during their studies and later on when using what they have studied for the glory of God our Lord."[^chunk:edab30e0-a1da-5398-8732-cb477ff6cee1]

### Spiritual Practices and Prayer

Spiritual life was carefully structured to balance devotion with scholarly pursuits. During the probationary period, scholastics followed the same regulations as those in houses, but once approved and devoted to studies, "care must be taken that they do not through fervor in study grow cool in their love of true virtues and of religious life."[^chunk:edab30e0-a1da-5398-8732-cb477ff6cee1]

The schedule for spiritual practices included: "in addition to confession and Communion every eight days and daily Mass, they will have one hour, during which they will recite the Hours of Our Lady, examine their consciences twice each day, and add other prayers according to each one's devotion to fill out the rest of the aforesaid hour."[^chunk:edab30e0-a1da-5398-8732-cb477ff6cee1] Importantly, "during this time of study, the pursuit of letters, undertaken with a pure intention of serving God, and demanding, so to speak, the whole man, will not be less pleasing to God our Lord, but rather more so."[^chunk:15808037-5d16-5c4b-9441-070f71db7b26]

Vow renewal provided additional spiritual structure. "Twice each year, at Easter and Christmas, it will be good for the scholastics to renew their simple vows, which will be treated later in the Fifth Part. They make this renewal to increase their devotion, to refresh their memory of the obligation they are under, and to confirm themselves more solidly in their vocation."[^chunk:15808037-5d16-5c4b-9441-070f71db7b26]

### Moderation of Devotional Practices

The Society deliberately limited excessive mortifications during the study period. Impediments to study such as "devotions and mortifications which are excessive, or are assumed without proper order" were to be removed as much as possible.[^chunk:27f097eb-40cc-5683-b95c-397a2ca7bc0b] This reflected a practical wisdom that scholastics would later have more opportunity for such practices after completing their studies.

## Academic Curriculum

### Subjects of Study

The Jesuit curriculum was comprehensive and structured around the ultimate goal of helping souls. "The end of the learning which is acquired in this Society is, with God's favor, to help the souls of its own members and those of their neighbors."[^chunk:edab30e0-a1da-5398-8732-cb477ff6cee1]

The required academic disciplines included:

"Help is derived from the humane letters of different languages, logic, natural and moral philosophy, metaphysics, scholastic and positive theology, and Sacred Scripture."[^chunk:edab30e0-a1da-5398-8732-cb477ff6cee1]

Clarifications specified that "in addition to grammar, rhetoric is understood to be under the classification of humane letters."[^chunk:27f097eb-40cc-5683-b95c-397a2ca7bc0b] Natural philosophy referred chiefly to Aristotelian physics, while moral philosophy focused on ethics in the Aristotelian tradition rather than Stoic authors.[^chunk:27f097eb-40cc-5683-b95c-397a2ca7bc0b]

### Flexible Application of the Curriculum

Despite the comprehensive curriculum, flexibility was built into the system. "According to the age, ability, inclination, and basic knowledge which a particular person has, or in accordance with the common good which is hoped for, he could be applied to all these subjects or to one or several of them. For whoever cannot distinguish himself in all of them should strive to do so in at least one."[^chunk:edab30e0-a1da-5398-8732-cb477ff6cee1]

"What particular studies each individual scholastic should make will likewise be left to the discretion of the superiors. Nevertheless, when someone has aptitude, the better he is grounded in the aforementioned subjects the better will it be."[^chunk:edab30e0-a1da-5398-8732-cb477ff6cee1]

Some scholastics were sent to colleges "not in hope of their coming out with the learning previously described but rather to lighten the burden of others; for example, a priest to hear confessions."[^chunk:edab30e0-a1da-5398-8732-cb477ff6cee1] For these individuals, "it would be good, following the superior's directions, to study what they can and try to make progress in the languages and cases of conscience and, in sum, in whatever can be of greatest use to them for the common good of souls."[^chunk:edab30e0-a1da-5398-8732-cb477ff6cee1]

### Aids to Efficient Study

Schollastics were instructed that "the scholastics should make it their chief endeavor to preserve purity of conscience and a right intention in studying. They should not seek anything in their studies except the glory of God and the good of souls."[^chunk:27f097eb-40cc-5683-b95c-397a2ca7bc0b]

The rector held authority over academic progression: "The rector will observe and decide by means of a suitable examination how much time must be given to one branch, and when the students should pass on to another."[^chunk:27f097eb-40cc-5683-b95c-397a2ca7bc0b]

Selection of appropriate texts was also important: "In each branch, they ought to follow the doctrine which is safer and more approved, and the authors who teach it. In the pagan books of humane letters nothing immoral should be read. What remains can be used by the Society like the spoils of Egypt."[^chunk:27f097eb-40cc-5683-b95c-397a2ca7bc0b]

### Latin Studies

While the sources emphasize humane letters broadly, Latin held a central place in Renaissance education. The mastery of classical languages, particularly through rhetoric and grammar, formed the foundation for advanced studies and the educated discourse expected of Jesuit priests.
