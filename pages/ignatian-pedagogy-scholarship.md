## Overview

Ignatian pedagogy is an educational philosophy rooted in the spiritual and intellectual tradition of St. Ignatius of Loyola, the founder of the Society of Jesus (Jesuits). This scholarly resource page provides comprehensive information about Ignatian pedagogy, key research, institutional implementations, and foundational concepts that guide Jesuit education worldwide.

## What is Ignatian Pedagogy?

Ignatian pedagogy is based on St. Ignatius's method of helping individuals become closer to Jesus through imaginative prayer, reflection, and discernment—a critical, internal consideration of a situation or question and how it relates to one's personal or professional vocation.[^chunk:62507f47-31f8-545a-9a08-516a164b9faa] This philosophy has distinguished Jesuit education for nearly 500 years and has made their order's educational institutions some of the most admired in the world.[^chunk:62507f47-31f8-545a-9a08-516a164b9faa]

At its core, Ignatian pedagogy is about "humanization," or forming students—not just teaching them—to be whole persons who live their lives for a greater good.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] Education in the Ignatian tradition is about awakening to the dignity of each student and colleague and leading them to discover their truest selves.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] For those familiar with Catholic theology, this means that education is about guiding students to become the persons God created them to be.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

Ignatian pedagogy is about accompanying students on a journey of learning, reflection, and self-discovery, fostering a deeper understanding of what it means to be human, so that by engaging in tough questions—whether scientific, literary, artistic, theological, or cultural—they come to see themselves and the world in a new way.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

## The Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm

The Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm consists of several interconnected elements:

### Context

Context is about understanding who we are, where we come from, who others are, where others come from, and why things are the way they are, including the impact of social systems and scientific paradigms.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] This encompasses what the Jesuit Martyrs of El Salvador called "la realidad," or lived reality—a reality that includes experiences of injustice and oppression.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

### Experience

Experience is about holistic learning that honors intellectual and affective experience.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] Affect and emotion are often overlooked in education, but they are vital to the Ignatian paradigm.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] Ignatius himself frequently uses the Spanish verb "sentir" in his writings to refer to a "felt knowledge," a way of knowing that attends to every aspect of human experience.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

### Reflection

Reflection is first and foremost about finding meaning through critical engagement with "la realidad" and conceptual-intellectual understanding, but it also includes self-reflection.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] In the context of Jesuit pedagogy, students don't just learn information; they consider the implications of what we know and how we know it, reflect on the ethical implications of scientific research and market capitalism, and assess the failings of history out of hope for a better world.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] Along the way, they also reflect on their own lives, and ideally they don't just come to know things; they come to know themselves.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

### Action

Action puts context, experience, and reflection in service of the world.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] What students learn has the potential to do good for the world.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] The Jesuits of El Salvador spoke of "proyección social," or social projection—the university's activity should be projected out to the world, especially the local community, in a spirit of "Magis," or service of the greatest good.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

### Evaluation

Evaluation asks critical questions, orienting our work toward self-actualization, humanization, and the flourishing of all things.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

## Cura Personalis: Care for the Whole Person

Cura personalis is a foundational concept in Jesuit education that means "care of souls" or personal attention to individual flourishing. While it might be understood simply as being kind or interested in students' needs and experiences or attending to their mental health, cura personalis operates at a deeper level.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

Jesuit educators commit themselves to cura personalis because it actualizes the Ignatian vision of humanization, justice, transformation, and flourishing.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] In this fuller sense, cura personalis is an active, intentional dedication to the flourishing of others.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] It can mean shaking students out of indifference, disrupting racist, sexist, homophobic, or transphobic views, and challenging the prioritization of profit over the good of all.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] It can even mean having hard conversations when students struggle in our classes and providing guidance to help them discover paths aligned with their authentic passions and gifts.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

Ultimately, cura personalis is about forming whole persons committed to serving the world.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] It is a way of proceeding, a practice of conscious intentionality that also forms students to practice that intentionality throughout their lives—with eyes and hearts open to the needs of the world.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

## Implementation of Ignatian Pedagogy at Jesuit Institutions

### Loyola University Maryland

Loyola has a strong tradition of Jesuit pedagogy, which forms students to be leaders who are for and with others.[^chunk:62507f47-31f8-545a-9a08-516a164b9faa] The Jesuit approach encourages teachers to walk alongside students in the learning process, taking into account the student's whole self—mind, body, and spirit.[^chunk:62507f47-31f8-545a-9a08-516a164b9faa] As Jesuit educators, Loyola faculty encourage students to reflect on their learning, consider the contexts that make their learning meaningful, and put their learning into just and compassionate action.[^chunk:62507f47-31f8-545a-9a08-516a164b9faa]

### Santa Clara University

Santa Clara University articulates Ignatian pedagogy through its mission of forming leaders who will build a just, humane, and sustainable world.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] The institution emphasizes the "3 Cs"—competence, conscience, and compassion—as expressions of its Ignatian mission.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] SCU maintains a robust core curriculum as a fundamental, indispensable expression of the Ignatian vision of forming the whole person.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] This holistic vision explains why artists are asked to study science, chemists to study ethics, and all students to engage questions of racial and gender justice.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]
