## Overview

Saint Joseph's University's working document *Forming the Future* (Draft 0.1, 23 August 2026) includes an implementation roadmap for the Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm (IPP). The roadmap's first phase explicitly calls for standing up a cross-functional steward and editorial group to govern the IPP source-of-truth document and guide the University's shared understanding of the paradigm.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

---

## Phase 1: Establish Shared Meaning

Phase 1 of the implementation roadmap contains five tasks, of which the steward and editorial group is the first and organizing action:[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

1. Designate a **cross-functional steward and editorial group**.
2. Review the draft with faculty governance, Mission, academic leadership, student-facing divisions, assessment, accessibility, and students.
3. Resolve terminology and approve an authority policy for sources.
4. Produce a concise public primer derived from the full guide.
5. Identify where existing SJU practice already embodies the paradigm.

The sequencing is significant: the steward group is constituted first, and the subsequent consultation work flows through it.

## Who Sits on the Cross-Functional Steward Group?

The source document does not enumerate specific named roles or a fixed membership list for the steward group.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] What it does specify is the range of *constituencies* that must be engaged in the Phase 1 review process, which implies the cross-functional composition the group is meant to represent:

- **Faculty governance** bodies
- **Mission** (the institutional mission office or equivalent)
- **Academic leadership**
- **Student-facing divisions**
- **Assessment** professionals
- **Accessibility** professionals
- **Students**[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

The document also states more broadly that the guide should be revised through "a named steward, version history, consultation with faculty and student-facing units, and periodic review against new Society documents and evidence."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] The word *named* indicates the steward role is to be formally assigned to an identifiable person or office, though no individual is named in Draft 0.1. The document control section lists the owner/steward field as "To be designated."[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

## How Is the Editorial Group Selected and What Does It Do?

The document does not prescribe a selection mechanism (e.g., election, appointment, or application) for the editorial group.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] Its functional responsibilities, inferred from the Phase 1 task list and the document's self-description, include:

- **Overseeing the review** of the source-of-truth draft across the constituencies listed above.
- **Resolving terminology disputes** and approving an authority policy governing which sources SJU may cite as definitive.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]
- **Producing a public primer** — a concise derivative document for wider campus use.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]
- **Maintaining version history** and scheduling periodic review. The recommended review cycle is annual during development and at least every three years after adoption.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]
- **Reviewing the source-of-truth document** against new Society of Jesus documents and emerging evidence.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## How Do Faculty Governance Bodies Participate in Stewardship?

Faculty governance is one of the named constituencies for the Phase 1 review.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] The document does not grant faculty governance a formal veto or a specific structural role within the editorial group itself, but it does reflect a broader principle that IPP implementation must not bypass academic norms.

The document's governance guardrails state that SJU "should not certify individuals as 'Ignatian' based on a short course, require performative mission language in every syllabus, or use the IPP rubric for personnel judgment without validated purpose, due process, and contextual interpretation."[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] These guardrails implicitly require faculty governance participation to be meaningful rather than ceremonial, since personnel and curriculum decisions are traditional domains of shared governance.

The guide also insists that faculty formation "should not become a loyalty test" and that mission effectiveness grows through "invitation, intellectual seriousness, protected time, resources, peer leadership, and evidence that the institution aligns workloads and rewards with the educational work it asks people to do."[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] These commitments presuppose ongoing faculty governance engagement rather than a one-time consultation.

## What Role Do Students Play in the Steward Model?

Students are explicitly named in the Phase 1 review list alongside faculty governance, Mission, and academic leadership.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] The document does not elaborate on whether students serve as voting members of the editorial group, as consultants, or in some other capacity.

In Phase 2, students are included in evaluation: the roadmap calls for community partners and students to be included "in appropriate evaluation" of pilots.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] This suggests that student participation is conceived as ongoing across phases, not limited to the initial review.

The broader document also reflects the view that the institution teaches through its arrangements and that policies, incentives, and decision processes form people as surely as syllabi do.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Including students in governance processes is therefore consistent with the IPP's own logic.

## How Does the Steward Group Relate to Academic Leadership?

Academic leadership is one of the constituencies the steward and editorial group must consult in Phase 1.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] The document frames this as a review process — academic leaders are participants in shaping the approved framework, not merely recipients of it.

The implementation roadmap notes that "implementation should be invitational, developmental, and evidence-generating while institutional expectations remain clear where mission responsibilities are formally assigned."[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] This implies that academic leadership retains authority over formal mission obligations, while the steward group manages the shared educational vocabulary and design standards.

The IPP's institutional decision-process framework also applies to leadership itself: an IPP-informed institutional decision asks who is affected, what evidence is needed, what assumptions require discernment, who decides and with what accountability, and what review mechanisms govern revision.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] The steward group's cross-functional composition is designed to surface these questions before the paradigm is formally promulgated.

## Relationship to the ICAJE Framework and Source Authority

A core editorial function is enforcing the source hierarchy. The document establishes four tiers of authority, ranging from foundational Society of Jesus documents (Tier 1) through official ICAJE education frameworks such as the 1993 *Ignatian Pedagogy: A Practical Approach* (Tier 2) and authoritative interpretive addresses (Tier 3) down to institutional practice and scholarship (Tier 4).[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] One of the Phase 1 tasks is to approve an "authority policy for sources," meaning the editorial group is responsible for operationalizing this hierarchy so that local slogans or secondary sources are not presented as Society definitions.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

## Governance Guardrails

The document is explicit that the steward model must not become an instrument of compliance enforcement. Specific guardrails include:[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

- No individual certification as "Ignatian" based on a short course.
- No requirement for performative mission language in every syllabus.
- No use of the IPP rubric for personnel judgment without validated purpose, due process, and contextual interpretation.
- Implementation must remain "invitational, developmental, and evidence-generating."

The rubric accompanying the design framework similarly states that it "evaluates a design, not the virtue of an educator or student" and should support peer consultation rather than become "a compliance score detached from context."[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]
