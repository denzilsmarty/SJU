## Overview

Jesuit institutions in the United States maintain formal structures and relationships to facilitate collaboration among themselves, with provincial leadership of the Society of Jesus, and with the broader Catholic Church hierarchy. These governance arrangements balance institutional independence with apostolic alignment and mutual accountability.[^chunk:6d0aa5a0-fc38-56ab-af44-26088f9006ff]

## Collaboration Among Jesuit Colleges and Universities

The [Association of Jesuit Colleges and Universities (AJCU)](#role-of-ajcu) serves as the primary coordinating body for the twenty-eight U.S. Jesuit colleges and universities.[^chunk:6d0aa5a0-fc38-56ab-af44-26088f9006ff] These institutions collaborate through multiple mechanisms to strengthen their Catholic, Jesuit identity. Each college and university has established "responsibilities, structures, and programs for the hiring, orienting, and developing of faculty and staff according to our Catholic, Jesuit mission."[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021] Collaborative formation programs include "special retreats, seminars in Ignatian spirituality and Jesuit education, programs and colloquia which seek to enhance Catholic, Jesuit identity, development and scholarship opportunities, service and immersion experiences, special events that focus on our mission."[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]

Regional and national programs facilitate leadership development across institutions. These include "the AJCU Seminar on Higher Education Leadership and the Ignatian Colleagues Program," which support formation of lay leaders throughout the network.[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]

## Governance Structure and Board Authority

"The full responsibility for the policy, governance, and operation of each of our colleges and universities rests with its board of directors, trustees, or regents as established by articles of incorporation and/or by-laws. Each of these institutions is legally and functionally independent of the Society of Jesus and its provinces and institutions."[^chunk:03ee3a20-dcf0-5cae-84e8-e9cdc45777fd] Provincials do not exercise direct authority in external or internal governance.[^chunk:03ee3a20-dcf0-5cae-84e8-e9cdc45777fd]

The board holds fiduciary responsibility for the institution's defining character. "Most importantly, it holds in trust the Catholic, Jesuit character of the college or university and has a responsibility to understand, assure, provide resources to support, foster, and assess this character."[^chunk:03ee3a20-dcf0-5cae-84e8-e9cdc45777fd] Boards have historically "brought us better management, sounder financial status, higher academic quality and education of the whole person, and a fuller realization of being distinctively Catholic, Jesuit universities and colleges."[^chunk:03ee3a20-dcf0-5cae-84e8-e9cdc45777fd]

## Relationship Between Presidents, Rectors, and Provincials

Three key leadership roles structure institutional governance and religious oversight. Presidents serve as "Directors of the Apostolic Work," while rectors function as religious superiors of individual Jesuits and Jesuit communities.[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730] These roles require careful coordination while remaining conceptually distinct.

"Presidents, as 'Directors of the Apostolic Work', and rectors, as religious superiors of individual Jesuits and of the Jesuit community (or a delegate of the rector in the instance where the rector is not involved in the college or university), need above all to have regular, open, and trusting communication."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730] Such communication may address "the work of individual Jesuits, new opportunities for Jesuit involvement and assignment, ideas for how the community might help animate the Jesuit character, ways in which the college or university can know more about and recognize the community in its identity, how it can promote vocations to the Society of Jesus, and other ways of mutual assistance to one another."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

"This dialogue should be kept clearly separate from the relation of the rector as religious superior with the president as Jesuit and as a member of the community."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

### The Vice President for Mission and Ministry Role

Open communication is required to clarify roles between the rector and the Vice President for Mission and Ministry (or equivalent positions). "While not an official of the university, the rector leads the community in its corporate animation of the apostolate and its initiatives as a community within the college or university."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730] The rector "supports each Jesuit in his apostolic work, helps the community discern common apostolic initiatives, leads the community in its hospitality of university colleagues and students, helps it decide how to promote vocations, and at times represents and articulates the Society's apostolic priorities and commitments."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

### Provincial Involvement in Presidential Selection

Provinçials play a structured role in presidential appointments. "We encourage provincials to play an important role in the process by which the university, through its governing board, chooses the president of the college or university as this is articulated in the joint Jesuit Conference and Association of Jesuit Colleges and Universities document: The Role of the Society of Jesus in the Selection of a President for a U.S. Jesuit College or University."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

"The kind of regular communication called for above between the provincial and the university, and especially its board, is foundational for how the provincial can be involved when this process of the university and its board occurs."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730] The provincial's ongoing knowledge of the university enables him to "identify or to encourage potential Jesuits, to discuss with them their availability and suitability, to confer with their own provincial, and to give his clearance for their service as a Jesuit in this apostolic role within the province."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

"From the experience of the ongoing communication with the university and board he can advise the search committee of opportunities or challenges he sees the president—whether Jesuit or not—as Director of the Apostolic Work will face. The provincial should be informed of candidates who are not Jesuits and know of their qualifications to be the Director of the Apostolic Work in furthering its Catholic, Jesuit character."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

Upon appointment, "the new president, whether Jesuit or not, appointed by the board, should be 'missioned' to this Jesuit apostolic aspect of his responsibility by the provincial on behalf of the Society of Jesus."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730] "Experience shows that this 'missioning', especially when done publicly, is very meaningful not only to presidents who are Jesuits but very supportive of presidents who are not Jesuits."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

### Board and Provincial Partnership

"The more the board and the president can have an open and ongoing dialogue with the provincial and a spirit of working in welcomed partnership between them, the more the issues and opportunities of relationship with the Society of Jesus—for the good of both the mission of the Society and the college/university—can be addressed and mutually supported. Most of what is needed in regard to all of the issues of relationship can be resolved by improved communication."[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]

## Partnership with Society of Jesus Leadership

While institutions maintain independence, they require an "actual, lived relationship with the Society of Jesus, especially as represented by the provincials of the Society."[^chunk:03ee3a20-dcf0-5cae-84e8-e9cdc45777fd] This relationship is characterized by: "open and good communication, mutual listening and learning, and committed partnership or collaboration with the Society of Jesus, and in particular with provincials."[^chunk:03ee3a20-dcf0-5cae-84e8-e9cdc45777fd]

"The provincial should not be a stranger to the university but be welcomed and—to the extent possible—known within it. It is vital that each college and university fiduciary board, as well as each president, have open dialogue with the provincial regularly initiated by the university and as requested by the provincial."[^chunk:03ee3a20-dcf0-5cae-84e8-e9cdc45777fd]

This dialogue provides context for "support of the Jesuit character, understanding of initiatives and apostolic emphases of the Society of Jesus, the solidarity of the university with other Jesuit ministries, knowledge about the formation, availability, and assignment of Jesuits for the ministry of Jesuit higher education, the promotion of vocations to the Society by the university, programs for the formation of lay apostolic partners, and the role of the provincial in the process of the board's appointment of a president."[^chunk:03ee3a20-dcf0-5cae-84e8-e9cdc45777fd]

## Jesuit Community Roles and Integration

Jesuit communities on campuses serve distinct functions within institutional apostolates. "The role of the Jesuit community as a community first of all is corporately incarnating, supporting, and animating the Catholic, Jesuit character of our colleges and universities."[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021] This role is performed "in collaboration with and in mutual support of faculty, staff, students, and board members."[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]

"It makes a difference to how our colleges and universities are Jesuit that there is a Jesuit community at the heart of the university in addition to there being individual Jesuits. Because the Jesuit community—both for its own integrity and for the sake of the college/university—remains outside ordinary university structures and governance, it has the unique opportunity to make more personal and communal the lived meaning of the Jesuit character."[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]

Community members provide ministry that includes "Their knowledge of the Spiritual Exercises, leading retreats for faculty, staff, and students, giving spiritual direction, assisting in lay apostolic formation, bringing a Jesuit perspective to teaching, colleagueship, and committees, and significant ministerial and sacramental service."[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]

## Coordination with Catholic Church Hierarchy

### Relationships with Bishops

Jesuit colleges and universities occupy a unique position relative to episcopal authority. "The Jesuit colleges and universities in the United States are in both an advantageous and challenging position in regard to relationships with bishops."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730] These institutions "are often the ministry and dimension of the Society most in contact and communication with bishops and therefore have an opportunity of collaboration, mutual support, and personal relationship which is unique and is of special significance for the relationship of the whole Society of Jesus with the episcopacy."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

However, this relationship also involves inherent tensions. "We also, however, live a tension in our relationship with bishops because of our visibility, our representation of the Church in the minds of many, our attempts to live the reality of being in harmony with the Magisterium together with upholding academic freedom, the way in which we are called to be Catholic differently from other ministries, the academically credible work of our theologians which serves the wider purposes of the Church, and the ways in which colleges and universities necessarily make decisions in a different mode from how they are made by bishops."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

Pope Benedict XVI acknowledged this tension as described in his address to Jesuits attending the 35th General Congregation, noting it is "a particularly sensitive and demanding point for you and not a few of your confreres, especially those engaged in theological research, interreligious dialogue and dialogue with the contemporary culture."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

### Direct Communication with Bishops

"We have learned that there is no substitute for direct and frequent communication between presidents and bishops. This can help resolve or at least ameliorate some of the lived tension we experience."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730] Presidents note that "At times the anxieties which bishops experience and the pressures they are under because of complaints of others come from not being in regular contact with presidents and not being knowledgeable of relevant university matters. As presidents we can do better in this regard."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

Institutional leaders seek improved dialogue whereby bishops can "1) understand and appreciate better what we are as Catholic universities, 2) see and publicly make known that our colleges and universities play an important—though unique—role among ministries of the Church and that we are not extraneous to its ministry, and 3) communicate directly with us and we with them rather than through provincials or other third parties about matters of concern."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

Presidents also express a desire to learn from bishops: "We would welcome knowing from bishops what they most need from us and what are ways they see we can more fully serve the Church with our educational resources and within our proper identity as Catholic, Jesuit universities."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

## Role of AJCU

The Association of Jesuit Colleges and Universities serves as the collective voice and coordinating body for U.S. Jesuit higher education. The organization was formally established through collaborative effort of institutional presidents, representing the twenty-eight Jesuit colleges and universities.[^chunk:6d0aa5a0-fc38-56ab-af44-26088f9006ff]

AJCU supports institutional governance through multiple mechanisms:

1. **Leadership Formation**: The organization provides the AJCU Seminar on Higher Education Leadership and the Ignatian Colleagues Program.[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]

2. **Collaborative Development**: AJCU coordinates with the Jesuit Conference to establish policies and procedures, including "the joint Jesuit Conference and Association of Jesuit Colleges and Universities document: The Role of the Society of Jesus in the Selection of a President for a U.S. Jesuit College or University."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

3. **Mission Articulation**: The organization facilitates consensus-based statements on institutional character and apostolic priorities, such as the statement on "The Jesuit, Catholic Mission of U.S. Jesuit Colleges and Universities" developed through dialogue among institutional presidents and provincial leadership.[^chunk:6d0aa5a0-fc38-56ab-af44-26088f9006ff]

## Lay Leadership and Apostolic Partnership

As Jesuit presence on campuses diminishes, lay leadership becomes increasingly important to institutional governance. "In our colleges and universities, Jesuits comprise less than five percent of the total number of faculty, staff, and administration. These institutions depend overwhelmingly on 'lay apostolic partnership' or colleagueship."[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]

"Already the leadership of our institutions, whether as deans, directors of programs, or central administration, is overwhelmingly exercised by persons who are not Jesuits and it is foreseen that fewer of our presidents in the future will be Jesuits."[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]

Institutions welcome this evolution as an opportunity: "We believe that the integrity of our mission and its robust quality can be stronger in the future if we know how to welcome, to invest in, to support, and to work with apostolic partners in the way called for by GC34, GC35, and the U.S. provincials' document 'Responding to the Call of Christ'. Essentially, we see this evolution as an opportunity."[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]
