# Program-Level Coherence: Mapping IPP Across a Curriculum

This article addresses how the Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm (IPP) can be made visible and developmental across an entire program at Saint Joseph's University—not merely in isolated courses—and how its five dynamics of context, experience, reflection, action, and evaluation can be sequenced to deepen over time.

---

## Why Program-Level Coherence Matters

The IPP is described in its authoritative 1993 source as a recursive, scalable pattern: "A cycle may operate within a class meeting, a course, a clinical rotation, a program, an advising relationship, or an institutional decision."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] The paradigm is therefore not exhausted at the course level. When it functions only as a checklist within individual syllabi, it risks becoming what the source document explicitly warns against: a five-box template or a brand claim whose credibility depends on enacted practice rather than labeling.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

General Congregation 34's Decree 17 calls Jesuit universities to examine themselves and be accountable for whether their actual priorities serve the mission of rigorous teaching, research, interdisciplinary work, service, and the integration of faith and justice.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Program-level coherence is one concrete form that institutional accountability takes.

## What a Curriculum Map Reveals

A useful curriculum map for IPP coherence identifies where, across the arc of a program, learners repeatedly encounter significant experiences, practice reflection, act with increasing responsibility, and receive formative feedback.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dff-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0] The SJU source document specifies that the aim is **developmental coherence, not five-fold labeling of every course**.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dff-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

Specifically, a program-level map should identify:

- **Introduction, reinforcement, and advanced demonstration** of key capabilities across the sequence of courses;
- **Signature experiences** and which students can access them;
- **Structured reflection** designed before, during, and after each major experience;
- **Increasing authenticity and consequence** in action as students advance;
- **Points where core learning and professional learning are integrated**;
- **Advising and co-curricular contributions** to the overall formation arc; and
- **Program evidence**, including alumni, employer, licensure, community-partner, and student data.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dff-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

This mapping exercise makes visible what would otherwise remain implicit: whether the program as a whole creates opportunities for the full IPP cycle or whether certain dynamics—most commonly action and evaluation—are consistently underdeveloped. Research on U.S. Jesuit higher education has found that action tends to be less prominent than context, experience, and reflection in actual implementation.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

## Mapping Repeated Significant Experiences

The concept of a *significant experience* in the IPP sense is broader than a service placement or fieldwork rotation. A student's sustained encounter with a difficult theorem, a contested historical archive, a clinical case, a design critique, or a community partner can each qualify when the encounter is intellectually real, personally appropriated, and open to reflection.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

At the program level, mapping significant experiences means asking:

1. **Where do learners encounter the defining realities of the field?** This includes texts, data, patients, clients, works of art, communities, professional dilemmas, or empirical phenomena that are authentic enough to reveal the complexity of the subject.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
2. **Are these encounters distributed and scaffolded across the program?** A single capstone experience, however rich, cannot do the developmental work that repeated cycles can accomplish.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dff-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]
3. **Who can access each signature experience?** Equity of access to formative encounters is a direct expression of the program's commitment to cura personalis.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dff-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]
4. **Is preparation adequate?** Exposure without preparation can confirm prejudice rather than deepen understanding; learners need prior knowledge and skill before an experience can be genuinely appropriated.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## Deepening Reflection and Increasing Authenticity Over Time

Reflection is the pivotal dynamic connecting experience to responsible action.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] At the program level, the design question is not merely whether reflection occurs in each course but whether it **deepens** as students advance.

### A Developmental Sequence for Reflection

The source document describes reflection as moving through five kinds of question: attention to what happened, interpretation of patterns and causes, examination of interior response, analysis of ethical and social meaning, and discernment of a proportionate response.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Developmental coherence means that earlier courses emphasize the first levels—careful attention and interpretation—while advanced courses and capstone experiences foreground ethical and social analysis, perspective-taking, and discernment.

Programmatic reflection should:

- Move from **opinion-sharing** in introductory contexts toward **disciplined analysis of evidence, assumptions, power, and consequence** in advanced work;[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- Use diverse forms—analytic memos, structured debriefs, portfolio annotations, peer dialogue, case conferences, and positionality statements—so that personal appropriation does not depend on a single medium;[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- Protect learner freedom throughout: reflection should never become coercive or require disclosure of trauma, faith practice, or other sensitive personal information.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dff-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

### Increasing Authenticity in Action

Action in the IPP begins with interior choice—a revised judgment, a professional commitment, a new habit of attention—and may extend to outward conduct.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] A developmentally coherent program designs action to become progressively more authentic and consequential:

- **Early-stage action** may be a revised interpretation, a proposed design, or a reasoned argument;
- **Mid-program action** may involve applying knowledge in supervised practice, communicating findings to a real audience, or making a constrained professional decision;
- **Advanced action** may include clinical or field practice with meaningful scope, policy advocacy, community-engaged work under reciprocal terms, or a research contribution with professional stakes.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Action should remain proportionate to the learner's knowledge, role, and power at each stage.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] Programs should not promise community impact they cannot sustain, and they should plan for continuity, reciprocity, and closure in any community-engaged work.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## Integrating Core and Professional Learning

One of the eight institutional commitments proposed for SJU is **integration**: ensuring that core, professional, co-curricular, and relational learning reinforce rather than fragment one another.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dff-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0] The curriculum map is the primary instrument for making this integration visible.

General Congregation 34's Decree 17 provides the normative warrant: a Jesuit university is called to join rigorous intellectual work with human and moral formation, pastoral care, and the service of faith and justice—not as adjacent programs but as an integrated institutional purpose.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

### How the Map Creates Integration

A program-level curriculum map can identify **points of integration** where disciplinary or professional content and broader formative questions meet:

- In **health professions**, this may be the clinical encounter where scientific reasoning, patient dignity, social determinants of health, and professional duty converge in a single reflective moment;[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dff-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]
- In **business and organizational leadership**, it may be the moment a case analysis requires students to move from financial modeling to structural analysis of stakeholders, externalities, and the common good;[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dff-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]
- In **education and human development**, it may be the field placement where candidates must integrate learning theory, identity, institutional conditions, and responsibility to particular learners;[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dff-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]
- In **natural sciences and engineering**, it may be a design or research project where students must examine assumptions, dual-use risks, and affected communities alongside technical rigor.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dff-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

The IPP's design canvas can be applied at the **program level** as well as the course level, asking: What significant realities will learners encounter? Where and how will structured reflection occur across the sequence? What forms of action are expected at each stage, and how do they develop? What does evaluation across the program reveal about learning and about the design itself?[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dff-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

### The Role of Evaluation in Program Coherence

Evaluation at the program level has at least four objects: academic learning demonstrated across the sequence; quality of judgment in integrating evidence, values, and perspectives; action and its impacts; and educational design—including what the program should change.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Program-level evidence should draw on student work, course and program assessment, belonging and access data, qualitative accounts, partner feedback, faculty learning, alumni outcomes, and institutional indicators.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

Programs should use **calibrated claims**: what a particular IPP implementation demonstrably achieves in belonging, ethical reasoning, professional judgment, or community impact requires local evidence, not merely the IPP label.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] Evaluation should examine the design actually implemented, look for differential effects, and attend to unintended burdens on students or community partners.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

## The Relationship to SJU's Outcomes and Strategic Commitments

SJU's undergraduate learning outcomes in communication, critical thinking and inquiry, ethical reasoning and social justice, and Ignatian values and diverse perspectives can all be **designed and assessed through repeated IPP cycles**.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dff-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0] The IPP functions as an integrating architecture across these aims without replacing the General Education Program, Cornerstone Core, professional accreditation, learning outcomes, or unit strategies—it gives them a common process language.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dff-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

SJU's strategic commitments to expanded experiential learning, integrated academic and co-curricular opportunity, belonging and human connection, and formation in Ignatian spirituality and pedagogy all find operational expression in a coherent, mapped curriculum.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dff-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

## Practical Guidance for Programs

Programs beginning a coherence-mapping process can use the following questions, derived from the SJU IPP design canvas and curriculum design standard:[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dff-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0][^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

1. Where in the program do learners first encounter the defining realities of the field? Where do they encounter them again with greater depth and stakes?
2. Which courses primarily provide experience, which deepen reflection, and which demand consequential action? Are all five dynamics meaningfully present somewhere in the arc?
3. Where does structured reflection move beyond personal response toward disciplined analysis of evidence, power, and consequence?
4. How does the expected form and consequence of student action change from the first semester to the last?
5. Where are core questions—ethical, social, vocational, civic—integrated with professional or disciplinary content, rather than siloed in separate courses?
6. What program-level evidence is collected, and how does it feed back into design revision?
7. Who can access signature experiences, and what does the answer reveal about the program's commitment to equity?

These questions do not require uniform course formats or prescribed labels. They do require that programs be able to answer, with evidence, how the IPP's recursive dynamics operate across the full learning arc.
