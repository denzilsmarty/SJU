# Service of Faith and Promotion of Justice in Higher Education

## Overview

The 32nd General Congregation of the Society of Jesus articulated a transformative vision for Jesuit apostolic mission centered on the inseparable unity of faith and justice. This mission—formulated as "the service of faith, of which the promotion of justice is an absolute requirement"[^chunk:3b4035cf-fd23-5cdc-b151-2b768fd08cec]—has profound implications for how Jesuit institutions, particularly universities, understand their educational purpose and institutional identity.

## The Core Mission: Service of Faith and Promotion of Justice

### Definition and Theological Foundation

The promotion of justice is not presented as a separate apostolic activity but as constitutive to the very proclamation of the Gospel. As the General Congregation declared: "The mission of the Society of Jesus today is the service of faith, of which the promotion of justice is an absolute requirement. For reconciliation with God demands the reconciliation of people with one another."[^chunk:3b4035cf-fd23-5cdc-b151-2b768fd08cec]

This integration rests on a fundamental theological insight: "There is no genuine conversion to the love of God without conversion to the love of neighbor and, therefore, to the demands of justice."[^chunk:5d0b8518-d9b1-5803-a2e8-6fd8938a1873] The Gospel itself demands "a life freed from egoism and self-seeking, from all attempts to seek one's own advantage and from every form of exploitation of one's neighbor."[^chunk:3b4035cf-fd23-5cdc-b151-2b768fd08cec]

### Justice as Integral to Evangelization

The General Congregation emphasized that evangelization and justice promotion are indivisible dimensions of a single apostolic mission. "The promotion of justice is, therefore, an integral part of evangelization."[^chunk:f18a9a32-2d4d-5be0-b04c-e7aa8c43f2e1] This reflects a conviction that authentic preaching of Christ requires visible commitment to justice: "It will not be possible to bring Christ to people or to proclaim His Gospel effectively unless a firm decision is taken to devote ourselves to the promotion of justice."[^chunk:5d0b8518-d9b1-5803-a2e8-6fd8938a1873]

## Integration of Faith and Justice in Institutional Mission

### Structural and Personal Dimensions

Effective service of faith and justice cannot remain at the level of individual charity or spiritual consolation. The Congregation recognized that "the structures of society are among the principal formative influences in our world, shaping people's ideas and feelings, shaping their most intimate desires and aspirations."[^chunk:f18a9a32-2d4d-5be0-b04c-e7aa8c43f2e1] Therefore, "the struggle to transform these structures in the interest of the spiritual and material liberation of fellow human beings is intimately connected to the work of evangelization."[^chunk:f18a9a32-2d4d-5be0-b04c-e7aa8c43f2e1]

Yet structural change alone is insufficient. The Congregation insisted that "our efforts to promote justice and human freedom on the social and structural level, necessary though they are, are not sufficient of themselves. Injustice must be attacked at its roots which are in the human heart by transforming those attitudes and habits which beget injustice and foster the structures of oppression."[^chunk:f18a9a32-2d4d-5be0-b04c-e7aa8c43f2e1]

### Educational Institutions as Strategic Sites

The General Congregation specifically identified educational institutions as crucial for this mission. The document calls for "a review of our ministries and the deployment of our available manpower and resources" with special attention to "the role in the service of faith and the promotion of justice which can be played by our educational institutions, periodicals, parishes, retreat houses, and the other apostolic works for which we are responsible."[^chunk:846843ec-de33-5aeb-8db4-b66fe62d3986]

Universities, in this framework, are not merely places where students receive academic training. They are apostolic institutions called to form "young people and adults to live and labor for others and with others to build a more just world."[^chunk:846843ec-de33-5aeb-8db4-b66fe62d3986] Especially, institutions should "help form our Christian students in such a way that animated by a mature faith and personally devoted to Jesus Christ, they can find Him in others and having recognized Him there, they will serve Him in their neighbor."[^chunk:846843ec-de33-5aeb-8db4-b66fe62d3986]

## Accompaniment of the Poor and Solidarity

### A Dimension of All Apostolates

Solidarity with the poor is not optional or peripheral to Jesuit education. The Congregation declared: "For us, the promotion of justice is not one apostolic area among others, the 'social apostolate;' rather, it should be the concern of our whole life and a dimension of all our apostolic endeavors."[^chunk:f18a9a32-2d4d-5be0-b04c-e7aa8c43f2e1]

Furthermore, "solidarity with men and women who live a life of hardship and who are victims of oppression cannot be the choice of a few Jesuits only. It should be a characteristic of the life of all of us as individuals and a characteristic of our communities and institutions as well."[^chunk:32f197b0-5791-5bb2-ada0-bc0f8644f55b]

### Living Poverty in Solidarity

This solidarity demands more than intellectual commitment or programmatic assistance. The Congregation called for personal proximity to the poor: "It will therefore be necessary for a larger number of us to share more closely the lot of families who are of modest means, who make up the majority of every country, and who are often poor and oppressed."[^chunk:32f197b0-5791-5bb2-ada0-bc0f8644f55b]

This accompaniment is essential to authentic service: "If we have the patience and the humility and the courage to walk with the poor, we will learn from what they have to teach us what we can do to help them. Without this arduous journey, our efforts for the poor will have an effect just the opposite from what we intend, we will only hinder them from getting a hearing for their real wants and from acquiring the means of taking charge of their own destiny, personal and collective."[^chunk:32f197b0-5791-5bb2-ada0-bc0f8644f55b]

When Jesuits share this journey with the poor, something transformative occurs: "Through such humble service, we will have the opportunity to help them find, at the heart of their problems and their struggles, Jesus Christ living and acting through the power of the Spirit."[^chunk:32f197b0-5791-5bb2-ada0-bc0f8644f55b]

## Inculturation and Evangelization

### Adapting Faith to Cultural Contexts

The General Congregation recognized that effective evangelization requires attentiveness to cultural contexts. Communities must "be attentive to 'inculturation;' that is, we must take pains to adapt our preaching of the Gospel to the culture of the place so that men and women may receive Christ according to the distinctive character of each country, class or group and environment."[^chunk:f18a9a32-2d4d-5be0-b04c-e7aa8c43f2e1]

This is not mere cultural accommodation but a necessary dimension of authentic Christian witness. The Church seeks "to take on the identity of nations and peoples, to align herself with their aspirations, both toward a socio-economic development and an understanding of the Christian mystery, in accord with their own history and traditions."[^chunk:3b4035cf-fd23-5cdc-b151-2b768fd08cec]

### The Spiritual Exercises as Pedagogical Model

The Spiritual Exercises embody a pedagogy of inculturation and discernment essential to contemporary apostolic work. The Exercises feature "a deep respect for the exercitant as he is and for the culture, background and tradition that have gone into making him what he is."[^chunk:3b4035cf-fd23-5cdc-b151-2b768fd08cec] The pedagogy is fundamentally one of discernment, teaching individuals "to discover for himself where God is calling him, what God wants him to do, as he is, where he is, among his own people."[^chunk:3b4035cf-fd23-5cdc-b151-2b768fd08cec]

Beyond individual formation, the Exercises help develop Christians who can "stand back from the spurious absolutes of competing ideologies, and because of this detachment can play a constructive part in the reform of social and cultural structures."[^chunk:3b4035cf-fd23-5cdc-b151-2b768fd08cec]

## Apostolic Unity and Community Mission

### Individual Mission Within Communal Apostolate

While individual Jesuits exercise diverse ministries, their work must be understood within the overarching apostolic mission of the Society. The Congregation emphasized that "it is important that whether a Jesuit works in a team or whether he works alone, he must be, and must feel himself to be, sent."[^chunk:846843ec-de33-5aeb-8db4-b66fe62d3986] The superior bears responsibility for ensuring "that the apostolic work of each is properly integrated into the global mission of the Society."[^chunk:846843ec-de33-5aeb-8db4-b66fe62d3986]

This integration demands that "solidarity with the Society is primary. It ought to take precedence over loyalties to any other sort of institution, Jesuit or non-Jesuit. It ought to stamp any other commitment which is thereby transformed into 'mission.'"[^chunk:846843ec-de33-5aeb-8db4-b66fe62d3986]

### Communities as Apostolic Bodies

Jesuit communities themselves must be authentically apostolic. The "principle of unity" for these communities should be "the apostolic spirit," regardless of whether members "are engaged in different ministries."[^chunk:846843ec-de33-5aeb-8db4-b66fe62d3986]

Communities provide spiritual resources and apostolic discernment: "Each one of us should be able to find in his community—in shared prayer, in converse with his brethren, in the celebration of the Eucharist—the spiritual resources he needs for the apostolate. The community should also be able to provide him with a context favorable to apostolic discernment."[^chunk:846843ec-de33-5aeb-8db4-b66fe62d3986]

## Contemporary Challenges and Responses

### The World's Cry for Justice and Meaning

The Congregation identified a fundamental paradox of the modern world: "It is now within human power to make the world more just but we do not really want to."[^chunk:5d0b8518-d9b1-5803-a2e8-6fd8938a1873] Despite technological advancement, "new mastery over nature and man himself is used, often enough, to exploit individuals, groups and peoples rather than to distribute the resources of the planet more equitably."[^chunk:5d0b8518-d9b1-5803-a2e8-6fd8938a1873]

Yet simultaneously, "people today are somehow aware that their problems are not just social and technological, but personal and spiritual."[^chunk:5d0b8518-d9b1-5803-a2e8-6fd8938a1873] People hunger "not just for bread, but for the Word of God."[^chunk:5d0b8518-d9b1-5803-a2e8-6fd8938a1873] The Gospel can respond to this hunger if Jesuits "contemplate our world as Ignatius did his, that we may hear anew the call of Christ dying and rising in the anguish and aspirations of men and women."[^chunk:5d0b8518-d9b1-5803-a2e8-6fd8938a1873]

### Involvement in the World

The General Congregation recognized that Jesuits often remain "insulated from any real contact with unbelief and with the hard, everyday consequences of injustice and oppression."[^chunk:f18a9a32-2d4d-5be0-b04c-e7aa8c43f2e1] This isolation prevents them from "being able to hear the cry for the Gospel as it is addressed to us by the men and women of our time."

Therefore, "a deeper involvement with others in the world will be a decisive test of our faith, of our hope, and of our apostolic charity."[^chunk:f18a9a32-2d4d-5be0-b04c-e7aa8c43f2e1] This involvement requires rigorous study and analysis: "We cannot be excused from making the most rigorous possible political and social analysis of our situation. This will require the utilization of the various sciences, sacred and profane, and of the various disciplines, speculative and practical, and all of this demands intense and specialized studies."[^chunk:f18a9a32-2d4d-5be0-b04c-e7aa8c43f2e1]

### Cost and Commitment

The Congregation was realistic about the demands of this mission: "Any effort to promote justice will cost us something. Our cheerful readiness to pay the price will make our preaching of the Gospel more meaningful and its acceptance easier."[^chunk:f18a9a32-2d4d-5be0-b04c-e7aa8c43f2e1]

## Collaborative Apostolate and Ecumenism

### Working with Diverse Partners

The General Congregation insisted that the service of faith and justice cannot be accomplished in isolation. Jesuits are called to "closer collaboration with other members of the local churches, Christians of other denominations, believers of other religions, and all who hunger and thirst after justice."[^chunk:f18a9a32-2d4d-5be0-b04c-e7aa8c43f2e1]

This collaboration is particularly important in contemporary contexts: "Today it is essential for the preaching and acceptance of the Gospel that this spirit of ecumenism embrace the whole of mankind, taking into account the cultural differences and the traditional spiritual values and hopes of all groups and peoples."[^chunk:f18a9a32-2d4d-5be0-b04c-e7aa8c43f2e1]

## Institutional Renewal and Discernment

### Ongoing Evaluation of Apostolic Work

The Congregation recognized that commitment to faith and justice requires continuous institutional self-examination. Provinces should undertake "a program of reflection and a review of our apostolates to discover what action is appropriate in each particular context."[^chunk:846843ec-de33-5aeb-8db4-b66fe62d3986]

This process engages the Ignatian method of spiritual discernment: "The general method to be followed to produce this awareness and to engage in this discernment may be described as a constant interplay between experience, reflection, decision and action, in line with the Jesuit ideal of being 'contemplative in action.'"[^chunk:846843ec-de33-5aeb-8db4-b66fe62d3986]

The evaluation should address critical questions: "Where do we live? Where do we work? How? With whom? What really is our involvement with, dependence on, or commitment to ideologies and power centers? Is it only to the converted that we know how to preach Jesus Christ?"[^chunk:846843ec-de33-5aeb-8db4-b66fe62d3986]

## Synthesis: Faith and Justice as Inseparable Mission

The 32nd General Congregation's vision fundamentally reframes the relationship between faith and justice. They are not two separate apostolates that happen to coexist but constitute an organic unity. "To promote justice, to proclaim the faith and to lead others to a personal encounter with Christ are the three inseparable elements that make up the whole of our apostolate."[^chunk:32f197b0-5791-5bb2-ada0-bc0f8644f55b]

For Jesuit universities and educational institutions, this means that formation in faith cannot be divorced from education in justice. Students must be formed to recognize Christ present in the suffering and oppressed, and to respond with authentic commitment to building a more just world. Simultaneously, this commitment to justice must be animated by authentic Christian faith and the hope that Christ brings.

The mission demands that Jesuit institutions demonstrate through their own structures, policies, and practices a visible commitment to the poor and oppressed. It requires that they cultivate in their communities and students a spirituality rooted in the Spiritual Exercises—a spirituality that develops both profound personal encounter with God and rigorous apostolic engagement with the structural injustices that afflict humanity. Only through this integration of contemplation and action, faith and justice, can Jesuit higher education authentically serve the contemporary world and respond to the deepest aspirations of the men and women of our time.
