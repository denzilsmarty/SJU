# Ignatian Pedagogy and Mission Week

## Core Principles of Ignatian Pedagogy at Saint Joseph's University

Saint Joseph's University is guided by the principles of Jesuit education, a holistic approach to learning that integrates intellectual development with moral and spiritual growth.[^chunk:635ff496-128f-5afb-9a87-47e24e515de8] Rooted in the teachings of St. Ignatius of Loyola, Jesuit education seeks to form individuals committed to knowledge, faith, justice, and service.

The core principles that define Ignatian pedagogy at SJU include:

**Faith and Reflection**: Encouraging critical thinking in pursuit of truth.[^chunk:635ff496-128f-5afb-9a87-47e24e515de8]

**Service and Social Justice**: Working toward a just and equitable world.[^chunk:635ff496-128f-5afb-9a87-47e24e515de8]

**Cura Personalis**: Caring for each individual's intellectual, moral, and spiritual development.[^chunk:635ff496-128f-5afb-9a87-47e24e515de8] This principle reflects the personal love and care that educators witness for their students so they might attain the knowledge and freedom to achieve their full potential as young men and women created in God's image and likeness.[^chunk:9a224493-8715-5317-89bc-4400b1d2576a]

**Magis**: Striving for excellence in all that we do.[^chunk:635ff496-128f-5afb-9a87-47e24e515de8] The Magis calls students to become persons of the magis, asking always what more they can do for God's greater honor and glory.[^chunk:9a224493-8715-5317-89bc-4400b1d2576a]

The university's mission statement articulates this commitment: "As Philadelphia's Jesuit Catholic University, Saint Joseph's provides a rigorous, student-centered education rooted in the liberal arts. We prepare students for personal excellence, professional success and engaged citizenship. Striving to be an inclusive and diverse community that educates and cares for the whole person, we encourage and model lifelong commitment to thinking critically, making ethical decisions, pursuing social justice and finding God in all things."[^chunk:635ff496-128f-5afb-9a87-47e24e515de8]

## How Magis Guides Educational Practice

The concept of Magis—the striving for excellence in all endeavors—serves as a foundational guide in Ignatian educational practice at Saint Joseph's.[^chunk:635ff496-128f-5afb-9a87-47e24e515de8] Magis is not merely about achieving more, but about pursuing greater impact and deeper commitment to service.

In the Ignatian Educator's Prayer, the community of educators commits to forming students "into persons of intellectual competence, moral integrity and religious conviction whose actions are informed by conscience, infused with compassion and inspired by a commitment to a faith that seeks justice for all your people, particularly among the poor, the suffering and the neglected."[^chunk:9a224493-8715-5317-89bc-4400b1d2576a] This prayer explicitly calls educators to guide their students toward becoming "men and women of the magis, asking always what more they can do for your greater honor and glory."[^chunk:9a224493-8715-5317-89bc-4400b1d2576a]

Magis encourages educators to teach as Jesus did, so that students will grow to be "men and women in service with and for others" and become leading "discerners of your Spirit in the communities in which they live, work and worship."[^chunk:9a224493-8715-5317-89bc-4400b1d2576a]

## The Purpose of Mission Week

Mission Week occurs annually in February at Saint Joseph's University.[^chunk:9a224493-8715-5317-89bc-4400b1d2576a] During this week, faculty are encouraged to dedicate 5-15 minutes at the start of a class to discuss how the university's mission relates to their subject area.[^chunk:635ff496-128f-5afb-9a87-47e24e515de8] Outside of Mission Week, such discussions are welcome anytime.

Mission Week serves as a dedicated period for the university community to explicitly connect their academic disciplines and work to the broader Jesuit mission. Through guided classroom discussions, students are invited to reflect on essential questions such as: How does the subject we study contribute to the greater good? How can we apply the principles of social justice, ethical leadership, and lifelong learning to our field?[^chunk:635ff496-128f-5afb-9a87-47e24e515de8]

## Ignatian Educators and Resources

The university draws on the wisdom of prominent Jesuit educators to inform its approach. Notable resources include podcasts and presentations featuring Fr. Greg Boyle, S.J., founder and executive director of Homeboy Industries in Los Angeles;[^chunk:635ff496-128f-5afb-9a87-47e24e515de8] Fr. James Martin, S.J., editor at large of America magazine;[^chunk:635ff496-128f-5afb-9a87-47e24e515de8] and Fr. Michael Himes, S.J., Boston College theology professor,[^chunk:635ff496-128f-5afb-9a87-47e24e515de8] who share insights about living Ignatian values and finding God in all things.

The Ignatian Educator's Prayer frames the commitment of faculty and staff to this mission, asking for guidance to "continually grow as a community of companions in the ministry of teaching, working with and learning from one another to discern how best to accompany our students on their journeys."[^chunk:9a224493-8715-5317-89bc-4400b1d2576a]
