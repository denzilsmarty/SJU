## Global Networks Connecting Jesuit Universities for Social Justice

Jesuit universities worldwide are connected through collaborative networks designed to amplify their social justice commitments. These networks serve as critical infrastructure for sharing resources, coordinating research, and amplifying advocacy efforts across borders and continents.

### Regional Network Structures

Several regional associations organize Jesuit higher education institutions:

- **AUSJAL (Latin America)**: The Association of Jesuit Universities of Latin America has been structuring networks for over 20 years with significant progress.[^chunk:78db57c0-d9f6-5b27-af7d-6d4094c88d71] AUSJAL has developed homologous networks bringing together academicians from similar fields across different universities to exchange information and best practices.[^chunk:78db57c0-d9f6-5b27-af7d-6d4094c88d71] The network established an "Observatory of Poverty" for comparative study of poverty and its evolution across Latin America.[^chunk:78db57c0-d9f6-5b27-af7d-6d4094c88d71]

- **AJCU (United States), JHESA (South Asia), and UNIJES (Spain)**: These regional collaborative networks have been active for decades and now offer support for international collaboration.[^chunk:78db57c0-d9f6-5b27-af7d-6d4094c88d71]

### International Research Networks

Loyola University Chicago exemplifies international coordination efforts. In coordination with the Secretariat for Higher Education, it started a network of approximately forty researchers from Jesuit institutions around the world focusing on ecological concerns for use by universities and secondary schools.[^chunk:78db57c0-d9f6-5b27-af7d-6d4094c88d71] Loyola University Chicago is also sponsoring a three-year international research project on "Democracy, Culture and Catholicism" involving six Jesuit universities worldwide.[^chunk:78db57c0-d9f6-5b27-af7d-6d4094c88d71]

The Global Ignatian Advocacy Network (GIAN) operates networks addressing five major themes from an international perspective: migrations, ecology, the right to education, governance of natural and mineral resources, and peace and human rights.[^chunk:78db57c0-d9f6-5b27-af7d-6d4094c88d71] Although most network members come from the social apostolate, all networks include people from academic institutions.[^chunk:78db57c0-d9f6-5b27-af7d-6d4094c88d71]

Twenty-one mostly Jesuit institutions across the United States, Latin America, Africa, Europe, and India collaborated in publishing "30 Years of HIV-AIDS," offering an assessment of three decades of confronting AIDS and presenting prevention perspectives.[^chunk:78db57c0-d9f6-5b27-af7d-6d4094c88d71]

## The Kino Border Initiative: University Engagement with Vulnerable Populations

The Kino Border Initiative exemplifies how Jesuit universities engage directly with vulnerable migrant populations.[^chunk:78db57c0-d9f6-5b27-af7d-6d4094c88d71] Located on both sides of the Mexican-American border near Nogales, this institution serves migrants who are deported.[^chunk:78db57c0-d9f6-5b27-af7d-6d4094c88d71]

The initiative functions as a dual platform: it provides service opportunities for American university students wishing to serve the community while simultaneously offering researchers access to data on border realities.[^chunk:78db57c0-d9f6-5b27-af7d-6d4094c88d71] The knowledge gathered is used "creatively and effectively for advocacy."[^chunk:78db57c0-d9f6-5b27-af7d-6d4094c88d71] This model demonstrates integration of teaching, research, and social service in direct response to human suffering.

A similar project has begun on the border between Mexico and Guatemala, sponsored by the Mexican and Central American provinces.[^chunk:78db57c0-d9f6-5b27-af7d-6d4094c88d71]

## Addressing Global Inequalities and Migration

Jesuit universities play multiple roles in addressing global inequalities and the migration crisis:

### Advocacy and Policy Engagement

The **Ignatian Solidarity Network** in the United States engages individuals, especially students at Jesuit universities and high schools, in legislative advocacy for immigration reform, economic equality, and environmental justice.[^chunk:28130dc0-eb1b-5ed7-9a23-2969857c90b7] Students visit their legislators, participate in email campaigns, and hold prayerful demonstrations at the US Capitol Building.[^chunk:28130dc0-eb1b-5ed7-9a23-2969857c90b7]

### Institutional Commitments and Services

The university system of the Jesuits in Mexico signed an agreement with the social sector regarding provision of services for migrants and indigenous peoples.[^chunk:78db57c0-d9f6-5b27-af7d-6d4094c88d71] The universities offer training to personnel of social centers and develop specific research projects addressing these populations.[^chunk:78db57c0-d9f6-5b27-af7d-6d4094c88d71]

**Santa Clara University** in California offers The Global Social Benefit Fellowship, providing a comprehensive program of mentored, field-based study and action research for undergraduate juniors, combining a fully funded 6-7 week international summer field experience in the developing world with two quarters of academically rigorous research.[^chunk:66ce0db4-e85f-5bb1-8e33-0e07ccb45450]

### Educational Access and Inclusion

**Universidad Iberoamericana** in Mexico offers nearly complete scholarships for poor students who wish to attain university degrees in technical subjects.[^chunk:66ce0db4-e85f-5bb1-8e33-0e07ccb45450] This commitment reflects the principle that "the option for the poor and for justice is the litmus test by which we can judge the commitment that Jesuit secondary and higher education has to the Gospel."[^chunk:66ce0db4-e85f-5bb1-8e33-0e07ccb45450]

**Ateneo de Manila** in the Philippines, through its protocol on university public positions, demonstrates institutional commitment to social issues even when controversial. After a serious study of the environmental, social, and economic impact of mining in the Philippines, the Ateneo called for a moratorium on extractive activities, despite resulting criticism both within and outside the university.[^chunk:2905a776-7d6e-53aa-8427-18885f513772]

### Research and Analysis

The Indian Social Institute in New Delhi functioned as a center for many universities and colleges to work on the sociology of human displacement resulting from development.[^chunk:78db57c0-d9f6-5b27-af7d-6d4094c88d71] The Centre worked with Jesuits of Loyola School of Social Work in Kerala and other colleges.[^chunk:78db57c0-d9f6-5b27-af7d-6d4094c88d71]

## Challenges and Opportunities in Global Collaboration

Despite the Jesuit network's potential, Fr. General Adolfo Nicolás raised concerns at a 2010 conference on Jesuit higher education in Mexico about whether institutions were adequately utilizing the global network available to them.[^chunk:78db57c0-d9f6-5b27-af7d-6d4094c88d71] In his words: "until now, we have not fully made use of this 'extraordinary potential' for 'universal' service."[^chunk:78db57c0-d9f6-5b27-af7d-6d4094c88d71]

The global phenomena affecting education—environmental deterioration, transformation of ecosystems, migratory shifts, economic dynamics, consumerism, and exploitation of resources—require collaborative management across borders.[^chunk:28130dc0-eb1b-5ed7-9a23-2969857c90b7] While these phenomena affect all peoples, they do so in very different ways depending on social position.[^chunk:28130dc0-eb1b-5ed7-9a23-2969857c90b7]

Cross-border collaboration between universities and social centers is increasingly formalized through agreements that express firm institutional commitment rather than depending solely on individual goodwill.[^chunk:78db57c0-d9f6-5b27-af7d-6d4094c88d71]
