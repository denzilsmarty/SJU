# Applying the IPP to Generative AI Contexts

The integration of generative AI into educational practice presents both opportunities and challenges for Ignatian pedagogy. Rather than treating AI as a separate concern, the Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm (IPP) provides a coherent framework for understanding how to use these tools responsibly while maintaining the human relationships and accountabilities at the heart of Jesuit education.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## How Should Educators Integrate Generative AI into an IPP-Informed Course?

The IPP's five dynamics—context, experience, reflection, action, and evaluation—offer a comprehensive approach to thoughtful AI integration rather than uncritical adoption.

### Context: Understanding the System and Its Pressures

Before deploying AI tools, educators must establish context by identifying the system itself, its owner, data practices, model limitations, access differences, disciplinary norms, environmental and labor costs, and institutional policy. This contextual analysis should also examine what pressures lead students or employees to use generative AI in the first place.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

Context includes recognizing that generative AI changes the conditions of nearly every educational encounter by making fluent synthesis, drafting, translation, tutoring, simulation, and feedback widely available. However, these systems can also enable fabrication, surveillance, bias amplification, deskilling, epistemic passivity, and the counterfeit appearance of understanding or relationship.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

### Experience: Preserving Human Encounters

In an AI-shaped context, educators must be intentional about which experiences remain fundamentally human. Rather than replacing all interactions with AI-assisted alternatives, the IPP preserves experiences whose formative value depends on human presence or accountable authorship.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

Learners can meaningfully engage AI by inspecting outputs, comparing models, verifying citations, observing failure, documenting prompts and revisions, and encountering the people or realities that AI representations can obscure.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] This transforms AI from a shortcut into a subject of study and a tool for learning.

### Experience Design Standards

Strong educational experience in the IPP framework is aligned to significant learning purposes, authentic enough to reveal complexity, accessible and safe for participation, prepared by necessary knowledge and skill, structured so learners must notice actively, capable of engaging appropriate affect without exploitation, and followed by reflection, response, and feedback.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

When AI tools are present, educators must ask: Does this technology support these conditions, or does it undermine them? Exposure to an AI output alone is not experience in the IPP sense. Unprepared encounter can confirm prejudice rather than deepen understanding.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## What Does Reflection Look Like When Students Are Working With AI Outputs?

Reflection is the hinge of the IPP, and it becomes especially important when generative systems are involved.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Rather than accepting AI outputs passively, students should examine what the system made visible or invisible, whose language and assumptions it reproduced, where fluency masqueraded as truth, what cognitive work was delegated, and how use affected confidence, agency, relationship, and responsibility.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

### A Disciplined Approach to Reflective Inquiry

Reflection in IPP contexts moves through five kinds of questions:[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

1. **Attention:** What happened? What do the evidence and the perspectives actually show?
2. **Interpretation:** What patterns, causes, assumptions, or concepts explain it?
3. **Interior response:** What draws, troubles, resists, or energizes me? What may that response reveal or conceal?
4. **Ethical and social meaning:** Who benefits, who bears risk, whose voice is absent, and what consequences follow?
5. **Discernment:** Which possible response is more truthful, responsible, feasible, and conducive to human flourishing and the common good?

When applied to AI-generated work, these questions help students move beyond surface-level acceptance. For example, when examining an AI-drafted argument, students might notice how the system smoothed over genuine tensions in the evidence, whose perspectives were included or excluded from training data, or how the fluent writing masked conceptual confusion.

### Safeguarding Against Performative Reflection

Reflection can become distorted when it asks only "How did you feel?" or when instructors predetermine the morally acceptable insight. In AI contexts, this risk is heightened: students may learn to perform criticality of AI tools while avoiding genuine engagement.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Faculty should provide options for private or impersonal reflection, explain how reflective work will be evaluated, and grade the quality of attention and reasoning—not the student's emotional response or conformity to the instructor's preferred stance on AI.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## How Does the IPP Address Bias and Transparency in AI Tools?

The IPP does not explicitly use the language of algorithmic bias or model transparency, but its core commitments address these concerns directly through attention to context, power, and consequence.

### Context and Power Analysis

The IPP requires that context include not only learner characteristics but also the histories, power relations, disciplinary norms, and material conditions shaping the work.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] For generative AI, this means understanding whose data trained the model, what labor produced it, whose interests shaped its design, and how its errors are distributed across different user populations.

Context can become stereotyping if educators infer beliefs or capacities from group identity without evidence; the same principle applies to accepting AI classifications of people or problems without critical examination.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

### Reflection on Assumptions and Bias Reproduction

The IPP's reflective dynamic explicitly calls for examination of assumptions, causes, perspectives, and the interests embedded in what one encounters.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Applied to AI, this means students should examine whose language and assumptions the system reproduced, where it treated patterns in biased training data as universal truths, and how its outputs might reinforce or challenge existing inequities.

### Action and Responsible Use

The IPP insists that insight without response is incomplete. Students may revise, verify, reject, disclose, cite, constrain, or responsibly deploy an AI-assisted product.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] This includes refusing to use an AI system if its known biases make it unsuitable for a particular purpose, or escalating concerns about bias to appropriate institutional decision-makers.

### Institutional Context and Policy

The IPP recognizes that the institution itself teaches through policies, incentives, technologies, spaces, workloads, partnerships, and decision processes. An institution that procures AI systems without transparency about their limitations or that pressures students to use them despite known risks creates a context that undermines responsible judgment.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## What Counts as Responsible Human Action When AI Is Involved in the Learning Process?

In the IPP framework, action is not synonymous with visible activity or activism. It begins in interior appropriation—a judgment, choice, priority, value, or commitment that follows reflection—and may or may not become outward conduct.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

### Forms of Responsible Action With AI

Students may responsibly act by:

- **Verifying and correcting:** Checking AI-generated citations, catching hallucinations, and revising outputs to be accurate
- **Disclosing and attributing:** Clearly stating which parts of work used AI assistance and how, allowing readers to judge credibility
- **Constraining use:** Deciding not to use AI for tasks where human judgment or accountable authorship is essential
- **Escalating concerns:** Reporting bias, unfairness, or misuse to appropriate authorities rather than remaining silent
- **Designing responsibly:** When in roles of authority (faculty, administrators, technology leaders), redesigning systems and policies to protect learning and justice
- **Non-use as reasoned action:** Choosing not to use a tool when its limitations or ethical implications make that the more responsible choice[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

### Action Must Be Proportionate

The IPP emphasizes that action should be related to the learner's role, knowledge, power, and responsibilities.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] A student cannot be expected to fix systemic bias in a generative AI model, but they can be expected to recognize it, name it, and refuse complicity. A faculty member has greater responsibility to understand model limitations and to design assessments that do not accidentally reward students for using AI in ways that harm learning. An administrator or board member shares responsibility for procurement, policy, and accountability decisions.

### The Relational Boundary

A crucial distinction: AI can assist relationships; it must not counterfeit them. Systems can help educators identify patterns, prepare options, translate materials, and offer low-stakes practice. They cannot bear care, mutual obligation, fiduciary responsibility, or moral accountability.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

When students interact with AI tutoring systems or decision-support tools, they should know they are interacting with a machine, how decisions are escalated to a person, and which matters will never be delegated without meaningful human review.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] This transparency is itself a form of responsible action by educators and institution leaders.

### The Interior Dimension of Action

Responsible action in AI contexts includes interior choices that may not produce immediate outward results. A student who resolves to think more critically about AI outputs, to invest time in understanding rather than accepting fluency, or to develop greater agency in the face of automation has taken genuine action in the IPP sense.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## Assessment in an AI-Shaped Context

### Resilient Assessment Design

AI-resilient assessment is not synonymous with surveillance or return to high-stakes exams. IPP-informed design favors significant experience, staged work, oral explanation, process evidence, local and personal application without compulsory disclosure, collaborative critique, and revision. The goal is to make thinking and judgment visible and to preserve productive intellectual friction.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

### Evaluation and Feedback

Evaluation should assess both the final work and the quality of the human process: verification, attribution, judgment, revision, privacy, and consequence. Faculty should evaluate whether the technology improved access and learning or displaced essential practice.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

## The Broader Institutional Question

The proper question for an AI-shaped Jesuit university is not simply whether generative AI is allowed. It is what forms of human judgment, authorship, relationship, and responsibility the educational design is meant to cultivate.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

In an AI-shaped university, the IPP becomes more—not less—important. Generative systems can assist research, feedback, accessibility, and rehearsal. They cannot assume responsibility, enter a mutual relationship, discern a vocation, or substitute for the human encounter at the heart of cura personalis.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
