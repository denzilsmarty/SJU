## Overview

This article explains the authority framework that Saint Joseph's University (SJU) uses to interpret the Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm (IPP), identifies the minimum primary-source corpus required for IPP work, and provides guidance on resolving conflicts between foundational Society of Jesus documents and local institutional practice.

---

## 1. The Four-Tier Source Hierarchy

Not every text about Ignatian pedagogy carries the same authority. The SJU draft source-of-truth document establishes a four-tier hierarchy to govern how claims about the tradition are made and adjudicated.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

| Tier | Kind of Source | Examples | Proper Use |
|------|---------------|----------|------------|
| **1** | Foundational and constitutional sources of the Society | *Spiritual Exercises*; *Constitutions*, Part IV; decrees of General Congregations | Establish deep grammar, mission, and authoritative commitments |
| **2** | Official education frameworks promulgated or endorsed by the Society | *Characteristics* (1986); *Ignatian Pedagogy* (1993); *Human Excellence* (2015); *A Living Tradition* (2019) | Define the modern global framework and its development |
| **3** | Authoritative interpretive addresses and sector frameworks | Arrupe, Kolvenbach, Nicolás, and Sosa addresses; IAJU strategic agenda; AJCU resources | Interpret the charism for particular periods and for higher education |
| **4** | Institutional practice and scholarship | SJU mission and curricula; peer-reviewed studies; faculty guides; case reports | Adapt, test, critique, and operationalize the tradition |

The hierarchy does not render Tier 4 unimportant—institutions and scholars are where the paradigm is tested against real learners and conditions. What the hierarchy *prevents* is a local slogan, consultancy model, or secondary source being presented as though it were the Society's own definition.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

### Why the tiers are ranked as they are

The ranking reflects the locus of promulgating authority within the Society of Jesus. Tier 1 texts—the *Spiritual Exercises*, the *Constitutions*, and General Congregation decrees—issue from Ignatius of Loyola or the highest governing body of the Society and establish the anthropological and missional grammar that all subsequent frameworks interpret.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Tier 2 documents are formally promulgated by the International Commission on the Apostolate of Jesuit Education (ICAJE) under Superior General authority; they translate Tier 1 commitments into actionable educational frameworks.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Tier 3 addresses by Superior Generals Pedro Arrupe, Peter-Hans Kolvenbach, Adolfo Nicolás, and Arturo Sosa interpret the charism for specific historical periods and institutional types, particularly higher education.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Tier 4 encompasses local adaptations and empirical scholarship, which are essential for operationalizing the tradition but do not possess Society-level promulgating authority.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## 2. The Primary-Source Corpus

For IPP work at SJU, the following minimum primary-source corpus is required.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

### Tier 1: Foundational and Constitutional Sources

- **Ignatius of Loyola, *The Spiritual Exercises*** (1548), especially annotations 2, 15, and 22; the Principle and Foundation; the rules for discernment; and the Contemplation to Attain Love. The *Exercises* privilege appropriated understanding over accumulation, protect learner freedom, and ground the relational and anthropological grammar of the IPP.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **The *Constitutions of the Society of Jesus*, Part IV**, especially the chapters on colleges and universities. These chapters established education's durable institutional location within the Society's mission and repeatedly called for adaptation to regions, times, places, and persons.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **The 1599 *Ratio Studiorum***, read *historically* rather than as a contemporary code. It coordinates curriculum, roles, assessment, and faculty responsibilities and models a Jesuit pattern of consultation, experimentation, and codification—but it is far more prescriptive than the IPP and reflects early modern European assumptions.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **General Congregation 32, Decree 4**, "Our Mission Today: The Service of Faith and the Promotion of Justice" (1975). This decree established that the promotion of justice is an *absolute requirement* of contemporary Jesuit mission—not an optional activity adjacent to academic work.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **General Congregation 34, Decree 17**, "Jesuits and University Life" (1995). The key congregational text for higher education, covering teaching, research, interdisciplinary work, pastoral care, and institutional self-evaluation.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

### Tier 2: Official Education Frameworks

- **ICAJE, *The Characteristics of Jesuit Education*** (1986). The vision document to which the IPP is the practical companion. It articulates 28 characteristics grouped into nine sections and locates academic excellence within "human excellence."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **ICAJE, *Ignatian Pedagogy: A Practical Approach*** (1993). The controlling modern source for the paradigm, promulgated under Superior General Peter-Hans Kolvenbach. It deliberately avoids prescribing a universal curriculum and invites educators to adopt sound methods that support integral formation. Cite by numbered paragraph, since pagination varies across editions.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **ICAJE, *Human Excellence*** (2015). Defines the Four Cs—conscience, competence, compassion, and commitment—as desired qualities of Jesuit-educated persons.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]
- **ICAJE, *Jesuit Schools: A Living Tradition in the 21st Century*** (2019). Complements, rather than replaces, the 1986 *Characteristics* and the 1993 IPP; offers ten contemporary global identifiers. Written principally for schools; requires disciplined translation for higher education.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]
- **Society of Jesus, Universal Apostolic Preferences, 2019–2029**. Four global preferences: discernment and the *Exercises*; walking with the excluded; journeying with youth; care for our common home. They sharpen the contexts, experiences, and actions to which Jesuit education must attend but do not alter the five dynamics.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

### Tier 3: Authoritative Interpretive Addresses

- **Pedro Arrupe, S.J., "Men for Others"** (1973). A landmark critique of education for self-advancement and a call to learning ordered to justice and solidarity.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **Pedro Arrupe, S.J., "Our Secondary Schools: Today and Tomorrow"** (1980). Develops human formation, Jesuit identity, lay collaboration, and the danger of technical excellence without humanity.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **Peter-Hans Kolvenbach, S.J., the Santa Clara address** (2000). Argues that the measure of a Jesuit university is *who students become* and that educated solidarity normally requires contact with the suffering of others joined to intellectual inquiry and moral reflection.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **Peter-Hans Kolvenbach, S.J., "The Jesuit University in the Light of the Ignatian Charism"** (2001). Recovers the fourfold account of university purpose associated with Diego de Ledesma: *utilitas* (practical usefulness), *iustitia* (justice and the common good), *humanitas* (full human development), and *fides* (faith and ultimate meaning).[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **Adolfo Nicolás, S.J., "Depth, Universality, and Learned Ministry"** (2010). Names the "globalization of superficiality" as a threat to depth of thought and imagination.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **Arturo Sosa, S.J., "The University as a Source of Reconciled Life"** (2018). Locates the Jesuit university within a mission of reconciliation—with God, with one another, and with creation—and identifies the digital ecosystem as a new cultural paradigm.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

## 3. Resolving Conflicts Between Foundational Society Documents and Local Institutional Practice

The four-tier hierarchy provides the operative rule: **Tier 1 and Tier 2 sources are controlling for claims about the tradition**; Tier 4 institutional documents, slogans, and local guides are subordinate to them.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

The SJU draft document states this explicitly: "The primary documents of the Society remain controlling for claims about the tradition."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Consequently:

1. **A local slogan or adaptation cannot redefine Society terminology.** For example, SJU institutional variants of the Four Cs should not be confused with the globally promulgated ICAJE formulation of conscience, competence, compassion, and commitment.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
2. **Tier 4 practice is the testing ground, not the authority.** Institutional and scholarly work adapts, tests, critiques, and operationalizes the tradition—it does not supersede it.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
3. **Higher-education use of ICAJE documents requires disciplined translation.** Many official ICAJE texts were written principally for the schools apostolate; supplementing them with General Congregation 34's decree on university life and the higher-education addresses of Kolvenbach, Nicolás, and Sosa (Tier 3) is required rather than optional.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
4. **The interpretive document itself is not a promulgated policy.** The SJU draft source-of-truth document is explicitly described as "an interpretive working document, not a promulgated policy of Saint Joseph's University or the Society of Jesus." Where it interprets the tradition for SJU, it says so.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
5. **Claims must be calibrated by tier.** Practitioners should distinguish what is well supported by primary sources (the IPP's meaning and purposes), what is supported by accumulated practice and related research (the general value of structured reflection and authentic action), what is promising but requiring local evidence (claims about specific implementation outcomes at SJU), and what is not established (that labeling a course "IPP" causes formation).[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

In practice, when a local policy or institutional habit conflicts with a Tier 1 or Tier 2 source, the practitioner should name the tension explicitly, cite both sources, and allow the higher-tier document to govern the authoritative claim—while the Tier 4 document may legitimately govern local operational decisions within the space the higher-tier source permits.

## Citation Conventions

The 1993 IPP is cited by numbered paragraph (e.g., "IPP, nn. 42–46") because pagination varies across editions. The *Spiritual Exercises* are cited as "SE" followed by exercise or annotation number. The *Constitutions* are cited as "Const." followed by document numbering.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
