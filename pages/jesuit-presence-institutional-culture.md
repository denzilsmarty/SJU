## Overview

Jesuit presence and institutional leadership are foundational to maintaining the Catholic and Jesuit character of universities affiliated with the Society of Jesus. This presence operates at multiple levels—through active Jesuit community members, through formation programs for lay leaders, and through intentional hiring practices that prioritize mission alignment. The challenge of sustaining strong Jesuit presence while developing lay leadership represents a central tension in contemporary Jesuit higher education.

## Jesuit Community Role in Institutional Leadership

Jesuit communities contribute significantly to institutional leadership and culture through both formal and informal channels. Jesuit faculty, administrators, campus leaders, and campus ministers play public roles in university life by modeling the Ignatian tradition and providing visible witness to the institution's Catholic and Jesuit identity.[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9]

Universities are called to maintain a strong cohort of capable Jesuits who can contribute to the life of the institution meaningfully. This commitment extends to maintaining vibrant Jesuit communities on campus and ensuring open lines of communication between the Jesuit provincial superior, the university leadership, and the board of trustees.[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9] Collaborative relationships at local, regional, national, and international levels—particularly through the Association of Jesuit Colleges and Universities (AJCU) and partnerships with other Jesuit institutions worldwide—strengthen the institutional network and shared commitment to mission.[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9]

## Declining Jesuit Presence and Lay Leadership Formation

A significant tension confronts Jesuit universities: while institutions benefit from the corporate investment of the Society of Jesus and wish to maintain strong Jesuit presence, the number of Jesuits continues to decline on campuses.[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9] This reality demands intentional formation of educated lay leaders capable of assuming leadership roles that preserve institutional identity and mission.

The challenge is multifaceted. Universities have inadequately trained lay persons in the spiritual tradition of the Society of Jesus, and the Spiritual Exercises—a central resource for maintaining the spirit and character of Jesuit universities—require both better promotion and targeted hiring of persons interested in and capable of engaging in this practice.[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9]

The Society of Jesus and university leadership must engage in substantive, intentional formation of lay leadership. Additionally, a prophetic call is needed from the Society itself for lay leaders to recognize and embrace the importance of their universities' Catholic and Jesuit character in an unapologetic and inspiring manner.[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9]

## Programs Supporting Leadership Development Aligned with Ignatian Values

### Formation for Mission and Leadership

Universities address the challenge of lay formation through deliberate programming. Institutions offer programs on Jesuit history, mission, and pedagogy to build knowledge among faculty, staff, and administrators.[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9] Robust programs in the Spiritual Exercises in Everyday Life (18th and 19th Annotations) serve faculty, staff, and administrators, cultivating a core contingent who develop deep familiarity with Ignatian spirituality and become carriers of this tradition.[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9]

Universities also create opportunities for professional development and advancement for all employees, recognizing that formation for mission extends throughout the institutional community.[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9]

### Mission-Centered Hiring Practices

Institutions approach hiring and recruitment with intentionality toward preserving and augmenting Jesuit and Catholic mission across the institution. Mission-specific language in job postings signals the Catholic and Jesuit mission and identity to prospective candidates.[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9] Universities seek to recruit faculty and staff willing and able to augment and support institutional mission and identity, with search committees and department chairs engaging prospective hires in substantive conversations regarding the institution's Jesuit and Catholic mission.[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9]

Candidates are offered materials clearly stating the institution's guiding principles and philosophy. Deans, department chairs, managers, and directors share commitment to hiring for mission, making this priority visible to their faculties and supporting these interests as essential to institutional well-being.[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9]

### Mission Priority Examen

The Mission Priority Examen (MPE) represents a systematic approach to institutional leadership development aligned with Jesuit values. Initiated by the Superior General of the Society of Jesus and coordinated by a joint committee of Provincials and University Presidents within the AJCU, this process enables institutions to reaffirm their Jesuit Catholic identity in ways that honor community voices and experiences.[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5]

The MPE invites institutions to engage in honest and inclusive discernment, naming strengths and successes in living out the mission alongside struggles, failings, and future goals.[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5] The Characteristics of Jesuit Higher Education document serves as a reference and guide for this comprehensive process.[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5]

For example, the University of San Francisco's 2022 Mission Priority Examen affirmed priorities to build capacity for substantive conversations about Jesuit Catholic identity, develop transparent plans to strengthen Jesuit mission with diversity and equity commitments, strengthen sustainability engagement, and cultivate collaborative structures modeling *cura personalis* (care of the individual person) and Ignatian discernment.[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5]

## Vocational Discernment and Jesuit Identity

Universities foster vocational discernment and Jesuit identity through multifaceted approaches. Institutions create cultures in which students are supported in thinking through important decisions about themselves as people and the career paths and lives of service they will pursue after graduation.[^chunk:cb2464e4-28b9-5530-b7aa-f402d6bd4953] Specific programs provide students with tools and space to consider seriously the possibility of serving the Church as priests, sisters, brothers, and lay pastoral ministers.[^chunk:cb2464e4-28b9-5530-b7aa-f402d6bd4953]

Universities encourage vocational discernment for all students while ensuring programs specifically promote vocations to the Society of Jesus.[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9] Campus events and liturgical celebrations—including the Mass of the Holy Spirit, religious observances, and civic commemorations—reflect institutional mission and support spiritual formation.[^chunk:cb2464e4-28b9-5530-b7aa-f402d6bd4953] By drawing upon the cycle of the Church calendar, universities create academic and social events around feast days and significant dates, promoting awareness of Catholic time-keeping and its intersection with university life.[^chunk:cb2464e4-28b9-5530-b7aa-f402d6bd4953]

## Challenges in Maintaining Institutional Identity Amid Diversity

Jesuit universities face an explicit challenge in fostering unity in mission amid diversity in background and gifts. While institutions rightly value welcoming people of diverse religious traditions and no faith tradition, this commitment to diversity can challenge the creation and maintenance of a strong sense of Jesuit and Catholic community and identity.[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9]

Traditions—whether of faith or intellect—live in people. Universities increasingly find it difficult to maintain that Jesuit and Catholic university life, in part, depends upon hiring significant numbers of gifted, Catholic faculty and administrators conversant in and believing members of the tradition.[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9] In a cultural moment where diversity and multiculturalism have become preeminent values and where organized religion is sometimes dismissed as intellectually unsophisticated, institutions struggle to communicate the importance of maintaining vibrant Catholicity.[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9]
