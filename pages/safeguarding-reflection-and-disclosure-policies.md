## Overview

The Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm (IPP), as practiced at Saint Joseph's University (SJU), places **reflection** at the heart of learning. Because reflection invites learners to examine evidence, feeling, assumptions, and values, it can touch on sensitive personal territory. SJU's working draft framework establishes clear safeguarding policies to protect students, define the boundary between academic reflection and therapeutic practice, and clarify faculty obligations when sensitive information arises. This article synthesises those policies for educators, students, and administrators.

---

## What the IPP Means by Reflection

In the IPP, reflection is defined as "the disciplined process by which learners grasp the significance of experience." It draws on memory, understanding, imagination, and feeling; examines causes, assumptions, motives, implications, alternatives, and consequences; and seeks greater freedom for choice.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Reflection is explicitly described as **epistemic, affective, ethical, social, and—when appropriate—spiritual**; it asks both "What is true?" and "What is happening in me and among us as I encounter it?"[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Critically, the source document clarifies what Ignatian reflection is **not**: it is not synonymous with journaling, introspection, or stating an opinion.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] It can be solitary or communal, written or oral, analytic or creative.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
