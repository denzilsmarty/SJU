## Overview

Justice-oriented research represents a fundamental reorientation of university scholarship toward serving the poor and addressing structural inequality. As articulated by Ignacio Ellacuría, SJ, and developed through Jesuit educational mission, this approach transforms how universities generate knowledge, collaborate with communities, and contribute to social transformation.

## Science for Those Who Have No Science

Ignacio Ellacuría, SJ articulated a distinctive vision for university research: "The university should incarnate itself among the poor so as to become science for those who have no science, an informed voice of those who have no voice, and intellectual backing for those who in their lived reality know truth and right but lack academic arguments that could justify and legitimize their truth and right."[^chunk:2097a7d6-37db-5fbd-aff2-b1c756502acb]

This concept challenges the notion that research is neutral or objective. Fr. Kolvenbach emphasized that "it is not truly neutral because it always carries with it the bias of the values and particular conceptual structure of human beings."[^chunk:2097a7d6-37db-5fbd-aff2-b1c756502acb] Rather than serving existing power structures, justice-oriented research deliberately positions itself "from the perspective of the poor for the sake of bettering their lives, for it is in their suffering that the inhumanity of unjust structures becomes clearly manifest."[^chunk:2097a7d6-37db-5fbd-aff2-b1c756502acb]

Ellacuría went further, arguing that "the proper theoretical plane for focusing on the more serious social problems is normally that of the popular majority—as regards both correct interpretations and practical solutions."[^chunk:2097a7d6-37db-5fbd-aff2-b1c756502acb]

## University Research Addressing Social Inequality

### Research Perspective and Methodology

Research conducted from a justice orientation fundamentally transforms how scholars approach knowledge creation. All research begins with certain initial interests and chooses pertinent questions, but justice-oriented research explicitly asks "for whom and for what" research is being done.[^chunk:2097a7d6-37db-5fbd-aff2-b1c756502acb] This matters because "reality is not seen the same way when viewed from below as when viewed from above. The researcher does not choose the same questions or analyze them the same way, nor do the same consequences become relevant."[^chunk:2097a7d6-37db-5fbd-aff2-b1c756502acb]

### Interdisciplinary Approaches

Addressing complex social problems requires breaking down disciplinary silos. Fr. Kolvenbach noted that "in recent centuries knowledge has progressed by means of the separation of disciplines that concentrate on ever more specific aspects of reality. Today an interdisciplinary approach is needed which can pull together this fracturing of knowledge."[^chunk:2097a7d6-37db-5fbd-aff2-b1c756502acb]

The interdisciplinary approach should "flow toward coherent knowledge which begins from rigorous scientific specialization in just one aspect of reality and proceeds, with the same rigor, to situate it within the larger framework of human self-realization."[^chunk:2097a7d6-37db-5fbd-aff2-b1c756502acb]

Theology plays a crucial role in this integration. It "cannot develop independently; it must work jointly with other sciences as it seeks to interpret the presence of God in historical phenomena and events. Theology can in turn contribute elements of judgment to other disciplines to help them transcend themselves in their search for truth."[^chunk:2097a7d6-37db-5fbd-aff2-b1c756502acb]

Interdisciplinary teams should include "discussion of economics, politics, culture, science, and theology" with "special attention to the ethical dimension of the problems."[^chunk:2097a7d6-37db-5fbd-aff2-b1c756502acb] Some universities employ transdisciplinary approaches that emphasize "a triple 'beyond': 1) beyond existing knowledge and its patterns by creating new approaches to knowledge; 2) beyond the mere academic work taking into account concretely lived human and creational realities (particularly the suffering ones), and 3) beyond the mere description of reality moving into applied science that can give ground to sustainable advocacy."[^chunk:2097a7d6-37db-5fbd-aff2-b1c756502acb]

### Financing and Research Priorities

One of the principal obstacles to justice-oriented research is financing, which "often enough favors a research agenda far removed from this perspective."[^chunk:2097a7d6-37db-5fbd-aff2-b1c756502acb] Universities committed to justice-oriented research must speak frankly with research funders about this orientation.

## Models of Collaboration: University-Social Apostolate Partnerships

### The Nature of Social Apostolate Collaboration

The social apostolate of the Society brings together "institutions, communities, and individuals who accompany, serve, and defend the poor and who labor directly in the promotion of justice."[^chunk:2097a7d6-37db-5fbd-aff2-b1c756502acb] These social centers vary greatly in their activities—some focus on social action, others on charitable services, research and publication, or political impact.[^chunk:2097a7d6-37db-5fbd-aff2-b1c756502acb]

Fr. Kolvenbach warned that Jesuits "in the universities run the real risk of living separate from or… emotionally distant from the realities which are found outside the university campus."[^chunk:2097a7d6-37db-5fbd-aff2-b1c756502acb] Social centers can become crucial partners for universities seeking to advance justice work.

### Mutual Benefits

Collaboration between universities and social apostolate institutions creates mutual enrichment. Universities seek social centers for research platforms that are "dynamic, complex, and intellectually challenging," while centers offer "insights that arise from practical involvement and direct commitment with that reality."[^chunk:2097a7d6-37db-5fbd-aff2-b1c756502acb] Some social centers produce their own studies, enabling them "to form part of the interdisciplinary teams and thus enrich the university work."[^chunk:2097a7d6-37db-5fbd-aff2-b1c756502acb]

For social centers, collaboration with academia "adds rigor to their analysis of reality, enriches their reflection, fosters the solidity of their proposals, and facilitates their contact with public decision-makers who influence the situation in which they work."[^chunk:2097a7d6-37db-5fbd-aff2-b1c756502acb]

### Challenges and Progress

Significant challenges exist in university-social apostolate relationships: "The rationales and the interests of the two sectors do not always coincide, and this can lead to the misunderstandings and frustrations that inevitably occur."[^chunk:2097a7d6-37db-5fbd-aff2-b1c756502acb] Nevertheless, "there has been noteworthy progress in recent years; gradually and certainly, we are learning from experience."[^chunk:2097a7d6-37db-5fbd-aff2-b1c756502acb]

### Practical Example

Loyola University Chicago's Center for Urban Research and Learning exemplifies collaborative research. It "promotes equity and opportunity in communities throughout the Chicago metropolitan region. It builds and supports collaborative research and educational efforts. These partnerships connect Loyola faculty and students with community and nonprofit organizations, civic groups, and government agencies. Such collaborations effectively link knowledge in the community with knowledge in the university. This helps to build sustainable communities that enhance the quality of life for all citizens."[^chunk:2097a7d6-37db-5fbd-aff2-b1c756502acb]

## University Research Policy and Justice

University research policy plays a key role in prioritizing justice-oriented work. Universities can establish "policies to make this possible" when wishing "to foster and prioritize relevant research in fields related to the promotion of justice," combining policies with "concrete incentives."[^chunk:12381671-3c8f-55b2-a634-8e60520f4f14]

Effective policies support "cross-disciplinary research, orientation from the viewpoint for the poor and from among the poor, direct contact with the social reality, development of proposals, political advocacy, and participation in networks."[^chunk:12381671-3c8f-55b2-a634-8e60520f4f14]

Many universities dedicate institutional resources to justice-oriented research priorities. The Catholic University of Córdoba, Argentina, for example, "has defined five problem areas in which they will concentrate research funding: marginalization, discrimination and human rights, public health, environment and sustainable development, and institutional practices and public policy. This research has produced political advocacy initiatives in the areas of environmental justice and access to land."[^chunk:12381671-3c8f-55b2-a634-8e60520f4f14]
