## Overview

The Mission Priority Examen (MPE) is a cyclical self-study and peer review process conducted every seven years across the Association of Jesuit Colleges and Universities (AJCU) network.[^chunk:bfb2e01d-24df-5703-b47b-c13b43a3736e] This process invites institutional communities to undertake serious reflection on their Jesuit, Catholic, and liberal arts mission, establish goals for future development, and realize those goals through integrated planning.[^chunk:70820e52-0b8f-596b-bb8a-cf1b24e5b108] By examining how peer institutions across the AJCU network approach mission integration, distinct institutional contexts, priorities, and strategies emerge.

## Distinctive Features of MPE Implementation Across Institutions

### Seattle University: Environmental Stewardship and Mission Integration Infrastructure

Seattle University's most recent MPE concluded in Fall 2024, with distinctive priorities centered on strengthening Jesuit identity, inclusive excellence, and ecological commitment.[^chunk:bfb2e01d-24df-5703-b47b-c13b43a3736e] The university identified three mission priorities: strengthening understanding and commitment to its Jesuit and Catholic educational mission; promoting inclusive excellence as a Jesuit and Catholic value; and advancing care for our common home through implementation of its Laudato Si' Action Plan.[^chunk:bfb2e01d-24df-5703-b47b-c13b43a3736e]

Seattle University's approach to inclusive excellence explicitly grounds the Institutional Commitment to Diversity and Inclusion in Catholic intellectual traditions and the Jesuit commitment to justice, aligned with the Universal Apostolic Preference on "Walking with the excluded in a mission of reconciliation and justice."[^chunk:bfb2e01d-24df-5703-b47b-c13b43a3736e] For environmental priorities, the university continues development and implementation of its Laudato Si' Action Plan with four specific priorities: integrating integral ecology across curriculum and research; achieving climate neutrality and reducing environmental footprint; practicing socially responsible consumption and investment; and fostering a sustainable campus climate with deeper community engagement.[^chunk:bfb2e01d-24df-5703-b47b-c13b43a3736e]

### Fordham University: Mission-Centric Hiring and Multifaith Community Formation

Fordham University articulated four mission priorities in its 2024-2025 MPE that reflect an emphasis on institutional culture change, interfaith dialogue, transformative teaching, and public impact research.[^chunk:4558a42e-859a-573c-bdc1-5cc18cc8ecba] Priority 1 focuses on strengthening Jesuit and Catholic mission-based culture through a developmental approach to mission education from recruitment through retirement, affecting faculty hiring, staff development, trustee formation, and alumni engagement.[^chunk:4558a42e-859a-573c-bdc1-5cc18cc8ecba]

Fordham's Priority 2 reflects a distinctive institutional response to religious pluralism: forging community by supporting faith and spirituality across all religious traditions. The university implemented this priority through hiring a rabbi and imam, enabling ministry to Catholic, Orthodox, Jewish, and Muslim communities.[^chunk:017e4fb4-b98a-5fa1-8242-8b3e4943a9c6] This represents a distinctive feature of Fordham's mission implementation—advancing models for appreciating differences and learning how to engage constructively and be transformed by others from different backgrounds and faith perspectives.

Priority 3 emphasizes transformative teaching through the First-Year Experience, revised Core Curriculum with emphasis on Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm, and high-impact practices including community engaged learning and guaranteed internships.[^chunk:4558a42e-859a-573c-bdc1-5cc18cc8ecba] Priority 4 advances public impact research through a transdisciplinary research hub focusing on migration, climate change, AI, and democracy, with explicit commitment to implementing Laudato Si' through a seven-year Green Plan for Environmental Justice.[^chunk:4558a42e-859a-573c-bdc1-5cc18cc8ecba]

### Santa Clara University: Depth of Ignatian Engagement and Inclusive Mission Living

Santa Clara University completed its second MPE in 2026, having engaged a robust inclusive process that gathered input from 27 listening sessions, 200 campus community members during visiting team meetings, and 3,450 survey respondents.[^chunk:49df75cb-d5aa-5e89-9bf5-14e574cb155e] The university established two mission priorities: promoting greater depth in living its Ignatian mission and guiding values; and advancing institutional commitments that reflect its mission in the concrete circumstances of community members' lives.[^chunk:49df75cb-d5aa-5e89-9bf5-14e574cb155e]

Santa Clara's distinctive approach emphasizes that the MPE process serves to help people "enhance their understanding of our mission and make it their own," recognizing that as a community it continuously evolves in appropriating values and expressing them.[^chunk:49df75cb-d5aa-5e89-9bf5-14e574cb155e] This framing distinguishes Santa Clara's MPE as focused on deepening individual and communal ownership of mission rather than top-down implementation.

### Loyola University New Orleans: Structured Self-Study Guided by Ignatian Examen

Loyola University New Orleans is currently undertaking its 2024-25 MPE process, with an organizational structure guided by the centuries-old Ignatian Examen prayer practice.[^chunk:2ef4a51b-60cb-5c1d-99e9-4fb79da388ab] The self-study process invites community reflection organized around three Ignatian questions: What gifts does Loyola bring to mission? What challenges exist? How can Loyola improve in living its mission, and what priorities should be pursued over the next several years?[^chunk:2ef4a51b-60cb-5c1d-99e9-4fb79da388ab]

The Loyola process occurs in three defined phases: Preparing and Listening (Fall 2024), Dialoguing (Fall 2025), and Celebrating (Spring 2026).[^chunk:2ef4a51b-60cb-5c1d-99e9-4fb79da388ab] This distinctive temporal structure integrates the natural rhythm of the academic calendar with the deliberate pacing of Ignatian discernment.

### College of the Holy Cross: Shared Mission Ownership and Environmental-Social Integration

College of the Holy Cross engaged its second Mission Priority Examen during 2025-26, completing a self-study and welcoming a peer visiting team in October 2026.[^chunk:f51c9710-2994-5521-a258-17f39961daf8] The college identified three proposed mission priorities: "A Sense of the Whole": Shared Mission Ownership, calling for deepened shared ownership and recognition of every person as essential; Sustainability of Persons and Planet, reflecting General Congregation 36's call to right relationships with God, one another, and creation; and Both/And: Authentically Jesuit, Fully Catholic, continuing a theme from the 2019 MPE to integrate Catholic and Jesuit dimensions throughout academic, residential, pastoral, and co-curricular life.[^chunk:f51c9710-2994-5521-a258-17f39961daf8]

Holy Cross's distinctive framing of "Sustainability of Persons and Planet" integrates ecological commitment with relational integrity, reflecting an approach that views environmental justice as inseparable from interpersonal community.

### University of Scranton: Committee-Based Inclusive Representation

The University of Scranton is undertaking its self-study during spring 2026, with a committee structure explicitly organized by the seven characteristics of Jesuit higher education.[^chunk:afd6104b-d6ee-5611-be43-fbcc822d249d] Committee membership intentionally represents students, faculty, staff, and administrators across each characteristic domain, ensuring that mission reflection occurs through multiple institutional perspectives.

### Rockhurst University: Structured Phases and Community Engagement

Rockhurst University's MPE process comprises distinct phases: formation of a self-study committee that facilitates listening sessions and surveys; composition of a self-study report proposing two to four mission priorities; peer review team visit and reporting; and submission to the Jesuit Superior General for reaffirmation and guidance.[^chunk:70820e52-0b8f-596b-bb8a-cf1b24e5b108] The institution then implements its mission priorities through connection to strategic planning and other institutional documents.

## Institutional Contexts Shaping the MPE Process

### Urban Context and Public Impact: Fordham's Mission Territory Framework

Fordham University's institutional context as a research university in New York City significantly shapes its MPE approach. The university explicitly frames itself as operating in "mission territory," acknowledging that many community members choose Fordham for academic excellence and location rather than mission.[^chunk:35f3f8d1-0109-5418-87ec-fb90d21615a7] This contextual reality led to priority emphasis on mission communication from recruitment through employment, on interfaith engagement, and on public impact research addressing urgent urban problems.

The institutional challenge of balancing academic excellence and marketplace sensitivity while remaining rooted in Jesuit Catholic identity reflects Fordham's specific urban context.[^chunk:35f3f8d1-0109-5418-87ec-fb90d21615a7] An Ignatian discernment exercise conducted with trustees in Rome explicitly explored this tension: "What does it mean for Fordham to be rooted in its Jesuit and Catholic identity and mission and yet be attentive and committed to a financial and business model that requires it to be sensitive to the marketplace?"[^chunk:7c1e5dbd-be5a-5a5b-85fb-5af80f42479b]

### Silicon Valley Context: Santa Clara's Mission Evolution

Santa Clara University's institutional context in Silicon Valley shapes its MPE as an ongoing process of community members making mission "their own" within rapidly changing circumstances.[^chunk:49df75cb-d5aa-5e89-9bf5-14e574cb155e] The university recognizes that as new voices join the community daily, the institution must continuously evolve in appropriating and expressing Jesuit values while responding to the specific needs of its Silicon Valley location.

### First Studies Program and International Jesuit Formation: Fordham's Distinctive Apostolate

Fordham's hosting of a First Studies Program for Jesuit scholastics, now in its fourth year with approximately 30 young Jesuits from the Jesuit Conference of Canada and the United States (JCCU), shapes institutional identity around formation and international collaboration.[^chunk:87232d98-8417-5a2d-a0fe-baa25ea62147] Scholastics participate in coursework, faculty mentorship, spiritual direction, and liturgical life, while also serving the local Bronx community—creating a distinctive institutional context where Jesuit formation and university mission align directly.

### Religious Pluralism and Interfaith Infrastructure: Fordham and Seattle

Both Fordham and Seattle University's institutional contexts involve significant religious diversity requiring intentional interfaith engagement. Fordham's hiring of a rabbi and imam and Seattle's inclusive excellence priority rooted in justice reflect institutional recognition that their communities include people from multiple faith traditions.[^chunk:017e4fb4-b98a-5fa1-8242-8b3e4943a9c6][^chunk:bfb2e01d-24df-5703-b47b-c13b43a3736e]

## Emerging Priorities Across Institutions

### Mission-Conscious Hiring and Formation Infrastructure

Multiple institutions identified similar emerging priorities around institutional hiring, formation, and development: Fordham developed a comprehensive approach to recruitment, hiring, orientation, and ongoing formation for faculty, staff, and trustees from recruitment through retirement.[^chunk:4558a42e-859a-573c-bdc1-5cc18cc8ecba] Seattle University similarly identified institutionalizing mission-conscious hiring, onboarding, professional development, and promotion as a priority.[^chunk:bfb2e01d-24df-5703-b47b-c13b43a3736e]

### Environmental Justice and Laudato Si' Implementation

Environmental commitment emerged as a shared priority across institutions: Fordham advanced a seven-year Green Plan for Environmental Justice and received a $50 million EPA grant for environmental justice work;[^chunk:4558a42e-859a-573c-bdc1-5cc18cc8ecba][^chunk:35f3f8d1-0109-5418-87ec-fb90d21615a7] Seattle University implemented a Laudato Si' Action Plan with concrete priorities for climate neutrality and sustainable practices;[^chunk:bfb2e01d-24df-5703-b47b-c13b43a3736e] and Holy Cross integrated environmental sustainability with relational integrity in its "Sustainability of Persons and Planet" priority.[^chunk:f51c9710-2994-5521-a258-17f39961daf8]

### Public Impact Research and Transdisciplinary Collaboration

Fordham's emphasis on transdisciplinary research addressing migration, climate change, artificial intelligence, and democracy reflects an emerging institutional priority across Jesuit universities toward research "that matters to the world."[^chunk:4558a42e-859a-573c-bdc1-5cc18cc8ecba] This priority aligns with Universal Apostolic Preferences and connects research directly to the mission of justice and care for the common home.

### Deepening Ignatian Engagement and Mission Ownership

Santa Clara University's emphasis on promoting "greater depth" in Ignatian engagement reflects an emerging priority toward moving beyond awareness to transformative appropriation of mission values.[^chunk:49df75cb-d5aa-5e89-9bf5-14e574cb155e] Similarly, Seattle University identified developing "a shared understanding of the Jesuit and Catholic educational mission" as foundational.[^chunk:bfb2e01d-24df-5703-b47b-c13b43a3736e]

### Inclusive Excellence and Justice as Theological Foundation

Multiple institutions identified priority to ground institutional commitments to diversity, equity, and inclusion in Catholic social thought and Jesuit justice tradition rather than treating them as separate from mission. Fordham, Seattle, and Holy Cross each prioritized integration of diversity commitments with theological and spiritual foundations.[^chunk:bfb2e01d-24df-5703-b47b-c13b43a3736e][^chunk:4558a42e-859a-573c-bdc1-5cc18cc8ecba][^chunk:f51c9710-2994-5521-a258-17f39961daf8]

## Peer Visiting Experiences Across Institutions

### Composition and Leadership of Peer Visiting Teams

Peer visiting teams consist of faculty, administrators, and mission leaders from other AJCU institutions, ensuring that external perspectives inform institutional self-assessment.[^chunk:bfb2e01d-24df-5703-b47b-c13b43a3736e] For Seattle University's 2024 peer visit, team members included Xavier Cole, President of Loyola New Orleans; Michael Kaufman, Dean of School of Law at Santa Clara; Claire Noonan, Vice President for Mission Integration at Loyola Chicago; and Tania Tetlow, President of Fordham University and Visiting Team Chair.[^chunk:bfb2e01d-24df-5703-b47b-c13b43a3736e]

Fordham's 2025 peer visiting team included Thomas Poon (Executive Vice President and Provost at Loyola Marymount University), Matthew Carnes, S.J. (Vice President for Mission and Ministry at Santa Clara), Ellen Maccarone (Acting Vice President for Mission Integration at Gonzaga), and Nancy Tuchman (Founding Dean of School of Environmental Sustainability at Loyola Chicago).[^chunk:4558a42e-859a-573c-bdc1-5cc18cc8ecba]

Loyola New Orleans's peer visiting team includes John Sebastian (Vice President for Mission and Ministry at Loyola Marymount), Mia Bertagnolli (Interim Provost at Gonzaga), Andrew Downing, S.J. (Director of Ignatian Programs at Georgetown), and Nicki Gonzalez (Professor of History and Vice Provost for Diversity at Regis).[^chunk:2ef4a51b-60cb-5c1d-99e9-4fb79da388ab]

### Nature of Peer Visit Engagement

Peer visiting teams engage in comprehensive campus dialogue with "various campus constituencies."[^chunk:70820e52-0b8f-596b-bb8a-cf1b24e5b108] Santa Clara's visiting team met with 200 campus community members during their visit, reflecting the breadth of engagement.[^chunk:49df75cb-d5aa-5e89-9bf5-14e574cb155e] Peer teams both affirm institutional strengths and offer mission-based recommendations for growth.

### Peer Visit as Celebration and Challenge: Seattle University Example

Seattle University's 2024 peer visiting team provided feedback emphasizing that "Seattle University is, without question, a thoroughly Jesuit and Catholic University."[^chunk:bfb2e01d-24df-5703-b47b-c13b43a3736e] The team noted that the community has "worked hard to preserve its core mission" while "swimming against a tide of increasing secularism and cynicism," and has done so "with a particularly Jesuit way of proceeding, with discernment, innovation, and ambition."[^chunk:bfb2e01d-24df-5703-b47b-c13b43a3736e]

### Fordham's Peer Visit Experience: Honesty and Transparency in Self-Assessment

Fordham's peer visiting team, upon arrival in January 2025, explicitly appreciated the "honesty and transparency about challenges to living the mission" evident in the self-study report.[^chunk:4558a42e-859a-573c-bdc1-5cc18cc8ecba] The team noted that the MPE process at Fordham was "truly modeled as intended after an Ignatian Examen," suggesting that distinctive value emerges from authentic self-examination rather than mere celebration.

The peer team emphasized Fordham's deep commitment to Jesuit and Catholic mission while also noting "areas for growth."[^chunk:4558a42e-859a-573c-bdc1-5cc18cc8ecba] Following the visit, Fordham's Vice President for Mission Integration met with the Mission Council and president to discuss addressing and implementing preliminary findings, demonstrating how peer visit feedback becomes institutionalized through formal structures.

### Role of Peer Visiting Teams in Broader Process

The peer visiting team's report contributes to a formal escalation process in which the Provincial of Jesuits West, the President of the AJCU, and all US and Canadian Provincials add their endorsements before materials are sent to Rome, where the Jesuit Superior General reviews all submitted materials to reaffirm the school's Jesuit, Catholic mission and provide guidance for growth.[^chunk:bfb2e01d-24df-5703-b47b-c13b43a3736e]

## Integration and Implementation of Mission Priorities

### Strategic Plan Alignment

Institutions deliberately align mission priorities with strategic planning to ensure implementation. Fordham's mission priorities "either reinforce or complement key goals articulated in Fordham's emerging strategic plan," positioning them for integration through multiple institutional channels.[^chunk:4558a42e-859a-573c-bdc1-5cc18cc8ecba] Santa Clara recognized "great resonance" between its two mission priorities and its strategic plan, Impact 2030.[^chunk:49df75cb-d5aa-5e89-9bf5-14e574cb155e]

### Cross-Departmental Collaboration for Implementation

Fordham identified specific departments responsible for implementation: University Marketing and Communications with Mission Integration for clearer mission communication; Academic Affairs, Student Affairs, and Mission Integration for First-Year Experience development; Provost's Office, Deans, and Chairs with Mission Integration for faculty recruitment and formation; Office of Human Resources with Mission Integration for staff formation; and Development and University Relations with Mission Integration for alumni and donor engagement.[^chunk:017e4fb4-b98a-5fa1-8242-8b3e4943a9c6]

### Connection to Universal Apostolic Preferences

Multiple institutions positioned their mission priorities in relation to the Society of Jesus's Universal Apostolic Preferences. Fordham's environmental justice priority aligns with UAP No. 4 (Care for Our Common Home), explicitly articulated in the UEA Province Priority 3 as "engaging with greater freedom in the mission of ecological and social justice."[^chunk:017e4fb4-b98a-5fa1-8242-8b3e4943a9c6] Seattle University similarly linked inclusive excellence to the Universal Apostolic Preference on "Walking with the excluded in a mission of reconciliation and justice."[^chunk:bfb2e01d-24df-5703-b47b-c13b43a3736e]

## Conclusion

The Mission Priority Examen process reveals both convergence and distinctive adaptation across Jesuit universities. Common emerging priorities include mission-conscious hiring and formation infrastructure, environmental justice and Laudato Si' implementation, public impact research, deepened Ignatian engagement, and integration of inclusive excellence with theological foundations. Simultaneously, institutional contexts—whether urban research universities like Fordham, Silicon Valley innovation centers like Santa Clara, or smaller liberal arts colleges—shape how these shared priorities are articulated and implemented. Peer visiting experiences function as crucial moments of mutual accountability and affirmation, enabling institutions to learn from trusted colleagues in the AJCU network while maintaining fidelity to shared Jesuit and Catholic traditions.
