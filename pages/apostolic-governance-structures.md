## Overview

Apostolic governance structures within the Society of Jesus are designed to integrate two complementary dimensions: *cura apostolica* (apostolic care) and *cura personalis* (personal care).[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51] These structures involve multiple leadership roles working in coordination to ensure both the mission of the Society and the accompaniment of its members are effectively maintained.

## Primary Roles and Responsibilities of Jesuit Governance Structures

### Major Superiors and Local Superiors

The Major Superior holds authority over an entire Province or Region and is responsible for ensuring care of the life-mission across all members, communities, and apostolic works under their jurisdiction.[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c] They employ several key instruments, including annual visitations to communities and works, and the account of conscience given by Jesuits under their responsibility.[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c]

The Local Superior occupies a critical position within this network of governance offices and functions. They are responsible for strengthening members in their apostolic mission and for visiting apostolic environments to engage with the Director of the Work.[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c] When a community is responsible for an apostolic work, the Local Superior encourages Jesuit members of the apostolic team and cooperates with the Director to foster the Ignatian and Jesuit identity of the institution.[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c]

### Directors of Work

Directors of Work—increasingly lay men and women, diocesan priests, religious women, or other religious—serve as leaders of apostolic institutions and projects.[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51] They carry significant responsibility for the apostolic animation and mission implementation of their respective works, though they do so in conjunction with governance structures of the Society.

### Role of Delegates and Territorial Coordinators

In large administrative units, both demographically and geographically, delegated authority is distributed to other persons to accompany different areas of life-mission.[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c] In several large Provinces, Delegates have been appointed to care for specific populations—such as Jesuits of the third age or those in formation—or for particular apostolic areas.[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c]

## Collaboration Between Local Superiors and Directors of Work

### Foundational Principles

Understanding *cura apostolica* and *cura personalis* as inseparably linked means that care for life-mission requires close cooperation between the Local Superior and the Director of the Work.[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c] This cooperation is not meant to be tension-free; rather, the goal is to live the tension in a healthy and constructive manner.[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c]

Critical to this collaboration is the establishment of procedures and regular formal conversations between the Local Superior and the Director of the Work to foster care of the mission.[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c] Depending on circumstances, these exchanges should take whatever form proves most effective and may be complemented by other types of meeting or dialogue.[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c]

### Quality Communication and Shared Responsibility

Good practices frequently mentioned in apostolic governance include the reaffirmation of apostolic co-responsibility, honest and open cooperation, facilitation of personal understanding among those who participate in the mission, and joint formation within each apostolic work.[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51] The most important element is quality communication—frequent and regular, both formal and informal—between Superiors, Jesuit communities, Delegates, Secretaries, and Directors of Work.[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51]

In apostolic works with management boards or other collegial forms of governance, it is important to foster communication between the Local Superior and board members without interfering in the internal arrangements of the work.[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51] Meetings of Local Superiors and Directors of Work from a wide geographical area or entire Province/Region help gain a broader vision of the mission and insight into members' apostolates.[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51]

### Formation and Leadership Development

Care cannot be improvised; it requires personal conversion and self-awareness, as well as strengthening of skills and formation procedures aimed at shared leadership.[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c] The Local Superior can arrange formation with the Director of the Work to help each other and support one another in a spirit of sharing.[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c]

Provision of adequate personal accompaniment for all Directors of Work is necessary, including a well-planned offer of ongoing formation that encompasses the history, tradition and spirituality of the Society, discernment in common, apostolic planning, cooperation, leadership and management, and the relationship between the two curae.[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51]

## Recent Changes from the 36th General Congregation

### Review and Adaptation of Guidelines

The 36th General Congregation has directed that the "Guidelines for the Relationship between the Superior and the Director of Work" (1998) be reviewed and their implementation evaluated.[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51] This review should take into account the increasing number of lay Directors of Jesuit Works and adapt the Guidelines as needed for the realities of the Conferences.[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51]

### Emphasis on Spiritual Conversation and Discernment

The 36th General Congregation stresses the importance of spiritual conversation as a habitual mode of exchange in communities and apostolic works, which prepares participants for discernment in common as the ordinary way of reaching decisions in the life-mission of the Society.[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c] This emphasis commits the Society to offering good formation programmes in each Province/Region and to creating methods for reaching decisions in apostolic works inspired by spiritual conversation and discernment in common.[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c]

### Cooperation and Apostolic Networks

A significant challenge identified is to grow in cooperation, because the vitality and apostolic mission of the Society increases when potential is united with efforts of many other people of good will.[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51] The 36th General Congregation also highlights the challenge of managing apostolic networks that extend beyond the frontiers of Provinces and Conferences into a variety of territories, and finding ways to relate these networks to the governance of the Society.[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51]

### Lay Apostolic Partnership

The General Congregation has affirmed an evolution toward lay apostolic partnership in Jesuit works. In U.S. Jesuit colleges and universities, Jesuits comprise less than five percent of the total faculty, staff, and administration, and leadership positions are increasingly held by lay persons and non-Jesuits.[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021] This shift is recognized both as calling for special initiatives to assure Jesuit character and as an opportunity for new apostolic vitality.[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]

## Challenges in Current Governance Structures

The new structures of governance are themselves an opportunity for greater integration, but they are often complex.[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51] Large territorial and/or demographic dimensions of governance units can jeopardize the accompaniment of both curae if adequate delegation strategies are not employed.[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51]

The figure of the Local Superior risks being blurred and weakened, as issues of apostolic mission tend to be discussed directly with sector or territorial Delegates or with the Major Superior, without including the Local Superior.[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51] In multi-apostolic communities, Local Superiors may find it difficult to accompany apostolic concerns whose structure or areas of activity are unfamiliar to them.[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51]

In some cases, Directors of Work enjoy excessive autonomy with respect to local and provincial government, stemming from personal identification with their work and resulting in a loss of freedom for apostolic spiritual discernment.[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51] These difficulties become acute where there is lack of clarity regarding the identity of a work, apostolate, or institution in its relationship with the Society.[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51]

## Delegation and Authority in Governance

Authority in the apostolic body flows through a structured process of delegation. The General Congregation delegates authority to the Superior General for the care of the universal body; the Superior General in turn delegates required authority to Major Superiors for their administrative unit; and Local Superiors and Directors of Work receive delegated authority from the Major Superior, with authorization from Father General.[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c]

The effectiveness of delegation depends on establishing permanent contact between Delegates and the Major Superior, who always retains overall authority over members and apostolic works of the Province or Region.[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c]

Recent General Congregations have promoted processes of restructuring that generate new forms of support for apostolic governance, different from and complementary to the delegation of authority, intended to help coordinate apostolic areas, accompany development of apostolic plans, and organize communities in given geographical areas.[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c]

## Lay Leadership and Partnership

The role of lay apostolic partners has expanded significantly. In the U.S. Jesuit higher education context, boards of trustees now hold fiduciary responsibility for maintaining and fostering the Catholic, Jesuit character of institutions.[^chunk:03ee3a20-dcf0-5cae-84e8-e9cdc45777fd] Presidents, whether Jesuit or lay, serve as "Directors of the Apostolic Work" and should be "missioned" to this role by the provincial on behalf of the Society of Jesus.[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

Structures and programs have been created for hiring, orienting, and developing faculty and staff according to Catholic, Jesuit mission, including special retreats, seminars in Ignatian spirituality, and formation programs.[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021] This evolution of responsibility and leadership by apostolic partners, if properly supported and fostered, is viewed as potentially strengthening the integrity and quality of the Jesuit apostolic mission.[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]

## Instruments for Care of Mission

Spiritual conversation and discernment in common are identified as excellent instruments for care of mission in which all involved should participate.[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c] Spiritual conversation presupposes attentive listening and opens participants to a new perspective through shared experience, preparing them for discernment in common as the ordinary way to reach decisions.[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c]

The account of conscience, the Jesuit tradition of openness and honesty, and the desire to seek God's will together constitute cornerstones of this approach.[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c]
