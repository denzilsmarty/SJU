## Cura Personalis: Personal Care and Individual Attention

**Cura Personalis** (alumnorum cura personalis) means "a genuine love and personal care for each of our students."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe] It is one of the overriding principles of Jesuit pedagogy, deriving directly from Ignatius's conviction that "God gives different gifts to each of us."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe]

In practice, cura personalis guides teachers to know their students deeply. The Ratio of 1591 advises that "masters study their pupils at length and reflect upon their aptitudes, their defects and the implications of their classroom behaviour," and that "at least some of the teachers" become "well acquainted with the student's home background."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe] Teachers are called to "respect the dignity and personality of the pupils," to "be patient with students and know how to overlook certain mistakes or put off their correction until the apt psychological moment," and to "be much readier with praise than blame."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe]

Cura personalis extends beyond academic performance. It "attends to the intellectual, emotional, moral, spiritual, and relational dimensions of the learner."[^chunk:2950a02d-cf58-5099-ba47-65483b0e8a49] It requires educators to "create a space in which students can engage honestly and safely, while still being challenged to grow."[^chunk:2950a02d-cf58-5099-ba47-65483b0e8a49] The teacher becomes "a companion in the student's formation journey—working with the learner, not simply delivering content to the learner," involving "knowing students by name, learning something of their stories, designing from their context rather than only from a syllabus, making time for personal conversation, attending compassionately to struggle, and forming conscience as well as competence."[^chunk:2950a02d-cf58-5099-ba47-65483b0e8a49]

Authentic personal relationships are essential. A Jesuit school is "a face-to-face community in which an authentic personal relationship between teachers and students may flourish." Without such a relationship of friendship, "much of the unique force of our education would be lost, for an authentic relationship of trust and friendship between the teacher and pupil is an invaluable dispositive condition for any genuine growth in commitment to values."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe]

## Magis: The Pursuit of Greater Excellence

**Magis** means "the more, the greater glory of God."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe] It embodies Ignatius's insistence on the "fullest development of the human person" and holds out expectations that "go beyond mastery of the skills and understandings normally found in the well informed and competent students."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe]

Magis applies both to academics and to action. In their training, "Jesuits are traditionally encouraged by various experiences to explore the dimensions and expressions of Christian service as a means of developing a spirit of generosity."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe] Jesuit schools should develop this impulse "into programmes of service which would encourage the student to actively experience and test his or her acceptance of the magis. By this service, the student can be led to discover the dialectic of action and contemplation."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe]

Magis demands demanding expectations that extend beyond ordinary mastery. It extends "to action through programs of service that let students test generosity and discover the dialectic of action and contemplation."[^chunk:67b8da83-3884-579a-827d-636006857615]

However, not all action reflects the true magis. "Ignatius offers a way to discover and choose God's will," and **discernment** becomes pivotal in determining what truly serves greater glory.[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe]

## Discernment: Weighing Values and Making Free Choices

**Discernment** is the process by which students learn to discover God's will amid competing values. It is "to be taught and practiced in schools as the means of weighing motives, values, priorities, and the impact of decisions on the poor."[^chunk:67b8da83-3884-579a-827d-636006857615]

Discernment requires rigorous reflection. It "requires getting the facts and then reflecting, sorting out the motives that impel us, weighing values and priorities, considering how significant decisions will impact on the poor, deciding, and living with our decisions."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe] With competing values bombarding students, "making free human choice is never easy," yet "discernment becomes crucial" when "there is always a pull and tug."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe]

## Formation of Men and Women for Others

The ultimate purpose of Jesuit schools is to **form men and women for others in imitation of Christ Jesus.** This is rooted in the Incarnational principle: "The reality of the Incarnation affects Jesuit education at its core."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe] Response to Christ's call "demands that we be, and teach our students to be, for others," and such formation is fundamentally "humanistic" rather than self-centered.[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe]

This principle rejects egotism, which "not only does not humanize the material creation, he dehumanizes people themselves." The egoist "changes people into things by dominating them, exploiting them, and taking to himself the fruit of their labour," and in doing so "dehumanizes himself."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe]

## The Four Cs: Competence, Conscience, Compassion, and Commitment

The formation of men and women for others is expressed through four interrelated qualities:

**Competence** means excellence in one's field: "rigorous knowledge, developed skill, and the ability to contribute effectively."[^chunk:2950a02d-cf58-5099-ba47-65483b0e8a49] Jesuit schools "must strive for high academic quality" through "rigorous, probing study of crucial human problems and concerns."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe] This education rejects "the facile and superficial world of slogans or ideology, of purely emotional and self-centred responses, and of instant, simplistic solutions."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe]

**Conscience** means "ethical discernment rooted in values and faith."[^chunk:2950a02d-cf58-5099-ba47-65483b0e8a49] Education must form "the conscience of learners (their beliefs, values, attitudes and their entire way of thinking) in such a manner that they are led to move beyond knowing, to undertake action."[^chunk:abab7d20-18ad-5a48-a87f-0445438f8e4e]

**Compassion** means "genuine care for others, especially people who are marginalized or students who struggle with comprehension, writing, reading, or confidence."[^chunk:2950a02d-cf58-5099-ba47-65483b0e8a49]

**Commitment** means "active engagement with justice and the world, supported by real cases and situations that call students to sustained response."[^chunk:2950a02d-cf58-5099-ba47-65483b0e8a49]

These four dimensions work together in integral formation. The document notes that "for the effective exercise of a moral force in society, we must insist that the process of education takes place in a moral as well as an intellectual framework."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe] This is not indoctrination but rather "a framework of inquiry in which the process of wrestling with big issues and complex values is made fully legitimate."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe]

## Ignatian Pedagogy: Attending to All Dimensions of the Person

Ignatian pedagogy attends to intellectual, emotional, moral, and spiritual dimensions of the learner through several principles:

**Formation of the whole person**: Ignatius conceived of the human person with "awe and wonder in appreciation for God's gifts of creation." Thus "the role of imagination, affection, will, as well as intellect are central to an Ignatian approach," ensuring that "Jesuit education involves formation of the whole person."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe] This enables students "to discover the realm of meaning in life, which can in turn give direction to our understanding of who we are and why we are here" and "provide criteria for our priorities and crucial choices at turning points in our lives."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe]

**Respect for human freedom**: Ignatius's approach "rules out any semblance of indoctrination or manipulation in Jesuit education."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe] Jesuit pedagogy "should enable students to explore reality with open hearts and minds," helping them "to be critical of their societies in a positive as well as negative sense, embracing wholesome values proposed, while rejecting specious values and practices."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe]

**Experience and reflection**: Learning proceeds through the constant interplay of experience, reflection, and action. Through reflection, students use "memory, understanding, imagination and feelings...to capture the meaning and the essential value of what is being studied, to discover its relationship with other aspects of knowledge and human activity, and to appreciate its implications in the ongoing search for truth and freedom."[^chunk:abab7d20-18ad-5a48-a87f-0445438f8e4e]

**The teacher's role**: The teacher's presence and personal example are critical. Teachers are "called to be men and women of the Spirit," and "what you are speaks louder than what you do or say."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe] In today's world, "students do not listen seriously to teachers but to witnesses; and if they do listen to teachers, it is because they are witnesses."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe] The teacher's "living example will lead them beyond reflection to aspire to live what the words mean."[^chunk:a315032f-8033-5fa7-902a-336a332be9fe]
