## Overview

Between 2015 and 2019 the International Commission on the Apostolate of Jesuit Education (ICAJE) and the Society of Jesus issued three documents that updated and extended the global Jesuit educational framework: *Human Excellence* (2015), *Jesuit Schools: A Living Tradition in the 21st Century* (2019), and the *Universal Apostolic Preferences* (2019–2029). During the same period, Superior General Arturo Sosa, S.J., delivered a landmark address framing the Jesuit university as a site of reconciliation. Together, these texts constitute the contemporary layer of the tradition that educators must read alongside the foundational 1986 *Characteristics* and the 1993 *Ignatian Pedagogy: A Practical Approach*.

## 1. Human Excellence (2015) and the Four Cs

### 1.1 Origin and authority

*Human Excellence* was promulgated by ICAJE in 2015 and sits at Tier 2 of the source hierarchy used in Jesuit educational institutions—that is, among the official education frameworks promulgated or endorsed by the Society of Jesus that define the modern global framework and its development.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

### 1.2 The Four Cs defined

The document articulates the desired qualities of students in four terms, collectively known as **the Four Cs**:[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300][^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

| Term | Brief description |
|---|---|
| **Conscience** | The formed capacity to perceive moral reality and make truthful, responsible judgments |
| **Competence** | The disciplinary, professional, and practical mastery required for effective service |
| **Compassion** | The affective and relational orientation toward others, especially the excluded |
| **Commitment** | The sustained, deliberate disposition to act for the good of others and creation |

These four qualities are best understood as **formative qualities or habits**, not as sequential stages.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] The authoritative glossary entry in Jesuit educational documents is precise: "Conscience, competence, compassion, and commitment, articulated in ICAJE's 2015 *Human Excellence* document as qualities of the person Jesuit education seeks to form."[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

### 1.3 Relationship to earlier documents

The Four Cs do not replace the 1986 *Characteristics* or the 1993 IPP. They name outcomes and habits toward which repeated IPP cycles may contribute.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Importantly, institutional variants on this vocabulary—such as local adaptations that add or rename one of the Cs—should not be confused with the globally promulgated framework.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## 2. Arturo Sosa and the University as Reconciled Life (2018)

### 2.1 Context of the address

Arturo Sosa, S.J., elected Superior General in 2016, delivered "The University as a Source of Reconciled Life" in 2018. The address is classified among the authoritative interpretive addresses of the Superior General that interpret the charism for particular periods and for higher education specifically.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

### 2.2 Reconciliation as the framing mission

Sosa located the Jesuit university's mission explicitly within the horizon of **reconciliation**—with God, with one another, and with creation.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0] This framing integrates three dimensions that other documents had addressed separately:

- *Vertical reconciliation* — the relationship between the human person and God, expressed through the tradition of faith and discernment;
- *Horizontal reconciliation* — justice, solidarity, and right relationship among persons, communities, and peoples;
- *Ecological reconciliation* — care for our common home, anticipating the language of the Universal Apostolic Preferences.

### 2.3 The digital ecosystem as a new cultural paradigm

Sosa also identified the **digital ecosystem** as a new cultural paradigm that the Jesuit university must engage critically and creatively.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0] This signals that the university's formative mission is not confined to inherited disciplinary content but must include the ethical and cultural analysis of digital knowledge systems—an insight that subsequent Jesuit pedagogical reflection has developed under the heading of AI-shaped education.

### 2.4 Place in the broader sequence

Sosa's 2018 address extends a line of Superior General addresses that progressively deepened the social and global dimensions of Jesuit higher education: Arrupe's call for "men for others" (1973), Kolvenbach's insistence on educated solidarity through contact with suffering (2000), and Nicolás's diagnosis of the "globalization of superficiality" (2010). Together these texts extend reflection from private introspection to disciplined depth, global interdependence, institutional collaboration, and the ethical design of knowledge systems.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

## 3. A Living Tradition in the 21st Century (2019)

*Jesuit Schools: A Living Tradition in the 21st Century—An Ongoing Exercise of Discernment* (2019) is the third major ICAJE document in this period. It explicitly **complements rather than replaces** the 1986 *Characteristics* and the 1993 IPP.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0] The document names **ten global identifiers** for Jesuit schools:

1. Catholic identity and faith dialogue
2. Safety
3. Global citizenship
4. Care for creation
5. Justice
6. Accessibility
7. Interculturality
8. Networking
9. Human excellence
10. Lifelong learning[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

Because *A Living Tradition* was written principally for the schools apostolate, its application to higher education requires disciplined translation, supplemented by the superior general addresses and the specific higher-education decree of General Congregation 34.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## 4. The Universal Apostolic Preferences (2019–2029)

### 4.1 What they are

The Universal Apostolic Preferences (UAPs) were approved for the decade 2019–2029 and represent the Society of Jesus's statement of where it will focus its collective energy across all apostolates. They are classified at Tier 2—official education frameworks promulgated or endorsed by the Society—and constitute part of the minimum primary-source corpus for Jesuit pedagogical work.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

### 4.2 The four preferences

The Society committed itself to four global preferences:[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0][^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

1. **Show the way to God through discernment and the Spiritual Exercises**
2. **Walk with the excluded, the poor, and those whose dignity has been violated**
3. **Accompany young people in creating a hope-filled future**
4. **Collaborate in the care of our common home**

### 4.3 How the UAPs shape Jesuit education today

The Universal Apostolic Preferences **do not alter the five dynamics** of the Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm (context, experience, reflection, action, evaluation).[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0] Their effect is different and more targeted: they **sharpen the contexts, experiences, and actions to which Jesuit education must attend**.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

In practical terms this means:

- **Context** must include the concrete situations of excluded communities, the ecological crisis, the digital ecosystem, and the cultural world of young people;
- **Experience** should create authentic encounter with the excluded, with ecological realities, and with the movements of the Spirit discerned through Ignatian tools;
- **Action** should be oriented toward solidarity with the marginalised, care for creation, and the accompaniment of youth;
- **Evaluation** should ask whether the educational design is actually serving the priorities the Society has publicly named.

At the institutional level, Jesuit universities that have incorporated the UAPs into strategic planning treat them as a framework for examining priorities—which experiences the university creates, which communities it partners with, and which forms of knowledge production it privileges.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

## 5. General Congregation 34 and University Life

Although GC 34 predates this period (1995), its Decree 17, "Jesuits and University Life," remains the foundational congregational text for the higher-education context within which all subsequent documents—including *Human Excellence*, Sosa's address, *A Living Tradition*, and the UAPs—operate. GC 34 affirmed the university as a setting for rigorous teaching, research, interdisciplinary work, service, pastoral care, and the integration of faith and justice, and called Jesuit universities to examine themselves and be accountable for whether their actual priorities serve this mission.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## 6. Synthesis: How the Documents Interrelate

| Document | Year | Primary contribution |
|---|---|---|
| GC 34, Decree 17 | 1995 | Congregational foundation for higher education |
| *Human Excellence* (ICAJE) | 2015 | Four Cs as formative qualities: conscience, competence, compassion, commitment |
| Sosa, "University as a Source of Reconciled Life" | 2018 | Reconciliation (with God, others, creation) as university mission; digital ecosystem as new cultural paradigm |
| *A Living Tradition* (ICAJE) | 2019 | Ten global identifiers; complements 1986 and 1993 documents |
| Universal Apostolic Preferences | 2019–2029 | Four decade-long priorities sharpening the contexts and actions of Jesuit education |

These documents together constitute a **living tradition**: none abolishes its predecessors, each responds to new conditions, and all converge on the conviction that Jesuit education must form whole persons—in conscience, competence, compassion, and commitment—who engage a fragile, interdependent world with discernment and responsibility.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0][^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
