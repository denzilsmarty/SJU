Reflection is the pivotal third dynamic of the Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm (IPP), occupying what the 1993 source text calls the hinge connecting experience to responsible action.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] At Saint Joseph's University, understanding reflection with precision matters because it is both the most intellectually demanding element of the paradigm and the one most susceptible to misuse—reduced to journaling, collapsed into therapy, or steered toward a predetermined answer.

## What Reflection Is—and Is Not

The 1993 *Ignatian Pedagogy: A Practical Approach* (ICAJE, nn. 47–58) defines reflection as the disciplined process by which learners grasp the significance of experience.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0] It draws on memory, understanding, imagination, and feeling; examines causes, assumptions, motives, implications, alternatives, and consequences; and seeks greater freedom for choice.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]
