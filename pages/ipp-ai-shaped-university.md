## Overview

Generative AI has entered the context of nearly every educational encounter. At Saint Joseph's University (SJU), the guiding framework for thinking through that entry is the **Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm (IPP)**—a recursive, relational process whose five dynamics are **context, experience, reflection, action, and evaluation**. Rather than treating AI as simply permitted or prohibited, the IPP asks a more fundamental question: *what forms of human judgment, authorship, relationship, and responsibility is this educational design meant to cultivate?*[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

The framework described here draws on *Ignatian Pedagogy: A Practical Approach* (ICAJE, 1993), the working source-of-truth document prepared for SJU (Draft 0.1, 23 August 2026), and key addresses by Superior Generals Adolfo Nicolás, S.J., and Arturo Sosa, S.J.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300][^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

## Why the IPP Becomes *More* Important in an AI-Shaped University

The SJU working document states plainly: "In an AI-shaped university, the IPP becomes more—not less—important." Generative systems can assist research, feedback, accessibility, and rehearsal. They cannot assume responsibility, enter a mutual relationship, discern a vocation, or substitute for the human encounter at the heart of *cura personalis*.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Adolfo Nicolás's 2010 address warned against the "globalization of superficiality" as a threat to depth of thought and imagination.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Arturo Sosa's 2018 address identified the digital ecosystem as a new cultural paradigm and located the Jesuit university within a mission of reconciliation with God, one another, and creation.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0] AI-generated fluency is precisely the form of superficiality these addresses anticipate: information that is instantly synthesized, elegantly expressed, and potentially empty of the appropriated understanding that genuine formation requires.

Using AI does not inherently violate the IPP. Use is judged by context, purpose, transparency, learning, relationship, responsibility, and consequence. Delegating essential judgment or counterfeiting human care, however, *is* inconsistent with the paradigm.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

## Applying the Five Dynamics to Generative AI

### 1. Context

Context in the IPP is not a one-time demographic preface; it is an ongoing inquiry into what makes learning possible, difficult, meaningful, risky, or distorted.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] For AI, contextual inquiry requires faculty to identify:

- the system's owner, data practices, and model limitations;
- access differences among students (device, bandwidth, digital literacy);
- disciplinary norms about AI use;
- environmental and labor costs of large-scale model inference;
- institutional policy;
- the pressures—financial, academic, social—that lead students or employees toward AI use.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

In online learning contexts, context already encompasses "the conditions under which students are using AI."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Unequal access to premium AI tools is itself a justice issue requiring contextual attention.

### 2. Experience

IPP experience is any direct or vicarious encounter with subject matter that the learner appropriates through the senses, intellect, imagination, memory, and affect—*cognitive grasp joined to affective response*.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] An AI output alone is not such an encounter; it is raw material that must be worked.

The SJU document recommends that faculty *let learners inspect outputs, compare models, verify citations, observe failure, document prompts and revisions, and encounter the people or realities that AI representations can obscure.* Equally important is preserving experiences—"conversation, practice, struggle, observation, creation"—whose formative value depends on human presence or accountable authorship.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

A key guardrail: exposure alone is not experience in the IPP sense. An unexamined AI output, like an unexamined service day, may confirm prejudice or produce the *appearance* of understanding without its substance.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

### 3. Reflection

Reflection is the pivotal dynamic connecting experience to responsible action. It is epistemic, affective, ethical, social, and—when appropriate—spiritual.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Applied to AI outputs, the SJU document proposes that students examine:

- what the system made visible or invisible;
- whose language and assumptions it reproduced;
- where fluency masqueraded as truth;
- what cognitive work was delegated to the machine;
- how use affected confidence, agency, relationship, and responsibility.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

The disciplined sequence of reflection questions (attention → interpretation → interior response → ethical and social meaning → discernment) maps directly onto AI critique:[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

| Reflection level | AI-focused question |
|---|---|
| **Attention** | What did the model actually produce? What did it omit or distort? |
| **Interpretation** | What patterns, training biases, or conceptual gaps explain the output? |
| **Interior response** | Does this output make me feel confident in a way that may not be warranted? |
| **Ethical and social meaning** | Whose voices are underrepresented? Who bears the risks of error? |
| **Discernment** | Should I use, revise, reject, or disclose this output—and why? |

Reflection becomes thin when it asks only "How did you feel?" It becomes coercive when the instructor predetermines the acceptable conclusion.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Both failures are possible in AI-focused reflection: students may perform AI skepticism without genuine inquiry, or faculty may treat distrust of AI as the only acceptable outcome. The standard is disciplined openness to truth.

### 4. Action

In the IPP, action begins as an interior choice—a judgment, commitment, or revised priority—and may become outward conduct. Action is irresponsible if it bypasses reflection; reflection is incomplete if it produces understanding without considering response.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

For AI, responsible action may include:

- revising, verifying, rejecting, or disclosing an AI-assisted product;
- citing the AI tool and documenting the human judgment applied to it;
- constraining deployment based on identified risks;
- *non-use* as a reasoned, principled choice.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

At the faculty and institutional level, action may mean redesigning assessment, reconsidering procurement, improving access equity, establishing escalation protocols, or revising policy.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Action must be proportionate to the learner's role, knowledge, and power—students cannot be asked to solve AI governance problems they lack authority to address.

### 5. Evaluation

Evaluation in the IPP judges academic learning, quality of judgment, action and impact, and educational design—and returns the learner and educator to a newly understood context.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] For AI, this means assessing:

- the final work *and* the quality of the human process: verification, attribution, judgment, revision, privacy protection, and consequence;
- whether the technology improved access and learning or displaced essential practice.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Evaluation should look for differential effects—AI tools may benefit some students and disadvantage others—and for unintended burdens such as over-reliance, deskilling, or surveillance.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## The Relational Boundary: What AI Cannot Do

The SJU document draws a firm line: "AI can assist the relationship; it must not counterfeit it." Systems can help educators identify patterns, prepare options, translate materials, and offer low-stakes practice. They cannot bear care, mutual obligation, fiduciary responsibility, or moral accountability.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Students should know:
- when they are interacting with a machine;
- how decisions are escalated to a human;
- which matters will never be delegated without meaningful human review.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

This applies with particular force in advising and mentoring: "AI tools may assist scheduling, information retrieval, and option comparison, but consequential conversations require identifiable human responsibility and escalation."[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

## Academic Integrity in an AI-Enabled Classroom

The IPP does not offer a list of permitted and prohibited AI uses. It offers a process for reasoning about them. Key principles from the SJU document include:

**Integrity as reflective authorship.** The IPP holds that action must be discerned and accountable. Submitting AI-generated text as one's own thinking bypasses the reflection and interior appropriation that make action genuinely one's own.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

**Assessment design, not surveillance.** "AI-resilient assessment is not synonymous with surveillance or return to high-stakes exams." IPP-informed design favors significant experience, staged work, oral explanation, process evidence, local and personal application without compulsory disclosure, collaborative critique, and revision. "The goal is to make thinking and judgment visible and to preserve productive intellectual friction."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

**Transparency as a norm.** Disclosure, citation, and honest documentation of AI assistance are forms of responsible action consistent with the paradigm. Hiding AI use violates the transparency that truthful reflection and honest evaluation require.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

**Freedom of conscience, not moral surveillance.** "Faculty may assess the quality of ethical analysis, reflective practice, professional responsibility, collaboration, perspective-taking, and evidence-based action when these are legitimate learning outcomes. They should not claim to measure the state of a student's conscience, compassion, faith, political virtue, or human worth."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Academic integrity standards should focus on observable behaviors—disclosure, process documentation, verification—not on policing a student's moral character.

## Helping Students Reflect Critically on AI Outputs and Their Biases

The reflection dynamic provides the most direct guidance. Several practices from the SJU document are directly applicable:

- **Analytic memos** documenting prompts, model outputs, and the human revisions made;
- **Comparison of model outputs** on contested or culturally inflected questions;
- **Error analysis**: identifying where the model hallucinated, omitted, or systematically distorted;
- **Positionality statements**: examining whose knowledge traditions the model was trained on and whose are absent;
- **Ethical matrices**: mapping who benefits and who bears risk from a given AI output or deployment;
- **Adapted examen of learning**: a structured pause asking what the AI made easy and what it may have foreclosed.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

The guardrail is that reflection must remain "open to truth rather than manufacture agreement."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] A faculty member who treats skepticism of AI as the only acceptable outcome is engaging in the same coercive reflection the IPP forbids in every other domain.

## Responsible Human Action When AI Is Part of Learning

The IPP's account of action is twofold: interior choice and outward conduct. Both are required. The SJU document's list of possible actions—including *non-use* as a reasoned choice—reflects the paradigm's insistence that action be discerned, proportionate, and accountable rather than reflexive.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Responsible human action in an AI-shaped learning environment looks like:

1. **Verification**: checking AI outputs against authoritative sources before relying on them;
2. **Attribution**: citing AI tools used and describing the human judgment applied;
3. **Boundary-setting**: identifying tasks where AI assistance serves learning and tasks where it displaces it;
4. **Escalation**: knowing when to bring AI-flagged concerns to a human with appropriate authority;
5. **Principled refusal**: declining to use AI where its use would undermine the learning purpose, harm a community, or obscure authorship;
6. **Institutional action**: faculty and leaders redesigning assessment, policy, and procurement when AI changes the conditions of learning.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

The IPP requires that action be related to the learner's role, knowledge, power, and responsibilities—distinguishing "urgency from haste; intent from impact; charity from justice; service for others from solidarity with others."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## Institutional Implications

The SJU document's eighth institutional commitment is *institutional congruence*: "policies, resources, incentives, and technologies support the educational commitments they proclaim."[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] An AI policy that simply bans or simply permits is insufficient; an IPP-informed institution asks what its AI-related arrangements cause people to experience, value, and do.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

For AI specifically, institutional IPP-informed practice means:
- transparent procurement decisions that include contextual, relational, and ethical analysis;
- access equity so that AI tools do not advantage only well-resourced students;
- faculty formation that builds genuine AI literacy, not performative compliance;
- evaluation of AI-related educational designs for differential effects and unintended burdens.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## Summary: IPP Design Checklist for AI-Integrated Courses

| Dynamic | Key AI-focused questions |
|---|---|
| **Context** | Who has access? What are the system's limits and data practices? What pressures shape use? |
| **Experience** | Do students inspect, test, and verify outputs? Are formatively essential human experiences preserved? |
| **Reflection** | Can students examine bias, delegation of cognition, and consequences of AI use? |
| **Action** | Is disclosure, verification, revision, or refusal a graded expectation? |
| **Evaluation** | Does assessment capture human process and judgment, not only the polished product? |
