# Mission Priority Examen and Self-Evaluation Processes

## Overview

The Mission Priority Examen (MPE) is a comprehensive institutional process designed to help Jesuit colleges and universities reaffirm their Jesuit Catholic identity and establish strategic priorities for their educational missions. It serves as a mechanism for honest and inclusive discernment across entire campus communities, inviting diverse stakeholders to reflect on institutional strengths and areas for growth.

## What is the Mission Priority Examen?

The Mission Priority Examen is an opportunity for Jesuit colleges and universities to reaffirm their Jesuit Catholic identity in a way that honors the voices and experiences of each member of the community.[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5] Initiated by the Superior General of the Society of Jesus and coordinated by a joint committee of Provincials (regional leaders of the Jesuits) and University Presidents within the Association of Jesuit Colleges and Universities, the process is designed to communicate to the Vatican institutional responses to fundamental questions.[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5]

These central questions are: Do you want to continue to be a Jesuit, Catholic University? If so, what are the two to four mission goals (and accompanying strategies) that you will prioritize for the next few years?[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5]

## The Examen as Discernment Process

The process is called an examen because it is intended to foster honest and inclusive discernment, inviting the entire institutional community to name strengths and successes in living out the mission as well as struggles, failings, and goals.[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5] The Characteristics of Jesuit Higher Education document serves as a reference and guide for the MPE.[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5]

## Institutional Governance Support

The Mission Priority Examen provides a structured opportunity for Jesuit colleges and universities to undertake serious discussions about how the campus community understands its mission; establishes goals for its future; and realizes those goals in the years to come.[^chunk:885dda4e-4d13-5a28-a7a1-1d96cb56ba36]

The process engages broad institutional participation. At Loyola University Chicago, for example, in the 2018-2019 academic year, more than 90 small groups across all campuses came together to explore and share how the institution lives its Jesuit mission in contemporary times.[^chunk:b66a1805-9802-5205-9938-5543ea9d81c9] Nearly 800 students, faculty, and staff participated in this comprehensive self-evaluation effort.[^chunk:b66a1805-9802-5205-9938-5543ea9d81c9]

## Outcomes and Institutional Benefits

### Georgetown University (2023-2024)

Georgetown University conducted its Mission Priority Examen throughout the fall of 2023.[^chunk:885dda4e-4d13-5a28-a7a1-1d96cb56ba36] The process included structured feedback opportunities, with all faculty and staff invited to provide input to the Mission Priority Examen Steering Committee.[^chunk:885dda4e-4d13-5a28-a7a1-1d96cb56ba36]

### Loyola University Chicago (2018-2019)

At Loyola University Chicago, the community input from the Mission Priority Examen process was collected in the institution's Mission Priority Self-Study, providing a current picture of work in advancing the Jesuit educational mission.[^chunk:b66a1805-9802-5205-9938-5543ea9d81c9] The self-study indicated where there were opportunities to deepen institutional work and respond to the challenges of the future.[^chunk:b66a1805-9802-5205-9938-5543ea9d81c9]

From the Loyola process, priorities emerged around mission structure, programming, assessment and hiring.[^chunk:b66a1805-9802-5205-9938-5543ea9d81c9] These priorities provided a framework and direction for the institution's 2025 strategic planning process.[^chunk:b66a1805-9802-5205-9938-5543ea9d81c9]

### University of San Francisco (2022)

The University of San Francisco completed its Mission Priority Examen in 2022, with results affirmed by Father General Arturo Sosa, SJ.[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5] The following Mission Priorities guided the university for the coming seven years:[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5]

1. Build capacity and opportunity among faculty, staff, students, and leaders to understand and engage in substantive conversations about Jesuit Catholic identity in the context of higher education, to share worldviews and visions for how the university can embody a faith that does justice and ensure institutional accountability.[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5]

2. Develop a transparent, sustainable, university-wide plan to strengthen Jesuit mission with diversity, equity and inclusion priorities, supported by a well-resourced, visible structure to ensure institutional accountability.[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5]

3. Strengthen the Office of Sustainability so that it promotes cross-disciplinary faculty participation in shaping the curriculum, motivating university-wide engagement, and ensuring institutional accountability in meeting bold science-driven sustainability goals inspired by Laudato si.[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5]

4. Cultivate collaborative structures among university stakeholders that model cura personalis, practice Ignatian discernment, and respect the legitimate autonomy of the several unions that represent subsets of employees.[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5]

## How Self-Evaluation Supports Mission Institutional Identity

The comprehensive nature of these self-evaluation processes supports institutional governance by creating structured opportunities for the entire community to reflect on mission and identity. Documentation of outcomes—including self-study reports and peer visiting committee assessments—provides institutional leaders with clear guidance for strategic planning and resource allocation.[^chunk:b66a1805-9802-5205-9938-5543ea9d81c9] The process ensures that mission priorities are grounded in authentic community discernment rather than administrative decisions alone, strengthening institutional accountability to both internal stakeholders and the broader Jesuit educational community.
