## Overview

Jesuit education is grounded in a distinctive set of foundational documents and pedagogical approaches that have evolved over five centuries to form educators and students rooted in Ignatian spirituality.[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210] These documents serve as guides for discernment and renewal, helping Jesuit schools worldwide maintain their identity while responding to contemporary challenges and opportunities.

## The 28 Characteristics of Jesuit Education (1986)

The *Characteristics of Jesuit Education* was published in 1986 following discussions that began in September 1980 among an international group focused on secondary education.[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210] Superior General Peter Hans Kolvenbach promulgated the document on December 8, 1986, describing its purpose as helping those working in Jesuit education "exercise the essential task of apostolic discernment." He stated the document could "be the basis for renewed reflection on the experience of the educational apostolate and, in light of that reflection, for evaluation of school policies and practices: not only negatively ('What are we doing wrong'), but especially positively ('How can we do better?')."[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210]

The document contains **28 Characteristics of Jesuit Education organized in 9 sections**, highlighting Ignatius's spiritual experience as the ultimate source of Jesuit education.[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210] Kolvenbach emphasized that individual countries or regions should reflect on the meaning of these characteristics for their own local situations and develop supplementary documents applying the universal document to specific needs.[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210]

## Ignatian Pedagogy: A Practical Approach (1993)

Seven years after the publication of the *Characteristics*, on July 31, 1993, Father Kolvenbach promulgated *Ignatian Pedagogy: A Practical Approach*, which aimed to complement and build upon the *Characteristics* document.[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210] Recognizing the diverse settings of Jesuit schools, Ignatian Pedagogy put forth a flexible approach based on broad principles from the Spiritual Exercises.[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210]

Aimed at teachers, the document recommends a **five-dimension pedagogical style**:[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210]

1. **Context**: Possessing an astute understanding of the context in which teaching takes place
2. **Experience**: Creating experiences that stretch the intellect and imagination of students
3. **Reflection**: Fostering an ongoing habit of reflection to go deeper into any experience
4. **Action**: Providing opportunities to put the values and skills acquired into action, so that some positive transformation in the learner, the teacher, and the world happens—in service to the Gospel, as Saint Ignatius claims love is shown more in deeds than in words
5. **Evaluation**: Engaging in rich, multifaceted assessments of teaching and learning, coupled with a willingness to change and modify as appropriate[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210]

This five-dimension approach became "a pedagogical staple for many generations of Jesuit educators" working to continue the living tradition of Jesuit education, especially in the crucial space where Jesuit education happens: the classroom.[^chunk:a6f5e553-0b1a-56f3-91a9-328a958a6180]

## Jesuit Schools: A Living Tradition (2019)

*Jesuit Schools: A Living Tradition in the 21st Century — An Ongoing Exercise of Discernment* was published in September 2019 by the International Commission on the Apostolate of Jesuit Education (ICAJE).[^chunk:15dc6111-23dd-5ee9-9743-fde6f85f1b10] This document continues the tradition of assisting the educational apostolate in reflecting and discerning the particular challenges and opportunities of the contemporary world.[^chunk:15dc6111-23dd-5ee9-9743-fde6f85f1b10]

Unlike its predecessor documents, *A Living Tradition* is intentionally designed as an unfinished, living text that invites educators to enrich it with their reflections, experiences, and contributions.[^chunk:a6f5e553-0b1a-56f3-91a9-328a958a6180] This approach models the discernment it aims to inspire and reflects the accelerating pace of change in contemporary society.[^chunk:a6f5e553-0b1a-56f3-91a9-328a958a6180]

### Structure and Three Framing Questions

*A Living Tradition* is structured around three foundational questions echoing the Spiritual Exercises, reflecting what Saint Ignatius invites retreatants to contemplate before the cross:[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210]

- **What have we done for Christ?** Looking back over the past thirty years of profound changes, the document ponders the ways that the mission of Jesuit education has been renewed in documents and new initiatives.[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210]
- **What are we doing for Christ?** The document examines current realities in the world and in schools, with links to contemporary statistics, documents, and resources.[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210]
- **What shall we do for Christ?** The document proposes that essential identifiers must now be part of every Jesuit school worldwide, regardless of local condition or context.[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210]

## The Ten Global Identifiers of Jesuit Schools

*A Living Tradition* presents **10 key identifiers that should be present in all Jesuit schools**, establishing commitments every Jesuit school can make regardless of context:[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210]

1. **Catholic**: Committed to in-depth faith formation in dialogue with other religions and worldviews[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210]
2. **Safe and Healthy**: Committed to creating a safe and healthy school environment for all[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210]
3. **Global Citizenship**: Committed to global citizenship[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210]
4. **Care of Creation**: Committed to the care of all creation[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210]
5. **Justice**: Committed to justice[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210]
6. **Accessibility**: Committed to be accessible for all[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210]
7. **Interculturality**: Committed to interculturality[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210]

## Human Excellence in Jesuit Education

Jesuit education aims to develop women and men of **four integrated qualities** that reflect the human excellence the Society of Jesus seeks for the youth entrusted to its care:[^chunk:9de43838-db1d-5aba-806c-c95f66998307]

- **Conscience**: Knowledge and experience of society and its imbalances, coupled with the ability to internalize and cultivate a spiritual life
- **Compassion**: The capacity to open one's heart, be in solidarity with others, and assume their suffering
- **Commitment**: Honest striving toward faith and, through peaceful means, working for social and political transformation to achieve justice
- **Competence**: The ability to create, understand, and use knowledge and skills to live in one's context and transform it; to be part of a changing, diverse world; and to develop the intellectual, academic, emotional, and social skills required for professional and human achievement[^chunk:9de43838-db1d-5aba-806c-c95f66998307]

## The Universal Apostolic Preferences (2019–2029)

On February 19, 2019, Father General Arturo Sosa promulgated the *Universal Apostolic Preferences of the Society of Jesus, 2019–2029*, which guide all works of the Society for ten years.[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210] These four universal preferences confirm and strengthen the descriptions, challenges, and calls to action in *A Living Tradition*:[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210]

1. **To walk with the poor, the outcasts of the world, those whose dignity has been violated**, in a mission of reconciliation and justice
2. **To show the way to God** through the Spiritual Exercises and discernment
3. **To collaborate in the care of our Common Home**
4. **To accompany the young** in the creation of a hope-filled future[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210]

## EducateMagis: The Global Network Platform

*A Living Tradition* is available in print and as an interactive webpage located at **EducateMagis**, an online community that connects educators from Jesuit and Ignatian schools worldwide.[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210] At the end of each section of the webpage, educators can enrich *A Living Tradition* through interactive conversations, videos, photographs, and links, reflecting the rich diversity of the six Jesuit conferences: Africa and Madagascar, Asia Pacific, Europe, Latin America, North America, and South Asia.[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210]

The establishment of EducateMagis in 2015 created an interactive online community serving as a platform for interactions among students and educators in Jesuit schools around the world.[^chunk:229bf754-4ddc-56eb-ae1e-d2673e3212c6]

## Service of Faith and Promotion of Justice

Ignatian spirituality holds polarities in tension in service of a more loving and just world: contemplation and action, rights and responsibilities.[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210] Jesuit education is about educating students to share the perspective of the Trinity looking upon the world and seeking to make it more loving and just.[^chunk:1ec06229-98d0-597a-b622-069fc2aac210] This gift—rooted in the Spiritual Exercises—is what Jesuit schools offer to the next generation.

The mission of education within Jesuit schools is fundamentally structured around **reconciliation and justice**.[^chunk:a6f5e553-0b1a-56f3-91a9-328a958a6180] Schools are understood as privileged spaces to fulfill this mission within the framework of the four Universal Apostolic Preferences for 2019–2029.[^chunk:a6f5e553-0b1a-56f3-91a9-328a958a6180]

## Living Tradition as Ongoing Discernment

The title *A Living Tradition* reflects the document's purpose: to renew the living tradition in education as an ongoing exercise of discernment.[^chunk:15dc6111-23dd-5ee9-9743-fde6f85f1b10] The document does not replace the *Characteristics* or the *Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm*; rather, it complements and updates them.[^chunk:15dc6111-23dd-5ee9-9743-fde6f85f1b10] The three should be taken together as the contemporary framework of Jesuit education.

Like the Spiritual Exercises itself, *A Living Tradition* is not meant for reading once or twice over but is intended to become a living text that will change over time according to feedback and discernment it initiates.[^chunk:9de43838-db1d-5aba-806c-c95f66998307] It should guide dynamic and deep conversations in Jesuit schools and other institutions within educational networks, with conversations gradually incorporated within the text.

## Recent Milestones in the Global Jesuit Educational Conversation

The contemporary renewal of Jesuit education has been marked by significant global gatherings:[^chunk:229bf754-4ddc-56eb-ae1e-d2673e3212c6]

- **2012**: The first International Colloquium in Jesuit Secondary Education in Boston brought together educators worldwide to articulate a unifying vision and celebrate the network's rich diversity
- **2014**: SIPEI (Seminario Internacional de Pedagogía y Espiritualidad Ignacianas) gathered leaders to reflect upon the four C's (conscience, competence, compassion, and commitment)
- **2015**: The establishment of EducateMagis
- **2017**: The Congress JESEDU-Rio in Rio de Janeiro, producing the Action Statement for the network
- **2020**: The Second Colloquium JESEDU-Jogja was planned to introduce *A Living Tradition* for virtual colloquium experience[^chunk:229bf754-4ddc-56eb-ae1e-d2673e3212c6]
