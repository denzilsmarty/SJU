# Institutional Leadership and Faculty Formation Programs

Jesuit institutions sustain their educational mission across generations through comprehensive programs designed to develop faculty and staff in Ignatian pedagogy and leadership. These initiatives create structured pathways for educators to deepen their understanding of Jesuit values and transform their teaching practice.

## Ignatian Pedagogy as a Teaching Framework

Ignatian pedagogy represents a 500-year-old Jesuit teaching model rooted in the Spiritual Exercises of Saint Ignatius of Loyola[^chunk:243f0d86-7093-5485-847d-f4cb4538f91f]. Rather than focusing solely on content delivery, this approach emphasizes the formation of the whole person through five interconnected elements: context, experience, reflection, action, and evaluation[^chunk:372ad930-d08b-5946-a15f-9b1161584cf9].

The framework operates on the principle that Jesuit education goes beyond the mere transmission of information from professor to student, instead creating an experience that affects students on cognitive, emotional, and behavioral levels[^chunk:243f0d86-7093-5485-847d-f4cb4538f91f]. The goal is to develop "persons for others"—well-rounded, intellectually competent individuals committed to justice and service[^chunk:0d07887a-c766-5f47-8381-77a42ac2fc83].

### The Five Elements of Ignatian Pedagogy

**Context**: Faculty are encouraged to understand their students as whole persons, including their backgrounds, prior knowledge, lived experiences, identities, motivations, and challenges[^chunk:372ad930-d08b-5946-a15f-9b1161584cf9]. This principle of *cura personalis* (personal care and concern for the individual) requires educators to become conversant with learners' life experiences and the dynamics of their classroom environment[^chunk:243f0d86-7093-5485-847d-f4cb4538f91f].

**Experience**: Rather than passive reception of information, Ignatian pedagogy invites students into active engagement with ideas, people, texts, communities, and problems[^chunk:372ad930-d08b-5946-a15f-9b1161584cf9]. This involves intellectual, emotional, and imaginative participation that helps students connect academic content to their own values and emerging sense of purpose[^chunk:372ad930-d08b-5946-a15f-9b1161584cf9].

**Reflection**: Through reflection, students consider the meaning and implications of their learning, asking deeper questions about significance, personal understanding, and emerging responsibilities[^chunk:372ad930-d08b-5946-a15f-9b1161584cf9]. Reflection serves as the connective element that holds the pedagogical framework together[^chunk:372ad930-d08b-5946-a15f-9b1161584cf9].

**Action**: Reflection naturally leads to action, where students apply their learning in ways that promote growth, justice, service, and responsible engagement with the world[^chunk:372ad930-d08b-5946-a15f-9b1161584cf9]. This may involve civic engagement, ethical decision-making, advocacy, leadership, or changes in personal perspective and behavior[^chunk:372ad930-d08b-5946-a15f-9b1161584cf9].

**Evaluation**: Ignatian evaluation extends beyond exams and grades to assess students' development in critical thinking, self-awareness, ethical reasoning, and engagement with others[^chunk:372ad930-d08b-5946-a15f-9b1161584cf9]. It includes opportunities for students to recognize their progress and identify areas for growth[^chunk:372ad930-d08b-5946-a15f-9b1161584cf9].

## Institutional Programs Supporting Faculty Development

### Ignatian Leadership Program

Saint Joseph's University's Ignatian Leadership Program (ILP), founded in 2005, exemplifies how institutions structure faculty formation around Ignatian principles[^chunk:1c748c91-8e4f-537b-9a0d-d0edc5812051]. The program fosters the use of long-standing Ignatian principles—self-awareness, discernment, continuous learning, and concern for the common good—within the educational community[^chunk:1c748c91-8e4f-537b-9a0d-d0edc5812051].

The ILP operates through two distinct cohorts: administrators and faculty, each meeting monthly during the academic year[^chunk:1c748c91-8e4f-537b-9a0d-d0edc5812051]. Both groups participate in facilitated learning, discussion, and reflection to expand their leadership capacity, though with different focal points[^chunk:1c748c91-8e4f-537b-9a0d-d0edc5812051]. The administrative cohort learns how to lead and manage more effectively, while the faculty group examines the leadership dimensions of teaching, research, and scholarship from an Ignatian perspective as applied to academic community[^chunk:1c748c91-8e4f-537b-9a0d-d0edc5812051].

### Ignatian Pedagogy Workshop

The Ignatian Pedagogy Workshop represents another institutional approach to comprehensive faculty formation. At Saint Joseph's University, the 2026 Ignatian Pedagogy Workshop took place June 23-25th[^chunk:1c748c91-8e4f-537b-9a0d-d0edc5812051], providing faculty with intensive formation in this pedagogical model. Supporting this workshop, the university developed the Ignatian Classroom Discussion Guide, designed to help faculty integrate the Jesuit mission into classroom discussions, offering resources and materials for meaningful conversations with students[^chunk:1c748c91-8e4f-537b-9a0d-d0edc5812051].

### Specialized Faculty Development Programs

Multiple Jesuit institutions have established centers and programs dedicated to pedagogical transformation. Seattle University's Center for Faculty Development provides comprehensive resources on the Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm, including milestones throughout the term, syllabus templates, learning outcomes frameworks, and guidance on active learning and early feedback[^chunk:243f0d86-7093-5485-847d-f4cb4538f91f].

Marquette University offers extensive resources on Ignatian Pedagogy Tips for Faculty through its Center for Teaching, Research, and Learning[^chunk:0d07887a-c766-5f47-8381-77a42ac2fc83]. These materials provide practical implementation guidance for each element of the pedagogical framework, including sample syllabus statements that articulate how Jesuit education aims to transform how students view themselves, other people, social systems, and creation[^chunk:0d07887a-c766-5f47-8381-77a42ac2fc83].

### Business School Programs

Fairfield University's Charles F. Dolan School of Business developed the Dolan Ignatian Fellows Program specifically for business faculty[^chunk:0c6c0eee-5ee0-58cc-b61b-e7056fc4319e]. The inaugural cohort of eleven faculty members met regularly during the academic year to explore Ignatian pedagogy and discuss what it means to be a Jesuit business school[^chunk:0c6c0eee-5ee0-58cc-b61b-e7056fc4319e]. This program paired deeper study of the Ignatian Paradigm for Jesuit Business Education with collaborative scholarly work; participating faculty simultaneously developed research integrating Ignatian pedagogy into business curricula[^chunk:0c6c0eee-5ee0-58cc-b61b-e7056fc4319e].

## Supporting Infrastructure and Resources

Jesuit institutions maintain ongoing commitment through multiple support structures. At Saint Joseph's University, the Office of Mission oversees faculty and staff programs, maintaining a mailing list and offering one-time reflection opportunities and events throughout the academic year[^chunk:1c748c91-8e4f-537b-9a0d-d0edc5812051]. The university also offers a Mission Service Leave Policy, allowing eligible employees to participate in university-sponsored retreats, reflections, and approved mission-related service activities[^chunk:1c748c91-8e4f-537b-9a0d-d0edc5812051].

Institutions provide supplementary resources to extend faculty learning. Loyola University New Orleans curated resources specifically for its Loyola Week (October 18-24), inviting faculty to learn about Ignatian spirituality and integrate elements of Jesuit mission into courses[^chunk:372ad930-d08b-5946-a15f-9b1161584cf9]. These resources include recommended readings, videos, journal articles, and examples from across disciplines[^chunk:372ad930-d08b-5946-a15f-9b1161584cf9].

## Measuring Program Effectiveness

While the sources do not provide extensive data on formal assessment metrics, they indicate several approaches through which institutions evaluate outcomes:

**Faculty Scholarship and Application**: Programs encourage faculty to conduct research on their implementation. At Fairfield, program participants simultaneously developed scholarly work applying Ignatian pedagogy to their disciplines, such as research on teaching entrepreneurship within an Ignatian framework[^chunk:0c6c0eee-5ee0-58cc-b61b-e7056fc4319e].

**Reflective Practice Integration**: Institutions assess how faculty integrate reflection into courses through blogs, journals, and course evaluations[^chunk:0d07887a-c766-5f47-8381-77a42ac2fc83]. Faculty development programs encourage educators to evaluate their own learning and development alongside student learning[^chunk:243f0d86-7093-5485-847d-f4cb4538f91f].

**Formative Feedback**: The Ignatian pedagogical framework itself emphasizes continuous feedback and evaluation, with teaching practices using formative assessment creating regular opportunities to evaluate both instructor teaching and student learning[^chunk:243f0d86-7093-5485-847d-f4cb4538f91f].

**Community Impact and Belonging**: Program descriptions note that participants report enhanced sense of belonging and community, suggesting social and institutional cohesion as outcomes[^chunk:0c6c0eee-5ee0-58cc-b61b-e7056fc4319e].

## Sustaining Institutional Commitment

Jesuit institutions sustain their pedagogical commitments through several mechanisms:

**Cyclical Programming**: Programs operate on recurring schedules (monthly meetings, annual workshops, rolling cohorts), ensuring continuous engagement rather than one-time interventions[^chunk:1c748c91-8e4f-537b-9a0d-d0edc5812051].

**Faculty Leadership**: Institutional programs are typically co-led by faculty and administrators, ensuring peer mentorship and distributed leadership capacity[^chunk:0c6c0eee-5ee0-58cc-b61b-e7056fc4319e].

**Connection to Mission**: All programs explicitly link pedagogical practices to institutional mission, helping faculty understand how their teaching contributes to broader institutional values[^chunk:372ad930-d08b-5946-a15f-9b1161584cf9].

**Resources and Support**: Institutions provide concrete tools—syllabus templates, discussion guides, sample assignments—that make Ignatian pedagogy accessible and practical for faculty across disciplines[^chunk:1c748c91-8e4f-537b-9a0d-d0edc5812051].
