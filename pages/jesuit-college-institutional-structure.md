## Overview

Jesuit colleges operated under a hierarchical administrative structure designed to ensure effective governance while maintaining the spiritual mission of the Society of Jesus. The rector served as the chief executive, with authority distributed among several key officials including chancellors, secretaries, and consultors, while ultimate oversight remained with the Provincial and the General of the Society.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb]

## Key Administrative Roles

### The Rector

The rector held complete charge of the supervision and government of the university or college.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb] He could be the same person who governed the principal college of the Society and was required to possess qualities enabling him to direct the institution in both learning and habits of conduct.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb] The task of selecting the rector belonged to the General of the Society or someone to whom he entrusted it, such as the provincial or a visitor, though the General always retained confirmation authority.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb]

The rector could not change principal lecturers or officials such as the chancellor without informing the provincial or the General if he was nearer, unless the higher superior had specifically entrusted the matter to him.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb] This requirement ensured accountability up the hierarchical chain.

### The Chancellor

The chancellor served as a person distinguished for learning and great zeal, capable of judging wisely in matters entrusted to him.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb] His primary duty was to act as general representative of the rector in carefully organizing studies, directing disputations in public acts, and judging the competence of students to be admitted to public examinations and degrees.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb] The chancellor himself conferred the degrees.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb] If the rector possessed sufficient capability, the functions of rector and chancellor could be vested in one person.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb]

### The Secretary

The secretary, who must be a member of the Society, maintained a register of all students in regular attendance and received their promise to obey the rector and observe the Constitutions.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb] He kept the seal of the rector and of the university, performing these duties without cost to students.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb]

### The Notary

A notary provided public certification to degrees and other matters that occurred in the institution.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb]

### The Beadles

Two or three beadles served the university, one in the faculty of languages, another in arts, and another in theology.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb] These beadles were not members of the Society but received good salaries due to the volume of their work, and one of them could serve as the corrector.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb]

### The Dean

Each of the three faculties (languages, arts, and theology) had a dean and two other representatives chosen from those who better understood the affairs of their faculty.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb] When summoned by the rector, they could advise him on matters advantageous to their faculty's welfare, and they should inform the rector of such matters even without being summoned.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb]

### The Syndic (or General Censor)

A general syndic provided information to the rector, provincial, and General about persons and things deemed noteworthy, and was required to be a person of great fidelity and judgment.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb] In addition to this general syndic, the rector had particular syndics who reported on matters in each class requiring attention.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb]

### The Consultors

The rector had four consultors or assistants who aided him in general matters pertaining to his office and discussed matters of importance with him.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb] One of these could be a collateral associate if deemed necessary by the superior general.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb] If personnel was insufficient for all these positions, the best possible arrangement should be made.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb]

## Responsibilities of the Rector

The rector bore extensive responsibilities for the operation and governance of the college or university. He supervised and governed the entire institution, ensuring its direction in both learning and conduct.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb]

The rector was responsible for ensuring that newcomers were examined and placed in appropriate classes with suitable teachers.[^chunk:369cc494-fa1e-543f-827d-e5b13f8e18da] After hearing counsel from those deputed for this purpose, he decided whether students should remain longer in the same class or advance to another.[^chunk:369cc494-fa1e-543f-827d-e5b13f8e18da] He determined whether the study of languages other than Latin should precede or follow the arts and theology, and how long each student should study these languages.[^chunk:369cc494-fa1e-543f-827d-e5b13f8e18da]

The rector consulted with faculty deans and deputies on matters pertaining to individual faculties, and when matters affected all faculties, he consulted representatives of all of them.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb] Although the decision did not depend upon their votes, it was proper to consult and hear them, and the rector should give fitting account of the opinion of those more cognizant.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb] However, if all consulted had an opinion contrary to his, he should not proceed against them all without consulting the provincial.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb]

The rector bore responsibility for student discipline. When students failed to show required diligence in studies or good conduct and kind words and admonitions did not suffice, the rector could employ a corrector (who was not a member of the Society) to maintain discipline.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb] If a student proved incorrigible and a scandal to others, the rector could dismiss him from classes rather than allow him to harm the institution.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb]

The rector determined the proper amount and times of relaxation from academic work according to the circumstances of persons and places.[^chunk:369cc494-fa1e-543f-827d-e5b13f8e18da]

## Authority Structure: Rectors, Provincials, and the General

The authority structure of Jesuit colleges reflected a clear hierarchical chain of command within the Society of Jesus.

### Authority of the Rector

The rector possessed primary authority over the college or university, holding complete charge of its supervision and government.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb] However, this authority was not absolute; it was exercised within limits set by higher superiors.

### Authority of the Provincial

The provincial held significant oversight authority. The rector was required to inform the provincial about principal lecturers and major personnel changes, or the General if he was nearer, unless the higher superior had already entrusted such matters to the rector.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb] The rector was expected to keep the higher superior informed about all matters.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb]

When the rector faced significant decisions where all his consulted advisors held opinions contrary to his own, he was required to consult the provincial before proceeding.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb]

Reporting structures flowed through the provincial. The collateral associate, syndic, and board of consultors wrote about the rector and others once a year to the superior general and twice to the provincial.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb] The provincial then informed the General about whatever seemed appropriate.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb]

### Authority of the General

The General of the Society held ultimate authority over college governance. The task of selecting the rector belonged to the General or someone to whom he entrusted it, such as the provincial or a visitor, but the General always retained confirmation of the appointment.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb]

The rector wrote to the General about teachers and other persons of the Society.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb] The General received reports from multiple sources: the admonitor, censor, and board of consultors wrote about the rector and all others once every year to the General, and twice to the provincial.[^chunk:46647514-842e-5cac-98cd-c89c2ac4c108] When the General or provincial desired more complete information, not only should the collateral associate, syndic, and board of consultors write about the rector and all others, but each of the teachers and approved scholastics as well as formed coadjutors should write their opinions about all of them, including the rector.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb]

The purpose of this extensive reporting system was to proceed in everything with greater circumspection and care to ensure each person did his duty.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb]

### Financial Oversight

The General retained significant authority over college finances and property. The rector took charge of administering and economically managing the temporalities (temporal affairs and possessions) of the college.[^chunk:5637c890-3421-5def-9821-8581bf652557] The rector kept account of the entire establishment to provide it at any time to whomever the General might appoint.[^chunk:5637c890-3421-5def-9821-8581bf652557] However, the General could not apply the temporal goods of the colleges to his own use, that of his relatives, or of the Professed Society, ensuring that superintendence of college property tended purely toward the greater glory and service of God.[^chunk:5637c890-3421-5def-9821-8581bf652557]

## Consultative Governance

While the rector held primary authority, the governance structure incorporated extensive consultation. The rector maintained consultors or assistants who aided him in matters pertaining to his office and with whom he discussed matters of importance.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb] For matters pertaining to one faculty alone, the rector consulted the chancellor and his assistants, along with the dean and deputies of the involved faculty.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb] For matters affecting all faculties, he consulted the deans and deputies of all faculties.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb]

If it seemed wise to the rector, he could also consult others from within and without the Society to learn the opinions of all and thereby decide better upon what was expedient.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb]

Though the decision did not depend upon the votes of those consulted, they should be consulted and heard, with the rector taking fitting account of the opinions of those more cognizant.[^chunk:402cf4e2-2287-519a-8f91-f94d212311bb]
