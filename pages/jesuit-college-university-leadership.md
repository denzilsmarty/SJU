## Overview

Leadership and governance in Jesuit colleges and universities involve a complex interplay of roles and responsibilities distributed across institutional presidents, Jesuit community rectors, boards of trustees, and the broader Society of Jesus. This structure reflects the institutions' identity as both independent universities and apostolic works of the Catholic Church and the Society of Jesus.

## Presidents as Directors of the Apostolic Work

University presidents serve as "Directors of the Apostolic Work" within Jesuit institutions.[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730] This role carries significant responsibility for ensuring the institution maintains its Catholic and Jesuit character while operating as an independent academic enterprise. Presidents are appointed by the board of trustees and may be either Jesuits or lay Catholics committed to the mission.[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

When a president is appointed—whether Jesuit or lay—the provincial should formally "mission" this person to the Jesuit apostolic aspect of their responsibility on behalf of the Society of Jesus.[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730] This public "missioning" has proven meaningful for both Jesuit and lay presidents, offering support and clarifying the connection between the presidency and the broader apostolic mission of the Society.

## The Rector and Religious Community Leadership

The rector serves as the religious superior of individual Jesuits and of the Jesuit community at the college or university (or a delegate of the rector in instances where the rector is not involved in the institution).[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730] The rector's role differs fundamentally from that of the president and is not a university administrative position. Rather, the rector leads the community in its corporate animation of the apostolate and its initiatives as a community within the college or university.[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

Key responsibilities of the rector include:
- Supporting each Jesuit in his apostolic work
- Helping the community discern common apostolic initiatives
- Leading the community in hospitality toward university colleagues and students
- Helping decide how to promote vocations to the Society of Jesus
- Representing and articulating the Society's apostolic priorities and commitments[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

The apostolic assistance of the rector is likely to increase as fewer presidents are themselves Jesuits, making the rector's role as animator of the Jesuit community increasingly significant.[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

## Board of Trustees and Institutional Governance

The board of trustees holds comprehensive responsibility for the policy, governance, and operation of each college and university, as established by articles of incorporation and bylaws.[^chunk:03ee3a20-dcf0-5cae-84e8-e9cdc45777fd] Each institution is legally and functionally independent of the Society of Jesus and its provinces.[^chunk:03ee3a20-dcf0-5cae-84e8-e9cdc45777fd]

The board has specific fiduciary responsibilities:
- Holding in trust what enables the institution to be what it is
- Most importantly, holding in trust the Catholic, Jesuit character of the college or university
- Understanding, assuring, and providing resources to support, foster, and assess this character[^chunk:03ee3a20-dcf0-5cae-84e8-e9cdc45777fd]

Boards have enhanced Jesuit institutions through better management, sounder financial status, higher academic quality, and fuller realization of being distinctively Catholic, Jesuit universities.[^chunk:03ee3a20-dcf0-5cae-84e8-e9cdc45777fd]

## Communication Between Leaders and Provincials

Regular, open, and trusting communication among presidents, rectors, and provincials is essential for effective governance.[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730] This communication may address:
- The work of individual Jesuits
- New opportunities for Jesuit involvement and assignment
- Ideas for how the community might help animate the Jesuit character
- Ways the college or university can recognize and promote the community's identity
- Promotion of vocations to the Society of Jesus
- Mutual assistance and support[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

It is critical that this dialogue remain clearly separate from the rector's relationship with the president as a religious superior with a member of the community.[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

The provincial should not be a stranger to the university but be welcomed and known within it.[^chunk:03ee3a20-dcf0-5cae-84e8-e9cdc45777fd] Each fiduciary board and president should have open dialogue with the provincial regularly initiated by the university and as requested by the provincial.[^chunk:03ee3a20-dcf0-5cae-84e8-e9cdc45777fd]

## Distinguishing Roles: Vice President for Mission and Ministry vs. Rector

Open communication must clarify the distinction between the institutional role of the Vice President for Mission and Ministry (or similar positions) and the role of the rector as religious superior.[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730] While the Vice President for Mission and Ministry is an official of the university, the rector is not and operates outside ordinary university structures and governance.[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

## Lay Apostolic Partnership and Shared Leadership

Jesuits comprise less than five percent of the total number of faculty, staff, and administration in Jesuit colleges and universities, making lay apostolic partnership central to institutional operation.[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021] Leadership of institutions—whether as deans, directors of programs, or central administration—is overwhelmingly exercised by lay persons, and fewer presidents in the future will be Jesuits.[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]

Boards themselves are a critical part of the reality of lay apostolic partnership.[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021] This evolution calls for special initiatives to ensure the Jesuit character of institutions while representing an opportunity for new apostolic vitality in the future.[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]

Each college and university has created responsibilities, structures, and programs for hiring, orienting, and developing faculty and staff according to Catholic and Jesuit mission.[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021] These include special retreats, seminars in Ignatian spirituality and Jesuit education, programs and colloquia enhancing Catholic and Jesuit identity, development opportunities, service experiences, and special mission-focused events.[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]

Some institutions have established special institutes of Jesuit and Catholic studies, and many participate in regional and national programs such as the AJCU Seminar on Higher Education Leadership and the Ignatian Colleagues Program.[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]

## Presidential Selection and Provincial Involvement

Proincials play an important role in the process by which the board chooses a college or university president.[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730] Regular communication between the provincial and the university, especially its board, is foundational for this involvement. The provincial's ongoing knowledge of the university allows him to:
- Identify or encourage potential Jesuits for the presidency
- Discuss their availability and suitability
- Confer with their own provincial
- Give clearance for their service as Jesuits in this apostolic role[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

The provincial should be informed of candidates who are not Jesuits and know their qualifications to be Director of the Apostolic Work in furthering the institution's Catholic and Jesuit character, and should be able to discuss his views about this responsibility with the candidate and search committee.[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

The provincial's involvement in the search process reassures the college or university and the province of their unity of purpose and mutual support.[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

## Formation and Mission Leadership

Institutions seek committed Catholics or those with deep familiarity with the Jesuit tradition for leadership positions, particularly president and vice presidents.[^chunk:387f2052-d956-5e9d-bf9b-68d11a7d4d14] The President, Chief Academic Officer, and those responsible for student life should be able to articulate the key principles comprising the Catholic and Jesuit identity and provide guidance consistent with Church teaching and Catholic intellectual heritage.[^chunk:387f2052-d956-5e9d-bf9b-68d11a7d4d14]

Deans must be supportive of and familiar with the Jesuit and Catholic mission, serving as competent spokespersons who can communicate the importance of the institution's mission and identity.[^chunk:387f2052-d956-5e9d-bf9b-68d11a7d4d14] They should understand how mission might be integrated into curricular and co-curricular life.[^chunk:387f2052-d956-5e9d-bf9b-68d11a7d4d14]

Universities offer programs on Jesuit history, mission, and pedagogy, and robust programs in the Spiritual Exercises in Everyday Life to create opportunities for professional development and advancement.[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9]

## Association of Jesuit Colleges and Universities

The Association of Jesuit Colleges and Universities (AJCU) serves as a coordinating body for the 28 U.S. Jesuit colleges and universities.[^chunk:1365ed6d-fe18-5907-9d0e-a85b74088b03] Participation in AJCU and its various conferences is expected, and institutions collaborate with other Jesuit institutions throughout the world.[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9]
