## Overview

The Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm (IPP) as articulated for Saint Joseph's University (SJU) treats inclusion, accessibility, and safeguarding not as afterthoughts appended to an otherwise complete framework, but as intrinsic requirements flowing from the paradigm's deepest commitments: *cura personalis*, attention to the concrete learner, and respect for freedom of conscience. This article explains how each of those commitments shapes course design for diverse and differently abled learners, what universal design looks like within an IPP-informed curriculum, how safeguarding policies protect students during reflective exercises, and whether reflection can be required without compelling personal disclosure.
