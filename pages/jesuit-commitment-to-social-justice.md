# Jesuit Institutional Commitment to Social Justice

The Society of Jesus has developed a distinctive institutional commitment to social justice that has evolved significantly over the past seventy years. This commitment emerged from the Society's foundational mission to serve people's needs and has become increasingly central to Jesuit identity and work, particularly in higher education.

## Fr. Janssens's 1949 Instruction on the Social Apostolate

Fr. General Janssens issued a historic letter to the entire Society in 1949 regarding the social apostolate, marking an important moment in the Society's explicit commitment to justice work.[^chunk:6d064983-ced2-5ba2-9ccd-9ced6fe112aa] This instruction insisting on the importance of the social apostolate preceded by over two decades the more formal articulation of justice as a central mission. The social apostolate, as defined in the Society's documents, includes those Jesuits and Jesuit institutions that make the promotion of justice a principal goal of their ministry.[^chunk:6d57e7d9-44bd-5906-8506-1269f2caa002]

## General Congregation 32 and the Service of Faith and Promotion of Justice

The most significant institutional turning point came with General Congregation 32 (GC 32) in 1975, when the Society formally declared that "the mission of the Society of Jesus today is the service of faith, of which the promotion of justice is an absolute requirement."[^chunk:6d064983-ced2-5ba2-9ccd-9ced6fe112aa] This formulation—Decree 4 of GC 32—consecrated service of faith and promotion of justice as two inseparable dimensions of mission that should be evident in all the Society's ministries. Although the decree was carefully formulated, its length and novelty created difficulties for many Jesuits in understanding and accepting this commitment, with some believing that concern for justice should remain the responsibility of the laity.[^chunk:6d064983-ced2-5ba2-9ccd-9ced6fe112aa]

The decree's reflections on justice were subtle and nuanced. While it encouraged work for the transformation of economic, political, and social structures, it also recognized that injustice was embedded in the human heart and called for transformation of attitudes and social tendencies.[^chunk:6d064983-ced2-5ba2-9ccd-9ced6fe112aa] Decree 4 further issued a call for Jesuits and their institutions to consider the poor when determining their way of life and proceeding.[^chunk:6d064983-ced2-5ba2-9ccd-9ced6fe112aa]

## General Congregation 33 and Friendship with the Poor

In 1983, General Congregation 33 (GC 33) deepened the commitment to justice by calling Jesuits to a "deeper involvement in the lives of people around us in order to hear 'the joys and hopes, the griefs and the anxieties of the people of this age, especially those who are poor or in any way afflicted.'"[^chunk:6d57e7d9-44bd-5906-8506-1269f2caa002] The Congregation articulated that being involved means being friends. Friendship flows naturally from living in community with and in proximity to the poor, constituting a call to be inserted in the lives of the poor by being friends of the poor.[^chunk:6d57e7d9-44bd-5906-8506-1269f2caa002]

At the time Decree 4 was promulgated, many places experienced a dualism between faith and justice, viewing them as separate concerns.[^chunk:6d57e7d9-44bd-5906-8506-1269f2caa002] Subsequent General Congregations placed great stress on the close bond uniting the service of faith and the promotion of justice, speaking of the "faith-justice commitment," the "faith that does justice," or the "justice that is born of faith." These expressions attempt to highlight the dynamic unity existing between these two dimensions of mission—a justice rooted in the Gospel and practiced within the Ignatian tradition.[^chunk:6d57e7d9-44bd-5906-8506-1269f2caa002]

## Evolution Through Later General Congregations

General Congregation 34 (GC 34) in 1995 made an extensive endorsement of justice, with Decree 3 entirely dedicated to "Our Mission and Justice."[^chunk:6d064983-ced2-5ba2-9ccd-9ced6fe112aa] This Congregation insisted on the need to modify socio-cultural structures—including the culture itself—since these form the base of political and economic structures.[^chunk:6d57e7d9-44bd-5906-8506-1269f2caa002] GC 34 also stressed the unity of the Society's mission by joining together inseparably the service of faith, promotion of justice, dialogue with culture, and dialogue with other religious traditions.[^chunk:6d57e7d9-44bd-5906-8506-1269f2caa002] Decree 3 of GC 34 placed special stress on the spiritual sources of the promotion of justice, claiming they were found mainly in sharing in the lives of the very poor and those who work on their behalf, thus reinforcing the centrality of the option for the poor.[^chunk:6d57e7d9-44bd-5906-8506-1269f2caa002] The Congregation saw communities as essential instruments for promoting cultural solidarity, calling such groups "communities of solidarity."[^chunk:6d57e7d9-44bd-5906-8506-1269f2caa002]

In 2008, General Congregation 35 (GC 35) confirmed the Society's mission as expressed in GC 32 and reflected on it through the theological concept of reconciliation. Decree 3 of GC 35 states that reconciliation should be established with God, with fellow human beings, and with natural creation.[^chunk:6d57e7d9-44bd-5906-8506-1269f2caa002] The Congregation introduced concern for ecology as an integral part of the Society's mission, such that today the promotion of justice includes a commitment to care for creation.[^chunk:6d57e7d9-44bd-5906-8506-1269f2caa002] GC 35 emphasized that justice can be understood within a covenantal relationship framework, leading to defending the excluded, standing with the marginalized, and studying unjust social structures.[^chunk:6d57e7d9-44bd-5906-8506-1269f2caa002] The Congregation stressed the need for Jesuits to be on the world's social, cultural, and religious frontiers building bridges of dialogue and understanding.[^chunk:6d57e7d9-44bd-5906-8506-1269f2caa002] It called for bridges to be built between the rich and the poor, including political advocacy leading to collaboration between those wielding political power and those whose interests are hardly considered.[^chunk:6d57e7d9-44bd-5906-8506-1269f2caa002]

## The Social Justice and Ecology Secretariat

The Social Justice and Ecology Secretariat plays a crucial institutional role in promoting and coordinating the Society's commitment to justice across its various works and ministries.[^chunk:6d57e7d9-44bd-5906-8506-1269f2caa002] This secretariat provides leadership, resources, and direction for ensuring that the promotion of justice remains central to Jesuit institutional identity and practice. The secretariat works to develop policy frameworks, produce educational materials and resources, facilitate collaboration between different apostolic sectors, and ensure that justice concerns are integrated throughout Jesuit institutional life.

## Characteristics of Jesuit Understanding of Justice

Through the work of the General Congregations and subsequent reflection, the Society has developed a distinctive understanding of justice. General Congregations have not provided a normative definition of justice but rather articulated its essential characteristics: it is firmly rooted in faith, based on the centrality of the option for the poor, includes a commitment to creation, and works in dialogue with cultures and religions.[^chunk:6d57e7d9-44bd-5906-8506-1269f2caa002] The promotion of justice refers to all dimensions of the Society's mission.

The Ignatian tradition emphasizes that the frontiers of poverty, marginalization, injustice, and inhumanity are privileged spaces in which one encounters God and enters more deeply into the mystery of reality. The best access to truth is from below, from the poor, from their suffering, struggles, and hopes. Ignatian spirituality requires critical and prophetic perspectives: while encountering reality first generates an attitude of praise and gratitude, it also adopts a critical attitude observing the chasm between the justice and dignity God desires for all people and the historical reality that is anything but just and dignified.[^chunk:6d57e7d9-44bd-5906-8506-1269f2caa002]
