# Mission Priority Examen: Overview and Historical Development

## What is the Mission Priority Examen?

The Mission Priority Examen (MPE) is the joint process of the Society of Jesus and the Association of Jesuit Colleges and Universities (AJCU) for reaffirming the Jesuit, Catholic mission of higher-education institutions in the United States, Belize, and Canada.[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e] Established at the request of the Jesuit Superior General by the North American Provincials and the AJCU presidents, and launched in 2016, it integrates the Ignatian practice of the Examen with a structured cycle of self-study, peer review, and response from Rome.[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e]

The MPE serves dual purposes: toward the Society of Jesus, by connecting it regularly with its higher-education ministries, informing sponsorship decisions, and enabling the Superior General to confirm each institution's Catholic and Jesuit identity publicly; and toward the schools, by structuring deep reflection on identity, producing explicit mission priorities for the future, and strengthening the network among institutions.[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e]

Crucially, the process is both evaluative and developmental—an honest look at institutional life ordered to growth, not an audit ordered to compliance.[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e] While it bears a surface resemblance to accreditation, the AJCU is explicit that its spirit is different: a collaborative, Ignatian experience among the campus community, peer institutions, the sponsoring Province, and the association, undertaken for the purpose of mission.[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e]

The underlying questions framing the MPE are: What makes a college or university Jesuit? What does Jesuit sponsorship of a college or university require today? How will the member institutions live their Jesuit and Catholic mission now and in the future?[^chunk:d7983857-9c88-5df3-9729-b449b3d8acfe]

## Historical Development and Evolution

### Origins and First Round

The canonical warrant for the MPE comes from General Congregation 34, Decree 17, which establishes that for an institution to call itself Jesuit, periodic evaluation and accountability to the Society are necessary, so that the Society can judge whether its dynamics are developing in line with the Jesuit mission.[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e]

The first self-evaluation document, "Some Characteristics of Jesuit Colleges and Universities" (2013), originally commissioned by the Board of Directors for the AJCU and Jesuit Provincial Superiors to serve as a tool for internal review and improvement, ultimately became the primary resource document for the initial round of self-studies and peer review visits that took place between 2016 and 2020.[^chunk:6855f939-6c7c-5ec8-9e40-b24804c193a8]

### 2021 Revision and Second Round

The 2021 revision, titled "Characteristics of Jesuit Higher Education: A Guide for Mission Reflection," updated the instrument to govern the second round of examinations, running from 2021–22 through 2027–28.[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e] This revision was informed by what was learned from the initial round of self-studies, peer visit reports, feedback from the Jesuit Superiors General, Presidents and Provincials, and suggestions from involved colleagues at the schools.[^chunk:6855f939-6c7c-5ec8-9e40-b24804c193a8]

The 2021 revision sought to pursue four specific aims:[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e]

1. Respond to new emphases of the Society of Jesus that have evolved in recent years, including the theme of reconciliation from General Congregation 36, and the Universal Apostolic Preferences guiding all Jesuit works[^chunk:6855f939-6c7c-5ec8-9e40-b24804c193a8]
2. Accentuate the importance of the MPE process as a primary mechanism for self-review by institutions on how they live out their Jesuit, Catholic mission, and as an essential tool for the Society to discern and re-affirm its continuing relationships with Jesuit colleges and universities[^chunk:6855f939-6c7c-5ec8-9e40-b24804c193a8]
3. Anchor the MPE process as a shared endeavor of the schools and the Society, with ultimate responsibility for affirming Jesuit identity resting with the Society while designing and conducting the MPE process should be a joint and proactive responsibility[^chunk:6855f939-6c7c-5ec8-9e40-b24804c193a8]
4. Communicate that recognition of an institution as a Jesuit college or university does not rely on the presence of Jesuits on campus, but rather on the demonstrated commitment of these institutions to live out their Jesuit mission and values in ways that are real, enduring, and grounded in a well-prepared community of leaders personally dedicated to an Ignatian vision[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e]

## The MPE and Jesuit Catholic Mission and Identity

The MPE is rooted in the Ignatian tradition. The Examination of Consciousness ("Examen") is a practice of spiritual self-reflection described by St. Ignatius Loyola in his Spiritual Exercises, rooted in self-knowledge and attuned to the invitations of God in daily life.[^chunk:d7983857-9c88-5df3-9729-b449b3d8acfe] By framing the MPE in this way, the Society of Jesus and AJCU affirm a process that is institutionally reflective, sensitive to the distinctive circumstances and potential of each school, and deeply connected to mission.[^chunk:d7983857-9c88-5df3-9729-b449b3d8acfe]

The MPE begins with gratitude for the centuries-old tradition of Jesuit higher education as a gift to each of the colleges and universities that are part of the AJCU network.[^chunk:6855f939-6c7c-5ec8-9e40-b24804c193a8] Affirming the ways in which the Jesuit, Catholic mission and identity is strong and vibrant, and discerning how the institutions might grow in mission effectiveness, are at the heart of the MPE experience.[^chunk:6855f939-6c7c-5ec8-9e40-b24804c193a8]

The MPE process is guided by the Universal Apostolic Preferences (UAPs), promulgated by Very Rev. Arturo Sosa, S.J., Superior General of the Society of Jesus.[^chunk:6855f939-6c7c-5ec8-9e40-b24804c193a8] These four orientations are:[^chunk:6855f939-6c7c-5ec8-9e40-b24804c193a8]

1. To show the way to God through the Spiritual Exercises and discernment
2. To walk with the poor, the outcasts of the world, and those whose dignity has been violated, in a mission of reconciliation and justice
3. To accompany young people in the creation of a hope-filled future
4. To collaborate in the care of our Common Home

Father Sosa emphasized that these Preferences are not a "checklist" but rather "orientations that go beyond 'doing something' and enable us to achieve our transformation as persons, as religious communities, and as apostolic works and institutions."[^chunk:6855f939-6c7c-5ec8-9e40-b24804c193a8]

## Role of the Superior General and Jesuit Provincials

### The Superior General's Authority

The current Superior General, Arturo Sosa, S.J., plays a central role in the MPE process. He is the recipient of each school's materials and the author of the reaffirming letter at the conclusion of the cycle.[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e] At the end of the MPE cycle, the Superior General decides whether the institution's Jesuit and Catholic identity is reaffirmed.[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e]

### Provincial Leadership and Administration

Administration of the MPE is shared between the Jesuit Conference of Canada and the United States and the five Provinces, who sponsor the process through the Provincials and their Provincial Assistants for Higher Education (PAHE), and the AJCU, which facilitates it.[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e]

The Provincial has multiple responsibilities in the MPE process:[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be]

- Following conversations with the Provincial Assistant for Higher Education during annual visits to each college or university, the local Provincial writes a brief letter to the President, inviting the school to conduct its MPE and recommending particular areas of emphasis[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be]
- The Provincial approves the slate of peer visitors[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be]
- MPE materials are reviewed by the local Provincial and the Jesuit Conference in April/May, after which they are sent to Father General with a cover letter from the local Provincial regarding the process, mission priorities, and implementation[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be]
- The Provincial receives a letter from Father General in early fall outlining his decision regarding the school's Catholic and Jesuit identity and any areas of affirmation or needed attention, and then writes a letter to the University President relaying Father General's decision[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be]
- PAHEs are tasked by their respective Provincials to follow up with the school on the implementation of its mission priorities, serving as the principal points of contact with the Province regarding progress toward goals[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be]

### Relationship to the Society

Jesuit and Catholic Universities recognize that their relationship to the Society both includes and exceeds their relationship with the local Jesuit community and the Jesuit Province in which the school is located.[^chunk:13e22662-b4b7-5bea-bedd-053e64046ff0] North American Jesuit Universities are part of a regional association (AJCU) and an international network (International Association of Jesuit Universities, IAJU), each of which coordinates common projects, shared aspirations, and communication among the schools.[^chunk:13e22662-b4b7-5bea-bedd-053e64046ff0] The University's relationship to and active participation in these networks is key to its Jesuit identity and to increasing the shared ability to serve the common good.[^chunk:13e22662-b4b7-5bea-bedd-053e64046ff0]

Further, as Universities sponsored by the Society of Jesus, all are accountable to the goals of the international Society, and specifically to the UAPs that offer an overall focus and direction.[^chunk:13e22662-b4b7-5bea-bedd-053e64046ff0]

## The Seven Characteristics Framework

The 2021 Guide organizes the self-study around seven characteristics, each elaborated in prose and operationalized as lettered reflection questions:[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e]

1. **Leadership and Public Commitment to Mission**: Trustee selection, orientation, and mission formation; criteria and preparation for presidents and senior leaders; visibility of AJCU network commitment; robust, funded mission office with university-wide reach; mission tied to diversity, equity, and inclusion; transparent hiring practices[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e]

2. **The Academic Life**: Core curriculum grounding students in the humanistic tradition and perennial questions; values and ethics in graduate and professional study; diversity and sustainability in the curriculum; Jesuit and Catholic intellectual traditions reaching every graduate; research serving the mission[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e]

3. **The Pursuit of Faith, Justice, and Reconciliation**: Social justice and reconciliation grounded in Catholic social teaching; anti-racism commitments; care for the common home; service and solidarity with the community[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e]

4. **Promoting an Ignatian Campus Culture**: Encounter with the Ignatian tradition; campus ministry's liturgical, retreat, and spiritual-direction life; Student Affairs integrated with Jesuit aims; dialogue and civil discourse; athletics contextualized in mission[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e]

5. **Service to the Church**: Preparing people for service in the Church; communication with the Bishop; partnership through expertise and research; supporting the Church on racism and integral ecology[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e]

6. **Relationship to the Society of Jesus**: Attracting, retaining, and supporting Jesuits on campus; regular dialogue between President, mission officer, Jesuit community, and Province; active participation in AJCU and IAJU; partnership with other Jesuit apostolates[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e]

7. **Institutional Integrity**: Just compensation and benefits; mission-engagement opportunities; equity commitments; transparent budgeting and shared governance; fair economic engagement; transparency about mission in hiring[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e]

## The MPE Process Cycle

Each school undertakes the MPE every seven years.[^chunk:d7983857-9c88-5df3-9729-b449b3d8acfe] The university's portion of the MPE process, from the Provincial's invitation at the beginning to the school's submission of all materials at the conclusion, is completed within two semesters.[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be] Additional time is necessary for consideration and response by the Society of Jesus.

The process follows eight steps:[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be]

1. **Agreement on Focus and Scope**: The Provincial invites the school and recommends areas of emphasis (Spring or earlier)
2. **Committee Assignment**: President names Self-Study and Peer Review Committee members (Spring/Summer)
3. **Self-Study Completion**: Committee drafts a 30 to 40-page self-study containing prior examen materials, review of implementation of previous priorities, significant campus changes, treatment of Provincial emphasis areas, and two to three updated Jesuit Mission Priorities (Summer/Fall)
4. **Peer Visit and Report**: Three to four colleagues from other AJCU schools spend two to four days on campus as companions in mission (by February 15)
5. **Final Submission**: All materials go to the Provincial, AJCU President, and Peer Team (March 15)
6. **Consideration by the Society**: Materials reviewed by Provincial and Jesuit Conference, then sent to Father General (Spring to early Fall)
7. **Campus Communication**: President shares Father General's decision with the campus community (Fall)
8. **Follow-up**: PAHEs follow up on implementation of mission priorities (Ongoing)
