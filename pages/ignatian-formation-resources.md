## Overview

Ignatian pedagogy represents a distinctive approach to education rooted in the Jesuit tradition, centered on the formation of whole persons rather than the transmission of knowledge alone. Across Jesuit institutions, faculty development programs and online resources support educators in integrating these pedagogical principles into their teaching practice.

## Understanding Ignatian Pedagogy

### Core Philosophy

Ignatian pedagogy is fundamentally about "humanization"—forming students to be whole persons who live their lives for a greater good.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] Rather than simply imparting disciplinary knowledge and skills, this educational approach guides students to become the persons God created them to be, fostering a deeper understanding of what it means to be human.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

Education in the Ignatian tradition awakens students to the dignity of themselves and others, leading them to discover their truest selves.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] At its best, this pedagogy forms students to recognize both flourishing and suffering in the world, enabling them to become agents of justice, humanization, and flourishing.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

### The Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm

The Ignatian approach encompasses several interconnected elements:[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

**Context** involves understanding who we are, where we come from, who others are, and why things are the way they are, including the impact of social systems and scientific paradigms.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

**Experience** is holistic learning that honors both intellectual and affective dimensions. Affect and emotion are vital to the Ignatian paradigm, reflecting Ignatius's own emphasis on "sentir" or "felt knowledge."[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

**Reflection** involves finding meaning through critical engagement with lived reality and conceptual-intellectual understanding, along with self-reflection.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] Students consider the implications of what is known and how it is known, reflect on ethical implications, and assess historical failings with hope for a better world.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

**Action** puts context, experience, and reflection in service of the world, reflecting the Jesuit concept of "proyección social" or social projection.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

**Evaluation** asks critical questions oriented toward self-actualization, humanization, and the flourishing of all things.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

### Connection to Institutional Mission

The "3 Cs" emphasized at Jesuit institutions—competence, conscience, and compassion—are not arbitrary commitments but expressions rooted in the long tradition of Ignatian pedagogy.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] This holistic vision explains why Jesuit schools maintain robust core curricula, ensuring students engage across disciplines rather than siloing their education.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

## Cura Personalis: Care of the Whole Person

Cura personalis, a foundational principle of Jesuit education, extends beyond surface-level kindness to encompass an active, intentional dedication to the flourishing of others.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] This practice includes not only attending to students' needs and experiences but also disrupting indifference and challenging unjust perspectives.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

Cura personalis can mean making students uncomfortable when necessary, having difficult conversations about academic struggles, and helping students discover their authentic passions and gifts.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] Ultimately, cura personalis nurtures authentic discernment and forms whole persons committed to serving the world.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

## Online Resources for Understanding Ignatian Pedagogy

Jesuit institutions provide comprehensive online resources to support faculty understanding of Ignatian pedagogy. At Santa Clara University, the Center for Engaged Teaching and Learning (CETL) offers digital resources for teaching, including dedicated materials on Ignatian pedagogy.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

The university maintains a curated collection of foundational texts and essays available through its faculty development platforms, including:[^chunk:e45e0539-f826-5a57-b19c-8df3456a5cdf]

- Universal Apostolic Preferences of the Society of Jesus (2019-2029)
- Essays by foundational Jesuit thinkers including John O'Malley, SJ on Ignatian Humanism
- Essays on Jesuit Higher Education and Principles of Ignatian Pedagogy by Adolfo Nicolás, SJ (2010)
- Landmark addresses on Jesuit education and mission by Hans-Peter Kolbenbach and others
- Resources on Ignatian tradition, racial justice, and education
- Materials on liberation theology and the Jesuit Martyrs of El Salvador

## Faculty Development Support

Jesuit institutions support faculty development in Ignatian teaching methods through dedicated centers and programs. Santa Clara University's Office of the Provost oversees faculty development initiatives through the Center for Engaged Teaching and Learning (CETL).[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7] The university offers resources including a Center for Teaching Excellence, Tuesday Teaching Tips, Getting Started Teaching, and Teaching Grants.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]

These institutional structures ensure that faculty across disciplines can engage with Ignatian pedagogical principles and integrate them into their courses, supporting the broader mission of forming leaders who will build a just, humane, and sustainable world.[^chunk:30ded088-d2da-59bd-b8d6-5897bbe7fce7]
