## Overview

Formation—the intentional development of students, faculty, staff, and trustees aligned with Jesuit, Catholic, and Ignatian mission—represents a defining concept across U.S. Jesuit higher education, yet the field lacks universal definition and consistent operationalization.[^chunk:6f3e3e0d-b771-5fcd-a27f-ef29ad36fb12]

A systematic review of all 27 U.S. Jesuit colleges and universities reveals a field in convergence: 13 institutions publish explicit definitions or programmatic descriptions of formation, while 14—including several peer leaders—employ the term regularly without formal definition.[^chunk:6f3e3e0d-b771-5fcd-a27f-ef29ad36fb12] Where definitions exist, they cluster in five formats: program and center pages (4 institutions), mission and strategic documents (3), definitions spread across multiple units (3), dedicated definition pages (2), and strong implicit statements (1).[^chunk:6f3e3e0d-b771-5fcd-a27f-ef29ad36fb12]

## What Formation Means Across Institutions

### Explicit Definitions

Boston College stands as the field's benchmark, offering the most comprehensive infrastructure behind formation as an institutional concept.[^chunk:5fe2a98c-9f72-5518-8090-2d680bf079ee] The university defines student formation as "an explicit and intentional approach to helping students become their most authentic selves: intellectually, socially, and spiritually" and anchors this definition on a dedicated public page, "What Is Student Formation?"[^chunk:6f3e3e0d-b771-5fcd-a27f-ef29ad36fb12] This clarity is reinforced by institutional infrastructure: a Department of Formative Education treating formation as an academic field of study, and a Center for Student Formation serving as a staffed, public-facing hub that owns both programs and language.[^chunk:5fe2a98c-9f72-5518-8090-2d680bf079ee]

Other leading institutions articulate formation through their mission frameworks:

- **Creighton University** defines formation as "mission formation of all new members of our community… a deeper understanding of Ignatian spirituality," located in its Mission Reaffirmation Report.[^chunk:6f3e3e0d-b771-5fcd-a27f-ef29ad36fb12]
- **Gonzaga University** describes formation as "an in-depth exploration of the history, values, and practices of Jesuit higher education" on its Faculty & Staff Formation Cohorts page.[^chunk:6f3e3e0d-b771-5fcd-a27f-ef29ad36fb12]
- **Saint Louis University** frames formation through the axiom "A Jesuit education aims to form the whole person," delivered via its four-level framework.[^chunk:6f3e3e0d-b771-5fcd-a27f-ef29ad36fb12]
- **Santa Clara University** operationalizes formation as "three levels of deepening experience to encounter, explore and engage" the mission.[^chunk:6f3e3e0d-b771-5fcd-a27f-ef29ad36fb12]
- **Loyola Maryland** guides "students, faculty, and staff in their journey of faith, reflection, and service" on its dedicated Ignatian Formation page.[^chunk:6f3e3e0d-b771-5fcd-a27f-ef29ad36fb12]

Theological precision appears in an address hosted by Saint Peter's University: "Jesuit education was and is about the formation of more fully human persons," attributed to former Jesuit Superior General Peter-Hans Kolvenbach, and further defined as "a spiritual depth of affective bonding and a set of practices, including the Spiritual Exercises, which shape companionship into a common mission."[^chunk:5fe2a98c-9f72-5518-8090-2d680bf079ee]

### Compound Vocabulary and Two Registers

Where explicit definitions are absent, compound forms carry meaning:[^chunk:5fe2a98c-9f72-5518-8090-2d680bf079ee]

- **Spiritual formation**: used at Holy Cross and referenced in SJU's SEEL spiritual-direction certificate
- **Mission formation**: employed at Creighton, Saint Louis, Gonzaga, and University of San Francisco
- **Faith formation**: used at Georgetown, Loyola Marymount, and Detroit Mercy
- **Professional formation**: Seattle University names an Arts & Sciences pathway as "an ongoing journey of self-discovery"
- **Leadership and justice formation**: employed at Loyola New Orleans and Xavier

Critically, "formation" carries two distinct meanings that coexist across the field:[^chunk:5fe2a98c-9f72-5518-8090-2d680bf079ee]

1. **Jesuit religious formation**: the 10–12 year, seven-stage training path for Jesuit priests and brothers (explicitly defined only in Seattle's "Jesuitology" glossary)
2. **Institutional formation**: programs forming lay students, faculty, staff, and trustees into the mission—the sense nearly all 13 published definitions actually describe

## Formation Models and Frameworks

### Laddered Frameworks

Institutions distinguished by architectural clarity employ visible pathways that help community members locate themselves in a formation journey:

**Saint Louis University's Four-Level Mission Formation Framework**[^chunk:5fe2a98c-9f72-5518-8090-2d680bf079ee]
- Introductory → Deepening → Immersive → Fluency
- Applied uniformly across students, faculty, staff, administration, and board members
- One ladder for the whole community climbing the same staircase

**Santa Clara University's Three-Level Reflection-to-Action Loop**[^chunk:5fe2a98c-9f72-5518-8090-2d680bf079ee]
- Encounter → Explore → Engage
- Each level concludes in a period of reflection that leads to action

### Cohort Models

**Gonzaga University** operates four named cohorts:[^chunk:5fe2a98c-9f72-5518-8090-2d680bf079ee]
- Cardoner (first-year staff)
- Ignatian Mission Formation Program
- Arrupe Seminar
- Manresa Collective

Outcome: staff and faculty moved "from knowing the words of the written mission statement to working in ways that embody the Mission," per Gonzaga Magazine.[^chunk:5fe2a98c-9f72-5518-8090-2d680bf079ee]

### Structural Integration

Leading institutions attach formation to institutional structure so participation becomes designed, not accidental:[^chunk:5fe2a98c-9f72-5518-8090-2d680bf079ee]

- **Rockhurst**: Cardoner program required of all new full-time faculty, covering Ignatian spirituality, mission, and pedagogy
- **Marquette**: Formation of staff, faculty, and Board of Trustees named as Mission Priority #1, extending from hiring to supervision
- **Creighton**: Mission formation required for all new community members, plus ongoing development for existing staff

## Seven Cross-Institutional Themes

Despite definitional variation, seven themes recur across all 27 institutions, whether or not formation is formally defined:[^chunk:5fe2a98c-9f72-5518-8090-2d680bf079ee]

1. **The whole person**: Cura personalis operates everywhere; intellectual, spiritual, social, emotional, and physical growth are treated as one integrated project
2. **Outward orientation**: Formation aims at service, justice, and the common good—never self-improvement alone
3. **Mission-aligned**: Always tied to Jesuit, Catholic, and Ignatian identity; formation is how mission becomes personal
4. **For everyone, not just students**: Where defined, formation covers students, faculty, staff, administrators, trustees, and alumni
5. **A process, not a product**: "Journey," "ongoing," and "lifelong" recur constantly; no institution treats formation as an event
6. **Compound forms dominate**: Spiritual, mission, faith, professional, and leadership formation do conceptual work where no definition exists
7. **Two meanings coexist**: Jesuit religious formation (the 10–12 year path) and institutional formation of lay communities operate in parallel

## Formation Program Portfolio and Scope

### Programmatic Breadth

The Association of Jesuit Colleges and Universities (AJCU) maintains a searchable Formation Program Compendium cataloging 121 distinct formation initiatives across North American Jesuit institutions.[^chunk:b6d63861-5668-56e7-a6ac-f5b2c81873d0] Programs span multiple target audiences and pedagogies:

**Student-Focused Programs**
- Retreat and reflection models (e.g., "Encounter with Christ Retreat" at Creighton; Ignatian Silent Retreat at Boston College)
- Spiritual direction training (e.g., "Two-Year Spiritual Direction Certificate" at Fairfield)
- Leadership development (e.g., "Ignite: Leadership Rooted in Jesuit Traditions" at Santa Clara)

**Faculty and Staff Formation**
- Pedagogy-centered programs (e.g., "Ignatian Pedagogy Program" at Saint Joseph's; "Ignatian Pedagogy and Inclusive Teaching Program" at University of San Francisco)
- Mission integration (e.g., "Faculty Mission Integration Fellowship" at Marquette; "Bannan Mission Integration Grants" at Santa Clara)
- New hire onboarding (e.g., "New Employee Mission Orientation" at Detroit Mercy; "Newcomer Orientation Valuing Ignatian and Creighton Engagement [NOVICE]" at Creighton)
- Retreats and colloquia (e.g., "Halftime Retreat" at Boston College; "Faculty Research & Writing Retreat" at Creighton)

**Trustee and Administrative Formation**
- Board development (e.g., "Trustee Formation Program" at Gonzaga; "New Trustee Orientation" at Loyola New Orleans)
- Leadership retreats and pilgrimages (e.g., "Annual Trustee Ignatian Pilgrimage" at Fairfield; "Board of Trustees Rome Summit" at Fordham)

**Cross-Constituency Programs**
- All-community initiatives (e.g., "Ignatian Spirituality and Leadership Conference" at Saint Joseph's; "Mission Week" at Marquette)
- Seminar series and reading groups (e.g., "Jesuit Higher Education Faculty Seminar [JHEFS]" at Creighton; "Jesuit Education Today: A Mission for Justice Reading Group" at Santa Clara)

## Operationalizing Formation: How Institutions Define It

### Definition Formats and Locations

The 13 institutions that explicitly define formation distribute definitions across five location types:[^chunk:6f3e3e0d-b771-5fcd-a27f-ef29ad36fb12]

- **Program and center pages** (4 institutions): Gonzaga, Regis, Rockhurst, Santa Clara
- **Mission and strategic documents** (3): Creighton, Marquette, Saint Louis
- **Multiple dispersed units** (3): Loyola Chicago (Heart, Head, Hands via campus ministry, IPS, Stritch), Saint Peter's, Seattle
- **Dedicated definition pages** (2): Boston College, Loyola Maryland—the only institutions giving formation its own page
- **Strong implicit statements** (1): John Carroll ("Students are formed… as people who can examine experience, choose responsibly, and put learning to work for others")

### The Implicit Half

The 14 institutions using formation without formal definition employ it variously:[^chunk:5fe2a98c-9f72-5518-8090-2d680bf079ee]

- **Compound phrases only** (e.g., Canisius: "education and formation in the university's Jesuit heritage")
- **Mission statements and strategic plans** (e.g., Holy Cross, ASPIRE strategic plan; Fairfield, catalog and presidential statements)
- **Programmatic references** (e.g., Saint Joseph's: SEEL program, Hawk Hill Ready plan, Mission Week—frequent but undefined)
- **Rare or implicit usage** (e.g., Le Moyne prefers "the education and care of the whole person" over formation itself)
- **Specialized programmatic domains** (e.g., Georgetown: Residential Living mission, professional-education materials; Loyola New Orleans: Institute for Ministry, MPS/MRE programs)

## Assessment and Outcomes

### Evidence of Impact

Where formation frameworks are established, institutions document measurable shifts in community alignment:

**Gonzaga's Cohort Model Outcome**: Staff and faculty demonstrated movement "from knowing the words of the written mission statement to working in ways that embody the Mission," indicating that structured formation pathways correlate with mission-aligned practice rather than abstract knowledge.[^chunk:5fe2a98c-9f72-5518-8090-2d680bf079ee]

### Best Practices in Assessment

The field's leaders employ formation assessment through institutional infrastructure rather than standalone metrics:

1. **Definitional anchoring**: Boston College's published definition serves as a reference point for every program; this single decision cascades institutional coherence.[^chunk:5fe2a98c-9f72-5518-8090-2d680bf079ee]
2. **Structural embeddedness**: Saint Louis, Marquette, and Rockhurst attach formation to hiring, onboarding, and promotion processes, making assessment part of regular institutional rhythms.[^chunk:5fe2a98c-9f72-5518-8090-2d680bf079ee]
3. **Whole-community design**: Institutions covering all constituencies (students, faculty, staff, trustees, alumni) create feedback loops across the organization; no single cohort operates in isolation.[^chunk:5fe2a98c-9f72-5518-8090-2d680bf079ee]
4. **Program compendium participation**: Institutions systematically documenting programs in the AJCU compendium enable peer learning and comparative analysis.[^chunk:b6d63861-5668-56e7-a6ac-f5b2c81873d0]

## Implications and Field Convergence

The field is converging on formation as the defining concept of Jesuit education. Boston College demonstrates that the advantage of clarity is not more programming—it is a published definition that everything else can cite.[^chunk:5fe2a98c-9f72-5518-8090-2d680bf079ee]

The gap between leaders and peers is not activity; it is articulation and architecture. Saint Joseph's University, for example, maintains active programs (SEEL, Hawk Hill Ready plan, Mission Week) but lacks a published definition, named framework, or multi-constituency scope beyond students.[^chunk:5fe2a98c-9f72-5518-8090-2d680bf079ee] Similarly, institutions in the implicit half possess all the raw material—programs, language, and mission—but have not yet organized it into a coherent narrative.

Three pathways emerge for institutions seeking alignment with field leaders:

1. **Publish the definition**: One public page stating what formation means at the institution, modeled on Boston College's approach and Loyola Maryland's Ignatian Formation page
2. **Name the ladder**: A visible formation pathway with discrete levels (e.g., Saint Louis's four-level framework or Santa Clara's encounter–explore–engage loop) so community members can locate themselves in a journey
3. **Extend beyond students**: Formalize formation for faculty, staff, and trustees—from hiring through board development—following the model of Marquette, Gonzaga, and Rockhurst

The open question for the field: will individual institutions help define formation—or inherit someone else's definition?
