## Overview

The Mission Priority Examen (MPE) is the joint process of the Society of Jesus and the Association of Jesuit Colleges and Universities for reaffirming the Jesuit, Catholic mission of Jesuit higher education institutions in the United States, Belize, and Canada.[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e] Established at the request of the Jesuit Superior General by the North American Provincials and AJCU presidents, the MPE integrates the Ignatian practice of the Examen—a centuries-old spiritual reflection technique—with structured cycles of self-study, peer review, and response from Rome.[^chunk:d7983857-9c88-5df3-9729-b449b3d8acfe] Each school undertakes the MPE every seven years.[^chunk:d7983857-9c88-5df3-9729-b449b3d8acfe]

The process is both evaluative and developmental in orientation, ordered toward mission rather than compliance.[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e] While the MPE bears a surface resemblance to accreditation, the AJCU emphasizes that its spirit is fundamentally different: a collaborative, Ignatian experience undertaken for the purpose of growth in mission.[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e]

## Major Phases of the Self-Study Process

The MPE self-study process unfolds through distinct phases. First, a well-prepared Self-study Committee engages the campus in "preparation days"—academic and spiritual activities that spark interest in the school's mission and elicit engagement from all sectors of the campus community.[^chunk:d7983857-9c88-5df3-9729-b449b3d8acfe]

Second, the Self-study Committee conducts reflective listening sessions and gathers input from the entire campus community.[^chunk:d7983857-9c88-5df3-9729-b449b3d8acfe] Through a structured listening process, the committee considers the mission reflections of students, faculty, staff, members of the Jesuit Community, trustees, alumni/ae, and the local Bishop, writing a brief self-study that includes points of alignment with the mission (spiritual consolation) and areas of misalignment (spiritual desolation).[^chunk:d7983857-9c88-5df3-9729-b449b3d8acfe]

Third, the Self-study Committee and President discern two to three Jesuit Mission Priorities for the school, relying on the themes of *Characteristics of Jesuit Higher Education: A Guide for Mission Reflection*.[^chunk:d7983857-9c88-5df3-9729-b449b3d8acfe] The resulting self-study is typically 30 to 40 pages in length and includes the prior examen's self-study and peer report, a review of how previous mission priorities were implemented, significant campus changes, treatment of the Provincial's areas of emphasis, reflection on constituency feedback, and the updated Jesuit Mission Priorities with implementation plans.[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be]

## The Peer-Review Visit: Structure and Entailment

A Peer Team of three to four trusted AJCU colleagues—jointly appointed by AJCU and the Province—visits the campus to engage in the review process.[^chunk:d7983857-9c88-5df3-9729-b449b3d8acfe] The self-study must be submitted at least one month prior to the peer team visit, or even further in advance if the review period overlaps with the semester break.[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be]

During the visit, which lasts two to four days, the Peer Team meets with a wide range of campus community members including the President, key leaders, faculty, staff, students, alumni/ae, and trustees.[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be] The Peer Visitors function as "companions in mission, who wish to see the school's Jesuit, Catholic mission thrive and grow in every dimension of its work."[^chunk:d7983857-9c88-5df3-9729-b449b3d8acfe]

At the end of the campus visit, the Peer Team meets with the President and Self-study Committee Chair(s) to discuss the team's impressions and make initial recommendations.[^chunk:d7983857-9c88-5df3-9729-b449b3d8acfe] The Peer Team then authors a draft report that highlights the school's mission successes, along with selected areas for growth and improvement.[^chunk:b8cbedb2-1fea-53f8-903b-8ed72ad30e67]

An iterative exchange follows, during which the Self-study Chair and Peer Team Chair discuss possible corrections to the self-study and/or peer team report.[^chunk:b8cbedb2-1fea-53f8-903b-8ed72ad30e67] Either document may be adapted to reflect new insights gained through the visit, and any areas of disagreement are noted in writing.[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be]

## The Role of Ignatian Discernment in the MPE

The MPE is framed explicitly as an Examen because it applies the structure of Ignatian spiritual reflection at the institutional level.[^chunk:d7983857-9c88-5df3-9729-b449b3d8acfe] The Examination of Consciousness (Examen) is a practice of spiritual self-reflection described by St. Ignatius Loyola in his Spiritual Exercises, rooted in self-knowledge and attuned to the invitations of God in daily life.[^chunk:d7983857-9c88-5df3-9729-b449b3d8acfe]

The AJCU maps the institutional MPE process directly onto the steps of the personal Examen:[^chunk:1e96400d-3644-5cc2-bc81-6536c66e0697] 

- Grateful awareness becomes the committee's grateful reflection on current mission commitments
- The review of the day becomes structured listening that surfaces points of alignment (named as consolation) and misalignment (named as desolation)
- Asking insight on what sticks becomes the peer visit
- Taking responsibility becomes joint reflection on needed improvement
- Envisioning renewed living becomes the report, dialogic revision, and the priorities
- Closing in thanksgiving becomes the Society's review and reaffirmation[^chunk:1e96400d-3644-5cc2-bc81-6536c66e0697]

The framing is not merely decorative—it commits the process to being institutionally reflective, sensitive to each school's distinct circumstances, and ordered to mission rather than to compliance.[^chunk:1e96400d-3644-5cc2-bc81-6536c66e0697] The Society of Jesus insists that the exercise be entered prayerfully rather than administratively.[^chunk:1e96400d-3644-5cc2-bc81-6536c66e0697]

## Timeline and Duration of the MPE Cycle

The university's portion of the MPE process, from the Provincial's invitation at the beginning to the school's submission of all materials, should be completed within two semesters, with additional time necessary for consideration and response by the Society of Jesus.[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be]

The eight-step process follows a specific timeline:[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be]

**Step I (Spring or earlier):** Agreement by the Provincial and Institution on the Focus and Scope. The local Provincial writes a brief letter to the President, inviting the school to conduct its MPE and recommending particular areas of emphasis, typically providing at least a year's notice of the timing (except for Year One).[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be]

**Step II (Spring/Summer):** Assignment of Self-Study and Peer Review Committees. The President names committee chairs and members based on input from faculty, staff, students, alumni/ae, and trustees.[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be]

**Step III (Summer/Fall):** Completion and Submission of Self-Study. The self-study must be submitted at least one month prior to the peer team visit, or even further in advance if review overlaps with semester break.[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be]

**Step IV (Late Fall/Early Spring):** Peer Team Visit and Report. The peer team visit must occur no later than February 15.[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be]

**Step V (March 15):** Final Submission of MPE Materials. All documentation is due by March 15 for all schools, whether the MPE begins in fall or spring.[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be]

**Step VI (Spring to Early Fall):** Consideration by the Society of Jesus. MPE materials are reviewed by the local Provincial and the Jesuit Conference in April/May, then sent to the Superior General with a cover letter from the Provincial. The Superior General reviews all submitted materials to reaffirm the school's Jesuit, Catholic mission and provide guidance for its growth.[^chunk:d7983857-9c88-5df3-9729-b449b3d8acfe]

**Step VII (Fall):** Campus Communication. The President shares the Superior General's decision with trustees, the community, and stakeholders.[^chunk:1e96400d-3644-5cc2-bc81-6536c66e0697]

**Step VIII (Ongoing):** Follow-up. The Provincial Assistant for Higher Education (PAHE) follows implementation of the mission priorities through annual visits; AJCU stands ready with tailored resources. Implementation of the priorities and connection to the strategic plan and other key documents is described as the true completion of the process.[^chunk:1e96400d-3644-5cc2-bc81-6536c66e0697]

Following the Society's review, the Father General writes a letter to the Provincial in early fall stating his decision on the school's Catholic and Jesuit identity and areas of affirmation or needed attention, which is relayed to the President with copies to the AJCU President and the Bishop.[^chunk:1e96400d-3644-5cc2-bc81-6536c66e0697]

## Collaborative Structure

The MPE is fundamentally a collaborative, Ignatian experience.[^chunk:d7983857-9c88-5df3-9729-b449b3d8acfe] It involves collaboration among students, faculty, staff, and trustees in the college/university community; with other Jesuit institutions who generously make colleagues available to serve as Peer Visitors; with the Jesuit Province that sponsors the MPE; and with the Association of Jesuit Colleges and Universities, which facilitates the process.[^chunk:d7983857-9c88-5df3-9729-b449b3d8acfe] Input from the local Bishop regarding how the school can better serve the local Church is also a collaborative element of the MPE.[^chunk:d7983857-9c88-5df3-9729-b449b3d8acfe]
