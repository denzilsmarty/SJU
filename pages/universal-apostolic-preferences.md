# Universal Apostolic Preferences and Strategic Institutional Direction

## Overview

Universal Apostolic Preferences represent fundamental strategic orientations guiding the mission and apostolic direction of Jesuit institutions globally. Confirmed by Pope Benedict XVI's successor and articulated through the General Congregations of the Society of Jesus, these preferences shape how Jesuit colleges, universities, and other apostolic works align their institutional priorities with the broader mission of the Catholic Church and the Ignatian charism.

## How Universal Apostolic Preferences Guide Jesuit Institutional Mission

Universal Apostolic Preferences serve as foundational touchstones for Jesuit institutions' strategic planning and discernment processes. Very Rev. Arturo Sosa, S.J., Superior General of the Society of Jesus, has emphasized that these preferences constitute "basic orientations for our life-mission in the next ten years" and open "a new stage of our journey towards a better understanding and practice of the care of mission and people."[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51]

The Mission Priority Examen (MPE) represents a key institutional mechanism through which Jesuit colleges and universities operationalize these universal preferences. Initiated by the Superior General and coordinated through the Association of Jesuit Colleges and Universities, the MPE invites entire institutional communities to engage in honest discernment about their Jesuit Catholic identity. The process requires each institution to address foundational questions: "Do you want to continue to be a Jesuit, Catholic University? If so, what are the two to four mission goals (and accompanying strategies) that you will prioritize for the next few years?"[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5]

This process honors "the voices and experiences of each member of the community" while communicating institutional commitments directly to the Vatican.[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5] The Characteristics of Jesuit Higher Education document serves as the reference guide for this institutional reaffirmation.[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5]

## Strategic Priorities from Recent Papal Guidance and General Congregation Decisions

Recent General Congregations, particularly the 36th General Congregation, have articulated clear strategic priorities that shape institutional direction. These priorities emphasize the interconnection of apostolic mission (cura apostolica) and personal care (cura personalis) as inseparable dimensions of Jesuit work.[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51]

Key strategic directions include:

**Strengthening Shared Mission and Cooperation**: The Society increasingly recognizes that "partnership and shared mission" arise from the insight that "by fostering conversation, consultation and discernment in common, we are building up the apostolic body of the Society of Jesus."[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51] This reflects Vatican II ecclesiology regarding the role and responsibility of the laity in the Church's life and mission.[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51]

**Advancing Discernment in Common**: Spiritual conversation and discernment in common have been identified as essential instruments for institutional decision-making. The 36th General Congregation emphasizes that "spiritual conversation presupposes attentive listening to oneself and to others. It is a listening to the Spirit who speaks to us in the experience of sharing, opening us to a new look at reality."[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51] This methodology becomes "the ordinary way of reaching decisions in the life-mission of the Society."[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51]

**Rethinking Jesuit Identity in Apostolic Works**: The challenge for institutions involves examining "how Jesuits understand themselves as partners in the missio Dei" and inviting the Society "to inspire new and creative ways of living as partners."[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51] This reorientation applies "not only in those areas of the Society where the number of Jesuits and their presence in works is decreasing, but also in those areas where the presence of Jesuits in works is substantial."[^chunk:6f4937fe-63ab-5d7e-b5d0-240d7e30ca51]

## Implementing Sustainability and Social Justice Initiatives

Jesuit colleges and universities operationalize the Universal Apostolic Preferences through concrete institutional priorities that address contemporary challenges. The University of San Francisco's Mission Priority Examen (2022), affirmed by Father General Arturo Sosa, provides an illustrative example of how institutions translate apostolic preferences into strategic action.[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5]

**Integrating Faith and Justice**: USF's mission priorities include commitment to "build capacity and opportunity among faculty, staff, students, and leaders to understand and engage in substantive conversations about Jesuit Catholic identity in the context of higher education" with explicit focus on ensuring "a faith that does justice and ensures institutional accountability."[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5]

**Advancing Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion**: Institutions commit to "develop a transparent, sustainable, university-wide plan to strengthen Jesuit mission with diversity, equity and inclusion priorities, supported by a well-resourced, visible structure to ensure institutional accountability."[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5]

**Environmental Stewardship Aligned with Laudato Si**: Jesuit institutions strengthen sustainability initiatives to "promote cross-disciplinary faculty participation in shaping the curriculum, motivating university-wide engagement, and ensuring institutional accountability in meeting bold science-driven sustainability goals inspired by Laudato si."[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5] The papal encyclical provides both moral framework and specific ecological priorities for these institutional commitments.

**Modeling Ignatian Leadership Practices**: Institutions cultivate "collaborative structures among university stakeholders that model cura personalis, practice Ignatian discernment, and respect the legitimate autonomy of the several unions that represent subsets of our employees."[^chunk:b4c6be36-f526-5906-bb00-5bd39e971bf5]

## The Relationship Between Governance and Mission

The care of apostolic mission requires intentional structural relationships between local leadership and work directors. Very Rev. Arturo Sosa has emphasized that "The Local Superior contributes to making the community truly apostolic" and that "when the community is responsible for an apostolic work, it is up to the Local Superior to encourage the Jesuit members of the apostolic team and, cooperating with the Director, to foster the Ignatian and Jesuit identity of the institution."[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c]

This governance model depends on "personal conversion and self-awareness, but it also demands strengthening skills and formation procedures aimed at the exercise of shared leadership."[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c] Through regular visitations and "account of conscience" practices, Major Superiors ensure "the care of mission-life of the Society," maintaining connection between institutional leadership and apostolic works.[^chunk:66689b9c-34a3-54b7-8f49-1734d388da5c]
