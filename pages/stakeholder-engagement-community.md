## Key Stakeholders in the MPE Self-Study Process

The Mission Priority Examen (MPE) engages a broad array of institutional stakeholders in structured reflection on mission. The process deliberately involves multiple constituencies to ensure that mission priorities emerge from genuine community discernment rather than administrative imposition.[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e]

At Fordham University, the MPE Steering Committee included representatives from across institutional divisions: the Vice Provost for Academic Affairs, Deans from multiple colleges, the Assistant Vice President for Student Affairs for Diversity and Inclusion, faculty members including the Ignatian Faculty Fellow, the Dean of the Graduate School of Social Service, the Vice President for Development and University Relations, the Vice President for Human Resources, the Associate Director of the Center for Community Engaged Learning, and faculty specialists in academic development.[^chunk:8d42b709-f2bd-5012-a384-da5b57b21226] The steering committee structure ensures that mission reflection spans academic, student life, administrative, and external relations dimensions of institutional work.

## Faculty, Staff, and Student Engagement in Mission Reflection

Fordham's MPE process employed extensive listening sessions and surveys to gather input from all segments of the campus community. From October 2023 through January 2024, the Steering Committee conducted literature reviews, document coding, and focus groups to refine areas of inquiry.[^chunk:8d42b709-f2bd-5012-a384-da5b57b21226]

The February 2024 invitation to the campus community yielded substantial participation. Survey responses included: 237 full-time faculty members (31% response rate); 142 part-time faculty (14% response rate); 509 administrators and staff members; 594 undergraduates (12% response rate); and 946 graduate students (18% response rate).[^chunk:8d42b709-f2bd-5012-a384-da5b57b21226] Each listening session began with prayer and invitation to open-ended conversation to discern the movement of spirits, grounding the process in Ignatian spirituality.[^chunk:8d42b709-f2bd-5012-a384-da5b57b21226]

Faculty responses revealed both alignment and gaps in mission integration. While 66% of undergraduate respondents endorsed that Fordham's Jesuit mission is reflected in courses they take, only 23% of full-time faculty affirmed that faith and spirituality positively intersected with their teaching at Fordham, compared to 32% of part-time faculty.[^chunk:8d42b709-f2bd-5012-a384-da5b57b21226] Among administrators and staff, 40% strongly agreed that mission was central to their work.[^chunk:8d42b709-f2bd-5012-a384-da5b57b21226] Graduate students showed varying commitment: 29% stated that Fordham's Jesuit mission remains very important to them.[^chunk:8d42b709-f2bd-5012-a384-da5b57b21226]

Beyond surveys, the MPE included dedicated listening sessions with specific constituencies. On September 3, 2024, the VP for Mission Integration and Ministry conducted sessions with the Cabinet and President in two rounds—one examining where mission is well integrated and vibrant, the other focusing on areas needing improvement.[^chunk:8d42b709-f2bd-5012-a384-da5b57b21226] On September 9, 2024, a separate listening session engaged the local Jesuit community at Spellman Hall using the same two-round format.[^chunk:8d42b709-f2bd-5012-a384-da5b57b21226] On October 9, 2024, the VP met with facilities, public safety, and clerical workers to ensure that support staff voices were heard in mission reflection.[^chunk:8d42b709-f2bd-5012-a384-da5b57b21226]

The Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm (IPP) serves as a framework for how faculty and campus ministers engage students in mission-centered learning. The Guide for Mission Reflection identifies facility with the IPP—which moves through context, experience, reflection, action, and evaluation—as essential for faculty leadership.[^chunk:bf606938-d1a0-537f-82e1-483d0a08d9bb] Faculty, campus ministers, student development personnel, and service-learning professionals are called to lead diverse groups of students through reflection and analysis of their service experiences, with special attention to the economic, social, and cultural realities of communities they engage.[^chunk:bf606938-d1a0-537f-82e1-483d0a08d9bb]

## Trustee Involvement in Mission Priority Assessment

Boards of trustees hold a distinctive governance role in the MPE process. According to the governing process overview, trustees are apprised of the MPE at its beginning and updated on it regularly. Some trustees may be invited to participate in focus groups or conversations with peer team visitors.[^chunk:e645f4fd-a362-5cce-bdde-fdab0c0614be]

At Fordham, trustee engagement began early in the process. On September 28, 2023, the VP for Mission Integration and Ministry introduced trustee members of the Board Mission and Social Justice Committee to the upcoming MPE process and reviewed Mission Priorities from the prior 2017 examen.[^chunk:8d42b709-f2bd-5012-a384-da5b57b21226] On December 7, 2023, the same committee was informed of emerging mission priority areas, and trustees emphasized the importance of continuity in mission formation starting with first-year students throughout their lives.[^chunk:8d42b709-f2bd-5012-a384-da5b57b21226] Beyond committee participation, the VP for Mission Integration and Ministry focuses on initial and ongoing formation of trustees as agents of mission leadership in collaboration with the global Catholic Church and the Society of Jesus.[^chunk:87232d98-8417-5a2d-a0fe-baa25ea62147]

Trustee formation has included high-impact experiences. In June 2022 and June 2024, the VP accompanied groups of trustees and benefactors to Rome for conversations with Vatican dicasteries about the mission of Jesuit and Catholic higher education and had personal meetings with Pope Francis.[^chunk:87232d98-8417-5a2d-a0fe-baa25ea62147] These trustee engagement strategies reinforce that trustees function not only as governance bodies but as formational leaders in institutional mission.

## Community Input Informing Mission Priorities

Community input shapes institutional mission priorities through deliberate pathways of engagement. The Guide for Mission Reflection frames this engagement as reciprocal: the University works to foster virtuous life and learning in students, administrators, staff, faculty, alumni, and Board of Trustees, characterized by personal responsibility, compassion, forgiveness, respect, and integration of body, mind, and soul.[^chunk:bf606938-d1a0-537f-82e1-483d0a08d9bb]

Service and solidarity with the community characterizes how Jesuit universities inform their priorities. This institutional commitment is reflected through institutes, research and advocacy projects, clinics, and outreach programs, and is embedded in curriculum through pedagogical practices such as service learning.[^chunk:bf606938-d1a0-537f-82e1-483d0a08d9bb] Faculty and students approach community-based learning and research as a shared experience with the communities they engage, and the University develops healthy partnerships with community organizations that inform its pedagogy and research priorities.[^chunk:bf606938-d1a0-537f-82e1-483d0a08d9bb]

At Fordham specifically, the emerging mission priorities reflect community engagement. Faculty reported being "immersed in migration initiatives primarily via the Zolberg Foundation-supported Migrants, Migration and Human Dignity initiative," with one full-time faculty member noting that "all of this feeds me spiritually and I have now incorporated migrant justice as part of my pedagogy."[^chunk:017e4fb4-b98a-5fa1-8242-8b3e4943a9c6] This direct faculty voice on how community engagement informs teaching shaped Priority 3 (Advance Transformative Teaching and Education). Similarly, Priority 4 (Advance Public Impact Research) emerged from faculty who have "widely endorsed" focus areas including migration, climate change, AI, and democracy.[^chunk:017e4fb4-b98a-5fa1-8242-8b3e4943a9c6]

The University's Center for Community Engaged Learning exemplifies how community partnerships inform institutional direction. Fordham maintains a strong commitment to service to neighboring communities in the Bronx and Lincoln Square.[^chunk:87232d98-8417-5a2d-a0fe-baa25ea62147] The seven-year Green Plan for Environmental Justice, generated in response to Laudato Si' and Pope Francis's mandate to every Catholic University, generated a $50 million Flourishing in Community EPA Federal Grant to fund community-led strategies promoting ecological justice.[^chunk:87232d98-8417-5a2d-a0fe-baa25ea62147] Community partnerships thereby shape not only service priorities but also research agendas and institutional commitments.

Community input also informs how universities understand their responsibility to serve beyond the Catholic community. As an intellectual community valuing diversity and inclusion, Fordham acknowledges its responsibility to extend service to the Church beyond the Catholic community by explaining core values in language appealing to the broader University community.[^chunk:87232d98-8417-5a2d-a0fe-baa25ea62147] This reflects recognition that Jesuit universities in the United States function as mission territories where the legacy of translating Catholic doctrine into the language of diverse cultures and circumstances remains as relevant and necessary today as in the sixteenth century.[^chunk:87232d98-8417-5a2d-a0fe-baa25ea62147]

The ultimate role of community stakeholders in mission priority assessment appears in the peer visit itself. Three to four colleagues from other AJCU schools spend two to four days on campus as companions in mission, meeting the president, leaders, faculty, staff, students, alumni, and trustees to share their experiences with mission awareness and implementation and to be invited to think more deeply about aligning efforts with Jesuit and Catholic identity.[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e] This collaborative, Ignatian experience among the campus community, peer institutions, the sponsoring Province, and the association occurs for the purpose of mission rather than compliance.[^chunk:1b8e170f-7c0a-503a-9b71-3cba6453303e]
