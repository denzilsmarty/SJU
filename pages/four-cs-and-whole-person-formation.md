The **Four Cs**—conscience, competence, compassion, and commitment—are the authoritative formulation of formative qualities that Jesuit education seeks to develop in learners. They are the outcome-facing complement to the procedural dynamics of the **Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm (IPP)**, and together they provide Saint Joseph's University (SJU) with a coherent account of both *how* formation proceeds and *toward what* it aims.

## Origins and Authority of the Four Cs

The Four Cs were articulated by the **International Commission on the Apostolate of Jesuit Education (ICAJE)** in its 2015 document *Human Excellence*.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] That document defined the qualities—conscience, competence, compassion, and commitment—as a description of the person Jesuit education seeks to form.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

A subsequent ICAJE document, *Jesuit Schools: A Living Tradition in the 21st Century* (2019), confirmed that *Human Excellence* complements rather than replaces the 1986 *Characteristics of Jesuit Education* and the 1993 *Ignatian Pedagogy: A Practical Approach*—the two foundational modern texts of the Jesuit educational tradition.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0] The Four Cs therefore sit within a layered documentary tradition, not as a free-standing slogan.

SJU's working guide cautions that institutional variants of the Cs should not be confused with the globally promulgated ICAJE framework: the authoritative 2015 formulation is specifically *conscience, competence, compassion,* and *commitment*.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## What Each C Means

The Four Cs are best understood as **formative qualities, not stages or steps**.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] They name the integrated dispositions toward which repeated educational cycles can contribute over time:

- **Conscience**: the capacity for moral perception, deliberation, and judgment—knowing what is right and why it matters.
- **Competence**: disciplined mastery of knowledge and skill required for meaningful work in the world.
- **Compassion**: the affective and relational quality of genuine care for others, especially those who are vulnerable or excluded.
- **Commitment**: the willingness to act, persevere, and be held accountable in service to others and the common good.

These are not checkboxes achievable in a single course. They are lifelong integrations of learning, relationship, and responsible action.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

## The Four Cs and the 1993 IPP's Five Dynamics

The 1993 IPP identifies five recursive dynamics: **context, experience, reflection, action,** and **evaluation**.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] The Four Cs name the qualities that repeated cycles of those dynamics can cultivate. They are related but distinct:

| IPP Dynamic | Contribution to the Four Cs |
|---|---|
| **Context** | Grounds learning in the real lives of learners and communities, a prerequisite for conscience and compassion |
| **Experience** | Provides the affectively and intellectually significant encounters through which competence is built and compassion is awakened |
| **Reflection** | Develops conscience by examining evidence, assumptions, perspectives, values, and consequences with disciplined honesty |
| **Action** | Expresses and deepens commitment; interior choice and outward practice are both genuine action |
| **Evaluation** | Tests and reinforces all four Cs by reviewing mastery, judgment, quality of response, and consequence—and reopening the cycle |

The relationship is recursive: no single encounter produces a fully formed conscience or a permanently compassionate person. The IPP is "a characteristic way educational experiences can contribute" to the integration the Four Cs describe.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

### Reflection as the hinge for conscience

Reflection is described as the pivotal dynamic connecting experience to responsible action.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0] A disciplined reflective sequence moves through attention (what happened?), interpretation (what causes and patterns explain it?), interior response (what draws or troubles me?), ethical and social meaning (who benefits, who bears risk?), and discernment (which response is more truthful and responsible?).[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0] This sequence is precisely the exercise of conscience as a formed capacity—not mere feeling, but disciplined moral perception.

### Action and commitment

In the IPP, action begins in interior appropriation—a judgment, choice, or commitment—before it becomes outward conduct.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0] A student who lacks the authority to solve a structural problem during a course can still demonstrate commitment through a clarified professional intention, a revised research design, or a refusal of an unethical practice.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0] Commitment in this sense is not a grand declaration; it is the habitual disposition to act responsibly and to be accountable for consequences.

## Is the IPP Only About Academic Outcomes?

No. The 1993 source text explicitly invites educators to attend to broader human growth alongside conventional assessment of academic mastery.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0] The paradigm is described as "a recursive, relational process" through which learners are accompanied toward "greater freedom, competence, and care"—language that encompasses character and not merely information.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

At the same time, the paradigm is **academically serious**. Reflection without knowledge becomes mere opinion; action without analysis becomes impulse.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] The 1986 *Characteristics* located academic excellence within "human excellence"—the fullest development of each person's capacities for service, judged with attention to circumstances.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] The 1993 IPP inherits this integration: disciplinary rigor and whole-person formation are not alternatives.

Formation is defined as the "lifelong, accompanied integration of knowledge, experience, reflection, relationships, faith or ultimate meaning, and responsible action toward greater freedom and service."[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] The IPP contributes to this when its repeated cycles become habits: attending carefully, entering experience, reflecting deeply, choosing responsibly, and learning from results.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## How SJU Avoids Morally Scoring Students on Personal Virtues

The IPP's formative ambition creates a serious institutional risk: converting accompaniment into surveillance or assigning grades to a student's interior life.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0] SJU's working standard addresses this directly.

**What faculty may assess:**
- The quality of ethical analysis and reflective practice
- Professional responsibility and collaboration
- Perspective-taking and evidence-based action
- Specificity of observation and acknowledgment of uncertainty
- Examination of assumptions and engagement with affected perspectives
- Proportionality of response and revision after feedback[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

**What faculty should not assess:**
- The state of a student's conscience
- A student's compassion as a personal trait
- Faith, political conformity, or moral worth[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300][^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

Rubrics should describe **observable qualities in work**, not identity claims about the person.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0] For example, a weak learning outcome reads "Students will become compassionate leaders"; a stronger one reads "Students will analyze how a decision distributes benefits and risks among stakeholders, consult affected perspectives, and justify a proportionate response."[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

Reflection prompts should offer options for private or impersonal reflection; the quality of attention and reasoning is assessed, not the student's belief or emotional disclosure.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0] Similarly, the IPP does not require disclosure of trauma, faith practice, or other sensitive information.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

This boundary is also embedded in the evaluation dynamic's four legitimate objects: academic learning, quality of judgment, action and impact, and educational design—none of which is the moral worth of the student as a person.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

## The Four Cs and *Cura Personalis*

*Cura personalis*—care for the whole person in their concrete particularity—is the relational condition that makes formation possible, not an additional step in the IPP or a synonym for the Four Cs.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] It requires knowledge of learners, mutual trust, appropriate challenge, and genuine adaptation.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Without it, the five dynamics risk becoming mechanical; with it, each cycle of context, experience, reflection, action, and evaluation becomes a genuinely accompanied encounter in which conscience, competence, compassion, and commitment can take root.

## Relationship to Broader Jesuit Educational History

The line of development running from Arrupe's 1973 "Men for Others" address—criticizing education that produces people primarily for self-advancement—through the 1986 *Characteristics*, the 1993 IPP, and the 2015 *Human Excellence* document reflects a consistent broadening of what excellence means in Jesuit education.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] The Four Cs are the most recent and concise global articulation of that tradition's formative horizon. They do not stand alone but inherit and concentrate commitments to faith that does justice, to persons for and with others, and to the *magis*—the discernment of the greater service of God and neighbor.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]
