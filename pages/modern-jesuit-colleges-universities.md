## Overview

Jesuit higher education represents a distinctive Catholic educational tradition spanning nearly 500 years, with deep roots in the spirituality of Saint Ignatius Loyola and the Society of Jesus. Today, this network has evolved into a global enterprise serving over one million students across multiple continents.

## Number of Jesuit Institutions in the United States

**Twenty-seven Jesuit colleges and universities serve students across the United States.**[^chunk:d4f703b8-e359-5cf3-a646-0073f5112c5f] These institutions include examples such as John Carroll University in Northeast Ohio, which exemplifies the Jesuit Catholic educational mission in higher education.

## Global Network and Scale

The Jesuit higher education mission extends far beyond the United States. Today, the Society of Jesus counts **more than 200 higher education institutions in the Jesuit network in partnership with a great number of lay people.**[^chunk:1b557c1d-3806-5fcd-88f4-ae1e467218c5] These institutions collectively **educate more than 1 million students, from all kinds of religious, cultural, social and language backgrounds in 5 continents and more than 50 countries.**[^chunk:1b557c1d-3806-5fcd-88f4-ae1e467218c5] Jesuit higher education institutions **are devoted to an in-depth appreciation of the world that we live in. They promote an ever deeper understanding of the human person and the dynamics at work in our world - inter-personal, religious, social, physical and cultural.**[^chunk:1b557c1d-3806-5fcd-88f4-ae1e467218c5]

## What Distinguishes Contemporary Jesuit Higher Education

### Core Educational Philosophy

Jesuit education is defined as **a Catholic educational tradition rooted in the spirituality of St. Ignatius Loyola and the Society of Jesus. It combines rigorous inquiry with reflection, ethical discernment, service, and care for each student as a whole person.**[^chunk:d4f703b8-e359-5cf3-a646-0073f5112c5f] The educational approach **asks students to connect what they learn with who they are becoming and how they will contribute to the world.**[^chunk:d4f703b8-e359-5cf3-a646-0073f5112c5f]

### Key Principles of Jesuit Education

Several distinctive principles shape the contemporary Jesuit educational experience:

**Cura Personalis (Care for the Whole Person)**: **Students are known as individuals. Intellectual, emotional, social, physical, moral, and spiritual development are treated as connected parts of a meaningful education.**[^chunk:d4f703b8-e359-5cf3-a646-0073f5112c5f]

**Discernment and Reflection**: **Students learn to examine experience, recognize their values, consider consequences, and make thoughtful decisions rather than simply reacting to the next demand.**[^chunk:d4f703b8-e359-5cf3-a646-0073f5112c5f]

**Magis (Seeking the Greater Good)**: **Magis is not constant achievement for its own sake. It asks which choice can serve others more meaningfully and make the best use of a person's gifts.**[^chunk:d4f703b8-e359-5cf3-a646-0073f5112c5f]

**Care for the Shared Mission**: **Personal growth is connected to responsibility for communities and institutions. Students consider how their work can support a purpose larger than themselves.**[^chunk:d4f703b8-e359-5cf3-a646-0073f5112c5f]

**Faith That Does Justice**: **Jesuit education connects learning with human dignity, solidarity, and action. Justice is not a separate topic; it is a way of evaluating decisions and their effects.**[^chunk:d4f703b8-e359-5cf3-a646-0073f5112c5f]

**People for and With Others**: **Students are prepared to lead alongside others, listen across differences, share responsibility, and use their education in service of the common good.**[^chunk:d4f703b8-e359-5cf3-a646-0073f5112c5f]

### Ignatian Pedagogy

The pedagogical approach in Jesuit classrooms follows **the Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm is a five-part cycle that moves learning beyond information. Students consider context, engage with experience, reflect on meaning, choose a response, and evaluate growth.**[^chunk:d4f703b8-e359-5cf3-a646-0073f5112c5f] This cycle ensures that students understand content deeply and decide how to use what they have learned responsibly.

### Inclusivity and Openness to All Faiths

**All faiths Jesuit universities welcome students from different beliefs and backgrounds.**[^chunk:d4f703b8-e359-5cf3-a646-0073f5112c5f] Jesuit institutions affirm that **Jesuit education is part of the Catholic intellectual and spiritual tradition and is associated with the Society of Jesus, a Catholic religious order. It approaches faith and reason as complementary ways of pursuing truth and understanding human experience.**[^chunk:d4f703b8-e359-5cf3-a646-0073f5112c5f] However, **students are invited to explore questions of meaning, ethics, justice, and purpose while engaging respectfully with people whose beliefs may differ from their own.**[^chunk:d4f703b8-e359-5cf3-a646-0073f5112c5f]

## Mission, Justice, and Inclusivity in Modern Jesuit Institutions

### Historical Foundation

**The Society of Jesus was founded in 1540 by Saint Ignatius Loyola and his companions. The mission of the Jesuits is a mission of justice and reconciliation, working so that women and men can be reconciled with God, with themselves, with each other and with God's creation.**[^chunk:1b557c1d-3806-5fcd-88f4-ae1e467218c5]

### Online Learning and Access

Modern Jesuit institutions have adapted to contemporary challenges through innovative approaches. **Online learning is becoming increasingly prevalent and our network of Jesuit Worldwide Learning brings on-line learning opportunities to refugees in camps and to other remote places where people are without access to education. In addition the Jesuit Refugee Service also offers online courses at university level to refugees who can develop their intellectual capacities, even while they remain in camps, far away from university campuses.**[^chunk:1b557c1d-3806-5fcd-88f4-ae1e467218c5]

### Six Priority Issues for Jesuit Higher Education

Contemporary Jesuit institutions focus on six key priority areas:[^chunk:1b557c1d-3806-5fcd-88f4-ae1e467218c5]

1. **Civic and Political Leadership Formation**
2. **Formation of Ignatian Leadership**
3. **Education for Those at the Margins**
4. **Economic and Environmental Justice**
5. **Inter-Religious Dialogue/Secularism**
6. **Peace and Reconciliation Advocacy**

These priorities reflect contemporary Jesuit commitment to addressing social challenges while maintaining fidelity to their educational mission.

### Student Learning Outcomes

Jesuit higher education aims to develop specific competencies and values in its graduates. **The value is not one course or requirement. It is a durable way of learning, deciding, and relating to others that supports professional preparation and a meaningful life.**[^chunk:d4f703b8-e359-5cf3-a646-0073f5112c5f] This includes:

- **Critical Thinking**: Ask better questions, evaluate evidence, and examine assumptions before reaching a conclusion.[^chunk:d4f703b8-e359-5cf3-a646-0073f5112c5f]
- **Ethical Judgment**: Consider human consequences, competing responsibilities, and the common good when making decisions.[^chunk:d4f703b8-e359-5cf3-a646-0073f5112c5f]
- **Communication Across Differences**: Listen carefully, explain ideas clearly, and work constructively with people whose experiences differ from your own.[^chunk:d4f703b8-e359-5cf3-a646-0073f5112c5f]
- **Reflective Leadership**: Know your strengths, recognize limits, seek feedback, and lead with integrity rather than status alone.[^chunk:d4f703b8-e359-5cf3-a646-0073f5112c5f]
- **Applied Experience**: Connect academic knowledge with research, internships, service, global learning, and community projects.[^chunk:d4f703b8-e359-5cf3-a646-0073f5112c5f]
- **A Sense of Purpose**: Explore how your interests, abilities, work, relationships, and responsibilities can contribute to a life of meaning.[^chunk:d4f703b8-e359-5cf3-a646-0073f5112c5f]

## International Association of Jesuit Universities

Jesuit institutions coordinate their work through formal structures. **The International Association of Jesuit Universities (IAJU)** serves as the coordinating body for Jesuit higher education institutions worldwide. Information about locations and characteristics of Jesuit schools can be found through the IAJU website.[^chunk:1b557c1d-3806-5fcd-88f4-ae1e467218c5]
