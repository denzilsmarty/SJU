# Applying the IPP Across Disciplines: Humanities, Sciences, Business, Health, and the Arts

The Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm (IPP) — with its five dynamics of **context, experience, reflection, action, and evaluation** — is explicitly designed to be adaptable across cultures, institutions, subjects, and learners.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Far from being a universal curriculum or fixed sequence, it is a *flexible, recursive pattern* that educators can translate into the concrete demands of any discipline.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] This article describes what that translation looks like in six major disciplinary clusters at a Jesuit university such as Saint Joseph's University (SJU), answers common questions about technical and quantitative fields, and provides sample learning cycles drawn from each domain.

---

## Why the IPP Works Across All Disciplines

Before examining individual fields, two foundational points prevent common misconceptions.

### Experience Is Broader Than "Experiential Learning"

The IPP defines experience as *any direct or vicarious encounter with the subject matter that the learner appropriates through the senses, intellect, imagination, memory, and affect.*[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] This means that a sustained encounter with a difficult theorem or a close reading of a poem can be an Ignatian experience — provided it is intellectually real, personally appropriated, and open to reflection.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Laboratory experiment, archival research, clinical simulation, design critique, and mathematical proof all qualify.

### Reflection Is Not Just Journaling

Reflection in the IPP is epistemic, affective, ethical, and social. It asks both "What is true?" and "What is happening in me and among us as I encounter it?"[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] It can be analytic, dialogic, oral, visual, quantitative, embodied, or design-based.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] Personal appropriation matters; compulsory personal disclosure does not.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

### Action Includes Interior Choice

Action begins in interior appropriation — a revised judgment, professional commitment, new research question, or clarified value — and may then become outward conduct.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] A student who lacks the power to solve a structural problem during a course can still engage in genuine IPP action through a clarified professional commitment, a revised research design, or a refusal of an unethical practice.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## 1. Humanities, Theology, Philosophy, and Social Sciences

### How the IPP Looks in This Domain

IPP design in the humanities and interpretive social sciences centers on close encounter with texts, artworks, historical archives, languages, communities, and contested interpretations.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] Reflection joins critical analysis to the learner's own social location and to the public consequences of interpretation.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] Action may take the form of a revised interpretation, a public humanities product, an ethical argument, a civic intervention, or a new research question.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

The principal guardrail for these disciplines is preserving complexity: mission language must not short-circuit historical evidence, ambiguity, or critique.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

### Sample Learning Cycle: A Theology Seminar

| Dynamic | What Happens |
|---|---|
| **Context** | Students surface prior religious formation, cultural assumptions about a text, and the history of its interpretation — including who has been authorized to interpret it and who has not. |
| **Experience** | Close reading of a primary theological text alongside competing scholarly commentaries and a contemporary justice case; encounter with a community practitioner whose life the text addresses. |
| **Reflection** | Students compare interpretive frameworks, identify affective resonances and resistances, examine whose voice is absent, and ask what the text demands in their own context. |
| **Action** | A written theological argument addressed to a public audience; a revised position on a contested ethical question; or a plan for a community conversation. |
| **Evaluation** | Faculty assess textual accuracy, quality of argument, engagement with competing interpretations, and response to critique — not the student's personal belief. |

> **Key difference from other fields:** The subject matter itself often asks ultimate questions. The educator may invite engagement with those questions while protecting the student's freedom of conscience and never requiring simulated belief.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## 2. Natural Sciences, Mathematics, Computing, and Engineering

### Can the IPP Work in Quantitative or Technical Courses?

Yes. The IPP is academically serious and presupposes disciplined inquiry, mastery of subject matter, and valid methods.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Technical rigor and Ignatian formation are not alternatives.

In these disciplines, context includes the history and social organization of knowledge, access to data, environmental consequences, and the interests embedded in technical systems.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] Experience occurs in proof, experiment, modeling, observation, failure, and design.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] Reflection examines uncertainty, error, beauty, assumptions, dual use, risk, and affected communities.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] Action includes replication, redesign, communication, responsible deployment, and refusal of unsupported claims.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] Evaluation must maintain technical rigor while testing whether students can reason about use and consequence.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

Published practice literature includes a peer-reviewed case study of two STEM courses at SJU emphasizing active engagement, reflection, cura personalis, and course assessment.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

### Sample Learning Cycle: An Engineering Design Lab

| Dynamic | What Happens |
|---|---|
| **Context** | Students examine who commissioned the design problem, who will be affected, what regulatory and environmental constraints apply, and what their own assumptions about users are. |
| **Experience** | Teams work through a design challenge, encounter failure, test prototypes, and analyze data — including data from communities that would use or be displaced by the technology. |
| **Reflection** | Structured debriefs examine error, assumptions embedded in the model, dual-use risks, and the distribution of benefits and burdens across stakeholder groups. |
| **Action** | A revised design with a documented rationale for trade-offs; a communication plan for affected communities; or a stated refusal of an unsupported performance claim. |
| **Evaluation** | Faculty assess technical accuracy, design feasibility, quality of ethical reasoning about use and consequence, and the student's response to peer and instructor critique. |

> **Key difference from humanities:** Experience here is primarily empirical — laboratory work, data, proof, and failure. But reflection explicitly asks students to move beyond "Does it work?" to "For whom does it work, at what cost, and under whose authority?"[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

## 3. Business and Organizational Leadership

### What Does an IPP-Informed Business Course Look Like?

Cases, analytics, markets, clients, internships, and organizational systems provide experience in business education.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] Reflection should move beyond personal ethics to incentives, governance, externalities, labor, stakeholders, ecological limits, and the common good.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] Action may be a decision, policy, venture, investment criterion, operational redesign, or escalation of an ethical concern.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

Evaluation tests financial and strategic competence *together with* responsible judgment — not agreement with a faculty member's preferred business model.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

### Sample Learning Cycle: A Strategic Management Course

| Dynamic | What Happens |
|---|---|
| **Context** | Students examine market conditions, the firm's stakeholder ecosystem, applicable regulation, labor conditions in the supply chain, and their own prior assumptions about profit and responsibility. |
| **Experience** | Role-based case analysis of a real corporate decision, including financial data, employee testimony, community impact reports, and a competitor's alternative strategy. |
| **Reflection** | Teams compare ethical frameworks (utilitarian, stakeholder, deontological), identify incentive structures that distorted the decision, surface ecological and labor externalities, and examine whose voice was absent from the boardroom. |
| **Action** | Each team produces and defends a revised strategic recommendation with an explicit stakeholder impact analysis and an escalation protocol for ethical concerns. |
| **Evaluation** | Faculty assess financial rigor, strategic feasibility, quality of stakeholder reasoning, and accountability for unintended consequences. Students conduct a post-decision review that becomes the context for the next case. |

## 4. Health Professions and Nursing

### What Does an IPP-Informed Nursing or Clinical Course Look Like?

Clinical education naturally cycles through context, encounter, reflection, action, and review, making it one of the most naturally aligned settings for the IPP.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

- **Context** includes the patient's life, social determinants of health, team dynamics, evidence, regulation, and power.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]
- **Experience** must be bounded by consent, safety, supervision, and scope of practice.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]
- **Reflection** integrates scientific reasoning, emotion, uncertainty, dignity, bias, and professional duty.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]
- **Action** includes care, communication, advocacy, referral, and improvement.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]
- **Evaluation** should use observed performance, simulation, case reasoning, patient or preceptor feedback where appropriate, and attention to repair after error.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

A published sample learning cycle from the SJU source document centers on allocation of a scarce health resource:[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

### Sample Learning Cycle: Allocation of a Scarce Health Resource

| Dynamic | What Happens |
|---|---|
| **Context** | Students examine the health system, legal rules, histories of exclusion, institutional constraints, and their own prior assumptions about fairness and clinical priority. |
| **Experience** | Teams analyze a realistic case, relevant data, patient narratives, and the roles of clinicians, administrators, and families. |
| **Reflection** | Students compare ethical frameworks, identify affective responses and cognitive biases, test assumptions, and listen to perspectives with unequal power. |
| **Action** | Each team produces and defends an allocation protocol and communication plan; individuals articulate what they would do in role. |
| **Evaluation** | Faculty assess technical accuracy, ethical reasoning, feasibility, stakeholder engagement, and response to critique. Students conduct a post-decision review; feedback becomes the context for a revised case. |

> **A note on boundaries:** The IPP's reflective dimension can cross into therapeutic territory in health-professions education. Educators must distinguish academic reflection from counseling, maintain clear referral protocols, and never require disclosure of personal trauma.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## 5. Education and Human Development

### How the IPP Applies to Teacher and Counselor Formation

Teacher and counselor formation can use the IPP to connect theory, field experience, professional identity, institutional conditions, and responsibility.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] Candidates should learn to:

- diagnose learner context without stereotyping;
- design accessible experience;
- facilitate non-coercive reflection;
- act on evidence; and
- revise practice.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

Boundaries between teaching, counseling, spiritual accompaniment, and clinical care must be made explicit.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

A qualitative study of Ignatian pedagogy with teacher educators in India found the paradigm's potential for teacher identity and vocation while identifying the need for deeper social analysis, leadership support, and sustained formation.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

### Sample Learning Cycle: A Teaching Residency Seminar

| Dynamic | What Happens |
|---|---|
| **Context** | Candidates examine the school's community demographics, the history of the curriculum, their own educational background and identities, and the power dynamics of the classroom. |
| **Experience** | Supervised field placement; observation of an experienced mentor; analysis of real student work and assessment data from the cooperating classroom. |
| **Reflection** | Candidates compare their planned lesson with what actually happened, examine affective responses to student behavior, and analyze whose learning was most and least supported. |
| **Action** | A revised lesson plan with a written rationale; a change to an assessment rubric; or a targeted intervention for a student whose needs were previously unmet. |
| **Evaluation** | Supervisor and candidate together review observed performance, student outcomes, and the quality of reflective reasoning — not the candidate's personal beliefs about education. |

## 6. Arts, Design, and Communication

### How the IPP Applies to Making and Performance

Making and performance create rich, natural cycles of experience, critique, revision, and public action.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] Reflection in these disciplines includes aesthetic judgment, audience, representation, authorship, cultural appropriation, and the social consequences of media.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] Evaluation can integrate craft, concept, process, response to critique, and responsibility to subjects and audiences.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

A published application demonstrates that the IPP is not limited to service-learning: a Spanish and literature classroom study shows IPP cycles operating entirely within textual and cultural analysis.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

### Sample Learning Cycle: A Design Studio Critique

| Dynamic | What Happens |
|---|---|
| **Context** | Students examine the brief's intended audience, the cultural context in which the work will circulate, and histories of representation relevant to the project's subject matter. |
| **Experience** | Students produce a design artifact or performance; they encounter audience response through structured critique and peer review; they engage with source communities where relevant. |
| **Reflection** | Guided critique asks: What choices were made and why? Whose aesthetic and cultural assumptions shaped the work? What is the work doing to its subjects and audiences? What would a different set of assumptions produce? |
| **Action** | A revised artifact or performance with documented rationale; a public presentation or exhibition; or a written statement of the ethical commitments embedded in the creative process. |
| **Evaluation** | Faculty assess craft and technical skill, conceptual clarity, quality of response to critique, process documentation, and the student's accountability to subjects and audiences. |

## Common Questions Across All Disciplines

### Must every lesson use all five IPP elements?

No. The paradigm should be visible across an appropriate learning arc. Some moments primarily provide experience; others deepen reflection or evaluate action. Over a course or program, all five should be meaningfully present and connected.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

### Is the IPP compatible with technical or quantitative rigor?

Yes. The IPP presupposes disciplined inquiry, mastery of subject matter, and valid methods. Reflection without knowledge becomes opinion; action without analysis becomes impulse.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Evaluation must maintain technical rigor while testing whether students can reason about use and consequence.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

### Does action require community service or political advocacy?

No. Action includes interior choice and responsible application. Service and advocacy are important forms when educationally and ethically appropriate, but so are applying a concept to a novel problem, revising a design, making a professional commitment, or defining a new research question.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300][^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

### How does the IPP handle pluralism in a multi-faith classroom?

The paradigm is described in its source text as *profoundly human* and accessible to people who do not share Christian belief.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] At SJU, faithful pluralism means naming Catholic and Jesuit commitments without disguising them while protecting every student's freedom of conscience, allowing multiple traditions to illuminate inquiry, and never requiring simulated belief or private spiritual disclosure.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

### What does evaluation look like beyond a grade?

The IPP recognizes at least four objects of evaluation: academic learning, quality of judgment, action and impact, and educational design.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Faculty may assess the quality of ethical analysis, reflective practice, professional responsibility, and evidence-based reasoning. They should not claim to measure a student's conscience, faith, compassion, or moral worth.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
