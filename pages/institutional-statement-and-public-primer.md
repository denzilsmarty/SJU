## Overview

This article addresses Saint Joseph's University's (SJU) proposed institutional statement on the Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm (IPP), the rationale and audience for a public-facing primer, and how that primer relates to the full source-of-truth guide. It draws on the draft working document *Forming the Future* (Version 0.1, 23 August 2026).[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## SJU's Proposed Institutional Statement

The draft source-of-truth document proposes the following institutional statement for discussion:

> *At Saint Joseph's University, the Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm is a shared, adaptable way of designing and reviewing educational work. Rooted in the Jesuit tradition and open to people of all faiths and convictions, it begins with the concrete learner and context; creates rigorous encounters with knowledge, practice, persons, and the world; cultivates disciplined reflection; supports free and responsible action; and evaluates learning, impact, and design in order to begin again. It serves SJU's mission to prepare students for personal excellence, professional success, and engaged citizenship through education of the whole person.*[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

The document explicitly notes that this statement is an **SJU interpretation proposed for discussion** and is not a quotation from a Society of Jesus document.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

The statement is designed to function as an integrating architecture across SJU's General Education Program, Cornerstone Core, professional accreditation requirements, and unit strategies — giving them a common process language without replacing any of them.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

### Eight Institutional Commitments

If SJU formally adopts the IPP as a shared educational framework, the draft proposes eight corresponding commitments:[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

1. **Disciplinary rigor** — IPP work meets the epistemic and professional standards of each field.
2. **Whole-person attention** — Learning engages intellect, affect, imagination, embodiment, relationship, and responsibility.
3. **Freedom and pluralism** — Inquiry is non-coercive and protects conscience and academic freedom.
4. **Care with challenge** — *Cura personalis* joins personal knowledge and support to high expectations.
5. **Justice and the common good** — Context and consequence include excluded people, structural conditions, and creation.
6. **Integration** — Core, professional, co-curricular, and relational learning reinforce one another.
7. **Evidence and revision** — Claims are tested, consequences reviewed, and designs improved.
8. **Institutional congruence** — Policies, resources, incentives, and technologies support the educational commitments they proclaim.

## The Public-Facing IPP Primer

### What Is the Primer?

The Implementation Roadmap in the draft document calls for Phase 1 activities to include the production of **a concise public primer derived from the full guide**.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] The primer is positioned as a downstream product — created after the full source-of-truth document has been reviewed, terminology resolved, and an authority policy for sources approved.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

### Intended Audience

The draft does not enumerate a single audience for the primer in precise terms. However, its placement within Phase 1 — which focuses on establishing shared meaning across faculty governance, Mission, academic leadership, student-facing divisions, assessment, accessibility offices, and students — suggests the primer is aimed at the **broader SJU community**, including those who need accessible orientation without the full analytical depth of the source-of-truth document.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

The draft separately identifies three levels at which the full source-of-truth guide itself should be used: orientation sections for common understanding, design sections for faculty and staff, and governance sections for institutional leaders.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] The primer is understood to serve the orientation function for audiences who do not require the full design and governance detail.

### Will the Primer Be Available to Prospective Students and Families?

The draft document does not explicitly address whether the primer will be made available to prospective students or families.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] No claim to that effect appears in the available source material, and readers seeking a definitive answer on external distribution should consult SJU's communications or enrollment offices once the Phase 1 process is complete.

### How the Primer Differs from the Full Source-of-Truth Guide

The full draft guide (*Forming the Future*, Version 0.1) is an extensive, authority-ranked working document that:[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

- Establishes a four-tier source hierarchy for IPP claims (from foundational Society documents down to institutional practice and scholarship);
- Defines each of the five dynamics with sufficient precision to support curriculum, program, and policy design;
- Provides historical evolution from Ignatius through the 1993 ICAJE text and beyond;
- Includes a design canvas, a review rubric, a glossary, an annotated primary-source guide, and an implementation roadmap;
- Addresses specialized applications (STEM, health professions, arts, AI-shaped learning, co-curricular settings, and governance); and
- Critically evaluates the empirical evidence base and articulates calibrated claims.

The public primer, by contrast, is described only as **concise** and **derived from** the full guide.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] It is not itself a policy document or a controlling authority; the primary documents of the Society of Jesus remain controlling for claims about the tradition, and the full source-of-truth guide functions as the internal standard for SJU design and discussion.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## How the Institutional Statement Handles Pluralism and Non-Catholic Educators

Pluralism is treated not as a compromise of fidelity but as **compatible with fidelity**. The draft draws directly on the 1993 ICAJE source text, which presents the IPP as "faith-inspired and profoundly human, accessible to people who do not share Christian belief."[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

The proposed institutional statement itself opens with the phrase "open to people of all faiths and convictions," making pluralism explicit at the level of SJU's defining language.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

The document operationalizes this through a set of concrete commitments labeled **faithful pluralism**:[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

- The University names its Catholic and Jesuit commitments without disguising them.
- Students encounter theological and philosophical questions with intellectual seriousness.
- People of every faith and of no religious affiliation are treated as participants, not mission objects.
- No student is required to simulate belief or disclose private spiritual experience.
- Multiple traditions and lived perspectives can illuminate the search for truth and justice.
- Common educational goods — integrity, wisdom, solidarity, responsibility, care, and the common good — are pursued through reasoned dialogue.

Regarding **non-Catholic educators** specifically, the common-questions section of the draft answers directly: *"Yes. The source text explicitly presents the paradigm as accessible to those who do not share Christian faith. Authentic use requires honesty about the tradition's roots, respect for pluralism, and commitment to its human and educational demands."*[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

The institutional commitments reinforce this by requiring that "inquiry is non-coercive and protects conscience and academic freedom" and explicitly prohibiting evaluation of a student's faith, conscience, political conformity, or moral worth.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940][^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## Governance Guardrails for the Institutional Statement

The draft is careful to constrain how the institutional statement and its related framework may be applied institutionally. SJU should not:[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

- Certify individuals as "Ignatian" based on a short course;
- Require performative mission language in every syllabus; or
- Use the IPP rubric for personnel judgment without validated purpose, due process, and contextual interpretation.

Implementation is to be **invitational, developmental, and evidence-generating**, while institutional expectations remain clear where mission responsibilities are formally assigned.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]
