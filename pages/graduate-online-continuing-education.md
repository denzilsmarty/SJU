## Overview

The Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm (IPP)—with its five dynamics of **context, experience, reflection, action, and evaluation**—applies across all educational settings at Saint Joseph's University, including graduate, online, adult, and continuing education programs.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] The 1993 source text, *Ignatian Pedagogy: A Practical Approach*, explicitly names colleges, universities, and other educational services as settings for the paradigm, and states that it can structure academic and non-academic experiences alike.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Adapting the IPP to non-traditional formats requires disciplined translation rather than wholesale importation of assumptions built for residential, undergraduate, synchronous classrooms.

## The Adult Learner: Context Is Not Optional

The SJU source document states directly that **adult learners bring substantial professional and life experience**, and that **context should recognize—rather than romanticize—it**.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] This is a pointed caution: treating prior experience as mere biographical backdrop underserves adult learners, while treating it as automatically transformative romanticizes what still requires critical examination.

Context in the IPP is not a demographic preface completed once at the start of a semester. It is an ongoing inquiry into what makes learning possible, difficult, meaningful, risky, or distorted.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] For adult and continuing education students, relevant contextual factors include:

- **Work and caregiving conditions** that limit time and availability[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **Licensure standards, organizational constraints, and the moral ecology of professional practice**[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **Financial, family, and community obligations** that shape how and when learning occurs[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **Power relations within workplaces** that may constrain what learners can safely disclose or act upon[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

Context can become **stereotyping** if educators infer beliefs or capacity from professional role or life stage. It can become **voyeurism** if students are pressed to disclose workplace difficulties or private experience. It can become **accommodation without challenge** if care is detached from academic purpose. The proper standard is *informed adaptation with high expectations and appropriate support*.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## Translating the IPP to Online and Asynchronous Environments

### Context in Online Learning

Online and asynchronous settings introduce a distinct layer of contextual factors that must be explicitly designed for. The source document lists **time zones, bandwidth, digital literacy, surveillance, platform design, and the conditions under which students are using AI** as part of context in online learning.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Educators must also account for whether students are accessing course materials from a workplace, a home with dependents, or a location with unreliable connectivity.

The IPP's requirement of *cura personalis*—care for the person in their concrete particularity—does not disappear in an online modality; it becomes harder to enact without intentional design.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] The source document's design rubric describes strong relational design as involving **trust, clarity, availability, learner agency, and appropriate boundaries** that embody cura personalis.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

### Experience in Asynchronous Formats

The IPP defines experience broadly: **any direct or vicarious encounter with the subject matter that the learner appropriates through the senses, intellect, imagination, memory, and affect**.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] This means online learning is not structurally disadvantaged. Texts, data, testimony, cases, images, simulations, historical imagination, and close reading can all constitute Ignatian experience when learners engage them cognitively and affectively.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

The design standard requires that strong experience be:
- Aligned to a significant learning purpose
- Authentic enough to reveal the complexity of the subject
- Accessible and safe enough for meaningful participation
- Structured so that learners must *notice* rather than merely attend
- Capable of engaging appropriate affect without exploiting it
- Followed by reflection, response, and feedback[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

For online programs specifically, **compressed and online formats require intentionally designed presence, reflection, and feedback**.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] The word *intentionally* is load-bearing: what happens naturally through in-person proximity must be deliberately constructed in asynchronous design.

### Reflection Without Real-Time Dialogue

Reflection is the pivotal dynamic connecting experience to responsible action.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] In asynchronous environments, it need not be synchronous or spoken. Reflection may be **analytic, dialogic, oral, visual, quantitative, embodied, or design-based**; what matters is personal appropriation, not disclosure.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

Useful asynchronous practices derived from the source document include:
- Analytic memos and structured written debriefs
- Comparison of interpretations across discussion boards
- Portfolio annotations and research positionality statements
- Peer dialogue via asynchronous video or text
- Ethical matrices and pre- or post-mortems[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

A critical guardrail applies in all modalities: reflection becomes **coercive** when the instructor predetermines the morally acceptable insight, and **performative** when students learn to imitate mission language for a grade.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] These risks are heightened in online environments where students may feel less safe questioning an implied correct answer.

### Action in Professional Contexts

For adult and continuing education students, **action is often immediate application in a workplace**.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] This is a significant asset: the IPP cycle can close more quickly and authentically when a learner can test a revised clinical protocol, implement a redesigned team process, or apply a new ethical framework within days of a course session.

The source document clarifies that action need not be civic or service-oriented to be genuine IPP action. It encompasses:
- Applying a concept to a novel problem
- Revising a design, argument, diagnosis, or performance
- Changing a habit of attention or collaboration
- Making a professional or vocational choice
- Defining a new research question
- Committing to learn, consult, or repair before taking further action[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Action should be **proportionate to the learner's knowledge, role, and power**, and should distinguish urgency from haste, intent from impact, and service *for* others from solidarity *with* others.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## Ignatian Formation at a Distance: Is It Possible?

The question of whether graduate professional programs can maintain Ignatian formation at a distance is real, but the source document does not treat online delivery as categorically incompatible with formation. Formation is defined as **lifelong, accompanied integration of knowledge, experience, reflection, relationships, faith or ultimate meaning, and responsible action toward greater freedom and service**.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Formation is a horizon, not a location.

The IPP contributes to formation **when repeated educational cycles become habits**: attending carefully, entering experience, reflecting deeply, choosing responsibly, and learning from results.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Repetition of this cycle across a graduate program—even asynchronously—can build those habits.

However, the source document does draw a firm line around the relational core. **AI can assist the relationship; it must not counterfeit it.** Systems cannot bear care, mutual obligation, fiduciary responsibility, or moral accountability.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] The same logic applies to purely transactional online design: cura personalis in advising, mentoring, and feedback cannot be fully automated. The document states that for advising, "consequential conversations require identifiable human responsibility and escalation."[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

Formation at a distance is therefore possible but requires:
- Explicit design of relational touchpoints (not merely content delivery)
- Faculty and staff who know their learners as concrete persons[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- Structured progression across a program so that IPP cycles accumulate rather than remain episodic[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- Institutional policies, workloads, and technologies that support rather than undermine the educational work they proclaim[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940]

## Protecting Learners: Evaluation, Privacy, and Disclosure

### Evaluation of Transfer and Consequence

For continuing education and professional programs, **evaluation should examine transfer and consequence**—what learners did with learning after the course—**while protecting employees from demands to disclose confidential or professionally risky information**.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] This is a notable adaptation: what works as a reflective prompt in an undergraduate course may create genuine professional jeopardy for a mid-career learner asked to describe an ethical failure at their institution.

The broader evaluation standard requires that faculty **not claim to measure the state of a student's conscience, compassion, faith, political virtue, or human worth**.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Assessable evidence includes:
- Specificity of observation
- Use of relevant concepts
- Acknowledgment of uncertainty
- Examination of assumptions
- Engagement with affected perspectives
- Proportionality of response
- Revision after feedback
- Analysis of consequences[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

### Privacy and Reflective Boundaries

SJU practice should distinguish academic reflection from therapy, confession, and spiritual direction; never require disclosure of trauma, diagnosis, immigration status, faith practice, or other sensitive information unless essential, lawful, and appropriately protected; offer alternative prompts that preserve the learning objective; and explain who will read reflective work and how it will be used.[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] These guardrails are heightened in professional programs where learners may fear that personal disclosures could affect licensing, employment, or relationships with supervisors.

## AI and Online Learning: A Distinctive Challenge

The source document notes that generative AI changes the context of nearly every educational encounter, making fluent synthesis, drafting, translation, tutoring, simulation, and feedback widely available—while also enabling fabrication, surveillance, bias amplification, deskilling, and the counterfeit appearance of understanding.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] In online graduate and continuing education, where assessment surveillance is both more tempting and more contested, the IPP offers a principled alternative frame.

The proper institutional question is **not simply whether AI is allowed, but what forms of human judgment, authorship, relationship, and responsibility the educational design is meant to cultivate**.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

IPP-informed, AI-resilient assessment in online formats favors:
- Significant experience and staged work
- Oral explanation and process evidence
- Local and personal application without compulsory disclosure
- Collaborative critique and revision
- Making thinking and judgment visible while preserving productive intellectual friction[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

Context for online AI use requires identifying the system, owner, data practices, model limitations, access differences, disciplinary norms, environmental and labor costs, and institutional policy.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Students should know when they are interacting with a machine, how decisions are escalated to a person, and which matters will never be delegated without meaningful human review.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

## Assessing IPP Quality in Online Course Design

The SJU rubric for reviewing an IPP-informed design evaluates six dimensions: **purpose, context, experience, reflection, action, evaluation, and relationship**. The rubric distinguishes *emerging*, *developing*, and *strong* designs.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] Applied to online graduate and continuing education, the key indicators are:

| Dimension | Strong Design in Online/Adult Context |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Academic and formative purposes are integrated, specific, and assessable without moral scoring[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] |
| **Context** | Learner, disciplinary, social, institutional, and power contexts—including digital conditions, work constraints, and professional histories—shape the design[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] |
| **Experience** | Significant direct or vicarious encounter engages disciplined attention and appropriate affect; accessible across time zones and bandwidth constraints[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] |
| **Reflection** | Evidence, assumptions, affect, perspectives, consequences, and discernment are integrated; private or impersonal options are available; disclosure is not required[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] |
| **Action** | Interior and/or outward action is proportionate, free, role-aware, accountable, and immediately applicable in a professional setting[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] |
| **Evaluation** | Mastery, judgment, consequence, equity, and design are reviewed; feedback begins a new cycle; confidentiality risks are addressed[^chunk:a6c62be7-4932-5dfc-b4ea-52911d9de940] |
| **Relationship** | Trust, clarity, availability, learner agency, and appropriate boundaries embody cura personalis even at a distance[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] |

This rubric evaluates a design, not the virtue of an educator or student, and should support peer consultation and improvement rather than compliance scoring detached from context.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

A useful curriculum map for online programs identifies introduction, reinforcement, and advanced demonstration of key capabilities; signature experiences and who can access them; structured reflection before, during, and after experience; increasing authenticity and consequence in action; and program evidence including alumni, employer, and student data.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

## Evidence and Limitations

The IPP's empirical research base is more modest than its documentary and practice-based legitimacy within Jesuit education.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] DeFeo's 2009 study of U.S. Jesuit teaching and learning centers found broad familiarity and perceived viability but also uneven implementation, with **action less prominent than context, experience, and reflection**.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] This imbalance may be especially acute in compressed online formats where time for discerned action and its review is limited.

The literature generally reports feasibility, reflective depth, integration of values and disciplinary learning, or perceived formation, but less often uses comparative designs, longitudinal measures, or large multi-institutional samples.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] SJU should use calibrated claims and pursue a local evidence agenda combining student work, course and program assessment, belonging and access data, qualitative accounts, alumni outcomes, and institutional indicators.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]
