# International Association of Jesuit Universities and Global Perspectives

## Role of the International Association of Jesuit Universities

The International Association of Jesuit Universities (IAJU) serves as a coordinating body for institutional leaders from Jesuit universities across the globe.[^chunk:8820ebfc-cf61-5234-877e-01fcfad45b27] The organization convenes assemblies to reflect on the mission of Jesuit higher education in contemporary contexts and to develop strategic initiatives for institutional transformation.[^chunk:8820ebfc-cf61-5234-877e-01fcfad45b27]

## Educational Transformation and Contemporary Challenges

### The Call for Transformation

At the 2025 IAJU Assembly held in Bogota, Colombia, Father General Arturo Sosa urged Jesuit universities to become "witnesses of hope, creative presences, and spaces for dialogue" in an increasingly fragmented and secular world.[^chunk:8820ebfc-cf61-5234-877e-01fcfad45b27] This assembly brought together institutional leaders to address the mission of Jesuit education amid contemporary societal challenges.

### Strategic Objectives

Fr Luis Fernando Múnera, Rector of the host Pontificia Universidad Javeriana, and Fr Joseph Christie, Secretary of Fr General for Higher Education, introduced two primary strategic objectives: developing concrete plans for institutional transformation and adopting a new five-year strategic plan.[^chunk:8820ebfc-cf61-5234-877e-01fcfad45b27]

## Global Perspectives on Higher Education

### Regional Visions

Jesuit leaders articulate diverse yet complementary visions for contemporary higher education across different regional contexts:

**United States**: Dr Tania Tetlow from Fordham University highlighted the growing crisis facing universities in the United States and stressed the importance of forming students in hope and courage, with emphasis on building a loving community.[^chunk:8820ebfc-cf61-5234-877e-01fcfad45b27]

**Asia-Pacific**: Fr Roberto Yap from Ateneo de Manila University in the Philippines advocated for a reinvented educational model that fosters ethical reasoning, interdisciplinary growth, and integral formation.[^chunk:8820ebfc-cf61-5234-877e-01fcfad45b27]

**Global Resonance**: Participants from Kenya, India, Japan, France, and other locations echoed common themes including reconciliation, ecological justice, spiritual identity, and interdisciplinary learning as pathways for educational formation.[^chunk:8820ebfc-cf61-5234-877e-01fcfad45b27]

### Navigating Secularism and Faith

Jesuit leaders emphasize the need to maintain Catholic identity while engaging authentically with secular contexts. Mgr Carlo Maria Polvani, Secretary of the Dicastery for Education and Culture, reframed secularism as a sociocultural phenomenon rather than a hostile ideology, urging Jesuit institutions to promote dialogue between faith and reason while modeling a "culture of encounter."[^chunk:8820ebfc-cf61-5234-877e-01fcfad45b27]

### Best Practices in Inclusive Mission

Jesuit institutions worldwide demonstrate how to maintain spiritual identity within diverse student populations:

**Indonesia**: At Sanata Dharma University, Fr Albertus Bagus Laksana described how the university engages with secularism in a predominantly Muslim context, with the majority Muslim student body engaging Ignatian spirituality through inclusive retreats and shared moral commitments, embracing hybrid religious identities as tools for reconciliation.[^chunk:8820ebfc-cf61-5234-877e-01fcfad45b27]

**United States**: Dr Katia Passerini from Gonzaga University emphasized that although many students today do not identify with a religion, they remain deeply concerned about purpose, belonging, and meaning, recommending the integration of spiritual seeking into the curriculum through dialogue and the integration of faith and reason across disciplines.[^chunk:8820ebfc-cf61-5234-877e-01fcfad45b27]

**Latin America**: Father Rafael Fernández from Antonio Ruiz de Montoya University in Peru emphasized the importance of witnessing faith through action and urged institutions to deepen their mission using Ignatian pedagogy amid declining Catholic affiliation in the region.[^chunk:8820ebfc-cf61-5234-877e-01fcfad45b27]

## Vision for Contemporary Higher Education

Jesuit leaders articulate a comprehensive vision that emphasizes Catholic identity must coexist authentically with diversity, as inclusion enriches the Jesuit mission in universities. The call is to lead with creativity in building bridges across traditions while grounding education in both spiritual and intellectual depth.[^chunk:8820ebfc-cf61-5234-877e-01fcfad45b27]
