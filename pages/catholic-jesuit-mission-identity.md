## Overview

Catholic and Jesuit colleges and universities in the United States articulate and maintain their distinctive identity through a comprehensive framework rooted in both institutional mission and religious charism. The "Catholic, Jesuit" character is not merely one characteristic among others but rather the defining identity of these institutions.[^chunk:6d0aa5a0-fc38-56ab-af44-26088f9006ff] This identity shapes how these twenty-eight universities operate, govern themselves, and fulfill their apostolic mission within the broader context of the Catholic Church and the Society of Jesus.[^chunk:6d0aa5a0-fc38-56ab-af44-26088f9006ff]

## Defining Catholic and Jesuit Identity

### The Primary Mission

The defining mission of Jesuit colleges and universities centers on education and formation: "The real measure of our Jesuit universities lies in who our students become."[^chunk:6d0aa5a0-fc38-56ab-af44-26088f9006ff] This mission prioritizes "the education and formation of our students for the sake of the kind of persons they become and their wide influence for good in society in their lives, professions, and service."[^chunk:6d0aa5a0-fc38-56ab-af44-26088f9006ff]

As universities, these institutions maintain essential academic dimensions including "highly qualified professors and colleagues acting with academic freedom for the sake of the full pursuit of the truth and the students' free attainment of knowledge and values."[^chunk:6d0aa5a0-fc38-56ab-af44-26088f9006ff] They also "exercise an intellectual apostolate vital to the Society of Jesus and long a recognized characteristic of its tradition in teaching, in scholarship, and within the community of other scholars and intellectuals for the sake of the advancement of knowledge and the service of society."[^chunk:6d0aa5a0-fc38-56ab-af44-26088f9006ff]

### Catholic Identity and Ex Corde Ecclesiae

These institutions identify as "Catholic colleges and universities" and "see ourselves as an important ministry of the Catholic Church."[^chunk:6d0aa5a0-fc38-56ab-af44-26088f9006ff] They are "committed to and guided by the official understanding of a Catholic university as articulated in the Church's document Ex Corde Ecclesiae (From the Heart of the Church)."[^chunk:6d0aa5a0-fc38-56ab-af44-26088f9006ff]

Their Catholic mission encompasses multiple essential services to the Church:[^chunk:6d0aa5a0-fc38-56ab-af44-26088f9006ff]
- Educating and forming an adult Catholic laity
- Continuing to educate first-generation Catholic immigrant populations
- Developing dialogue between Church and culture
- Providing a forum to address important issues of Church and society
- Making available scholarly and educational resources to the Church
- Supporting ecumenical and interfaith dialogue
- Making contact with and representing the Church to many persons it would not otherwise encounter

This work occurs "within the essential framework of faith and reason, which mutually confirm and advance each other."[^chunk:6d0aa5a0-fc38-56ab-af44-26088f9006ff]

### Jesuit and Ignatian Character

As "Jesuit colleges and universities, we are a continuation of the Ignatian heritage and of the distinctive tradition of Jesuit education."[^chunk:6d0aa5a0-fc38-56ab-af44-26088f9006ff] This heritage means that "St. Ignatius, with his charism and his Spiritual Exercises, inspires and gives shape to how we educate in a way that seeks God in all things, promotes discernment, and 'engages the world through a careful analysis of context, in dialogue with experience, evaluated through reflection, for the sake of action, and with openness, always, to evaluation'."[^chunk:6d0aa5a0-fc38-56ab-af44-26088f9006ff]

The Jesuit identity includes "a clear relationship with the Society of Jesus which is formalized in written understandings, sharing the Society's 'commitment to a faith that does justice through interreligious dialogue and a creative engagement with culture',"[^chunk:6d0aa5a0-fc38-56ab-af44-26088f9006ff] and "a willingness to have our mission as Jesuit educational works evaluated as being in alignment with the overall mission of the Society of Jesus."[^chunk:6d0aa5a0-fc38-56ab-af44-26088f9006ff]

## Apostolic Mission and Academic Freedom

### Integration of Apostolic Mission with Academic Excellence

These institutions operate with the principle that "an institution cannot be an excellent Catholic and Jesuit university unless it fulfills its mission to be an excellent university within the American academy, and therefore values highly academic freedom and peer review."[^chunk:1365ed6d-fe18-5907-9d0e-a85b74088b03]

The relationship between apostolic mission and academic freedom involves navigating important tensions. The institutions "live a tension in our relationship with bishops because of our visibility, our representation of the Church in the minds of many, our attempts to live the reality of being in harmony with the Magisterium together with upholding academic freedom."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

### The Intellectual Apostolate

"Essential to our rationale as Jesuit colleges and universities is how we are committed and contribute to the Society's 'apostolic preference' of the intellectual apostolate."[^chunk:03ee3a20-dcf0-5cae-84e8-e9cdc45777fd] This commitment "emphasizes the Society's apostolic priority of the service of faith through the promotion of justice dynamically related to cultural and interreligious dialogue."[^chunk:03ee3a20-dcf0-5cae-84e8-e9cdc45777fd]

The intellectual apostolate is pursued "through formative teaching, conscientious scholarship, and public dialogue."[^chunk:03ee3a20-dcf0-5cae-84e8-e9cdc45777fd]

### Tensions Between Academic Freedom and Church Teaching

There is acknowledged tension regarding academic freedom and fidelity to Church teaching. "There is, at times, mutual misunderstanding between some bishops and some universities on what 'academic freedom' rightly requires of Jesuit, Catholic universities."[^chunk:cb2464e4-28b9-5530-b7aa-f402d6bd4953] Additionally, universities are called to "host and moderate open and vigorous debate on contested questions some of which can make Church officials anxious or cause them to presume that we are failing our mission when these questions entail the consideration of issues that are contrary to the Church's teaching."[^chunk:cb2464e4-28b9-5530-b7aa-f402d6bd4953]

## Governance and Trustee Responsibility

### Board Structure and Authority

"The full responsibility for the policy, governance, and operation of each of our colleges and universities rests with its board of directors, trustees, or regents as established by articles of incorporation and/or by-laws."[^chunk:03ee3a20-dcf0-5cae-84e8-e9cdc45777fd] Importantly, "Each of these institutions is legally and functionally independent of the Society of Jesus and its provinces and institutions."[^chunk:03ee3a20-dcf0-5cae-84e8-e9cdc45777fd]

### Fiduciary Responsibility for Mission

"The board has fiduciary responsibility for—holds in trust—what enables the institution to be what it is. Most importantly, it holds in trust the Catholic, Jesuit character of the college or university and has a responsibility to understand, assure, provide resources to support, foster, and assess this character."[^chunk:03ee3a20-dcf0-5cae-84e8-e9cdc45777fd]

### Board Composition and Commitment to Mission

The self-evaluation instrument for Jesuit institutions identifies key questions regarding board trustee commitment:[^chunk:387f2052-d956-5e9d-bf9b-68d11a7d4d14]
- Do the bylaws of the University state its mission as a university and its identity as Catholic and Jesuit?
- Are trustees regularly oriented to the implications of this mission and identity for an academic institution?
- Are they introduced to the Jesuit charism in higher education?
- Are there opportunities for trustees to grow in their own spiritual development and faith life?

### Leadership Appointment and Mission Fidelity

For university presidents, boards should "seek committed Catholics or those who have a deep familiarity with the Jesuit tradition as well as the traditions and commitments of the academy as its President."[^chunk:387f2052-d956-5e9d-bf9b-68d11a7d4d14] The President and cabinet should be "able and willing to articulate those key principles which comprise the Catholic and Jesuit identity of the institution."[^chunk:387f2052-d956-5e9d-bf9b-68d11a7d4d14]

### Dialogue with Provincials

A critical governance mechanism involves trustee engagement with Jesuit leadership. "The more the board and the president can have an open and ongoing dialogue with the provincial and a spirit of working in welcomed partnership between them, the more the issues and opportunities of relationship with the Society of Jesus—for the good of both the mission of the Society and the college/university—can be addressed and mutually supported."[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]

Regarding presidential selection, boards should ensure that "provincials to play an important role in the process by which the university, through its governing board, chooses the president of the college or university."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730] Once appointed, "the new president, whether Jesuit or not, appointed by the board, should be 'missioned' to this Jesuit apostolic aspect of his responsibility by the provincial on behalf of the Society of Jesus."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

## Institutional Mechanisms for Maintaining Identity

### Comprehensive Approaches

"Each of our colleges and universities in its own way gives priority of attention to being faithful to, deepening, and applying this Catholic, Jesuit character in what it does."[^chunk:6d0aa5a0-fc38-56ab-af44-26088f9006ff] The specific means employed "run into the hundreds, are well resourced and staffed within our institutions, are coordinated by a person charged with responsibility to further this character, and are shared across our schools and our association so that we learn from and build on the experience of one another."[^chunk:6d0aa5a0-fc38-56ab-af44-26088f9006ff]

### Leadership Commitment

"The University's leadership competently communicates and enlivens the Jesuit, Catholic mission of the institution."[^chunk:1365ed6d-fe18-5907-9d0e-a85b74088b03] This includes ensuring that presidents and academic deans are "able and willing to articulate those key principles which comprise the Catholic and Jesuit identity of the institution."[^chunk:387f2052-d956-5e9d-bf9b-68d11a7d4d14]

### Faculty and Staff Formation

Institutions have "created responsibilities, structures, and programs for the hiring, orienting, and developing of faculty and staff according to our Catholic, Jesuit mission."[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021] These include "special retreats, seminars in Ignatian spirituality and Jesuit education, programs and colloquia which seek to enhance Catholic, Jesuit identity, development and scholarship opportunities, service and immersion experiences, special events that focus on our mission."[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]

The challenge of lay leadership is significant. There is an "inadequately trained lay persons in the spiritual tradition of the Society of Jesus" and a need to "better promote the Spiritual Exercises as a resource for maintaining the spirit and character of our Universities."[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9] Moreover, "we need to hire persons interested in and capable of doing the Exercises."[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9]

### Hiring Practices for Mission

Institutions are encouraged to approach "hiring and recruitment with the intention of preserving and augmenting its Jesuit, Catholic mission across the institution."[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9] This includes incorporating "mission-specific language included in job postings that signals the Catholic and Jesuit mission and identity of the institution."[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9]

## Jesuit Presence and Community

### Value of Jesuit Presence

"The presence, work, and witness of Jesuits on our campuses with our students, colleagues, and alumni are of inestimable value."[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021] Students and colleagues "respect and identify with Jesuits as being at the heart of Jesuit education. The impression Jesuits make and the apostolic impact they have is profound."[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]

### Role of Jesuit Communities

"We see the role of the Jesuit community as a community first of all as corporately incarnating, supporting, and animating the Catholic, Jesuit character of our colleges and universities."[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021] "It is important that the community does this in collaboration with and in mutual support of faculty, staff, students, and board members."[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]

"It makes a difference to how our colleges and universities are Jesuit that there is a Jesuit community at the heart of the university in addition to there being individual Jesuits."[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021] The community's unique contribution includes "hospitality, prayer, cohesion, and residing presence as a group of Jesuits personalizes and concretizes the Jesuit character in a relational way."[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]

### Declining Numbers and Adaptation

"We are realistic that we will have fewer Jesuits in our colleges or universities in the future and that we will need with time to develop new models and strategies for missioning Jesuits."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730] However, "We do not believe they can realistically maintain their Jesuit identity unless Jesuits are assigned to and work within them."[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]

The impact of Jesuit presence "does not depend completely on there being a sizable number of Jesuits, but at least some, who visibly represent by their presence the commitment and engagement of Jesuits with the college or university."[^chunk:1368ef19-9790-5108-883f-b7bdeb233021]

## Relationship with Bishops and the Catholic Church

### Strategic Importance

"We are often the ministry and dimension of the Society most in contact and communication with bishops and therefore have an opportunity of collaboration, mutual support, and personal relationship which is unique and is of special significance for the relationship of the whole Society of Jesus with the episcopacy."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

### Communication and Understanding

"We have learned that there is no substitute for direct and frequent communication between presidents and bishops. This can help resolve or at least ameliorate some of the lived tension we experience."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730] "At times the anxieties which bishops experience and the pressures they are under because of complaints of others come from not being in regular contact with presidents and not being knowledgeable of relevant university matters."[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]

### Dialogue Goals

Presidents seek dialogue with bishops to:[^chunk:339d9fda-2982-5c71-85d3-bf9ea8e36730]
1. Understand and appreciate better what they are as Catholic universities
2. See and publicly make known that their colleges and universities play an important—though unique—role among ministries of the Church and that they are not extraneous to its ministry
3. Communicate directly with bishops and they with them rather than through provincials or other third parties about matters of concern

## Key Challenges and Tensions

### Maintaining Identity Amid Secularization

Institutions "struggle to maintain a vibrant identity in the face of increasingly uncatechised Catholics and increasing religious and moral diversity on our campuses."[^chunk:cb2464e4-28b9-5530-b7aa-f402d6bd4953]

### Diversity and Mission Coherence

There is acknowledged tension: "Across Jesuit, Catholic institutions, there is a desire to welcome and incorporate people of diverse religious tradition and no faith tradition. This noble goal of being a place of welcome that values diversity poses challenges to the creation and maintenance of a strong sense of Jesuit and Catholic community and identity."[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9]

Moreover, "Our Universities find it increasingly difficult to say that the life of a Jesuit, Catholic university, in part, depends upon our hiring a significant number of gifted, Catholic faculty and administrators who are conversant in and believing members of the tradition."[^chunk:39f28f20-1190-5d89-b5e4-2bbf33a311e9]

### Academic Standards and Mission Integration

"Our Universities, insofar as they are committed to the standard of 'excellence' in higher education, adopt the standards of the academy that value scholarly productivity in highly differentiated fields of study while devaluing an integrative understanding of knowledge that is rooted ultimately in transcendent questions."[^chunk:387f2052-d956-5e9d-bf9b-68d11a7d4d14]

### Service and Justice in Context

There is challenge in preventing "'Service and Justice' Excised from Their Originating Narrative." While "every university in the United States will espouse the value of service," the Jesuit Catholic university's particular challenge is to show how "'service' and 'justice' mean and require of us as a human community" grounded in the "inspiring Christian narrative."[^chunk:cb2464e4-28b9-5530-b7aa-f402d6bd4953]

## Conclusion

Jesuit colleges and universities maintain their Catholic and Jesuit identity through integrated governance structures, intentional mission communication, comprehensive formation programs, Jesuit presence, and ongoing dialogue with the Church and Society of Jesus. Their boards hold fiduciary responsibility for preserving this character while institutions navigate the complex balance between academic excellence, apostolic mission, and fidelity to Church teaching. The continuation and deepening of Catholic and Jesuit identity remains central to what makes these institutions distinctive within American higher education.
