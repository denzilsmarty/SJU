## Overview

The Mission Priority Examen (MPE) is a self-study and peer review process established by the Association of Jesuit Colleges and Universities (AJCU) and the North American Jesuit Provincials to reaffirm the Jesuit, Catholic mission of Jesuit colleges and universities in the United States and Belize.[^chunk:337d04ee-e27c-580d-b39f-38e2a72436f9] The process was requested by the Superior General of the Society of Jesus.[^chunk:337d04ee-e27c-580d-b39f-38e2a72436f9]

## What is the Mission Priority Examen and How Does It Work?

The MPE is grounded in the examen, a practice of Ignatian reflection.[^chunk:337d04ee-e27c-580d-b39f-38e2a72436f9] The process invites the campus community to reflect upon areas of strength in embodying Jesuit, Catholic identity and opportunities for deepening institutional mission.[^chunk:337d04ee-e27c-580d-b39f-38e2a72436f9] By welcoming AJCU colleagues to support one another's institutional self-reflection, the process aims to foster a more vibrant and collaborative network of Jesuit higher education.[^chunk:337d04ee-e27c-580d-b39f-38e2a72436f9]

### Process Structure

The MPE typically follows a structured timeline spanning multiple months:[^chunk:337d04ee-e27c-580d-b39f-38e2a72436f9]

- **January**: Steering committee convenes
- **February**: Focus group sessions
- **March-April**: Data aggregation and additional focus group sessions
- **May**: Data review and discernment of priorities
- **June/July**: Self-study report drafting
- **August**: Draft revision and discernment
- **September**: University-wide survey and submission to peer team
- **October**: Peer team visit
- **November**: Final submission to AJCU and Society of Jesuits provincial[^chunk:337d04ee-e27c-580d-b39f-38e2a72436f9]

### Community Engagement

The examen process involves broad participation from all sectors of the campus community. At Saint Louis University's 2024-25 MPE, the steering committee convened focus groups representing:[^chunk:337d04ee-e27c-580d-b39f-38e2a72436f9]

- Academic units (College of Arts and Sciences, schools of business, law, medicine, nursing, engineering, education, and others)
- Administrative departments (admissions, human resources, facilities, communications)
- Student and faculty bodies (faculty senate, graduate students, undergraduate organizations)
- Support services (dining, libraries, campus ministry)
- Off-campus constituencies (alumni, donors, community partners, parents)[^chunk:337d04ee-e27c-580d-b39f-38e2a72436f9]

## How Does the Examen Reflection Practice Strengthen Institutional Identity?

The MPE leverages the examen, a foundational Ignatian spiritual practice, to deepen institutional mission alignment. By grounding the process in this reflection practice, participating universities engage their communities in discernment about how their institution embodies Jesuit, Catholic identity.[^chunk:337d04ee-e27c-580d-b39f-38e2a72436f9]

The process strengthens identity through structured reflection on both current strengths and opportunities for growth.[^chunk:337d04ee-e27c-580d-b39f-38e2a72436f9] Rather than external evaluation alone, the examen invites internal community discernment, fostering ownership of mission across all institutional stakeholders. The peer review component adds external perspective while maintaining the collaborative spirit of the Jesuit network, creating accountability and mutual support among institutions committed to similar values.[^chunk:337d04ee-e27c-580d-b39f-38e2a72436f9]

## Leadership and Implementation

### Saint Louis University Example

Saint Louis University launched its second MPE process in December 2023 at the invitation of Thomas P. Greene, S.J., provincial superior of the Central and Southern Province.[^chunk:337d04ee-e27c-580d-b39f-38e2a72436f9] After consultation with David Suwalsky, S.J., vice president for Mission and Identity, President Fred Pestello invited a steering committee representing the university community to begin its work.[^chunk:337d04ee-e27c-580d-b39f-38e2a72436f9] The steering committee was composed of faculty, staff and administrators from every academic unit and was chaired by David Suwalsky, S.J., with Virginia Herbers serving as co-chair.[^chunk:337d04ee-e27c-580d-b39f-38e2a72436f9]
