## Overview

The Ignatian Pedagogical Paradigm (IPP), promulgated by the International Commission on the Apostolate of Jesuit Education (ICAJE) in 1993, organizes teaching and learning through five recursive dynamics: context, experience, reflection, **action**, and **evaluation**. This article examines the fourth and fifth dynamics in detail, addresses the rationale for their definitions, and explains how together they close—and reopen—the pedagogical cycle.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## Action: The Fourth Dynamic

### Definition

In the IPP, action is not synonymous with activity. The 1993 source text defines action as beginning in **interior appropriation**—a judgment, choice, priority, value, or commitment that follows from reflection—which may then become outward conduct.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] The executive synthesis of the Saint Joseph's University (SJU) working document states this plainly among its twelve governing conclusions:

> *Action includes interior choice and outward practice. A revised judgment, professional commitment, new habit, research question, design decision, civic response, or direct service may all count as action.*[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Paragraphs 59–62 of the 1993 document establish the twofold character: reflection is incomplete if it produces understanding without considering response; action is irresponsible if it bypasses reflection.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

### Why Interior Appropriation Is Included

The inclusion of interior appropriation alongside outward conduct reflects the IPP's Ignatian anthropological roots. Ignatius of Loyola's *Spiritual Exercises* privilege inwardly grasped and "tasted" truth over mere information accumulation, and the director is called to protect the exercitant's freedom rather than manufacture outcomes.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Translated pedagogically, this means that a student who lacks the authority, time, or resources to solve a structural problem during a course may nonetheless perform genuine action through a clarified professional commitment, a revised research design, a refusal of an unethical practice, or a plan for further inquiry.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Conversely, visible activity without changed understanding is, in IPP terms, pedagogically empty.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

This also guards against two symmetric distortions: requiring every course to prescribe activism, and rewarding only students with more time, money, or mobility to undertake outward action.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

### Forms of Action in Higher Education

The SJU document lists a wide range of legitimate actions:[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

- Applying a concept to a novel problem
- Revising a design, argument, diagnosis, or performance
- Changing a habit of attention or collaboration
- Making a professional or vocational choice
- Creating something useful, beautiful, or truth-bearing
- Serving with a community partner under reciprocal terms
- Communicating findings to a public audience
- Advocating, organizing, or participating civically
- Changing a policy or institutional process
- Defining a new research question
- Committing to learn, consult, or repair before taking further action

### Discerned, Proportionate, and Accountable Action

Action should be related to the learner's **role, knowledge, power, and responsibilities**. It should distinguish urgency from haste, intent from impact, charity from justice, and service *for* others from solidarity *with* others. Where action affects communities, evaluation should include those communities' account of the consequences.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

The SJU design canvas asks four questions before specifying action:[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

1. What decision, creation, practice, communication, service, research, or commitment follows?
2. Is it proportionate to the learner's knowledge, role, and power?
3. Who participates in defining the action and who bears its risks?
4. What accountability, continuity, or repair is required?

### Distortions and Guardrails

Civic action must not be reduced to one political position. Faculty should avoid promising community impact a course cannot sustain and should plan for continuity, reciprocity, and closure.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] DeFeo's 2009 study of U.S. Jesuit teaching and learning centers found that action was less prominent in actual implementation than context, experience, and reflection—a documented imbalance SJU should actively counteract.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## Evaluation: The Fifth Dynamic

### Definition

The IPP's 1993 source text affirms conventional assessment of academic mastery and asks educators to attend also to **broader human growth**. It suggests self-evaluation, mentoring, journals, and dialogue within relationships of trust. Evaluation provides feedback, uncovers blind spots, and—crucially—**begins a new cycle**.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Paragraphs 63–70 of the 1993 document govern this dynamic.

The SJU document identifies four objects of evaluation:[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

| Object | What is assessed |
|---|---|
| Academic learning | Knowledge and skills demonstrated |
| Quality of judgment | Integration of evidence, values, perspectives, and consequences |
| Action and impact | What was done and with what results |
| Educational design | What the teacher, program, or institution should change |

### What Makes an Evaluation Task 'Authentic'

Authentic evaluation tasks in the IPP framework are those that make thinking and judgment visible while preserving productive intellectual friction.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] Useful practices include:[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

- Authentic performance tasks
- Portfolios
- Oral defenses
- Simulations
- Structured self-assessment
- Peer and partner feedback
- Iterative drafts
- Follow-up data on impact
- Program-level review

Authentic tasks also attend to **unintended consequences and opportunity costs**. When no outward action is feasible, assessment can examine the learner's proposed action, reasoning, constraints, and plan for accountability.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Assessable evidence should include: specificity of observation; use of relevant concepts; acknowledgment of uncertainty; examination of assumptions; engagement with affected perspectives; proportionality of response; revision after feedback; and analysis of consequences.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

### Assessment Without Moral Scoring

The IPP's formative ambition creates a serious risk: converting accompaniment into surveillance, or assigning grades to interior life. The SJU standard is explicit:[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

> *Faculty may assess the quality of ethical analysis, reflective practice, professional responsibility, collaboration, perspective-taking, and evidence-based action when these are legitimate learning outcomes. They should not claim to measure the state of a student's conscience, compassion, faith, political virtue, or human worth.*

Rubrics should describe **observable qualities in work**, not identity claims about the person.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

### Why the Rubric Assesses Design Quality Rather Than Virtue

The SJU design-review rubric evaluates a **design**, not the virtue of an educator or student.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] This is a deliberate structural choice. The five dimensions of the rubric—purpose, context, experience, reflection, action, evaluation, relationship, and evidence—are each assessed on an Emerging / Developing / Strong scale that describes observable qualities of the educational design:[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

| Dimension | Strong descriptor |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Integrated, specific, assessable without moral scoring |
| Context | Learner, disciplinary, social, institutional, and power contexts shape the design |
| Experience | Significant direct or vicarious encounter engages disciplined attention and appropriate affect |
| Reflection | Evidence, assumptions, affect, perspectives, consequences, and discernment are integrated |
| Action | Interior and/or outward action is proportionate, free, role-aware, and accountable |
| Evaluation | Mastery, judgment, consequence, equity, and design are reviewed; feedback begins a new cycle |
| Relationship | Trust, clarity, availability, learner agency, and appropriate boundaries embody *cura personalis* |
| Evidence | Claims are calibrated; multiple forms of evidence drive revision |

The rubric is intended to support peer consultation and improvement, not to become a compliance score detached from context.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19] Assessing design rather than virtue also protects both educators and students: faculty cannot be evaluated for failing to produce a particular interior state in students, and students cannot be graded on faith, conscience, or political conformity.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## The Recursive Link: How Action and Evaluation Feed Back into the Cycle

The IPP is explicitly recursive and scalable. The five dynamics are not a linear sequence; they form a spiral.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] Evaluation does not end the process—it **reopens** it. The governing table in the SJU document describes evaluation's governing question as: *What was learned, what changed, what resulted, and what should happen next?*[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

Several mechanisms sustain this feedback loop:

- **Evaluation becomes new experience.** A post-decision review, a returned portfolio, or partner feedback on a community engagement project constitutes a concrete encounter that reengages the learner's intellect and affect, becoming the raw material of the next cycle's experience.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **New context emerges.** The consequences of action and the findings of evaluation alter what the learner knows, values, fears, and can do—changing the very context in which the next cycle begins.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]
- **Design itself is evaluated.** The fourth object of evaluation—educational design—means that what educators learn from one iteration shapes how they redesign context and experience for the next. The SJU rubric's strongest descriptor of evaluation reads: *feedback begins a new cycle*.[^chunk:514c92fa-872f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19][^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]
- **The sample learning cycle.** The SJU document's illustrative health-resource allocation case ends with a structured post-decision review; feedback from that review becomes the context for a *revised* case—a concrete instantiation of the loop.[^chunk:6b47b6d8-cbae-5d9f-b6f7-2ec82ea750b0]

The executive synthesis captures the formative aspiration: when the cycle repeats across a course, program, or career, it builds **habits of attending carefully, entering experience, reflecting deeply, choosing responsibly, and learning from results**—which is the process by which the IPP contributes to the lifelong integration called formation.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## Relationship to the Four Cs

The 2015 ICAJE document *Human Excellence* names four formative qualities—**conscience, competence, compassion, and commitment**—toward which Jesuit education aims.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300] These are not stages of the IPP and not additional dynamics; they are qualities that repeated action-and-evaluation cycles, well designed, may cultivate over time. Action supports the development of competence and commitment; evaluation, when it examines ethical reasoning and consequence, supports conscience and compassion. The Four Cs describe what learners may become through the process; the IPP describes the process itself.[^chunk:9a34ed4d-6e02-5417-886f-cc974e519300]

## Common Questions

**Does action require service or advocacy?**
No. Action includes interior choice and responsible application. It must fit the discipline, role, and context. Service and advocacy are important forms when educationally and ethically appropriate.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

**Can the IPP be assessed?**
Design quality, learning processes, demonstrated capabilities, and consequences can be assessed. A person's interior moral or spiritual state should not be scored. Institutional claims require evidence proportional to their strength.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]

**Must every lesson use all five elements?**
No. The paradigm should be visible across an appropriate learning arc. Some moments primarily provide experience; others deepen reflection or evaluate action. Over a course or program, all five should be meaningfully present and connected.[^chunk:514c92fa-878f-5bb4-b7dd-a668e30ffd19]
